## 3. Prior art for the guard form — established, no novelty claimed

* **Strict positivity is the definition, not a convention.** `‖z‖_w = max_i w_i |z_i|` is a
  norm iff `w > 0` componentwise; at `w_i = 0` it is a seminorm (the component is deleted),
  at `w_i < 0` it is not even non-negative. The module's own docstring names the norm and
  never states the precondition. Guarding a stated-but-unchecked precondition is the exact
  shape of legs 151/154.
* **Confirm-good rather than detect-bad polarity** — SEI CERT NUM07-J: ordered comparisons
  all return false on NaN, so `np.any(w <= 0)` fails *open* on NaN while `np.all(w > 0)`
  fails *closed*. This is the one-token polarity move **leg 152** adopted for
  `solver/hl_rescaled.py` after measuring that leg 117's prescribed guard imported the
  blind spot with the fix. Measured again here in §5, on this module's inputs.
* **The nearest in-repo template is leg 142/NSA V2** — a negative scalar silently deleting
  part of a norm — and **leg 152's G3**, non-positive scales. Both already landed.
* **Error type**: `BorderedHLDomainError(ValueError)`, following
  `HLRescaledDomainError` / `DecayCollocationDomainError` / `CriticalDissipationDomainError`
  / `HilbertPointwiseDomainError`. The `ValueError` base is load-bearing so callers already
  catching `ValueError` are unaffected.
* **Escape hatch by design, per leg 135**: `on_nonpositive="allow"` reproduces the
  pre-repair arithmetic exactly, so leg 198's measured magnitudes stay reproducible after
  the repair that its own finding authorised. Same decision leg 152 made
  (`on_invalid="warn"`) and leg 151 declined; here leg 198's battery is the reason to take it.

No mathematical or methodological novelty is claimed by this leg.

## 5. LEG 198's PRESCRIBED PREDICATE IS INSUFFICIENT — AND SO IS THE GATE'S OWN WORD

Leg 198 prescribes "reject non-positive `w_l`, `w_om`, `w_r` (and non-positive `nu`)" and
supports it with the claim that **"zero weights are REJECTED (`ZeroDivisionError`)"**, which
it uses to argue the module "already halts at the boundary of the admissible set". Both the
claim and the gate's word **"non-negativity"** are measured wrong here.

Pre-repair behaviour of `induced_sup_norm(M, [1,1], w_col)` on `M = [[1,100],[3,4]]`,
whose true weighted norm is **101.0**:

| `w_col` | returned | exception | warning | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(1, 1)` | **101.0** | — | — | control, correct |
| `(1, −1)` | **−1.0** | — | — | leg 198's headline, reproduces exactly |
| `(1, −1e−9)` | **−3.999999997e+09** | — | — | reproduces exactly |
| `(1, 0)` | **+inf** | **none** | `RuntimeWarning` | **leg 198's "ZeroDivisionError" is NOT this path** |
| `(1, nan)` | **nan** | — | — | non-finite |
| `(1, +inf)` | **3.0** | **none** | **none** | **finite, unwarned, understated 33.7×** |

Two corrections fall out, both of which change the predicate:

1. **`w = 0` is not rejected by the module.** The `ZeroDivisionError` leg 198 observed comes
   from `certificate_constants`'s *scalar Python* divisions (`Xmax/w_l`, `1.0/w_om`,
   `1.0/w_r`), not from the norm — inside `induced_sup_norm` the division is a NumPy array
   op and yields `+inf` with a warning. So the "already halts at the boundary" framing is
   path-specific, and a guard written to leg 198's literal text would leave the `w = 0`
   seminorm reachable through every direct `induced_sup_norm` call.
2. **A literal *non-negativity* guard (`w < 0`) is the weakest of the three candidates** and
   admits both remaining bad cases. Measured over the same inputs:

| predicate | negative | zero | NaN | −inf | +inf | positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `any(w < 0)` — the gate's literal word | reject | **admit** | **admit** | reject | **admit** | admit |
| `any(w <= 0)` — detect-bad | reject | reject | **admit** | reject | **admit** | admit |
| `not all(isfinite(w) & (w > 0))` — **adopted** | reject | reject | reject | reject | reject | admit |

The adopted predicate is `np.all(np.isfinite(w) & (w > 0))` in confirm-good polarity. Each
clause is measured necessary: `w > 0` for the headline and the seminorm, `isfinite` for NaN
(which `>` admits) and for `+inf`.

**`+inf` is declared as an IN-KIND EXTENSION, measured, not assumed** — the same shape leg
152 declared for `delta <= 0`. It is admitted today, returns a **finite, unwarned,
understated** `3.0` against a true `101.0`, and it understates by *deleting a component of
the norm*, which is leg 142/NSA V2's mechanism and the certificate-fabricating direction.
It is inside "the border-weight guard" because it is the same predicate on the same weights
at the same site, not a second repair.

**Also guarded: `w_row`.** Leg 198's mechanism is stated on `w_col`, but `w_row` multiplies
the row sums and a negative entry understates by the identical mechanism. Pre-repair,
`induced_sup_norm([[1,100],[3,4]], [1,−1], [1,1])` returns `101.0` — *correct by luck*,
because `np.max` happens to select the unflipped row. That luck is matrix-dependent and the
runner exhibits a matrix where it fails. Same predicate, same site, in-kind.

round-off-level quantities is not stable across BLAS build, threading or ordering, so the
`Z_1` *ratios* are environment-dependent while the `Z_1` *verdict* (negative-weight `Z_1`
collapses by 8–12 orders) is not. This is standing-discipline lesson 86 — a quantity
dominated by its own evaluation error is a statement about the code — applied to leg 198's
own headline column.

**Leg 198's verdict is untouched**: the sign flip still understates `Z_1` by ~1e8–1e12×,
still drives `Z_2` negative, and still converts a non-closing certificate into a closing
one. Only the two quoted `Z_1` ratios are re-measured here, and this leg quotes its own.
It is also the reason the bit-identity differential must run **pre- and post-repair modules
in the SAME process** and compare **float64 bytes with `==`**, not `allclose` — the method
