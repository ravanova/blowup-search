### `arXiv:2411.01891` — the primary candidate, and it disqualifies itself in its own abstract

Abstract, l.25–35, verbatim:

> *"We present **exact pole dynamics solutions** to the generalized Constantin-Lax-Majda (gCLM)
> equation in a periodic geometry with dissipation −Λ^σ … We derive new periodic solutions for
> **a = 0 and 1/2 and σ = 0 and 1**, for which a closed collection of (periodically repeated) poles
> evolve in the complex plane."*

The apparatus census over all 2704 lines: `interval arithmetic` **0**, `computer-assisted` **0**,
`computer assisted` **0**, `validated numerics` **0**, `Newton-Kantorovich` **0**, `radii
polynomial` **0**, `INTLAB` **0**, `rigorous enclosure` **0**, `verified enclosure` **0**. The lone
`computer-aided` hit is **line 54**, and it is about **somebody else**:

> *"…and Chen and Hou [2], who give a **computer-aided proof** of finite-time blowup for C^∞ initial
> data."*

**The method is closed-form pole dynamics plus phase-plane analysis, not a certificate.** Where
closed form runs out they say so — §3.2, l.2309: *"When σ = 1 closed form analytical solutions are
not available, and we resort to a **phase-plane analysis** using special variables."*

**Dissipation-exponent coverage, which is the gate's own qualifier.** Their Table 1 (l.2336–2356)
tabulates `(a, σ)` against `L²` and `B₀`. Exact pole-dynamics solutions exist at **σ ∈ {0, 1}
only**, at **a ∈ {0, 1/2} only**. Every `σ ∈ (1, ∞)` cell — which is where **σ = 2** lives — reads
**`GEIVP`**: *"global existence of solutions to initial value problem for small data"*. Every
`a ∉ {0, 1/2}` column reads **`NT`**: *"neither pole dynamics solutions nor our general theory
applies."* So even as *analysis*, σ = 2 on the circle is a small-data global-existence cell — the
**opposite** side of the question from a blow-up profile.

**And they say the γ = 2 case defeated them, in as many words** (l.2358–2363, verbatim):

> *"When a = 0 and σ = 2, a pole dynamics solution for the problem on the real line was found by
> Schochet [6]. … **Despite significant effort, we have been unable to generalize this solution to
> the periodic domain.** However, the general theory developed here paradoxically shows that
> collapse in the Wiener algebra occurs more readily for the problem on the real line with σ = 2
> than it does in the periodic problem for σ = 0!"*

**Their own stated next step is numerics, not certification** (l.2364–2369, verbatim):

> *"In future work, we intend to **numerically investigate** finite-time singularity formation in
> the gCLM equation with dissipation over the full range of a and for more general values of σ,
> both for problems on the real line and periodic domain."*

### `arXiv:2504.14346` — the near-miss, and it is on the γ = 2 object

Ambrose, Lopes Filho & Nussenzveig Lopes, *Existence and analyticity of solutions of nonlinear
parabolic model equations with singular data*. §5 (l.671–673) is literally the dissipative CLM
equation `ω̃_t = ω̃H(ω̃) − νΛ^σ ω̃`, and §6 (l.905) adds the advection term — the gCLM family, at
**general σ > 0, which includes σ = 2**. Apparatus census over 1632 lines: **0** on every one of the
ten terms, `computer-aided` included.

It is on the wrong side of the gate in the same two ways `2411.01891` is, and one more:

* **direction** — the results are *global existence* (l.693–694: *"for any σ > 0, sufficiently small
  periodic data in Y^{−σ/2} leads to existence of a **global solution**"*) and *analyticity* for
  σ ≥ 1. Not blow-up.
* **object** — `self-similar` **0**, `blowup` **0**, `blow-up` **1** across the whole paper. There is
  no profile in it to certify.
* **method** — pure analysis; its 2 hits for `contraction mapping` are a Banach fixed point in a
  Wiener-algebra scale, in exact arithmetic, with no enclosure of anything.

runner fails the run if either direction ever stops being true.
