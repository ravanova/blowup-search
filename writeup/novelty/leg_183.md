## 2. Xu §8's interval-arithmetic no-go, VERBATIM, with its hypotheses located

The sentence, in full, from p. 33–34 (`2607.19762.txt` l. 1922–1929) — quoted complete rather
than clipped at the memorable clause, because the clipped form is what makes it look broader
than it is:

> *"Likewise a naive interval-arithmetic resolvent enclosure is unavailable: a weight is a
> change of norm, eigenvalues are norm-invariant, and the truncated `L_a` genuinely carries
> essential-smear eigenvalues inside the strip (the compactified grid mis-renders the
> continuous line at the wrong real part), so no weighted enclosure can exclude them. The
> consequence is that the rigorous discrete exclusion must be analytical (the `a = 0` anchor
> plus continuation) rather than a black-box computer-assisted enclosure; where a
> computer-assisted proof does enter, it must first be given a spectrally correct rendering of
> the essential spectrum (a non-periodic log-Mellin operator at high resolution feeding an
> argument-principle count), not applied to the raw grid."*

Read in isolation, "**no weighted enclosure can exclude them**" sounds unbounded. Read with its
own antecedents, every one of its terms is pinned by the rest of the paper. **Its exact
hypotheses, each located:**

**(H-Xu-1) The object is the *truncated* `L_a`** — a finite matrix, not the closed operator.
The concrete instrument is §7 "Linearized spectrum" (i), l. 1834–1839: *"A dense
finite-difference Jacobian of the rescaled residual on the compactified grid (`N = 1024`-`8192`)
at the sampled advections `a = 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.65`"*, with a *"centroid-frequency filter
(`< N/16`)"* removing near-Nyquist pollution. **The sampled advections are all `a > 0`.** The
paper's `a = 0` case needs no enclosure at all: Theorem 2 (l. 566–572) already excludes discrete
spectrum there in closed form, and §8's own sentence puts `a = 0` on the *analytic* side of the
dichotomy — *"the rigorous discrete exclusion must be analytical (the `a = 0` anchor plus
continuation)"*.

**(H-Xu-2) The task is spectral exclusion from a region.** "Them" = *"essential-smear
eigenvalues inside the strip"*. The strip is Proposition 2's, eq (3.5) l. 408:
`sigma_ess(L_0) ⊇ {lambda : -1/2 <= Re lambda <= 3/2}` on the maximal `L^2` realization. The
task the enclosure would serve is `n_disc(a)`, the *"integer winding count ... along a
rectangular contour"* (§3.2, §7 "Evans-determinant count"). **It is a statement about locating
spectrum over a two-dimensional region of the `lambda`-plane.**

**(H-Xu-3) The discretization imposes no origin condition, so it renders the WRONG
realization.** This is the load-bearing hypothesis and it is stated in the abstract itself
(l. 30–31): *"A realization dichotomy identifies the smear that discretizations without an
origin condition place inside the strip as the faithful spectrum of the maximal `L^2`
realization, which origin-`H^2` removes."* §8's parenthesis is the same fact — *"the
compactified grid mis-renders the continuous line at the wrong real part"* — and §7 (ii)
confirms the diagnosis on Xu's own instrument: *"By the realization dichotomy (Proposition 2)
this fixed-lower-cutoff grid renders the maximal-`L^2` realization: at `a = 0.5` its rightmost
mode sits at `Re = +2.4931`, within 0.3% of the plain-`L^2` origin indicial line `+5/2`"*.
**The smear is not numerical error. It is the true spectrum of the realization the grid
actually built.**

**(H-Xu-4) The quantified class is WEIGHTS (norms), and the mechanism is norm-INVARIANCE.**
*"a weight is a change of norm, eigenvalues are norm-invariant"*. A weight acts by diagonal
similarity; similarity does not move eigenvalues; the eigenvalues are genuinely in the strip by
(H-Xu-3); therefore no choice of weight removes them. **The argument is valid exactly and only
for quantities that are similarity-invariant.**

**(H-Xu-5) It is a statement about a *black-box* route, with the prerequisite named.** *"rather
than a black-box computer-assisted enclosure; where a computer-assisted proof does enter, it
must first be given a spectrally correct rendering of the essential spectrum ... not applied to
the raw grid."* Xu does not say computer-assisted proof is impossible on his object; he says it
must not be applied to the raw grid, and names the prerequisite.

**What §8's no-go therefore DOES cover, stated positively so the question closes:** *for the
`a > 0` continuation task, applying a weighted interval-arithmetic resolvent enclosure to the
raw compactified-grid truncation of `L_a` — a discretization which imposes no origin condition
and hence faithfully renders the maximal-`L^2` realization, in which the strip genuinely is
spectrum — cannot exclude the strip eigenvalues, because a weight is a similarity and
eigenvalues are similarity-invariant.*

**Xu's own novelty claim, for the record** (l. 127, and it names nothing adjacent to NGX):
*"The novelty lies in the completeness of the point spectrum, the realization dichotomy, and
the constructive resolvent."*

## 4. The comparison — five disjointness axes, each separately checkable

| axis | Xu §8's no-go | Theorem NGX (leg 127) | disjoint? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. task** | exclude eigenvalues from a 2-D region (the strip) of the `lambda`-plane; serve the winding count `n_disc(a)` | bound `Z_1 = ‖I − AL‖_w` for the Newton–Kantorovich/radii-polynomial contraction, at the single point `lambda = 0` (invertibility of `L` itself) | **yes** |
| **B. quantified class** | over **weights** (norms): "no *weighted enclosure*" | over **bounded approximate inverses `A`** on one fixed space, `A21` free | **yes** |
| **C. mechanism** | similarity-**invariance**: a weight cannot move an eigenvalue | a similarity-**variant** quantity: `sigma_min` is `0` in `ell^1_w` at `s < 1` and `>= 1/2` (post-modulation) on origin-`H^2` **by Xu's own Theorems 1–3** | **yes — and this one is a proved impossibility, not merely a difference** |
| **D. space** | `L^2`-type realizations reached by weights on a compactified grid; the paper contains **no** `ell^1`, no weighted sequence space, no Fourier-coefficient space (leg 171's negative locators N1/N2; re-checked here, §6) | weighted `ell^1` over odd-sine coefficients, `w_k = (1+k)^s`, `s < 1` | **yes** |
| **E. object** | the **raw** grid truncation, *"without an origin condition"*, at sampled `a ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.65}` | the **bordered** `a = 0` matrix, carrying a **gauge row that is Xu's own `v'(0)` origin functional** (leg 181, `2d89233`) — i.e. not a raw grid, and at the one advection §8 puts on the *analytic* side | **yes** |

**Axis C is the decisive one and deserves stating on its own.** Xu §8's argument, in its
entirety, is: *the quantity in question is invariant under change of norm, therefore no choice
of norm helps.* Apply that template to `Z_1` and it is **false on Xu's own results**: `Z_1` and
`sigma_min` are not similarity-invariant, and the very same operator that has `sigma_min = 0` in
`ell^1_w` at `s < 1` has a spectral gap of `1/2` on origin-`H^2`. This is not this leg's
inference — it is exactly the constraint leg 127's own novelty pass already imposed on itself
(Finding 2, clause 1: *"Any no-go proved here is a statement about the `ell^1_w`
Fourier-coefficient realization, not about the `a = 0` CLM linearization as an operator"*).
**Leg 127 was already, in 2026-08, disclaiming precisely the generality that Xu §8 would have
pre-empted.** The scoping that protects the claim was in place before the question was asked.

**The runner makes axis C falsifiable on this repository's own matrix** (lesson 90 — a control
that cannot come out differently is not a control). Pre-registered here, before it was coded:
take leg 127's `bordered_linearization(K)` read-only, apply the weight as the diagonal
similarity the module already uses, and report **two** quantities across `s`:

* the **eigenvalues** of the scaled matrix, which must be invariant in `s` to machine precision
  — *this is Xu §8's mechanism, reproduced on this repository's object*;
* `sigma_min = 1/‖L^{-1}‖_w`, which must move by orders of magnitude in `s` — *this is NGX's
  quantity, and its motion is what Xu §8's argument cannot reach.*

**How it can come out the other way:** if `sigma_min` came back invariant in `s` alongside the
eigenvalues, Xu §8's norm-invariance argument **would** transfer to NGX's quantity, and this
leg's answer would have to be *partially* or *fully* rather than *not at all*. The control is
therefore live in both directions, and the gate answer is contingent on its outcome, not
decorative.

## 5. The one genuine adjacency, recorded rather than smoothed

Refusing to overstate the disjointness is as much a duty as refusing to overstate the novelty.
There is a real kinship, and it is **mechanistic, not logical**:

* Xu Proposition 2 says: **drop the origin condition and the operator loses its gap** — the
  strip fills, and invertibility is destroyed.
* Leg 181 (`2d89233`) measured: the origin functional `v'(0)` — half of Xu's own modulation
  projection, eq (A.13) — has **divergent dual norm on `ell^1_w` exactly for `s < 1`**
  (`sup_k k/(1+k)^s`; finite iff `s >= 1`), which is **exactly** the range in which leg 127
  measures `sigma_min → 0`.

So both obstructions are, at the level of mechanism, "the origin condition is not boundedly
imposable here". **That is a convergence worth recording and it is not a pre-emption**, for
three reasons, each checkable: (i) it is a kinship with Xu **Proposition 2**, not with §8 — the
gate asks about §8; (ii) Prop. 2's conclusion is `sigma_ess ⊇` strip **in `L^2`**, a spectral
statement about a different space, and no derivation of NGX's `ell^1_w` statement from it exists
in the paper or anywhere else; (iii) leg 181 is *this repository's* observation, landed two legs
ago, not Xu's — Xu never connects his origin condition to a weighted sequence space, because he
never writes one down. **Reported as a convergence, claimed by nobody, escalated by nobody.**
