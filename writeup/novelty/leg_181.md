## 2. What is new to the world: nothing

This is a reading of one published paper plus one derived criterion applied to this
repository's own modules. No new mathematics is claimed anywhere in this leg.

Two external checks, both confirming that the *technique* is standard and that the
*transferability question* is this repository's private one, not an open problem anyone is
working on:

- **Modulation is not Xu's technique and Xu says so.** He calls it "the standard modulation"
  every time (three times: abstract, §3.2, §A.1) and credits the method to
  Elgindi–Ghoul–Masmoudi: "*proved by Elgindi, Ghoul, and Masmoudi [15] using modulation, near
  a known family of self-similar solutions*" (§1, p. 2). That is
  [arXiv:1906.05811](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05811) / *Analysis & PDE* **14** (2021) 891,
  [Stable self-similar blow-up for a family of nonlocal transport equations](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/analysis-and-pde/volume-14/issue-3/Stable-self-similar-blow-up--for-a-family-of/10.2140/apde.2021.14.891.short).
  **There is no "Xu modulation technique" to transfer.** Whatever the leg finds, it cannot be
  a novel-technique claim.
- **The `ℓ¹` analogue of modulation is also standard, and is called a phase condition.**
  Quotienting a symmetry direction inside a radii-polynomial / weighted-`ℓ¹` certificate by
  giving it its own unfolding parameter is routine practice — e.g. the gap-soliton CAP
  [arXiv:2503.04701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04701) carries "a weight `ν` for the norm and a
  scaling factor `l` for the phase condition"; the Shimizu–Morioka case study
  [arXiv:2605.07500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07500) is a current survey of the same
  machinery. **This repository has already done it** — leg 52's bordering *is* the phase
  condition, aimed at the far-field direction rather than at a symmetry mode. So the
  "could the same move be applied in the `ℓ¹_w` line" question is not a novelty question at
  all; it is a *does it help* question, and §3/§4 answer it as one.

## 3. What is new **to this repository**

Four items, and nothing else may be claimed.

- **(N1) What modulation *is*, as an operator.** No file in this repository writes it down.
  Xu does, at **eq (A.13), §A.4, p. 37**: `P v = v(0) + v′(0) y`, `Q v = v − v(0) − v′(0) y`,
  with the reading "*Thus Q is the regular part at the collapse point: it removes the zeroth
  and first Taylor coefficients of v at y = 0, matching the origin-regularity mechanism of the
  realization X of Theorem 1.*" Modulation is a **rank-2 spectral projection onto the two
  origin Taylor functionals**, nothing more.
- **(N2) The transferability criterion**, which is this leg's own one-line abstraction of
  Xu's (A.13) + Definition A.2 + §A.4 and which Xu nowhere states as a criterion:

  > Modulation is available on a realization `Z` of this operator **iff** (a) the two exact
  > symmetry modes `y Ω′` and `Ω + c_l y Ω′` lie in `Z`, **and** (b) the two origin Taylor
  > functionals `v ↦ v(0)` and `v ↦ v′(0)` are **bounded** on `Z`.

  Condition (b) is the whole content, and Xu supplies his own worked failure of it (`Y_θ`,
  banked by leg 171 — cited, not claimed). The criterion is cheap and mechanical, which is why
  §4 escalates *the criterion*, not a construction.
- **(N3) "after modulation" is not a hypothesis.** It costs nothing and it buys nothing beyond
  deleting two already-known eigenvalues. Legs 127/163 have cited "gap 1/2 after modulation"
  as though the clause carried weight; read at depth it does not. What *would* carry weight —
  the dynamic closure — is **explicitly open**: "*Three inputs are still missing, and none is
  supplied here: … and the modulation closure that removes the two symmetry directions along
  the flow*" (§8, p. 33), and again at §6.1, p. 31: "*the required weighted dissipation-form
  bound, nonlinear estimate, and modulation closure are open, Appendix A*". No file in this
  repository records that the modulation closure is open.
- **(N4) The criterion applied to `ℓ¹_w`** — the leg's actual question. Carried out
  arithmetically by the runner (§4.3) and reported in `experiments/journal/leg_181.md`.

3. **The criterion, evaluated arithmetically, in a form that can report the other answer.**
   Condition (b) for `ℓ¹_w` with `w_k = (1+k)^s` is a dual-norm computation with a closed form:
   in the odd sine basis `v = Σ b_k sin kθ` the two functionals are `v(0) = 0` (free, by
   oddness) and `v′(0) = Σ_k k b_k`, whose weighted-`ℓ¹` dual norm is `sup_k k/(1+k)^s`.
   **Pre-registered prediction, before running:** finite iff `s ≥ 1`. If it comes out finite at
   `s = 0.3` the reading is wrong. A second, independent arithmetic check, using
   `solver/spectral_certificate.py` **read-only**: the overlap between leg 127's near-null
   witness (`tail_right_null`, supported on modes `K+1..M`) and the modulation span
   `span{e₁, e₂}` — **predicted exactly 0**, i.e. modulation is a no-op on the obstruction even
   where it is defined.
