## 2. THE CONVENTION AXES — what "convention" can even mean here, named before measuring

A classification is only as good as the set of alternatives it sweeps. Four candidate axes,
enumerated from the record:

| axis | what varies | prior measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **κ** — the `X`-block / border relative weight | the constant multiplying the coefficient Gram `G = I + J⁴` on the `X` block, with the border amplitude at weight 1 | leg 249 W5 swept the *border* weight over `10⁻²…10²` (ratio **12.509**); leg 277 P1 proved `κ` on the `X` block **is the same computation** as border weight `1/κ` (`2.14e−13`) |
| **the norm reading** | Xu Definition 4.1's half-line `(4.2)` (`κ = π`) vs the "equivalent" full-line odd-`H²(R)` (`κ = 2π`) vs the repository's unweighted `κ = 1` | leg 277 §3, measured `1.48e−08` / `2.33e−09` |
| **the whitening route** | Cholesky vs `eigh` symmetric square root vs leg 249's rescaling | leg 249 W3 `max_route_spread` `8.4e−13 … 1.8e−11`; leg 277 `3.7e−13` at `N = 512` |
| **the realification gauge** | the diagonal unitary of leg 249 §8 (`m`, `ℓ` purely imaginary) | leg 277 checked it at `5.9e−14 / 2.8e−15 / 2.4e−13` |

**Axis 2 is a sub-case of axis 1** (it just picks `κ ∈ {1, π, 2π}`), so the live sweep is
one-dimensional. Axes 3 and 4 are computational-route choices, and whether the certificate has
**exactly one** live convention axis is itself a claim this leg tests rather than assumes.

## 3. THE CLASSIFICATION, as three classes and not two

The dispatch asks for two classes. The record already contains a quantity that fits neither
cleanly: leg 277 measured `‖ℓ‖_{X*}` **moving** under `κ` (`0.8874 → 0.3540`) but moving by an
**exact closed-form law**, `κ^{−1/2}` to `9.25e−09`. That is neither convention-free nor
convention-relative-without-a-law. The classification is therefore:

* **CF — convention-free.** Invariant under the whole `κ` sweep, with a *mechanism* (`κ`
  multiplies domain and range Grams alike and cancels) and a measured invariance magnitude.
* **CX — convention-relative, exactly convertible.** Moves, but by a proved closed-form
  power of `κ`, verified to a measured residual. The quoted digit plus the law is a complete
  disclosure.
* **CR — convention-relative, no law.** Moves, and *not* by any power of `κ` — leg 277's P3
  measured `0.46287` against `κ^{−1/2} = 0.39894` and `κ^{−1/4} = 0.63162`, and leg 249's sweep
  is **non-monotone** (peaks at `w = 10`, falls back `31.75 %` by `w = 100`), which no power law
  does. These need their sweep range disclosed, and a named pinning convention.

Reporting `CX` as if it were `CR` would overstate the defect; reporting it as `CF` would
understate it. The three-class split is the leg's methodological contribution and is registered
here, before any number, so it cannot be fitted to the results.

## 4. FOUR PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS

Registered now, resolved in the journal — each can come out the other way.

* **Q1.** Every quantity that carries **no border coordinate** (`B1`–`B5`, `E1`–`E3`, `D3`) is
  **CF** to `≤ 1e−7` relative over the full `κ` sweep, because `κ` cancels between domain and
  range. *Falsifiable:* any of them moving above that threshold refutes it.
* **Q2.** `A1`–`A3`, `A6` are **CR** — non-monotone in `κ`, matching no power to better than
  a few percent. *Falsifiable:* a clean power fit would make them `CX`.
* **Q3.** `D1` is **CX** with exponent exactly `−1/2`; `D2` (the `ℓ²` companion) is **CF**,
  since `ℓ²` does not carry the `X` Gram at all. *Falsifiable:* either half.
* **Q4 — the one with a consequence.** `A4` (the ladder's **relative spread**, `0.139 %`) is a
  *ratio* of `σ_min`s at different `N` under the *same* `κ`, so it should be far closer to **CF**
  than `A1` is. **PUB2's §3.5 argument rests on the ladder's flattening, not on `0.0908`** — if
  `A4` is `CF`, then the load-bearing claim is convention-free even though its headline digit is
  not, and leg 277 §7's "the gate answer stands unchanged" acquires a *measured* basis rather
  than an argued one. *Falsifiable:* `A4` moving materially across the sweep would mean the
  flattening claim itself needs the convention disclosed.

**A fifth question, not a prediction, because the answer is not guessable.** PUB2 §4.5
reconciles leg 163 with leg 176 by the inequality **`0.0908 ≤ 0.71465`**. `E5` is a ratio of
`X`-norms with no border, so Q1 predicts it is **CF** — while `A1` is **CR** and moves. **Does
the reconciliation survive the sweep?** `sup_κ σ_min(κ)` over leg 249's measured range is
`0.13580`, comfortably under `0.71465`; but leg 249's sweep is `10⁻²…10²` and non-monotone, and
nobody has asked what happens outside it. This leg sweeps wider and reports `sup_κ σ_min /
0.71465` as a magnitude. A comparison between a convention-relative number and a
convention-free one is exactly the defect family legs 270/276/277 each found one instance of.

## 6. Outside prior art

The question "which constants of a computer-assisted certificate are normalization-invariant" is
not one the radii-polynomial literature poses: the standard papers
(Breden–Desvillettes–Lessard arXiv:1503.06315, Cadiot arXiv:2505.03091, van den Berg–Lessard's
surveys, and the Chen–Hou programme arXiv:2210.07191) each **fix** a weighted `ℓ¹` or `H^k` norm
in a definition and quote `Y₀, Z₀, Z₁, Z₂` in that one norm throughout, with no invariance
statement and no sweep. The absence is uniform enough that this leg claims **no** external
novelty for the *idea* of normalization-dependence — which is elementary — and claims only the
**measured classification of this certificate's own 48 quoted slots**, which is a statement about
this record and nothing else. No literature gate is opened, and `LITERATURE_CHECK.md` is not
edited.
