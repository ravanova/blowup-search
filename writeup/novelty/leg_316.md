## 0. Verdict of the novelty pass

`PROCEED_AS_EXTERNAL_VALIDATION`. arXiv:2410.05480 (Dähne–Figueras, *Self-Similar Singular
Solutions to the Nonlinear Schrödinger and the Complex Ginzburg–Landau Equations*) is
published and already used once by this repository — leg 48 (Route-V v0) re-derived their
branch/fold/zeros from the paper's own *equations*, in float64, to close stage V's novelty
gate. **This leg is a different exercise**: it does not re-derive from the equations, it
decodes and independently checks the paper's own **released rigorous proof-witness data**
(the actual interval-arithmetic output of their verification package, not a re-run of it),
in exact rational arithmetic. No mathematical claim of ours is available — the only claim
this leg can produce is about whether the paper's own machinery, on its own published
output, satisfies the paper's own stated corollary (the box-chaining condition of §6 of the
paper, quoted below). This is the leg-256/leg-61 posture: `PROCEED_AS_EXTERNAL_VALIDATION`
for the same structural reason leg 256 used `PROCEED_AS_INTERNAL_REPRODUCTION` and leg 61
used `PROCEED_AS_INTERNAL_AUDIT` — the result is proved and refereed.

## 4. What "reproduce the paper's own corollary from its published constants" means here

Paper §6 states the corollary this leg checks, verbatim (from the extracted text, located
independently of any code): for consecutive `ε`-subintervals `i`, the procedure produces
two boxes `μᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾×γᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾×κᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾ ⊊ μᵢ⁽ᵘ⁾×γᵢ⁽ᵘ⁾×κᵢ⁽ᵘ⁾` (existence strictly inside uniqueness), and
what "ensures [the segments] join together to form one long curve" is

> `μᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾×γᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾×κᵢ⁽ᵉ⁾ ⊆ μᵢ₋₁⁽ᵘ⁾×γᵢ₋₁⁽ᵘ⁾×κᵢ₋₁⁽ᵘ⁾` for `i = 2, …, N`.

Both conditions are checkable directly on the released CSV data with **no numerical
approximation at all**: `arb_dump_str` encodes each ball's midpoint and radius as an exact
dyadic rational (`mantissa · 2^exponent`, both integers, read off in hex), so decoding into
Python `fractions.Fraction` and comparing `mid ± rad` intervals is **exact rational
arithmetic**, not a float64 approximation of theirs. This is a stronger reproduction than
either leg 48 (float64 re-derivation) or leg 256 (float64 constants): here the "published
constants" being checked are the literal proof output, decoded exactly.

## 5. THE WINDOW — pre-committed here, not moved afterwards

**Target**: Case I, `j = 1` (the branch worked as the paper's own running example in §6,
and the same row leg 48 already cross-checked against Table 1). Three released segment
files (`top`, `turn`, `bottom`) plus `connection_points`.

**Gate, exactly as pre-committed in the leg brief**: does DF-CGL's published verification
package reproduce the paper's own corollary from its published constants, in this
environment? Operationalised as: for **every** row `i` in each segment, does
`(A)` `exists_i ⊊ uniq_i` hold componentwise, and `(B)` `exists_i ⊆ uniq_{i-1}` hold
componentwise for `i ≥ 2` — **all** checked in exact rational arithmetic against the
released data, independent of Arb/Julia/CAPD?

  - **YES** (both hold on every row, every segment) → bank the validation at full strength,
    per §0b's framing: this independently confirms the field's only Grade-A dissipative
    certificate on its own released output, using an independent decoder. Lane's second
    entry becomes dispatchable.
  - **NO**, but the failure is a decode/tooling mismatch on this leg's side (wrong byte
    offset, wrong special-value handling, wrong column semantics) → fix if possible; if not
    fixable within scope, report as a **finding** (a reproduction gap), explicitly NOT a
    challenge, per leg 256's attribution rule.
  - **NO**, and the released data itself — decoded correctly and cross-checked against the
    paper's own worked example (§6's `ε₁, μ₁, κ₁` numbers) — fails the paper's own stated
    corollary → **potential challenge. ESCALATE to the user before any write-up frames it
    that way**, per leg 256's attribution rule and the leg brief. Park the branch, do not
    push to `main`.

**Cross-check, pre-committed**: the decoded midpoint of the very first `top` row's
`μ_exists` (ε = [0,0]) is compared against the paper's own printed §6 starting value
`μ₁ = 1.23203752321003` (extracted text, located independently of the CSV). This is a
sanity gate on the decoder itself, run before the inclusion checks are trusted.

## 7. Performance note, per the standing rule added mid-leg

The released segment files for branch `j=1` total **49,465 data rows** (`top` 6588,
`turn` 33,281, `bottom` 9596) plus 2 connection-point rows. Decoding is pure integer/hex
parsing into `fractions.Fraction` (stdlib, exact, no numpy needed for this step) followed by
an O(1) interval-inclusion comparison per row — no iterative solve, no root-finding, no
external process per row. Estimated cost before running: parsing ~50k CSV rows with the
csv module and building ~5 `Fraction`s each is on the order of a few seconds to at most
low tens of seconds in pure Python; well under the 10-minute review threshold, so no
vectorisation/rewrite was needed before launch. The one-time network cost is `git clone`
of `CGL.jl` (~a few MB), done once and cached read-only in a `Papers/`-gitignored location,
never committed. Both estimate and measured wall time are recorded in
`experiments/journal/leg_316.md` after the run.
