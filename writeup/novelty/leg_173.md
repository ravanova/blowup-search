## 2. External prior art — are Xu's three gaps already-solved technology?

This is the part that decides whether "mature by orders of magnitude" means *invent* or
*port*. Answer: **two of the three are mature elsewhere; the third is not, and it is not the
one anybody would guess.**

**Gap (1), a uniform large-imaginary-part bound.** Mature, and standard. Barker,
*Numerical proof of stability of viscous shock profiles*, M3AS 26 (2016) 2451–2469,
[arXiv:1601.00837](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00837), derives an explicit radius `R :=
(γ + 1/2)^2` bounding the modulus of any unstable eigenvalue (§2.1, and Lemma 3.5), which is
exactly what collapses an infinite contour to a compact one, and then runs the winding count
**in interval arithmetic** (INTLAB) with the wrapping effect handled explicitly (§2.2–2.3).
This is the first rigorous numerical proof of viscous-shock stability by Evans-function
winding. Cost is on record and is worth quoting: one contour, *"10.3 hours to compute the
Evans function"* (§ results). Tooling is public — STABLAB,
<https://github.com/nonlinear-waves/stablab>.

**Gap (3), quadrature-error bounds in the trace norm.** Mature, with fully explicit
constants. Bornemann, *On the numerical evaluation of Fredholm determinants*, Math. Comp. 79
(2010) 871–915, [arXiv:0804.2543](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2543). Two separate results,
and the distinction matters for Xu:

* Theorem 5.1/5.2 — the **projection (Galerkin) route**, whose error is literally the trace-
  norm projection error `‖P_m A P_m − A‖_{J1}`, bounded via the singular-value tail
  `2 Σ_{n>N} s_n(A) + Σ_{n≤N} s_n(A)(‖u_n − P_m u_n‖ + ‖v_n − P_m v_n‖)` (eq 5.4). This is
  the route whose name matches Xu's phrase. Bornemann states plainly why he will not give a
  general quantitative version: it *"requires a carefully balanced choice of N (depending on
  m), which in turn requires some detailed knowledge about the decay of the singular values
  … and of the growth of the derivatives of the singular functions"*, and *"the results are
  sparse for the singular functions"* (§5).
* Theorem 6.2 — the **Nyström route**, which sidesteps that: for `K ∈ C^{k−1,1}([a,b]^2)`
  and a quadrature rule of order `ν ≥ k` with positive weights,
  `|d_Q(z) − d(z)| ≤ c_k 2^k (b−a)^k ν^{−k} Φ(|z|(b−a)‖K‖_k)`, and for `K` bounded analytic
  on a Bernstein ellipse `E_ρ × E_ρ`, `|d_Q(z) − d(z)| ≤ (4ρ^{−ν}/(1 − ρ^{−1})) Φ(|z|(b−a)
  ‖K‖_{L∞})`. Every constant is named and computable. Bornemann's own summary: *"the
  quadrature method in Section 6 does not require any such knowledge."*

  **The load-bearing hypothesis is `[a,b]^2` — a bounded interval, and a kernel continuous
  (indeed analytic) on it.** Recorded now because it is the hypothesis Xu's object does not
  obviously satisfy.

  Extension to unbounded domains and matrix kernels exists: Bornemann, *Robust and efficient
  solution of the drum problem via Nyström approximation of the Fredholm determinant*,
  [arXiv:1406.5252](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.5252).

**Gap (2), trace-ideal membership of the kernel.** The *theory* is settled and the
*verification* is not automatable. Latushkin & Sukhtayev, *Evans function and Fredholm
determinants*, Proc. R. Soc. A 471 (2015) 20140597,
<https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/full/10.1098/rspa.2014.0597>, prove the Evans
function equals the (2-modified) Fredholm determinant — but the theorem's hypothesis is
that the perturbation *is* trace class (or Hilbert–Schmidt for `det_2`). Establishing that
for a given operator is a bespoke analytic step, not a computation. Bornemann's §2 makes the
same point from the numerical side, listing the handful of *sufficient conditions* under
which a kernel's operator can *"(almost always) be proven to be trace class"* — all of them
smoothness/positivity conditions on a bounded interval. **There is no off-the-shelf
technology that certifies trace-ideal membership for a splitting you have not yet written
down.**

**The architecture Xu says he is a numerical analogue of** is real and landed: Hou, Wang &
Yang, *Nonuniqueness of Leray–Hopf solutions to the unforced incompressible 3D Navier–Stokes
equation*, [arXiv:2509.25116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25116) — coercive part + compact
perturbation, finite-rank approximation to controlled error, computer-assisted invertibility
**on the finite-rank image only**. Xu cites it as [13] and calls his own §3.2 diagnostic
*"a numerical analogue of this finite-rank-certification architecture"* (§1).

**Contour/argument-principle eigenvalue counting with controlled error on unbounded
domains** also exists independently of Evans functions: Beyn-type contour integration for
holomorphic Fredholm operator pencils via boundary-value problems,
[arXiv:1210.3952](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3952).

## 3. What is NOT found — the honest negative

Searched for, and not found:

* Any rigorous (interval-arithmetic or explicit-constant) Evans-function / Fredholm-
  determinant eigenvalue count for a **nonlocal 1D transport model** — gCLM, De Gregorio,
  CLM, Hou–Luo, Córdoba–Córdoba–Fontelos. Every rigorous Evans-winding result located is for
  an **ODE-Wronskian** Evans function (viscous shocks, travelling waves of local systems),
  where the "operator" is a finite-dimensional first-order ODE system and trace-ideal
  membership never arises.
* Any trace-class splitting for an operator whose nonlocal part is an **order-zero
  Cauchy-singular integral operator with a decaying coefficient on `ℝ`**.
* Any public code for Xu's Evans strand. §7 says *"the code is supplied for rerunning"*, but
  the Data availability statement (p. 34) says *"available from the author upon reasonable
  request"*, and §7 also says *"its run outputs are not retained."*

## 4. What this leg may and may not claim

* **MAY** claim: a located, quoted reading of what the three gaps are, where they sit in the
  text, and which of them has external technology.
* **MAY** claim: the presence or absence of numbers attached to those gaps in the paper.
* **MAY NOT** claim: that the three-gap phrase is a discovery of this leg (it is leg 127's).
* **MAY NOT** claim: that Barker's or Bornemann's machinery *applies* to Xu's operator —
  the novelty pass establishes only that the machinery exists; whether the hypotheses hold
  is the scoping leg's own measurement, and §2 above already flags the `[a,b]^2` hypothesis
  as the thing to check.
* **MAY NOT** build, port, or prototype anything. Territory is literature-plus-comparison.
* No ban in `plan_of_record.py` has a lift condition that this leg's finding could satisfy,
  in either gate direction. Confirmed against the printed ban list.
