## 2. Pre-committed inclusion rule and predicate — fixed before any classification

Written before a single row was scored, because a population question is decided by who is let
in, and post-hoc admission is how a negative result gets manufactured.

**A paper is IN the MS population iff all three hold:**

1. **BLOW-UP.** It claims a *proof* (not a numerical discovery) of finite-time blow-up that is
   self-similar, asymptotically self-similar, or nearly self-similar.
2. **INVISCID.** The equation certified carries **no dissipative term** — no `−νΔ`, no
   fractional `Λ^α` damping — in the equation the theorem is about. (3D/2D Euler, 2D Boussinesq,
   compressible Euler, CLM / De Gregorio / gCLM, the Hou–Luo model all qualify; nonlinear heat,
   CGL, Landau, Navier–Stokes do not.)
3. **CERTIFIED.** The proof is computer-assisted: some step is discharged by rigorous numerics
   (interval arithmetic or equivalent) rather than by pen and paper alone.

**Peer-review status is recorded per row, and is NOT used as a filter.** A filter on
publication status is exactly the knob that could exclude a falsifier; instead every row carries
its `journal_ref` as reported by the arXiv API (or `NONE`), and the gate is answered twice —
once over all rows, once over the peer-review-confirmed subset — so the reader can see whether
the answer depends on that cut.

**The predicate, fixed here:**

```
uses_diagonal_tail(row) == row.tail_treatment == ELL1_MULTIPLIER_TAIL
    #  i.e. the linearized operator's unbounded part is inverted on a Fourier/sequence tail
    #  by dividing through by a diagonal symbol, with the finite block handled by an
    #  approximate inverse -- the radii-polynomial / Newton-Kantorovich shape.

falsifies_lesson_87(row) == row.in_population and uses_diagonal_tail(row)
```

**Lesson 90 is honoured explicitly.** Two classes of row exist that make the predicate able to
report the other answer: (a) **real** dissipative rows that DO satisfy `uses_diagonal_tail` and
fail only on clause 2, and (b) a fictitious `SYNTHETIC_CONTROL` row that satisfies both
conjuncts. Admitting the synthetic row flips the gate to **yes**. Without those, a "no" would be
a property of the script rather than of the literature.

**Every classification must carry a locator** — section/equation number plus a verbatim
sentence, or an explicitly-labelled negative string search over the full text. Leg 53 lost a
claim by reading an abstract; that is a test here, not a habit (`unlocated_rows()` must be
empty).

### 3.3 The headline located statement — the obstruction is in print, in a peer-reviewed venue

Chen–Hou–Huang, *On the Finite Time Blowup of the De Gregorio Model for the 3D Euler Equations*,
**Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 74: 1282–1350 (2021)**, doi `10.1002/cpa.21991`, **§2.3**:

> *"There is another computer-assisted approach to establish the stability by tracking the
> spectrum of a given operator and quantifying the spectral gap; see, e.g. [2]. The key
> difference between this approach and our approach is that we do not use computation to
> quantify the spectral gap of the linearized operator `L` in (2.2). **In fact, the linearized
> operator `L` is not a compact operator due to the Hilbert transform `u_x = Hω` and the
> non-compact part of `L` cannot be treated as a small perturbation. Thus we cannot approximate
> the linearized operator by a finite rank operator which can be estimated using numerical
> computation.**"*

This is the single most valuable thing the leg found. It is the **finite-block-plus-tail
decomposition being declared unavailable, by the authors, in the peer-reviewed proof, on an
inviscid nonlocal transport model** — the same structural move legs 51–53 spent three legs
discovering by measurement. It is the exact complement of CLN §1, which states the *reason the
decomposition IS available* in the diagonal case:

> *"Therefore, the tail of `DF(U₀)` can be seen as a diagonally-dominant infinite dimensional
> matrix. From this, one can approximate the inverse of `DF(U₀)` as a finite matrix acting on a
> finite part of the sequence, and a tail operator (which is diagonal) which acts on the tail of
> the sequence."*

**One precision, and it is a real limit on what may be claimed.** CHH's stated reason is the
**non-compactness of the Hilbert transform**; this repository's measured obstruction is the
**shift structure** of the far-field block (`solver/spectral_certificate.tail_block`,
off-diagonal `~k/2`, diagonal exactly zero, leg 57's measured `M`-exponent `+1.021` at `mu=0`
against `+0.000` for every `mu ≥ 0.25`). Those are related mechanisms — in both, the unbounded
or non-compact part fails to be a multiplier that a Neumann series can invert — but they are
**not the same statement**, and this ledger does not assert the identification. What it records
is the weaker, checkable fact: the decomposition is declared unavailable in print for this class
of operator.

### 3.5 Where the prediction was too narrow, and the sharpening it earns

Lesson 87's prediction is phrased **dissipative vs inviscid**. Two rows show that is a **proxy**
for the real variable:

* **DS-NLS** (`2406.16597`) is a certified self-similar blow-up on an equation with **no
  dissipative term at all**, so it enters the population by the letter of clause 2 — and its
  unbounded part `−iΔ` **is a diagonal multiplier**, so the diagonal-tail framework was
  *available* to those authors. **They did not use it** (`Fourier`: 1 occurrence in 83 pages;
  the profile equation is an ODE discharged by fraction arithmetic). So the absence of a
  diagonal tail here is not explained by the operator shape.
* **BCG-IMP** and **CHEN-C13** reach the same `RIGOROUS_ODE_NO_TAIL_OP` bucket on inviscid fluid
  equations.

**The defensible sharpening — and it is a narrowing, not an upgrade:** what the certified
literature is actually split by is not viscosity but **whether an infinite-dimensional
linearized operator has to be inverted at all**. Self-similar profiles that reduce to ODEs never
build a tail operator, whatever their symbol; the papers that do face an infinite-dimensional
linearization on an inviscid transport model (the Chen–Hou family, 5 rows) reach for **weighted
energy estimates**, and the one place in the corpus where a genuine `ℓ¹` diagonal tail is run
against a blow-up problem is a **dissipative** equation. **This does not license the reverse
claim.** Nothing here shows a diagonal tail *cannot* work on an inviscid transport model; it
shows that **no published proof does it**, over 15 located rows and 8 in-population, which is a
statement about the literature and not a theorem about the operator.
