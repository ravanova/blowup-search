## 5. Lei–Liu–Ren [arXiv:1811.09754] — the functional, verbatim, with its hypotheses

Their equation (1.1) is the De Gregorio modification of CLM on `S¹`; the ground state is
`−sin θ`, and the perturbation is `η = ω + sin θ`.

**The space, verbatim (their (1.3)):**

> `H_DW = { η ∈ H¹(S¹) : η(0) = 0, ∫_{−π}^{π} |∂_θ η|² / sin²(θ/2) dθ < ∞ }`

**The inner product, verbatim (their (1.4)):**

> `⟨ξ, η⟩_g = (1/4π) ∫_{−π}^{π} ∂_θ ξ ∂_θ η / sin²(θ/2) dθ`

**The basis that diagonalises the linearised operator, verbatim (their (1.5)–(1.6)):**

> `ẽ_k^{(o)} = sin[(k+1)θ]/(k+1) − sin(kθ)/k , k ≥ 1`, and `⟨ẽ_k^{(o)}, ẽ_l^{(o)}⟩_g = δ_{kl}`

**How the damping is obtained.** Not by a pointwise multiplier. In the sine basis the
linearised operator is a pure two-sided **shift** (their (3.5)–(3.6),
`L e_k^{(o)} = A_k e_{k+1}^{(o)} + B_k e_{k−1}^{(o)}`, `L e_1^{(o)} = 0`) — the same shape
lesson 87 names and the same shape leg 111's own matrix has. In the `ẽ_k` basis it becomes a
**tridiagonal (Jacobi) form with a strictly negative diagonal**, verbatim:

> `L ẽ_k^{(o)} = −d_{k+1} ẽ_{k+1}^{(o)} − (d_{k+1} − d_k) ẽ_k^{(o)} + d_k ẽ_{k−1}^{(o)}`,
> `d_k = (k−1)²(k+1)/(2k²)`

from which, verbatim: *"L generates a strongly continuous semigroup with the desired decay
estimate `‖e^{tL}η(0)‖_Ỹ ≤ e^{−(3/8)t}‖η(0)‖_Ỹ`"* (their §3.1, via Hille–Yosida), and their
Proposition 3.1. **Their own remark on where it came from, verbatim:** *"the inner product in
(1.4) and the exact form of the basis vectors in (1.5) are still mysterious to us even though
we are fortunate enough to find them accidentally."*

**Hypotheses, complete:** `η ∈ H¹(S¹)`; `η(0) = 0`; `∫|∂_θη|²/sin²(θ/2) dθ < ∞`; and for the
nonlinear theorem (Thm 1.5) `∫_{S¹} η_in dθ = 0` and `‖η_in‖_{H_DW} < δ₀`. Thm 1.6 removes
the mean-zero constraint at the cost of an explicit `α(cos θ − 1)` shift.

## 6. J. Chen [arXiv:2010.12700] — why `γ = 2` is forced, and the extension

### 6.1 The norms, verbatim (Definition 1.1)

> "Define the singular weight `ρ = (sin x/2)^{−2}`, the weighted norms `‖·‖_H`, `‖·‖_X` as
> follows
> `‖f‖²_H ≜ (1/4π) ∫_{−π}^{π} |f_x|²/sin²(x/2) dx,  ‖f‖²_X ≜ ‖f‖²_H + ∫_{−π}^{π} |f_xx|² cos²(x/2) dx`
> and the Hilbert spaces `H`, `X`
> `H ≜ {f | f(0) = 0, ‖f‖_H < +∞}`, `X ≜ {f | f(0) = 0, ‖f‖_X < +∞}`"

> "The `H` norm and inner product `⟨·,·⟩_H` were first introduced in [19] for stability
> analysis."  ([19] = Lei–Liu–Ren.)

### 6.2 The exact identity that fixes the exponent — Lemma 2.4 and Remark 2.5, verbatim

> **Lemma 2.4.** "Let `f ∈ L²(ρ)`. We have `⟨sin x f_x, f ρ⟩ = (1/2)⟨f², ρ⟩`."

> **Remark 2.5.** "Formally, the above identity can be interpreted as that `sin^{−2}(x/2)` is
> an eigenfunction of the adjoint of `sin x ∂_x` with eigenvalue `1`. An analog of it is that
> on the real line, `x^{−k}` is an eigenfunction of the adjoint of `x∂_x` with eigenvalue
> `k − 1`, which plays an important role in the stability analysis in [4]. **It seems that the
> mysterious inner product `⟨·,·⟩_H` and singular weight `sin^{−2}(x/2)` constructed in [19]
> arise naturally from the viewpoint of energy estimates.**"

**This is the sentence the gate is about.** Chen's `sin x ∂_x` is *character-for-character*
this repository's own transport term: `solver/energy_coercivity.py` records the `a = 0` CLM
linearisation as `(L h)(θ) = cos θ · h − sin θ · (H h) − sin θ · h_θ`, i.e. transport
`−sin θ ∂_θ`. So the weight is not *chosen* to be singular enough — it is the **exact adjoint
eigenfunction of this repository's own transport field**, and it is unique for that job. There
is no "push `γ` past a threshold" step, because there is no threshold: the transport
contribution is an **exact constant multiplier over the whole circle**, not a quantity that
has to be made negative at one point.

### 6.3 The coercivity that is quoted from LLR, verbatim (Lemma 2.2)

> "(c) **Coercivity:** `⟨L₁ f, f⟩_Y ≤ −(3/8) ‖f‖²_Y`"

with `‖·‖_Y` two-sided equivalent to `‖·‖_H`, verbatim: `(1/2)‖f‖_H ≤ ‖f‖_Y ≤ ‖f‖_H`.
Chen then builds the weighted `H²` estimate on the **weighted derivative**, verbatim:
*"We introduce the weighted derivative `D_x = sin x ∂_x`."*

### 6.4 What is NOT claimed, and it is the whole caveat

Both papers are at **`a = 1`** (De Gregorio) and small `|a − 1|`; the repository's object is
**`a = 0` CLM**, where the Jacobi coefficients are different (leg 111's own matrix: column `k`
has row `k+1` entry `1 − k/2`, row `k−1` entry `k/2`, row `1` entry `−(−1)^k`) and where the
advection LLR's damping partly rides on is absent. **This leg therefore claims no gap and
computes none.** Two specific things a leg-111-v2 must confront and this leg does not touch:
the nonlocal terms (`−sin θ · Hh`, whose derivative produces `−cos θ · Hh − sin θ · H(h_θ)`),
which no weight controls — leg 111's own docstring already says *"the nonlocal Hilbert term is
what the weight cannot touch"* — and the `−sin θ · h` term the derivative form generates,
which couples the `Ḣ¹` form back to the `L²` one. Either could be fatal. **That is what makes
this an escalation and not a result.**

## 8. The one numeric check this leg runs — SPECIFIED HERE, BEFORE IT IS CODED

The escalation in §6.2 rests on a claim about **leg 111's own weight family**: that Chen's
Lemma 2.4 identity transfers to it, and that it does so at `γ = 2` **and at no other member of
the family**. That is checkable arithmetic on the weight alone — no operator, no Gram matrix,
no eigenvalue, **no coercivity gap** — and the runner does exactly it and nothing more.

Define the transport part of leg 111's own `D_φ` (its docstring's `D_φ` minus the `cos θ`
stretching term it adds):

    m_γ(θ) = (1/2)[ cos θ + sin θ (log φ^A_γ)'(θ) ],   φ^A_γ = (2 sin(θ/2))^{−γ}

**Predicted before coding**, from `(log φ^A_γ)' = −(γ/2)cot(θ/2)` and
`sin θ · cot(θ/2) = 1 + cos θ`:

    m_γ(θ) = (1/2)cos θ − (γ/4)(1 + cos θ),   so   m_2(θ) ≡ −1/2 for every θ.

- `γ = 2`: `max_θ m_γ − min_θ m_γ = 0` to quadrature precision, value `−1/2` — Chen's Lemma
  2.4 exactly, in leg 111's family. **This is the escalation's load-bearing number.**
- `γ = 0, 1, 3, 4`: variation strictly non-zero, `= |1 − γ/2|` by the formula above, so
  `1, 0.5, 0.5, 1`. **`γ = 2` is the only member with zero variation.**
- Instrument control (lesson 90 — a control that can come out differently): `m_γ` is

  weights, read-only. If the two disagree, the analytic formula above is not leg 111's weight

- Second control: the same identity is evaluated at leg 111's damping threshold `γ = 3`, where
