## 2. The discriminators, fixed before the evidence is applied

Leg 175's own two discriminators, restated:

* **D1** — was the technique *applied* to the resisting object, and did it still resist?
* **D2** — does the paper name a **different model class** in the passage where the
  obstruction is stated, and rank difficulty by model?

Leg 175 found `D1 = No` (0 of 4) and `D2 = Yes`, and returned `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`.

Two more are needed here, because all three of the follow-up's obstructions are by
construction *what is left after the fix*, so **D1 is true for all three and therefore
discriminates nothing in this leg** (lesson 90: a control whose value is identical on every
input is not a control — recorded, then set aside as non-decisive):

* **D3** — **cross-model recurrence.** In how many *distinct model classes* does the paper
  itself exhibit the same obstruction? An obstruction the paper displays in unrelated classes
  — including non-fluid ones — cannot be a property of any one model.
* **D4** — are all remedies the paper offers **apparatus-side** (loss weighting, network size,
  training stages, memory, hardware) rather than a change of model?

**Rule, pre-committed:** `MODEL_SPECIFIC` iff **D1 and D2**; `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC` iff
**D3 ≥ 2 and D4 and not(D1 and D2)**; otherwise `UNDETERMINED`.

## 4. Obstruction A (leg 196's **N1**) — the λ error bar. Verdict **TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC**

**The mechanism, in the paper's words.** Two compounding effects, §2.3.3:

> "On average, the magnitude of the non-smooth signal Rmax decays exponentially, reducing by
> one order of magnitude for each higher-order singularity." (l.678–679)

> "…the high-gradient nature of higher-order unstable modes still reduces the convergence rate
> of training. **For a fixed computational budget** (identical network size, iterations, and
> two training stages), the achievable background PDE residual for the smooth solution
> increases by approximately one order of magnitude for each successive singularity, which
> limits the non-smooth signal Rmax to be detected." (l.683–687)

> "This increased background residual, combined with the exponentially weaker Rmax signal,
> decreases the achievable λ accuracy for a fixed computational budget by approximately two
> orders of magnitude per model." (l.689–690 → l.769; the sentence is split by a figure block
> in the `-layout` extraction — this is leg 212's locator slip #1, and it is real)

**D1 = yes** (gradient normalization *and* multistage were applied; λ still stops at ±1e-7).
**D2 = NO** — and this is the decisive one. The entire §2.3.3 passage is internal to IPM and
attributes the loss to *mode order* and to *a fixed computational budget*. **No alternative
model class is named anywhere in it.** (The phrase "per model" at l.769 is, in context, "per
successive singularity" — the sentence's own subject is the mode index, and l.686 states the
same quantity as "for each successive singularity". Recorded because it is the one string in
the passage that could be misread as a cross-model claim; it is not one.)

**D3 = 3 distinct model classes**, this leg's own cross-model trace of the *same* structure
(a parameter of interest whose signal sits below the training background):

1. **IPM** — λ, §2.3.3 + Fig. 7(f) table (l.714–738).
2. **CCF** — λ, §2.3.1 l.466–475: the second-stage maximum residual at the origin "is
   spurious… there is no meaningful variation… prevents us from finding a more accurate smooth
   λs", and the sign "flips between experiments, demonstrating it is a numerical artifact".
3. **Gross–Pitaevskii vortices — a non-fluid elliptic BVP**, §3.2.1 l.941–944, on the core
   parameter `a_n`: "it shows that `a_n` decays exponentially with n when n < 10, while
   gradually stalling at a plateau for n > 10… **However, this plateau is a spurious artifact
   caused by the insensitivity of the standard loss function** to the extremely small value of
   `U_n` around the origin", with the fix stated at l.949–952 as minimizing *relative* error to
   `O(10⁻¹⁵)`.

Point 3 is the load-bearing one and is new to this leg: the identical obstruction — an
extracted parameter pinned worse than the residual, because the loss cannot see it — occurs in
an equation with **no fluid transport, no self-similarity and no λ at all**, and is removed by
the same normalization. That is not a property of any fluid model.

**D4 = yes.** Every remedy offered is apparatus-side (l.770–775): "larger networks with more
weights", "introducing a third stage", "Both approaches, however, require more memory", "a
single A100 GPU (80GB)", "more training stages in parallel on multiple GPUs".

**→ `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`.** In leg 173/175's vocabulary this is a **precision gap**, and its
magnitude, from Fig. 7(f) (l.714–738): residual `1e-13` against λ accuracy `1e-7` at IPM's 4th
unstable = **6.0 decades**; degradation **1.570 decades/mode** fitted over the five rows against
**2.0 decades/mode** stated (ratio **1.274×**), both reproducing legs 196 and 212.

## 5. Obstruction B (leg 196's **N2**) — CCF's 3rd unstable. Verdict **MODEL_SPECIFIC**

**The passage, §2.1.3, verbatim (l.275–281):**

> "While our gradient-normalization method effectively reduces the residual in the peak region,
> a persistent non-smooth signal at the origin (inset of Fig.3b) **remains for all tested
> λ ∈ [0.455, 0.4713]**. This indicates that **if a third unstable solution exists**, it is
> located at a λ value corresponding to an even higher-gradient profile. **The challenge of
> exploring the range λ < 0.455 and resolving such a feature in the 1D CCF model appears to be
> even greater than finding highly unstable solutions in the 2D IPM equation.** A full
> investigation into the existence and properties of this third unstable solution is a
> compelling direction for future research."

**D1 = yes, and here it means what it meant for leg 175.** The technique was applied to this
exact object and the object still resisted. Leg 175's classifier returns `MODEL_SPECIFIC`
precisely on `D1 = yes`; it recorded `D1 = 0 of 4` for the original paper and got
`TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`. This is the first `D1 = yes` in the two-paper record.

**D2 = YES, explicitly, and the paper names the class itself: the 2D Incompressible Porous
Media equation** (l.278–280). This is not an inference — it is a difficulty ranking *between two
named models*, the only such sentence in 27 pages, and its direction is the surprising one: the
**1D** model is rated **harder than the 2D** one.

**The paper's own reasoning, which the gate asks for precisely.** Three strands, all in §2.1.3
and Fig. 3:
1. **Profile geometry as a function of the model's own parameter.** "as λ decreases, the
   solution's peak amplitude increases dramatically, **reaching nearly 40 for λ ≈ 0.455**"
   (l.271–272), so "a potential third unstable solution would possess even more extreme
   gradients than the second" (l.273–274). The obstruction scales with a property of the CCF
   solution branch, not of the optimizer.
2. **The residual fix worked and was not the binding constraint.** "our gradient-normalization
   method effectively reduces the residual in the peak region" — and the non-smooth origin
   signal survived it anyway, across the whole tested window.
3. **Existence is conditional.** "**if** a third unstable solution exists"; "A full
   investigation into the **existence** and properties…". No amount of precision decides an
   existence question. This is the one strand of the three obstructions that is not a numerics
   statement at all.

**D3 = 1.** The frontier-stall does not recur: on IPM the frontier *advanced* (+1 mode, the 4th
unstable newly confirmed, l.105/l.131); on NLS excited states the analogous "can't reach the
target solution" problem was **solved** by targeted inequality constraints (§3.3.3 l.1187–1212).
CCF is the only class where the frontier moved **0 modes**.

**Magnitude, honestly stated — and it is not a decade count.** The searched window is
λ ∈ [0.455, 0.4713], **width 0.0163**; peak amplitude at the low end ≈ **40**; modes gained on
CCF this paper: **0** (the suite stays at three: stable, 1st, 2nd — l.157), against **+1** on
IPM. The remaining search is placed by the authors on the **open half-line λ < 0.455**, so
there is no finite gap to quote (lesson 73: when a quantity has no referent, say so rather than
bound it).

**→ `MODEL_SPECIFIC`. Alternative model class named by the paper: the 2D Incompressible
Porous Media equation.**

## 6. Obstruction C (leg 196's **N3**) — the double-float floor. Verdict **TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC**

**D2 = no** (no model class is named anywhere in the relevant passages). **D4 = yes** (same
l.770–775 budget list). **D3 = 4 distinct model classes**, and this is the sharpest
cross-model control in the paper — the floor is reported *by name* as double-float round-off
in every one:

| class | locator | floor as printed |
|---|---|---|
| CCF (1D nonlocal transport) | l.616–620, Fig. 5f | `O(10⁻¹³)`; "constrained by the round-off error of double-float precision" |
| IPM (2D Darcy) | l.1423–1424 | `O(10⁻¹¹)`–`O(10⁻¹³)` |
| NLS double-well (1D, non-fluid) | l.860–862 | loss plateau `10⁻²⁹`; "The random spikes show the round-off error of the double-float precision" |
| GP vortices (2D elliptic BVP, non-fluid) | l.948, l.1004–1005 | `O(10⁻¹⁵)`; "the relative residual reaches the round-off error" |
| NLS excited states (non-fluid) | l.1159–1161 | "reaching round-off errors throughout the domain" |

Five settings, four model classes, **three of them not fluid equations**. The paper's own
definition of success is the floor itself (§1 l.50–51: "we refer to machine precision as the
highest achievable accuracy, where the remaining PDE residuals are dominated by the inherent
round-off errors of double-float arithmetic, typically `O(10⁻¹³)` or lower").

**→ `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`.** In leg 173/175's vocabulary this is an **infrastructure gap**, and
its magnitude is a **zero, not a distance**: `extended precision` **0**, `quadruple` **0**,
`float64` **0**, `interval arithmetic` **0**, `enclos` **0** in 27 pages. A certificate needs
arithmetic *tighter* than the object it encloses; the pipeline is pinned at double by
construction, and the paper's whole discussion of how to do better is `memory` (1 occurrence)
and `GPU` (2). **The distance from this floor to a CAP is not measurable from this paper,
because the paper contains no quantity of that kind** — the same shape of negative leg 173
recorded for Xu's gap (2): not far from usable, but *undefined* off its own construction.
