## 1. What would have made this leg worthless

Two failure modes, named before the sweep ran.

**(a) Scoping by inherited defect list.** `verify_276` §10 diagnosed exactly this as the reason
PUB2 needed six correction passes: *each pass scoped its read-set to the prior pass's defect
list.* The dispatch for this leg names four legs (198, 218, 223, 228). Taking that as the
read-set would repeat the defect. **Pre-committed:** scope by **artifact / claim-sharing set** —
a repository-wide grep for either disputed number and its roundings, over every file type, with
the site list **enumerated in the report before a single edit is made**.

**(b) Rewriting history instead of annotating it.** The register's own convention (leg 263,
`CORRECTIONS.md` header) is that *corrections are marked, never hidden* — the original wording
stays standing with a correction block beside it. A leg that "fixed" legs 218/223 by editing
their sentences would destroy the evidence that the disagreement existed, which is the thing the
process record is for. **Pre-committed: append-only at every historical site.**

The gate's `no` branch is an escalation: *any site where the substitution would change an
argument.* Made operational in advance:

> A substitution changes an argument iff the site's **conclusion** depends on which of the two
> numbers is correct, or on the disagreement being **open**. It does *not* change an argument if
> the site already declines to adopt either number, or quotes the figure as a dated magnitude in
> a record of a decision already taken.

**Result: 0 sites failed this test.** The one live-prose site,
`TECHNICAL_P2_PUB3_V1.md` §5(a), already concludes *"Neither number is adopted here; the verdict
… is not in dispute, and the contamination status (zero) is not affected"* — which is leg 228's
conclusion verbatim, reached a leg early and for a weaker reason. The closure **strengthens** its
support and changes none of its three claims. Recorded rather than assumed.
