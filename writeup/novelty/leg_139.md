## Part 2 — external boundaries for the candidate failure families (links, not counts)

### B1 — the one-pass monotone solve has a *published* step-size condition, and the module
enforces it on one branch only.

`farfield_inverse_norm`'s docstring justifies its central trick — *"Every coefficient in either
recursion is non-negative, so feeding the extremal magnitude |g| = 1/w_g gives the induced norm
in ONE pass — no dense inverse"* — by non-negativity of the recursion coefficients. That is the
**monotone/inverse-positive matrix** argument: a non-singular M-matrix is inverse-positive, so
the extremal source realizes the induced norm ([monotone matrix / M-matrix,
inverse-positivity](https://grokipedia.com/page/monotone_matrix); the ‖A⁻¹‖_∞ diagonal-dominance
tradition it sits in is Varga's, surveyed with two-sided bounds in
[Cvetković–Kostić–Doroslovački, *Bounds for the infinity norm of the inverse for certain M- and
H-matrices*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379508004291)).

The discretization is the trapezoidal rule (θ = ½). The relevant published fact is that the
trapezoidal rule is **A-stable but not unconditionally positive/monotone**: its
positivity-preservation carries a finite step-size restriction, unlike its stability. See
[Bonaventura–Della Rocca, *Monotonicity, positivity and strong stability of the TR-BDF2 method*,
arXiv:1510.04303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.04303) (the trapezoidal substep is precisely the
one whose damping/monotonicity has to be bought back), and [Horváth–Mincsovics et al.,
*Positivity preservation of implicit discretizations of the advection equation*,
arXiv:2105.07403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07403) for the θ-method positivity threshold on an
advection operator — which is the operator here (`L h = −c h_X − h/X`).

For this module the threshold is explicit and the module *knows it*: `dtau < 2c`, raised as
`ValueError("grid too coarse: need dtau < 2c")` — **on the `alpha > 2` branch only**. The
`alpha < 2` branch runs the same coefficients `p, q` with no check.

> **P1 (registered prediction).** At a `dtau` above the module's own published threshold, the
> `alpha > 2` branch raises and the `alpha < 2` branch returns a finite, plausible, wrong norm.
> Falsifiable: if the `alpha < 2` recursion is unconditionally monotone, its value stays near
> the closed form `farfield_inverse_norm_finite` at every grid size and P1 dies.

### B2 — `lgamma` discards the sign of Γ; the fix pattern is standard, and the docstring's
"no Gamma of a negative argument" claim is checkable.

`cos_power_coeffs` builds `a[0]` from `math.lgamma(alpha+1) − 2·math.lgamma(alpha/2+1)` and its
docstring claims *"evaluated by the recursion so that no Gamma of a negative argument is ever
formed"*. `lgamma` returns **log|Γ(x)|** and the sign is dropped by construction — POSIX keeps
it in the external `signgam` (or `lgamma_r`)
([POSIX lgamma](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/functions/lgamma.html),
[cppreference](https://en.cppreference.com/c/numeric/math/lgamma),
[Boost lgamma](https://www.boost.org/doc/libs/latest/libs/math/doc/html/math_toolkit/sf_gamma/lgamma.html)),
and SciPy documents the same split explicitly:
`exp(loggamma(x+0j)) = gammasgn(x)·exp(gammaln(x))`
([scipy.special.loggamma](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.loggamma.html)).
`math.lgamma` has **no** `signgam` exposure in Python at all, so a negative Γ silently becomes a
positive number. Independently, `f_alpha = |cos(θ/2)|^α` is not integrable for `α ≤ −1`, so for
those α **no cosine-coefficient sequence exists** — anything returned is fabricated, not
merely mis-signed.

> **P2.** For `α < 0` with `α+1 < 0`, `cos_power_coeffs` returns a finite array whose `a[0]` has
> the wrong sign relative to the analytically continued value, contradicting its own docstring.
> **P3.** For `α ≤ −1` the returned array is not a coefficient sequence of anything: its entries
> grow with `k` and `eval_cos_series` diverges as `K` increases, with no exception raised.
> Falsifiable: a domain guard, or convergence to `|cos(θ/2)|^α`, kills both.

### B3 — the algebra constant is a supremum over **all** pairs; a truncated weight can only
under-report it.

The condition `sup_{j,k} v_{j+k}/(u_j u_k) < ∞` that `algebra_constant` evaluates is the
standard **Beurling submultiplicativity** condition under which a weighted ℓ¹ is a convolution
Banach algebra (`ω(n+m) ≤ ω(n)ω(m)`, giving `‖f∗g‖_ω ≤ ‖f‖_ω‖g‖_ω`) — see Gröchenig's survey
[*Weight functions in time-frequency analysis*](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/06-07/ISAAC/grochenig.pdf)
and [Willis, *Conjugation weights and weighted convolution algebras*](https://maths.anu.edu.au/files/willis-final.pdf).
The published condition is a supremum over the **whole** index set; the module evaluates it over
`j+k ≤ len(v)` and returns the result as a **sharp** constant with a two-sided bracket
`S/4 ≤ M ≤ S/2`. Nothing in the module ties `len(v)` to `2·(len(u)−1)`, which is exactly the
mode count `quadratic_coeffs` produces by default (`M = 2K`). A truncated or poisoned `v`/`u`
therefore yields a *lower* bound presented as sharp — the unsafe direction for a certificate.
No published treatment of these algebras offers a truncated supremum as a sharp constant; the
gap is in the code, not in the mathematics.

> **P4.** A `v` sized to `len(u)` rather than `2(len(u)−1)` returns a strictly smaller "sharp"
> constant with no warning. **P5.** An `Inf` planted in `u` is absorbed rather than propagated
> — it can only *lower* the reported constant, and at some positions changes nothing at all
> (bit-identical output, the leg-115 signature). Falsifiable: NaN/Inf propagation, or a length
> consistency check, kills both.

## Part 3 — what this pass changed about the leg

1. **The headline is now a half-enforced *published* precondition, not an invented threshold.**
   `dtau < 2c` is the module's own error message; the audit's job is to show one branch enforces
   it and the sibling branch does not, and to measure the cost — not to argue that some
   arbitrary grid is "too coarse".
2. **B2 gave the sign question a decisive oracle.** Without it the tempting claim would have
   been "negative α is out of scope"; with it the claim is checkable in two independent ways at
   once (wrong sign vs. the continued value, *and* a divergent series where no coefficients
   exist), and the α ∈ (−1, 0) case becomes a **negative control that can pass** — the expansion
   really is legitimate there.
3. **B3 forbids the lazy framing.** The truncation is *documented* in the docstring
   (`max_{j+k ≤ len(v)}`), so the finding cannot be "undocumented truncation". It has to be
   stated as what it is: a documented restriction that silently converts a two-sided sharp
   bracket into a one-sided under-estimate, with no consistency check against the mode count the
   module's own `quadratic_coeffs` emits.
4. **Negative controls were chosen before the run**, and each can fail: NaN propagation through
   `algebra_constant` and `quadratic_coeffs`; the exact `α = 2` resonance guard; `X0 = 0`;
   `α ∈ (−1,0)`; `_folded_angles` beyond `|θ| > π/2`; and `hilbert_of_cos_power` pushed three
   decades past its own documented `X ~ 1e4` ceiling against the exact far-field law.
