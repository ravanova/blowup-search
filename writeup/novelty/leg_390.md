## 0. What kind of pass this is

This leg makes **no new mathematical claim about Navier–Stokes**. It is a *scoping/arithmetic* leg:
it re-derives `CLAY_OBLIGATIONS.md`'s §1–§5 ledger against a different acceptance statement of the
same published problem, and puts magnitudes on the cost of moving the repository's ℝ³-anchored DSS
object onto `T³ = ℝ³/ℤ³`. Disclosed up front, because the honest novelty verdict on the mathematics
is **nil, and deliberately so**:

* The Clay problem statement (Fefferman, *Existence and smoothness of the Navier–Stokes equation*,
  official CMI problem description) is a published primary document. Its statement **(D)** and its
  conditions **(8),(9),(10),(11)** are its own text. Reading them is reading, not discovery.
* That a dilation `x ↦ λx` does not descend to `ℝ³/ℤ³`, and hence that a non-constant exactly
  `λ`-DSS field cannot be `ℤ³`-periodic, is elementary and standard (it is the reason the entire
  self-similar blow-up literature is posed on the whole space). It is stated here **with a measured
  magnitude**, not claimed as new.
* That the lattice image sum `Σ_{k≠0}|Lk|^{−α}` over `ℤ³` converges iff `α > 3` is the standard
  Epstein-zeta convergence condition. Standard.
* Periodic (Ewald-type) Green's-function corrections to the ℝ³ Biot–Savart / pressure kernel are
  classical in the numerics literature.

**What is new is repository-local only:** this repository has never (a) written down what statement
(D) does to each of §1–§5, (b) put a number on periodizing the DSS object, or (c) asked which of
§2's four screen rows are ℝ³-only. That is the whole claim, and it is a Tier-2-ceiling bookkeeping
claim, not a mathematical one. **No link of the `L1 → L4` chain moves. Clay stays ~0.05%.**

This leg **authorises nothing**. The retarget decision is the user's, and this leg makes none.
