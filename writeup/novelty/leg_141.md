## 3. Chen–Hou–Huang [arXiv:1905.06387] — the damping half, and the same shape of squeeze, published

**§2.1, "Derivation of the damping term", verbatim** (`x`-variable, weight `x^{-k}`):

> "For `x` close to `0`, we choose a singular weight `x^{-k}`, `k ∈ N⁺` to take advantage of
> the stretching term. … Using integration by parts, we obtain
> `I = ⟨−C(k−1)/2 + (c̄_ω + ū_x), ω² x^{-k}⟩ ≜ ⟨D, ω² x^{-k}⟩`.
> We will choose `k` so that the coefficient `D` is negative (we choose `k = 4` for `a = 1`
> and small `|a|`)."

This is leg 111's `D_φ` — the *same* object, obtained the *same* way (integration by parts of
the transport term into a single multiplier), on the *same* family — and "choose `k` so that
`D` is negative" is leg 111's "damping at the origin needs `γ > 3`", published, with the
same chosen exponent `4`.

**And the opposing constraint is published too, in the same subsection, verbatim** — located
at the far field rather than the origin:

> "In order to control the perturbation `ω` in the far field, we have to choose a weight `ϕ`
> that decays more slowly than `x^{-1}` so that the weighted `L²` norm of `ω` is not too weak
> for large `|x|`. As a result, `(c̄_l x + aū)ω_x` does not produce a damping term for large
> `|x|` in our weighted `L²` estimate after performing integration by parts. **This is one of
> the subtleties in our analysis.**"

That is leg 111's obstruction *shape* — the exponent that buys damping is excluded by the
exponent the norm can afford, on one and the same integration-by-parts identity — stated in
print, on this equation family, and named a "subtlety". CHH escape it at the far field by
taking the damping from a different term (`c̄_ω + ū_x`, which they observe is negative for
large `|x|`).

**Proposition 3.1 is the origin half, on this repository's own object.** Their weight (3.6),
verbatim:

> `ϕ = −1/(ω̄_x³) − 1/(b²ω̄_x) = (b² + x²)²/(b² x⁴)`, `b = 1/2`, and "Note that `ϕ ≍ x^{-4} + 1`"

with the profile `ω̄` of (3.5) obtained, in their words, "by using the exact formula of the
solution of (1.3) with `a = 0` given in [10]". **`b = 1/2` is leg 111's `Ω(y) = −y/(y² + 1/4)`.**
And the hypothesis, verbatim:

> "if `|a| < a₀` and the initial data `ω̄ + ω₀` of (3.4) (`ω₀` is the initial perturbation)
> satisfies that `ω₀` is odd, `ω₀ ∈ H²`, **`ω₀,x(0) = 0`** and `E(0) < c|a|`"

`γ = 4` (past leg 111's damping threshold `3`) **together with** `ω₀,x(0) = 0` (exactly the
condition leg 111's basis `sin kθ`, with `(sin θ)′(0) = 1`, fails). Both halves of the
coincidence, in one proposition, on the CLM profile.

## 4. Xu [arXiv:2607.19762] — a published weighted-energy/coercivity no-go on this operator, with an explicit carve-out

**§8 (Discussion), verbatim:**

> "Two natural approaches do not reach the gap. **A weighted-energy/coercivity estimate cannot
> certify the gap**: the far field is already coercive in plain `L²` with the sharp edge, but a
> local positive multiplier `+φ HΩ` near the origin (with `HΩ(0) > 0`) survives every
> `L²`-equivalent weight, so **no coercivity certificate in an `L²`-equivalent norm reaches the
> spectral gap.** (The small-`a` coercivity estimate of Elgindi, Ghoul, and Masmoudi
> [15, Prop. 2.1], for odd `f` with `f′(0) = Hf(0) = 0` in a singular-weighted space, **is not a
> counterexample but an instance of the realization mechanism of Section 3.1**: the singular
> weight and origin constraints define their own realization, in which a gap is certified; it
> neither contradicts nor supplies the `X`-gap here.)"

Two things at once, both binding:

1. A **published no-go for weighted-energy coercivity on exactly this operator** — but its
   quantifier is `L²`-*equivalent* weights (bounded above and below), which is a strictly
   different class from leg 111's singular `φ ~ θ^{-γ}`, and its mechanism is a different one
   (a surviving positive multiplier, not a threshold coincidence).
2. The parenthesis is the answer to leg 141's gate handed over directly: the singular-weight
   case **is** treated, it **is** attributed, and in it **a gap is certified** — under the
   origin constraints.

**Appendix A** carries the same tension in Xu's own construction, verbatim: the semigroup space
`Y_θ` has `‖v‖²_{Y_θ} = ∫₀^∞ |u(y)|²(y² + ¼)² y^{-2θ-1} dy`, `θ = 3/2` giving "core weight
`y^{-4}`", and

> "The two symmetry modes are excluded from `Y_{3/2}` by that weight: the integrands of
> `‖u₁‖²` and `‖u₀‖²` behave like `y^{-4}` and `y^{-2}` at the origin and diverge."

i.e. **the weight that puts the origin's essential line at the damped rate is the weight that
ejects the modes** — leg 111's coincidence, in Xu's variables, resolved (not called an
obstruction) by doing the modulation projection on the physical space `X` and mapping in:

> "Because the weight `y^{-2θ-1}` blows up at the origin, elements of `Y_θ` carry no pointwise
> origin traces; the modulation projection is therefore defined on the physical `X` (which has
> traces) and mapped into `Y_θ`, not applied within `Y_θ`."

## 5. Elgindi–Ghoul–Masmoudi [arXiv:1906.05811] Prop. 2.1 — the decisive statement, with hypotheses verbatim

Their equation (1.4) is the Okamoto–Sakajo–Wunsch `gCLM` family, and their own words:
"when `a = 2` we get the De Gregorio model and **when `a = 0` we get CLM model**". Their `a = 0`
profile, verbatim: `F₀(y) = y/(1 + y²)`, `HF₀(y) = −1/(y² + 1)` — leg 111's profile up to the
scaling symmetry the paper itself records (`F_{a,µ}(z) := F_a(µz)` leaves (1.7) invariant) and
the sign convention.

Their weighted space, verbatim: `L²_φ(R) = {f ∈ L²_loc : ∫_R |f|²φ < ∞}` with
**`φ = (1 + y²)²/y⁴`** — the same weight as CHH (3.6), `γ = 4` at the origin.

**Proposition 2.1, verbatim:**

> "There exists a universal constant `C > 0` so that if `a` is small enough and if `f` is odd,
> `f′(0) = Hf(0) = 0` and `∫_R |f|² φ(y) dy < +∞`,
> `∫_R f M_a f φ(y) dy ≤ −(1/2 − C|a|) ∫_R f(y)² φ(y) dy`."

At `a = 0` the constant is exactly **`−1/2`**.

**This is a published, positive weighted-energy coercivity gap of `1/2` for the `a = 0` CLM
linearization, in a singular-weighted space at exactly the exponent `γ = 4` that leg 111
measured as inadmissible.** The three hypotheses are precisely the two halves of leg 111's
coincidence, imposed as *hypotheses on the perturbation* rather than tested on a *fixed basis*:

- `∫|f|²φ < ∞` **is** leg 111's admissibility test;
- `f′(0) = 0` (with `f` odd) is what a function must satisfy to have any chance of it, since
  `f ~ cy` gives `∫₀ y² y^{-4} dy = ∫₀ y^{-2} dy = ∞`;
- and the gap is still `−1/2` at `γ = 4 > 3`, the damping half.

Their §3 states the price openly, verbatim: *"Since our coercivity estimate from the previous
section relies on `∂_y q(0) = H(q)(0) = 0`, we will use that we have the 'free' parameters `µ`
and `λ` to fix these conditions."* The window is bought with modulation.

## 6. The one numeric check this leg runs — SPECIFIED HERE, BEFORE IT IS CODED

Leg 111's `solver/energy_coercivity.admissibility` hardwires the trial function `sin θ`
(vanishing order `p = 1`). The check, using that module's own `graded_quadrature` and
`weight_values` **read-only**, is: recompute `‖sin^p θ‖²_φ` on the identical graded ladder for
`p = 1, 2, 3` at `γ = 3` and `γ = 4`.

**Predicted before coding** (from `∫₀ θ^{2p} θ^{-γ} dθ`, convergent iff `γ < 2p + 1`):

- `p = 1` must **reproduce leg 111's banked numbers bit-for-bit** (same integrand) — this is
  the instrument check, and it is a control that can report the other answer: a mismatch
  means my quadrature is not leg 111's;
- `p = 1`, `γ = 4`: divergent, ratio `≈ 1.678e7` (leg 111 banked `16777216.0000616` for the
  `n_grade → 2 n_grade` ratio);
- `p = 2` and `p = 3`, `γ = 4`: **convergent**, ratio `≈ 1`;
- window `(3, 2p + 1)`, width `2p − 2`: **`0` at `p = 1`, `2` at `p = 2`, `4` at `p = 3`.**

If `p = 2, 3` at `γ = 4` came back divergent, the reading in §5 would be wrong and the gate
would answer NO. That is what makes it a check rather than a decoration.
