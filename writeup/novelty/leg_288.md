## 1. The object: which rows are actually in play

Leg 281's `CLASSIFICATION_TABLE` has 32 rows. The convention axis this leg tests is `κ`
(281's axis 1 — the `X`-block/border relative weight; axes 2–4 were measured by 281 to be either
a sub-case of axis 1 or not live, at conditioning-level spreads of `1e-7`–`1e-14`). Restricting
to rows that actually move under `κ` (excluding CF, CF*, N/F, and CR-DISCLOSED — the last is a
*different* axis, the `ℓ¹_w` weight `s`, already disclosed at every call-site and out of scope
here per the task brief, which asks about the `κ`-axis caveat family specifically):

| row | quantity | class | behaviour registered by 281 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | `σ_min` bordered | CR | non-monotone, argmax `κ≈0.1` on an 11-point grid, best power-fit residual `790%` |
| A2 | `‖R‖_X = 1/σ_min` | CR | inherits A1, inverted |
| A4 | `σ_min` ladder relative spread | CR | monotone-decreasing in `κ` on every grid point tried |
| A6 | Xu-normalization images of A1 | CR | two points of A1's sweep |
| C1 | `Z₁` block-diagonal, `K=2` | CR | monotone-decreasing in `κ` on the whole tried range, `24%` spread |
| D1 | `‖ℓ‖_{X*}` | CX | **exact** law `= 0.88736 · κ^{-1/2}` (fit residual `1.1e-16`) |
| D3 | E4 two-route ratio | CR | `= A2` (281's own correction to its novelty pass) |
| E6 | "optimistic by" factor | CR | `=` CF `/` A1, inherits A1's shape |

A1, A2, A6, D3, E6 all move together (same mechanism, same or inverted shape). The genuinely
independent behaviours to reconcile are: **the A1-family** (has an interior extremum),
**C1** (monotone, no interior extremum, an asymptotic floor), and **D1** (monotone, no interior
extremum, no floor — an exact power law to `0`).

## 2. Candidate conventions, enumerated before computing anything new

The task brief names three; a fourth is added because the first three all sit on one side of
the tension found in §1 and a genuinely different criterion is needed to test the gate honestly:

| id | convention | κ value | how it is picked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C_repo** | the certificate's own `X`-norm choice | `κ = 1` | the repository's unweighted default (leg 176) |
| **C_xu_half** | Xu's half-line normalization | `κ = π` | Xu Definition 4.1 eq. (4.2), the displayed norm |
| **C_xu_full** | Xu's "equivalent" full-line norm | `κ = 2π` | Xu Def. 4.1, second sentence; leg 249's reading |
| **C_opnorm** | operator-norm-induced scaling | `κ = κ* ≈ 0.1` | the `κ` that **extremizes an operator norm quantity** — concretely, `argmax_κ σ_min` (equivalently `argmin ‖R‖_X`), i.e. the convention an operator-conditioning criterion would pick on its own, with no reference to any external paper's normalization |

`C_opnorm` needs its own value pinned. Leg 281's `E5b` block already ran a 61-point log-spaced
sweep of `κ` over `10⁻⁶ … 10⁶` at `N=256` for exactly this quantity (`sigma_min_over_the_fine_sweep`)
and reports `argmax_kappa = 0.10000000000000006`. This leg brackets that grid point with new,
small, targeted evaluations (`N=256`, ~8 points in `[0.06, 0.2]`) to confirm it is a genuine
local maximum and not a grid artifact, and separately probes `κ ∈ {1e2, 1e4, 1e6, 1e8}` for `Z₁`
and `A1` to see whether either approaches a genuine finite limit (a "floor", making that
direction a legitimate second candidate) or diverges without bound.

## 3. Falsifiable predictions, registered before the bracketing runs

- **P1 (interior extremum, A1-family).** `σ_min` has a genuine local maximum near `κ = 0.1`:
  values at `κ = 0.08` and `κ = 0.12` will both be measurably below the value at `κ = 0.1`
  (not grid noise — a real second-derivative sign, not just the endpoints of an 11-point log
  grid). **Falsified if** the bracket is monotone across `[0.06, 0.2]`.
- **P2 (no interior extremum, Z₁/D₁).** `Z₁` (C1) and the dual norm (D1) have **no** interior
  extremum on `(0, ∞)`: probing `κ` up to `10⁸` will show `Z₁` continuing to *decrease* (never
  turning back up) toward an asymptotic floor, and `D1` continuing to decrease toward `0` with
  no floor at all (consistent with its exact `κ^{-1/2}` law). **Falsified if** either develops a
  local minimum at finite `κ` and then increases again.
- **P3 (opposite ranking).** Ranking the four candidate conventions by how close each gets to
  A1's own ideal (`κ*≈0.1`) will be the **reverse** of ranking them by how close each gets to
  Z1's own ideal (`κ→∞`, the floor) — i.e., Spearman rank correlation `= -1` across the four
  named candidates. **Falsified if** any candidate is simultaneously top-2 on both rankings.
- **P4 (gate).** No single candidate convention makes every CR/CX row of §1 simultaneously
  "natural" (well-defined and within a small tolerance of its own family's best achievable
  value). **Predicted answer: NO.** The mechanism, predicted before computing it: A1's family
  wants `κ` pinned near its one interior extremum, while C1/D1 want `κ` pushed toward `+∞` —
  and the two directions are not reconcilable because **A1's own value collapses toward the
  float floor in exactly the limit (`κ → ∞`) that is best for C1 and D1** — i.e. the
  resolvent-norm quantity that the certificate actually needs bounded (`‖R‖_X = 1/σ_min`)
  diverges in the direction the other two rows prefer.

If P1–P3 hold and P4's mechanism is confirmed quantitatively, that mechanism *is* the answer to
"why did the caveat family keep recurring": there is a structural reason no single convention
was ever adopted, not an oversight.
