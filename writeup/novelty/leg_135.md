## 3. Two pre-commitments, made before any gate number is seen

**(i) A JSON-diff is the WRONG bit-for-bit test here, and this is not assumed — the repair
itself already documented why.** `experiments/p2_route_fia_v1_adversarial.py`'s own docstring
(lines 41-49, added by the repair) records that the **pre-repair** module gives
`X_c = 18.715770556159065` at `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` and `18.71577055615906` at 4 threads — a
last-ULP difference in the Newton solve that then propagates into every quantity derived from
`b`. So re-running any of these files and diffing against a committed JSON measures the BLAS
thread count, not the repair. This is the same trap leg 105 hit at `Y_0 ~ 3e-17` and named.
**Pre-committed consequence:** this leg's clause (b) is measured as a **same-process, in-process
bitwise differential** — the pre-repair `solver/first_integral.py` read out of
`git show 9c08287^:solver/first_integral.py` and imported alongside the current one in one
interpreter, one thread setting, one process — never as a comparison against any banked JSON.
Banked JSON values are read at most informationally and are excluded from the pass/fail
criterion.

**(ii) The uncovered surface this leg predicts, NAMED before it is measured.** The repair's own
A/B (`part_b`) enumerates its call sites as *"the three live `omega_of` call sites"* and *"the
two live `first_integral_defect` call sites"*, all of them in
`experiments/p2_route_d_v14_first_integral.py`. **It does not touch
`solver/reduced_certificate.py`** — the one *other solver module* that consumes the guarded
API, importing `ReducedProfile`, `even_cheb` and `graded_grid`, and calling `even_cheb` at four
sites (lines 97, 162, 163, 190-192) and `rp.e_of(b, v)` at line 130. Both of those functions had
their **default** changed by the repair (`even_cheb` and `e_of` now return `NaN` outside the
support where they previously returned a clipped/extrapolated value). **Falsifiable prediction,
registered now:** if any of those five call sites is ever handed a `v` outside `[-1, 1]`, the
repair silently converts a finite downstream certificate number into `NaN`, and *nothing
currently on `main` would catch it* — the bench A/B never runs `reduced_certificate.py`, and
`test_reduced_certificate.py` predates the repair. The honest alternative outcome, which is what
I expect, is that all five sites are in-support by construction and the surface is clean; **that
is a measurement either way**, and it is a surface the repair's own driver did not report on.
