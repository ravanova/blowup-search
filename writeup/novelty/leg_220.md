## R2. Is leg 204's own prescribed repair sufficient? **Yes on the predicate, insufficient by two sites.**

Legs 150, 151 and 152 all found the escalating leg's prescribed predicate incomplete or dead
on its own pins, so this is a standing check and not a courtesy. Leg 204's prescription, read
verbatim off its runner's `detail` field for the asymmetry case:

> "`compactify` computes `X_max = |X|.max()` and tests `inside = |Xt| <= X_max`. For a grid
> symmetric about 0 that is exactly the data interval; for any other grid it is strictly
> larger …"

The predicate it implies — window on `[X.min(), X.max()]` — is **correct and adopted
unchanged**. But changing the *membership test* alone leaves the far-field branch reading
the old symmetric assumption in two further places, and leg 204 named neither:

* **`edge = np.where(Xt[~inside] > 0, f_hi, f_lo)`** — "which grid edge does this sample
  continue from?" answered by the sign of `Xt`. On an asymmetric grid a sample at
  `Xt = -0.5` with `X_lo = -0.1` is *above* the data interval on the left but negative, so
  the old form continues it from `f[0]`, the wrong endpoint. Adopted: `Xt > X_hi`.
* **`ratio = np.abs(Xt[~inside]) / X_max`** — the `power` closure's normalisation, a single
  scalar for both sides. Each side must normalise by **its own** endpoint magnitude.
  Adopted: `np.where(above, X_hi, -X_lo)`.

Both reduce to the pre-repair expressions identically when `X_lo = -X_hi`, which is the
whole bit-identity argument and is measured, not asserted, in the runner's Part A.

**Scope held.** Leg 204 found seven silent mechanisms. This leg repairs **one** — the
windowing — because that is the declared territory ("the domain-guard windowing logic
only"). The other six (`domain_fields`' `int()` truncation, `analytic_tail`'s unvalidated
`C`/`N`, the fitter's `n_points` overstatement, descending `k` into `weighted_partial_sums`,
shuffled `X`/`f`, mismatched `c`) are **not touched and not closed**, and the journal says so
in those words. Repairing one mechanism per leg is the family's shape (leg 152 patched three
of four and documented the fourth rather than widen).

## R4. External prior art. **Nothing to claim, and nothing to check against.**

No external search was run and none is owed. The repair-family precedent (leg 152's own
framing) is that a guard leg's novelty exposure is zero when it lands input validation and
proves a no-op: there is no result, no bound and no mechanism to be pre-empted by. The
underlying *finding* — that a domain guard windowing on `max|X|` silently extrapolates on an
asymmetric grid — belongs to leg 204 and its novelty pass owns that exposure. This leg
claims only that the predicate now matches the docstring, and measures the cost as zero.

**No ban touched.** Checked against `plan_of_record.py`'s live list, one clause in
particular: *"closing the truncation gap by extending the domain"* is banned. **This leg
extends no domain.** It narrows the *accepted* window from `[-max|X|, +max|X|]` to
`[min X, max X]` — a subset — so it can only ever move a call from "silently accepted" to
"flagged", never the reverse, and it changes no `X_max`, no `rho_max` and no grid anywhere.
The banned move is the opposite sign of this one. No GA compute, no `ℓ¹`-Fourier or
collocation machinery, no re-measurement of leg 55's physics, and `capabilities.py` was
grepped before anything was written.
