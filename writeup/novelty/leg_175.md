## 1. What the paper actually is

Not a proof paper, and it does not present itself as one. It is a **numerical discovery**
paper: PINNs (physics-informed neural networks) with mathematical structure hard-coded into
the architecture, trained by a **full-matrix Gauss–Newton** optimizer, plus **multi-stage
training**, used to find self-similar blow-up profiles `Φ(y)` and their admissible scaling
parameters `λ`.

Three systems (p.3):

* **CCF** — Córdoba–Córdoba–Fontelos, 1D nonlocal transport (ref. 4 = Ann. Math. 162, 2005).
* **2D IPM** — incompressible porous media, with boundary.
* **2D Boussinesq** — which the paper states is "mathematically analogous (up to exponentially
  small terms) to the incompressible 3D Euler equations under the assumption of axial
  symmetry" (p.3). *This* is the "3D Euler with boundary" of the ledger row.

Headline mathematical content, verbatim (p.5):

> "This marks the first discovery of any smooth unstable self-similar solution to any unforced
> incompressible fluid equation, leading to a finite time blow-up."

And the empirical laws (p.5): `λ_n ~ 1/(1.4187n + 1.0863) + 1` for Boussinesq/Euler,
`λ_n ~ 1/(1.1459n + 0.9723)` for IPM. For CCF "a clear asymptotic relationship has not yet been
identified … making a reliable extrapolation premature" (p.5).

The Clay connection is the paper's own, stated (p.5):

> "One proposed approach to addressing the Clay Millennium Prize Problem involves finding a
> sequence of self-similar profiles … each with an increasing number of unstable directions. It
> is expected that higher-order instability makes it more feasible to treat viscosity as a
> perturbative error…"

**Note for this repository:** that is the *viscous* argument — higher instability order buys
you the right to treat viscosity perturbatively. It is asserted as an expectation with two
citations (Merle–Raphaël–Rodnianski–Szeftel, refs. 34/35), not proved here. It is directly
adjacent to Route-VBS's "missing rung" question (leg 174) and to stage `V`'s closed gate.

## 2. The obstruction, in the paper's own words

Four distinct statements, all locatable, quoted verbatim with page and section.

### 2.1 The certification threshold is stated to be MODEL-DEPENDENT, and claimed met for CCF only

p.8, §*Implications for Future Studies*:

> "Our approach is able to reduce PDE residuals by multiple orders of magnitude compared to
> those reported in the literature, achieving double-float machine precision for solutions of
> the CCF equation. **This level of accuracy proves to be sufficient for utilizing the numerical
> solution for computer-assisted proofs for this specific problem², although the required
> precision for such proofs is generally problem-dependent.**"

Footnote 2, p.8, in full: **"Manuscript in preparation."**

This is the single most important sentence in the paper for this leg's gate. It says three
things at once: (a) CAP-readiness is claimed, (b) it is claimed **for CCF specifically**, and
(c) the authors themselves flag that the required precision **varies by problem**. The abstract
(p.1) is looser — "This level of precision meets the stringent requirements for rigorous
mathematical validation via computer-assisted proofs" — but the abstract's antecedent is also
CCF: "for specific solutions (the Córdoba-Córdoba-Fontelos (CCF) stable and 1st unstable
solutions), we reach near double-float machine precision" (p.1).

### 2.2 The precision stratification — the actual numbers (Fig. 3b, p.6)

`log10` maximum equation residual, best solution found, verbatim from the figure table:

| Equation | Solution | log10 max residual |
|---|---|---|
| CCF | Stable | **−13.714** |
| CCF | 1st unstable | **−13.589** |
| CCF | 2nd unstable | −6.664 |
| IPM with boundary | Stable | −11.183 |
| IPM with boundary | 1st unstable | −10.510 |
| IPM with boundary | 2nd unstable | −8.101 |
| IPM with boundary | 3rd unstable | −7.526 |
| Boussinesq | Stable | −8.178 |
| Boussinesq | 1st unstable | −8.038 |
| Boussinesq | 2nd unstable | −7.772 |
| Boussinesq | 3rd unstable | −7.558 |
| Boussinesq | 4th unstable | −7.020 |

Two of twelve reach the stated CAP threshold. **The Boussinesq column — the model class that
is 3D Euler with boundary, i.e. the one nearest the Millennium problem — is the worst row
block in the table, and its best entry is 5.5 decades short of CCF's.**

### 2.3 The paper attributes its own precision floor to the TRAINING METHOD, not to any model

p.17, Methods §*Multi-stage Training*, first sentence:

> "**If we only employ methods described up to this point, we are unable to reach maximum
> equation residuals much below 10⁻⁸.** To discover higher-precision solutions, we employ
> multi-stage training⁴⁹ … In this study, we apply two-stage training to the stable and first
> unstable solutions of the CCF and IPM equations."

Restated in the main text, p.8:

> "We utilize multi-stage training for the stable and first unstable solutions of the CCF and
> IPM equations. For the solutions of the CCF equations, we are able to improve the maximum
> residuals by five orders of magnitude down to O(10⁻¹³) …"

**This is the crux, and it is decidable from the paper alone.** The `10⁻⁸` floor is named as a
property of *the method without multi-stage training*. Multi-stage training buys **five orders
of magnitude**. And it was applied to exactly four of the twelve solutions — CCF stable/1st and
IPM stable/1st. **Every solution sitting at `10⁻⁸`–`10⁻⁷` is a solution the precision technique
was never applied to** — not one where it was applied and failed. Boussinesq, in particular,
received no multi-stage training at all, and its whole column sits within a factor of ~15 of
the stated single-stage floor. The paper therefore does not exhibit a *model* wall for
Boussinesq; it exhibits an *unapplied technique*.

### 2.4 A structural CAP prerequisite is stated as *desirable* and left as an *assumption*

p.19, Methods §*Spectrum of Linearization*:

> "**For a computer-assisted proof to be feasible, it is desirable that the spectrum in the
> right-half µ-plane consists of a finite number of eigenvalues.**"

The paper's own stability analysis does not establish this. It is itself PINN-based and carries
two explicit restrictions, same page:

> "Here, the eigenfunction problem is **restricted to Ψ that lie within the same symmetry class
> as the original solution**."

> "…we learn the non-negative real eigenvalues of the corresponding linearized operators,
> **under the assumption that there exist eigenvalues with non-negative real part that lie on
> the real axis**."

And the completeness claim is hedged accordingly (p.5): "For the n-th unstable solution
discovered, we find n unstable modes **that respect the same symmetry assumptions as the
solution**. … this **suggests** that the family of solutions discovered so far are complete
within the range of admissible λ values considered."

**This is the obstruction that a certificate would have to discharge and that precision alone
does not touch.** For a *stable* singularity a CAP must control a contraction; for an
*unstable* one it must additionally enclose the unstable subspace exactly — its dimension, and
the absence of further unstable eigenvalues off the real axis and outside the symmetry class.
The paper assumes both. Assuming the unstable modes are real and symmetric is precisely the
step a CAP is for.

### 2.5 The λ-resolution limit, and one solution the paper itself declines to validate

p.18, Methods §*Validation of λ*:

> "We find that the log-symlog plot of smoothness signal has a bowl shape due to the numerical
> nature of the evaluation method. In other words, **there exists a limit to the resolution of λ
> at which this method can discern admissibility.**"

Significant digits of λ are read off the *width of the basin* of that bowl (p.18) — an
empirical, seed-averaged proxy `R̃(λ)` over five runs, not an enclosure. And the Boussinesq 4th
unstable is explicitly not validated (p.5):

> "Furthermore, we have identified a candidate for the fourth unstable solution. This solution,
> however, is not resolved to the same level of accuracy as the others; in particular, **we do
> not yet have a reliable evaluation of the accuracy of its corresponding λ value.**"

with Fig. 2f's caption (p.4) stating flatly: "The 4th unstable solution of the Boussinesq
equation is un-validated."

### 2.6 A reproducibility gap that is *not* stated as an obstruction but functions as one

Footnote 1, p.4:

> "The Supplementary Information to the paper will be uploaded in a forthcoming version of the
> manuscript."

The SI carries, by the paper's own repeated deferrals: the precise definition of the equation
residuals for CCF/IPM/Boussinesq (p.14), the validation grids (p.15), the sampling schemes and
loss weights (p.15), the per-problem λ-optimization combinations (p.16), the full optimizer
description (p.16), the linearized operators for the spectral analysis (p.19), and the details
of the un-validated 4th Boussinesq candidate (p.5).

**arXiv lists `[v1] Wed, 17 Sep 2025` and no other version** (checked
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14185>, this pass, 2026-08-06). **The SI has not appeared in ~11
months.** As published, the paper is not independently reproducible at the level a certificate
requires — every object a CAP would need to enclose is defined in a document that does not
exist publicly.

## 3. The decisive evidence: the same authors named the obstruction and removed it

`arXiv:2511.22819`, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22819> — Yongji Wang, Tristan Léger, Ching-Yao
Lai, Tristan Buckmaster, **"Resolving Sharp Gradients of Unstable Singularities to Machine
Precision via Neural Networks"**, v1, 28 Nov 2025, 27 pages. Fetched and read this pass
(`Papers/2511.22819.pdf`). Three of its four authors are corresponding or lead authors of
`2509.14185`, and it cites `2509.14185` as its `[1]` throughout.

Its abstract (p.1) states the obstruction of `2509.14185` in the authors' own words:

> "While this framework confirmed the existence of these singularities, an accuracy level
> approaching double-float machine precision … **was only achieved for stable and 1st unstable
> solutions of the 1D Córdoba-Córdoba-Fontelos model. For highly unstable solutions
> characterized by extreme gradients, the accuracy remained insufficient for validation. The
> primary obstacle is the presence of sharp solution gradients.** Those gradients tend to induce
> large, localized PDE residuals during training, which not only hinder convergence, but also
> obscure the subtle signals near the origin required to identify the correct self-similar
> scaling parameter λ of the solutions."

And it removes that obstruction with a **loss-function re-weighting**, on the **same models**
(p.2, §1):

> "This paper introduces a critical innovation to overcome this barrier: **a gradient-normalized
> loss function.** By normalizing the PDE residuals relative to the local gradients of the
> solution, we effectively rebalance the optimization landscape. … **We achieve O(10⁻¹³)
> residuals for the full suite of previously discovered CCF solutions and achieve accuracy
> between O(10⁻¹¹) and O(10⁻¹³) for the 2-D IPM solutions.** Furthermore, this enhanced precision
> enables the confirmation of the 4th unstable IPM solution."

**This settles the gate.** The barrier was `2509.14185`'s optimization landscape — "The primary
roadblock lies in the optimization landscape itself" (p.2) — not IPM's or Boussinesq's
mathematics. No model was changed. A re-weighting of the residual moved CCF's 2nd unstable from
`10^-6.664` to `O(10^-13)` and IPM's whole column into `10^-11`–`10^-13`. **An obstruction
removable by re-weighting a loss on an unchanged model is technique-specific by construction.**

### 3.1 What `2511.22819` did *not* do, and it matters

**Boussinesq is absent.** Searching the full text, "Boussinesq" occurs exactly **once** — in
the abstract's recap of `[1]` — and never in the body. The follow-up demonstrates on "two
canonical models: the 1D Córdoba-Córdoba-Fontelos equation and the 2D Incompressible Porous
Media equation" (p.2, §2), then extends to NLS solitons and Gross–Pitaevskii vortices.
**The model class that is 3D Euler with boundary did not receive the fix.** Its best published
residual remains `2509.14185` Fig. 3b's `10^-8.178` … `10^-7.020`.

### 3.2 The residual gap has a measured SHAPE, not just an endpoint (lesson 72)

`2511.22819` p.11, §2.3.3, measures two compounding ladders — this is the most useful
quantitative result of this pass:

> "On average, the magnitude of the non-smooth signal Rmax **decays exponentially, reducing by
> one order of magnitude for each higher-order singularity.** This feature confirms that
> resolving higher-order modes is intrinsically more difficult, as an exponentially weaker Rmax
> signal demands a progressively lower background PDE residual to be revealed."

> "While our enhanced multi-stage method is robust, the high-gradient nature of higher-order
> unstable modes still reduces the convergence rate of training. For a fixed computational
> budget (identical network size, iterations, and two training stages), **the achievable
> background PDE residual for the smooth solution increases by approximately one order of
> magnitude for each successive singularity** … For example, figure 7(c) shows that the best
> Rmax residual for the stable solution reaches O(10⁻¹⁴), while the one for the **4th unstable
> solution stagnates at O(10⁻¹⁰)**."

So, per additional unstable mode, at fixed compute: **the signal you must detect falls ~1
decade, and the noise floor you must detect it against rises ~1 decade.** The usable margin
closes at **~2 decades per instability order**. That is the real, quantified, still-open
technique gap — and it is *not* removed by gradient normalization, only pushed back. It is why
IPM's 4th unstable stagnates at `10^-10` while its stable solution reaches `10^-14`.

## 5. Does the escalation branch fire? — No, and here is the honest reason

The gate's MODEL-SPECIFIC branch instructs: if the alternative model class is one this
repository's infrastructure already touches (gCLM/CLM/Boussinesq family), escalate as a
candidate construction lane. **The gate answered TECHNIQUE-SPECIFIC, so that branch does not
fire.** Recorded here anyway, because the near-miss is worth the DM's attention and because
overstating it would be the easy error:

**CCF is *not* a member of this repository's gCLM `a`-family.** Checked directly against
`solver/gclm_family.py` (lines 11–13), whose model is

    omega_t + a u omega_x = omega H(omega),   u_x = H(omega),  u(0)=0

with `a=0` CLM and `a=1` De Gregorio. CCF is `theta_t + (H theta) theta_x = 0` — the velocity is
`H theta` itself rather than its antiderivative `U`, and **there is no stretching term at all**.
It is a relative, not a member. `grep -rn "CCF\|Córdoba\|Cordoba" solver/` returns **nothing**:
this repository has no CCF residual, and never has.

What *is* shared is model-agnostic 1D infrastructure — `solver/line_hilbert.py` (whole-line
dense Hilbert matrix), `solver/interval.py`, `solver/profile_newton.py`. That is a statement
about tooling, not about a lane. **This leg does not propose a CCF construction and claims no
adjacency beyond the tooling.**

## 6. Gap, stated in leg 173's terms, for comparability

Leg 173 (Route-XUM) classifies a gap as **precision** / **scope** / **infrastructure**. Applying
the same three-way split to `2509.14185`:

* **Precision — CLOSED for CCF and IPM, by `2511.22819`.** `O(10^-13)` for the full CCF suite;
  `O(10^-11)`–`O(10^-13)` for 2D IPM including a newly confirmed 4th unstable mode. Round-off
  limited. This is the part of the gap that was real in Sep 2025 and is not real now.
* **Precision — OPEN for Boussinesq / 3D Euler with boundary,** by **~5 decades**
  (`10^-7.020` … `10^-8.178` today vs the `10^-13` CCF threshold). Status: **untested, not
  measured-dead** — the fix exists and has simply not been published on this model. The
  quantified headwind is §3.2's two-decade-per-mode ladder.
* **Scope — OPEN, and this one is not a precision question.** §2.4: the finite-unstable-spectrum
  condition a CAP needs is stated as "desirable" and never established; the unstable-mode count
  is obtained under an assumed-real, symmetry-class-restricted PINN eigensolve. **No amount of
  residual reduction discharges this.**
* **Infrastructure — OPEN.** No CAP exists for any of these solutions (§4). The SI defining
  every object such a CAP would enclose has not been published in ~11 months (§2.6).

## 7. What this corrects in the user's framing

The framing under review was *"still one model class away."* The paper and its follow-up do not
support that reading. It was **one loss-reweighting scheme away** — and that scheme landed 72
days after `2509.14185`, on the same models, closing CCF and IPM to round-off.

What is genuinely still open is different from, and narrower than, "a model class": (a) the
same fix has not been published for Boussinesq/3D-Euler-with-boundary, (b) the two-decade-
per-mode margin ladder of §3.2, (c) the unstable-spectrum enclosure of §2.4, which is a
*proof* obligation and not a numerics one, and (d) the CAP announced in Sep 2025 that has not
appeared.
