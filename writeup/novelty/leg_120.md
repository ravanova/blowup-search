## What is being checked for prior art

* **(N1)** Is there a *published, exact* statement of the 2/3-dealiasing cutoff — specifically,
  is the alias-free condition on the largest retained wavenumber a **strict** inequality
  `K < N/3` or a non-strict `K <= N/3`? The module's `dealias_mask` uses `<=`. If the published
  condition is strict, the battery has a documented boundary to test against rather than an
  invented one, and the `n` divisible by 3 case is a *marginal* case with a name.
* **(N2)** Is the treatment of the Nyquist coefficient in an odd-order spectral derivative
  published as an algorithm, and in what *form* — as "set it to zero" (an assignment) or
  "multiply it by zero" (a product)? The two forms are identical on finite input and differ on
  non-finite input, so this decides whether the module's `d[-1] = 0.0` is the published
  algorithm or a deviation from it.
* **(N3)** Is "adversarial battery against a shared numerical module, checking that invalid
  input is propagated or flagged rather than silently absorbed" an established *method*, and is
  overwriting a NaN a named failure mode? (Leg 91's pass established this vocabulary for the
  fractional-gCLM module; re-checked here for the shared-core case, not re-derived.)

### Q2
`"2/3 rule" dealiasing N divisible by 3 boundary mode retained aliasing marginal cutoff pseudospectral`

- https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~bowman/talks/alias0.pdf — **John C. Bowman, *How Important is
  Dealiasing for Turbulence Simulations?*, University of Alberta, June 19 2013.** ← the settling
  reference.
- https://www.math.umd.edu/~tadmor/references/files/Hou%20&%20Li%20filter%20vs%202-3%20rule.pdf
  and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999107001623 — Hou & Li,
  *Computing nearly singular solutions using pseudo-spectral methods* (high-order Fourier
  smoothing vs the 2/3 rule).
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242807133_A_new_technique_for_a_parallel_dealiased_pseudospectral_Navier-Stokes_code
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10479, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21891, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17211,
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.0116, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22977 — the dealiasing/pseudospectral
  corpus again, none on the boundary case.

**On-topic finding for (N1), and it is the sharpest result of this pass — it sets the battery's
boundary from the literature rather than by invention.** Bowman's *Centered Convolutions* slide
(p. 29) states the condition exactly, and names the mechanism:

> "Here, one needs to pad to `N >= 3m - 2` to prevent **mode `m - 1` from beating with itself to
> contaminate the most negative (first) mode**, corresponding to wavenumber `-m + 1`. Since the
> ratio of the number of physical to total modes, `(2m - 1)/(3m - 2)` is asymptotic to 2/3 for
> large `m`, this padding scheme is often referred to as the 2/3 padding rule."

With `K = m - 1` the largest retained wavenumber, `N >= 3m - 2 = 3K + 1`, i.e. **`N > 3K`, i.e.
`K < N/3` strictly.** `dealias_mask` retains `k <= n/3`. For `3 ∤ n` that is `K = floor(n/3) <
n/3` and the mask is correct; for `3 | n` it is `K = n/3`, `N = 3K` exactly, violating Bowman's
condition by exactly one mode — and the failure it predicts is *precisely* "mode `K` beating with
itself to contaminate mode `-K`". The battery therefore does not have to guess what to look for:
it constructs the field supported at `k = K`, squares it, and measures the coefficient that lands
back on `K`. **This is a documented boundary, published in 2013, and the leg claims no novelty in
it whatsoever** — the only thing that could be new is that *this repository's* mask sits on the
wrong side of it.

**On-topic finding for (N2), and it gives the battery an exact target line.** Johnson's
**Algorithm 1** (compute the sampled first derivative) states step 2 as:

> "Multiply `Y_k` by `2πi/L k` for `k < N/2`, by `2πi/L (k - N)` for `k > N/2`, and by **zero**
> for `k = N/2` (if `N` is even), to obtain `Y'_k`."

and the accompanying footnote confirms the odd case — "*unless `N` is odd so that there is no
`Y_{N/2}` term*". Two consequences, both of which shape the battery:

1. **The module's even/odd split is the published algorithm, and is correct.** This is a
   *confirmation*, and it is recorded as loudly as any defect: leg 66's finding is fixed, the fix
   matches Johnson Algorithm 1 exactly, and this leg re-derives that rather than re-finding it.
2. **The published prescription is a *multiplication* by zero; the module performs an
   *assignment*.** `spectral_utils.derivative_hat` executes `d[-1] = 0.0`. On finite input the two
   are identical. On non-finite input they are not: IEEE-754 gives `0.0 * nan = nan` and
   `0.0 * inf = nan`, so the *published* form propagates a poisoned Nyquist coefficient and the
   *assignment* form erases it. That is a one-line, literature-anchored target, and the battery
   instruments it directly rather than inferring it.

## What this pass changed about the leg, before construction

1. **It set the dealiasing boundary from the literature, not by invention** (N1/Q2): Bowman's
   `N >= 3m - 2` makes the alias-free condition `K < N/3` **strict**, so `n` divisible by 3 is a
   *named marginal case*, and the failure it predicts — mode `K` beating with itself onto mode
   `-K` — is the exact experiment the battery runs. Without this, "the mask keeps one mode too
   many" would have been an assertion; with it, it is a measurement against a published
   inequality.
2. **It gave the NaN battery a one-line target and a reason to expect the two forms to differ**
   (N2/Q3): Johnson Algorithm 1 prescribes *multiply by zero*; the module *assigns* zero. The
   battery therefore probes the Nyquist slot specifically, with a non-Nyquist slot and an odd-`n`
   top mode as controls, instead of poisoning arrays at random.
3. **It ruled the leg's biggest false-positive risk out in advance** (N2, consequence 1): the
   even/odd split in `derivative_hat` is *correct and published*, so the battery must not report
   the Nyquist zeroing itself as a defect — only the erasure of non-finite values by the
   assignment form. Leg 66/69's finding is treated as fixed background and carries a regression
   control, never a re-find.
4. **It requires passes to be recorded as loudly as failures** (N3, inherited from leg 91): the
   inputs that correctly propagate NaN/Inf are part of the result, so the deliverable is a *map*
   of the module's behaviour, not a bug list.
5. **It pre-empts every novelty claim.** Nothing this leg can find is new mathematics. A defect
   is a defect in our code against a boundary already in print since 2011/2013; a clean result is
   a confirmation of robustness. The leg claims **no** novelty, makes **no** statement about any
   physics quantity, and every number it reports is a statement about code behaviour under
   degenerate or poisoned input.
