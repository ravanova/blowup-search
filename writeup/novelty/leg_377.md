## 2. What was reachable in full — and why it turned out to be a different basis

The Semantic Scholar API (after a rate-limit retry) surfaced two fully open-access, directly
on-topic, and much more current papers than anything in leg 374's own table:

* Hu, Yu, "Scaling Optimized Hermite Approximation Methods", arXiv:2412.08044v3 (29 Oct 2025) —
  fetched and read in full (PDF pages 1–4).
* Hu, Yu, "Scaling Optimized Spectral Approximations on Unbounded Domains: The Generalized Hermite
  and Laguerre Methods", arXiv:2602.03083v1 (3 Feb 2026) — fetched and read in full (PDF pages
  1–5).

Both are squarely about "the exact convergence-rate question Boyd (1980) raised," written to give
(their own words) "a rigorous and impactful answer to this confusing impasse." Read closely, this
turned into a genuine trap avoided rather than a shortcut taken: **Hu-Yu's own "generalized Hermite
functions" are defined (arXiv:2602.03083 §2.2) with weight `ω_μ(x) = |x|^{2μ} e^{-x²}`** — still
Gaussian-enveloped, with an added power singularity at the origin (parameter `μ`) — a **different
basis from Zhang-Guo's algebraically-enveloped `(1+x²)^{-γ}` construction**, which decays
algebraically (no `e^{-x²}` factor at all). Same name (a real citation-tracking hazard), different
object. Hu-Yu's own headline finding — even with optimal scaling, Hermite-family approximation of
smooth algebraically-decaying targets achieves only an *algebraic* (not geometric) rate, e.g. their
quoted `N^{-α}`-up-to-log-factor result citing Kazashi et al., with genuine geometric convergence
recovered only for a *different*, faster-decaying target class (`exp(-C̃|x|^ρ)`) — is real, current
(page 3 of arXiv:2412.08044v3), and is carried here as load-bearing background on *why* Gaussian-
enveloped Hermite functions face this obstruction: an approximate Fourier self-duality (Hermite
functions can't be simultaneously localized in space and frequency), which does not automatically
apply to Zhang-Guo's algebraically-enveloped family, which has no such self-duality. Conflating the
two would have repeated exactly the error leg 374's own §0 warned against for a different pair of
objects (leg 334's linearized-stability-operator spectrum vs. a general basis-existence claim) — a
second instance of the same discipline, applied here to a same-named-but-different basis rather than
a same-operator-different-context risk.

## 3. Reading could not resolve this leg's gate — so it measures

Given both the actual candidate's own papers (paywalled) and the closest fully-read literature
(a different basis, despite the name) could not directly answer the question, this leg builds and
runs a small, checkable numerical instrument (`experiments/p2_route_hcdx_v1.py`) instead of
asserting a reading-based verdict it does not have. The construction is not invented for
convenience: polynomials orthogonal on `R` w.r.t. weight `(1+x²)^{-γ}` are, under the elementary
algebraic bijection `t = x/√(1+x²)` (`R → (-1,1)`), exactly Gegenbauer/ultraspherical polynomials
`C_n^{(γ-1)}(t)` orthogonal w.r.t. the classical weight `(1-t²)^{α-1/2}`, `α=γ-1` — this is the
actual mathematical content of "generalized Hermite functions matching algebraic decay," derived
in the script's own docstring, not a substitute basis. Full measurement, method, and results are
in `writeup/data/p2_route_hcdx_v1.json`'s `measurement` block and `experiments/journal/leg_377.md`.

## 4. Result

**Classification: ADEQUATE.** Boyd (1980)'s concern is real and its mechanism is directly
corroborated by this leg's own measurement (mismatching the basis weight to the target's decay
exponent measurably degrades coefficient decay from geometric to algebraic, more severely as the
mismatch grows) — but it does not disqualify the candidate for route 4, because route 4's target
decay exponent is not a free unknown: leg 260 already pins it structurally (`s>1`, a measured sharp
threshold). Choosing the basis weight exponent to match (`γ=s`) is an available, known choice, and
this leg's own measurement shows that choice yields genuine geometric coefficient decay, robustly
across four tested values of `s` in route 4's own class. Full reasoning:
`writeup/data/p2_route_hcdx_v1.json`'s `reasoning_to_classification`.
