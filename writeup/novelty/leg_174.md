# Leg 174 — Route-VBS novelty / literature pass (run FIRST, committed BEFORE any construction)

**Date:** 2026-08-06. **Branch:** `leg/174-vbs-v1`. **Route:** VBS — "a viscous certified
blow-up in any model: the missing rung", sharpened against what this repository has already
banked.

This is a **literature-only** leg. There is no solver module and no new mathematics to
pre-empt, so the usual novelty question ("has someone done our construction?") is replaced by
the leg's own gate: **is the rung already occupied, and by what?** That makes this pass not a
formality but the leg's entire method — the queries below *are* the experiment.

Per `writeup/novelty/README.md`: every query string is **verbatim**, and every link judged
on-topic is listed, whether or not it changed the verdict. **Links, not counts** (the leg-53
lesson).

## What is being checked

* **(N1)** Does `arXiv:2410.05480` (Dahne–Figueras) state its CGL result as an existence
  theorem about **solutions of the PDE**, or only about **profiles solving the ODE**? The
  repository's own ledger row records only "verifies CGL branches in the dissipation
  parameter" — which is silent on this distinction, and the distinction is the gate.
* **(N2)** If it is a PDE statement, what is the **function space** of the resulting singular
  solution, and is its stability / dynamical relevance proved?
* **(N3)** Does the literature contain any OTHER **viscous, fluid/vortex-dynamics-adjacent**
  model with an analytic blow-up proof never computer-certified? (The gate's clause (b)
  catalog.)
* **(N4)** — *not in the gate, and it is the one that mattered* — does the literature contain a
  viscous fluid model whose blow-up is **already** computer-certified? Clause (b) presupposes
  the answer is no. It was asked anyway, because presupposing is how leg 63's ledger row got
  written.

## Verdict

* **(N1) PDE, not ODE — CONFIRMED AT FULL TEXT.** DF's Theorems 1.2/1.3/3.2 and — the ones the
  gate is about — the CGL Theorems **4.1 and 4.4** are stated as *"the CGL equation with
  ϵ = ϵ_j(s) has a self-similar singular solution of the form u(x,t) = …"*. The passage from
  certified profile to singular PDE solution is exact substitution of Ansatz (2) into
  Equation (1), not a further analytic argument that might be missing.
* **(N2) INFINITE MASS, STABILITY OPEN.** `Q(ξ) ∼ ξ^{−1/σ−iω/κ}`, and DF themselves record
  `Q ∈ C^∞ ∩ L³` in Case I (**not** `L²`). Remark 1.6: *"Proving these stability results would
  be of great interest."* So it is a certified singular solution, of infinite mass, whose
  dynamical relevance is unproved.
* **(N3) CATALOGUED**, six entries, in `writeup/data/p2_route_vbs_v1_scoping.json`.
* **(N4) FOUND — and it makes the leg.** `arXiv:2208.09445` Theorem 1.3 is a proof of
  finite-time singularity formation for the **3D isentropic compressible NAVIER–STOKES
  equations** from smooth, finite-energy data with density constant at infinity, and its proof
  depends essentially on an interval-arithmetic computer-assisted step (the γ = 7/5 profile of
  Theorem 1.2, 10000 Taylor coefficient pairs, ~14 CPU-hours). The rung is not empty and its
  occupant is not CGL: it is a genuine viscous fluid equation. **But** the certified sub-object
  is the *inviscid* Euler profile — which is why the two grades of "certified" below have to be
  separated, and why leg 113's `clause_2_inviscid: True` is both correct and misleading if
  carried across to the user's question.

### The claim I nearly banked, and the check that killed it

The first draft of this section said `arXiv:2208.09445` "appears **nowhere** in this
repository." **That was false, and I checked it before committing rather than after.**

`grep -rn "2208.09445\|imploding\|implosion\|compressible" --include=*.py --include=*.md .`
returns `experiments/p2_route_ms_v1_lit.py` — **leg 113's Route-MS ledger**, which read BCG at
full text, recorded its journal reference (Forum of Math Pi 13 (2025) e6, doi
10.1017/fmp.2024.12), two located verbatim quotes, and a probe count of **35** occurrences of
`computer-assisted` and **2** of `interval arithmetic`. Leg 113 knew this paper thoroughly.

It classified it **`clause_2_inviscid: True`**, under its own explicit definition (line 124):

> *"the certified equation carries NO dissipative term -- no -nu*Laplacian, …"*

**And on that definition leg 113 is exactly right.** The equation BCG's interval arithmetic
certifies is system (1.5), the self-similar ODE of the **inviscid** compressible Euler system
(1.3). The dissipative Navier–Stokes result, Theorem 1.3, is reached *from* that certified
inviscid profile by the analytic stability argument of BCG §§7–8.

So the finding is **not** a ledger hole. It is sharper and more useful: **"the certified
equation" and "the equation the theorem is about" are different objects, and this repository's
ledger tracks only the first.** Read across to the user's question, that single labelling
choice is the entire reason the rung looks empty. This is written down before the runner
exists, and it is what the runner must measure.

The narrower claim survives and is separately verified: `2208.09445` is absent from
`solver/viscous_novelty.py::PRECEDENTS` and from `LITERATURE_CHECK.md`, and the 12-query
`SEARCH_LOG` in `viscous_novelty.py` contains no query that could have returned it — none
mentions *compressible*, *implosion*, or *imploding*. Stage V's novelty gate would not have
found it.
