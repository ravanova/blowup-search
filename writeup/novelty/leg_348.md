## 3. Links banked (not counts), each read at abstract level (full LaTeX/PDF not fetched this leg)

1. **`math/0005247`** — Zgliczyński, *"Self-consistent a priori bounds..."* [repo's existing
   pin]. Abstract, fetched this leg: *"a new topological method for the study of the dynamics of
   dissipative PDE's... based on the concept of the self-consistent a-priori bounds, which allows
   to justify rigorously the Galerkin projection... a computer assisted proof of the existence of
   FIXED POINTS for the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation."* **Two things newly confirmed by reading
   the abstract rather than transcribing the repo's prior gloss**: (i) the canonical application in
   this specific paper is **fixed points**, not periodic orbits (the periodic-orbit extension is
   the Zgliczyński–Mischaikow programme more broadly, not evidenced by this abstract alone); (ii)
   KS in this line of work is standardly posed with **periodic boundary conditions** (the paper's
   own domain, not independently verified past the abstract this leg).

2. **`arXiv:2305.08221`** — van den Berg–Breden–Sheombarsing, *Validated integration of semilinear
   parabolic PDEs* [repo's existing pin, re-read at abstract]. Quote, newly load-bearing for this
   leg: *"a computer-assisted proof methodology to perform rigorous time integration for scalar
   semilinear parabolic PDEs **with periodic boundary conditions**. We formulate an equivalent
   zero-finding problem based on a variations of constants formula **in Fourier space**."*
   Explicitly periodic/compact domain, explicitly Fourier-space.

3. **`arXiv:1902.00384`** — van den Berg, Breden, Lessard, van Veen, *Spontaneous periodic orbits
   in the Navier-Stokes flow* (2019). **Correction made mid-pass**: this id is NOT new to the
   repository — `experiments/journal/leg_245.md:102-116` already located and read it, recording
   *"Maxime Breden has already done Grade-A CAP on genuine 3D Navier-Stokes with the viscous term
   inside the certified equation"* and flagging it as failing leg 245's own gate on two counts
   (predates leg 245's parent; certifies *"a periodic orbit of a smooth flow — a regular
   solution — not a self-similar blow-up profile. Wrong direction in time, wrong object in
   kind"*), while explicitly recommending *"a follow-up leg should start here."* This leg is
   that follow-up, on the domain-shape axis rather than the object-kind axis leg 245 was
   screening for. Abstract, re-read here for the domain-compactness question leg 245 did not
   ask: *"a general method to obtain constructive proofs
   of existence of periodic orbits in forced, autonomous Navier-Stokes equations **on the
   three-torus**... a zero finding problem posed on a Banach space of geometrically decaying
   Fourier coefficients [and] the Newton-Kantorovich theorem... proofs of existence of spontaneous
   periodic orbits in the Navier-Stokes equations with Taylor-Green forcing."* **This is the
   single most directly relevant paper located**: a periodic orbit of NS, computer-assisted, but
   (a) on a **compact** domain (3-torus, not R³), (b) via **NK-in-Banach-space of geometrically
   decaying Fourier coefficients** — the same apparatus family (radii-polynomial/NK,
   exponentially-decaying-coefficient basis) already measured DEAD in this repository across four
   realizations for the stationary-profile problem, now seen applied successfully to a
   *periodic-orbit* target but only on a domain whose geometry supplies the exponential coefficient
   decay for free.

4. **`arXiv:2409.09234`** — *Mathematically established chaos and forecast of statistics with
   recurrent patterns in Taylor-Couette flow* (2024). Abstract: *"a minimal periodic domain...
   unstable periodic orbits (UPOs) of the Navier-Stokes system"*, rigorous chaos statement via a
   discrete map. Compact/minimal-periodic domain again.

5. **`arXiv:2105.04148`** — Arioli, *Computer assisted proof of branches of stationary and
   periodic solution, and Hopf bifurcations, for dissipative PDEs* (2021). Abstract: worked
   example is *"the Brussellator system with diffusion and **Dirichlet boundary conditions**"* —
   bounded domain, not periodic, not unbounded.

6. **`arXiv:2009.12762`** — Arioli–Koch, *A Hopf bifurcation in the planar Navier-Stokes
   equations* (2020) — located via the controlled per-author query `au:"Arioli" AND au:"Koch"`
   (MF4-compliant: not a compound-name query). Abstract: NS *"on a square, satisfying Navier
   boundary conditions"* — 2D, bounded domain, periodic cycle from a Hopf bifurcation, *"proof is
   constructive and uses computer-assisted estimates."* This is the actual "Arioli-Koch line"
   applied to Navier-Stokes named in this leg's thesis, and it is bounded/square, not R³/unbounded.

7. **`arXiv:2308.01528`** (Huang–Qin–Wang–Wei) — *Exact self-similar finite-time blowup of the
   Hou-Luo model with smooth profiles* (2023). **Also not new to the repository** — already cited
   as `HQWW arXiv:2308.01528` at `experiments/journal/leg_125.md:161`,
   `writeup/novelty/leg_163.md:86` and `experiments/journal/leg_318.md:114`, though none of those
   citations engage the domain/decay question this leg needs. Abstract, re-read here for that
   question: *"the 1D Hou-Luo model **on the real line**"* — unbounded domain — *"rigorous
   estimates on the **algebraic decay rates** of the profiles in the far field... supplements the
   previous **computer-assisted proof** of self-similar finite-time blowup for the Hou-Luo
   model."* This is the one link, new or old, combining unbounded domain + algebraic far-field
   decay + a rigorous/computer-assisted existence result — but it is (a) a **stationary**
   self-similar profile, not a time-periodic orbit; (b) established by a **"purely analytic"
   fixed-point method** per its own abstract, refining an earlier computer-assisted proof whose
   own method is not established by this abstract; (c) 1D, no Leray projector, no analogue of
   route 4's non-axisymmetric 3D structure (leg 260 §2: axisymmetric DSS is excluded, so no
   symmetry reduction is available for route 4's object — this Hou-Luo result has no such
   constraint since 1D has no axisymmetry question).
