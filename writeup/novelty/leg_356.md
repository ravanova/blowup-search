found eight reproduce exactly; the ninth, `p2_route_e_v1_spectrum`, does not (`max_rel_move =

A repo-wide grep for `match_filter`, `canonical_order`, and `argsort.*real` (case-insensitive,

rediscovery.

## 1. What could have gone differently, and why it did not

**Hypothesis space entering this leg**, stated by the dispatch text itself: "eigenpair
ordering/sign instability... especially near a degeneracy (e.g. two eigenvalues crossing or
nearly equal)". Three sub-hypotheses were live before any measurement:

1. Two GENUINE (non-zero, well-separated-in-content) eigenvalues cross or nearly cross as `a`
   varies, and match_filter's descending-real-part sort flips their order at the crossing. This
   would be an environment-SENSITIVE crossing (bad, but at least a real dynamical fact about the
   operator) — ruled out by inspection: the a=0 sweep entry, where the crossing (if real) would
   have to be visible, is the WORST mover, but a=0 is also the one point in this whole module
   where the spectrum is analytically KNOWN in closed form (module docstring): everything off the
   two structural modes is continuous spectrum pinned to `Re lambda = 0` exactly. There is no
   genuine crossing available there. This sub-hypothesis was refuted by the module's own stated
   theory before it needed a numerical refutation, and the numerical refutation (§ below) agrees.
2. An eigenVECTOR sign convention flips (this is the more common instability in eigensolvers with
   degenerate/near-degenerate eigenvalues — LAPACK's `eigvals`, though, returns EIGENVALUES only,
   no eigenvectors, at this call site — `np.linalg.eigvals`, not `eig`). Ruled out immediately by
   reading `spectrum()`/`match_filter()`: no eigenvector is computed or stored anywhere in this
   pipeline, only eigenvalues. This half of the dispatch's "obvious candidate" wording does not
   apply to this code.
3. An eigenVALUE ORDERING instability from a sort key that is a floating-point near-tie for many
   entries, not just two — confirmed (see CORRECTIONS.md §25 for the measured evidence). The
   entire discretized ESSENTIAL spectrum (all of it, not two specific eigenvalues) shares the same
   theoretical real part (0, exactly, at a=0), so `match_filter`'s `-Re(lambda)` sort key is
   degenerate across the WHOLE kept set at loose tolerance, not at one isolated crossing point.
   This is a stronger and more structural failure than "two eigenvalues crossing" — it explains
   why 62 leaves moved (a whole array's worth of positions), not 2.

## 2. Registered prediction, checked after the fix (not before — this is a repair leg, and the
   fix's correctness is checked by direct re-run, not a held-out prediction)

If the mechanism is (3), a canonical re-sort keyed on a value ABOVE the noise floor and BELOW any
genuine content should make the array order reproducible across independent processes in one
environment, and should leave every consumer's actually-read leaf (which none of them draw from
the unstable `kept["<loose tol>"]` array — see CORRECTIONS.md §25(b)) untouched. Both checked
directly: three independent regenerations (two standalone, one full-pipeline) give a byte-
identical `E5_sweep[0].kept["0.1"]` array; `test_literature_gates.py` passes in full against the
repaired banked JSON.
