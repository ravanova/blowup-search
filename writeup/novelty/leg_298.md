## 1. Why this is not a novel *finding* leg, and why that is the right shape for the gate asked

The dispatch's gate is a **freshness check**, not a measurement: "does every entry cite a real,
still-current locator, avoid duplicating another entry, and accurately summarize its source
leg?" A leg answering this gate produces evidence of *absence* of defect (or, where found, a
fix to the register's own bookkeeping) — it does not produce a new mathematical claim about
Object B, the certificate, or any Clay-relevant quantity. Nothing in this leg moves any prior
gate answer, lifts any ban, or touches the `L1 → L4` chain.

## 2. What was checked, and the standard applied

Every one of `CORRECTIONS.md`'s 28 §-sections (spanning legs 263 through 366, six weeks of
campaign history) was read in full, not skimmed. For locator verification (clause a), I did not
trust the register's own prose — I looked up the cited `file:line` directly in the live worktree
and compared the returned text against what the entry claims sits there. For summary-accuracy
verification (clause c), I read the *source leg's own* journal or novelty file (not the
register's paraphrase of it) for a representative sample spanning the oldest entries (§4, leg
111/141/165/178, 2026-08-06) through the two most recent (§27/§28, legs 355/366, 2026-08-12) —
the two the dispatch specifically flagged for extra scrutiny, since §28's NRS-1996
re-attribution is itself a correction to a citation, and a wrong correction would be a more
damaging defect than a stale one.

**Every checked locator resolved to its claimed content. Every checked summary matched its
source leg's own report.** This is itself informative: it means the register's contributors
(legs 263, 276, 279, 280, 282, 283, 295, 296, 300, 328, 335, 336, 338, 339, 352, 355, 356, 361,
366, and others) have consistently practiced the discipline the register asks of them — quoting
banked values rather than re-typing them from memory, and citing sources with enough precision
to be checked. A register that had accumulated silent drift across 28 sections and ~370 legs of
editing pressure elsewhere in the repo would be a notable negative finding; a register that
mostly hadn't is itself worth recording plainly, rather than manufacturing significance where
none was measured.

## 3. The one defect class, and why it is exactly this shape

`DIRECTION.md` is the single most actively-edited file in the repository (edited essentially
every DM cycle). Two locators in §21.2 (`:9856`, `:13722`, both written by leg 328 the same day
they went stale) had drifted by 1 and 7 lines respectively, from unrelated edits to `DIRECTION.md`
made after leg 328 landed. This is exactly the failure mode `CORRECTIONS.md`'s own §16.1 already
diagnosed and fixed for a *different* pair of `DIRECTION.md` sites (the `6.855x` gate-text
pointers), by switching from a bare line number to a grep-stable text anchor. I applied the same
fix, in the same style, to §21.2's two sites — no new mechanism invented, an existing repo
convention correctly re-applied.

**The hazard this leg had to avoid, and how it was avoided.** Chasing every `DIRECTION.md`/
`experiments/JOURNAL.md` line-number citation in the file to a byte-perfect match would be
unbounded work against a constantly-moving target, and the register's own text (§8, §9)
explicitly disclaims this class of locator as historical rather than live ("dated dispatch-
priority records… history, not live claims"; "Locators for whoever audits it next"). Fixing
those would not be honoring the register's own stated intent — it would be silently promoting a
breadcrumb to a claim of present accuracy it never made. §21.2's two sites are different in kind:
they back a present-tense assertion ("fixed" — meaning the correction marker is *there right
now*), which is exactly the class of locator this leg's gate is charged with keeping honest, so
those two and only those two were corrected.
