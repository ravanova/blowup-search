unweighted case is the control.

## 2. The OBJECT's spectrum is published — and it supplies a KNOWN-ANSWER CEILING

| ref | what it says | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| [arXiv:2607.19762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19762) Xu, *The spectral picture of self-similar collapse in the Constantin–Lax–Majda equation* | on the **odd** realisation, linearising about `Ω(y) = −y/(y² + 1/4)`: the **full point spectrum over `ℂ` is exactly `{0, 1}`**, the scaling and time-shift symmetry modes, with **no embedded eigenvalues**; the essential spectrum meets `{Re λ ≥ −1/2}` in the **single vertical line `{Re λ = −1/2}`**; removing the two point modes by standard modulation leaves a **spectral gap of `1/2`** on the origin-`H²` space | **PRE-EMPTS ANY SPECTRAL CLAIM, AND ANCHORS THE INSTRUMENT** |

This repository already cites the same paper twice (`capabilities.py`, `rescaled_spectrum.py`
row and `critical_dissipation.py` row). Three consequences, all binding on this leg:

1. **No spectral claim is available.** `{0, 1}` and the gap `1/2` are published. This leg
   must not report either as a finding; it reports them as the **window** (lesson 84) its own
   instrument is checked against.
2. **The ceiling is `1/2`, externally.** A weighted-energy coercivity gap is a bound on the
   **numerical range**, and the numerical range contains the spectrum. With the essential
   spectrum sitting on `Re λ = −1/2`, **no weight whatsoever can give a modulated coercivity
   gap above `1/2`.** Any measurement of this leg's that exceeds `1/2` is an instrument bug,
   and that is a control that can report the other answer.
3. **The gap that is actually open is the one this leg measures.** Xu's own framing, verbatim
   in the abstract's discussion: `L₀` is **non-normal** and "a spectral gap does not by itself
   give a decay rate in the `X` norm" — they compute the semigroup decay separately, on a
   weighted space reached by a bounded transfer map. **A coercivity gap is exactly the missing
   quantity in that sentence**: it is the norm-level statement the spectrum does not give.
   Measuring it for a named weight family is therefore not a re-derivation of Xu.

A fourth reference, surfaced by the same query and recorded because it narrows the claim
further: coercivity estimates for the `gCLM` linearised operator `M_a` are established in the
Elgindi–Jeong-lineage literature on
[stable self-similar blow-up for nonlocal transport equations](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284939)
**under the hypothesis that perturbations vanish to high order at `0` together with their
Hilbert transform.** So "coercivity for this operator" is an existing subject with existing
positive results *under vanishing hypotheses*. This leg imposes no vanishing hypothesis; a
negative measurement here is therefore **not** a contradiction of that literature, and must
not be written as one.
