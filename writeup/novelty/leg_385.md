# Leg 385 novelty pass — Route-SCEL: samples → certified cell enclosures

**Committed BEFORE any construction** (ORCHESTRATION.md §5a, and leg 382's own precedent).
This file is the pass; the pre-registration, the module, the test, the runner, the JSON and
the quartet land in later commits on the same branch.

**Disclosure up front, in the same words leg 382 used, because the same thing is true here.**
This leg claims **no novel mathematics whatsoever**. "A monotone function on a cell is
bracketed by its endpoint values" and "an ω-continuous function is within ω(h/2) of the
nearer of two samples h apart" are textbook facts of validated numerics, older than this
repository by decades. **The only novelty claim made here is a repository claim:**
`solver/dssp_decay_enclosure.py:354` and `experiments/journal/leg_382.md:271` both assert
that the samples→cells conversion is *named but not provided* anywhere in this repository,
and this pass checks that assertion is (a) accurate as of `fd833d2` and (b) that providing it
does not re-enter a banned lane. That is the whole scope.

## 2. Ban compliance — `plan_of_record.py`, checked clause by clause

The build is: a pure function from `(r_i, f_i, hypothesis)` to `(r_lo, r_hi, f_lo, f_hi)`
plus a verdict, on `solver/interval.py`'s substrate.

* **`ℓ¹`-Fourier / radii-polynomial ban (all three re-posings, including the 2026-08-06
  re-pose and leg 304's CADX annotation).** No function space is chosen, no truncation, no
  approximate inverse, no defect, no Newton step, no radii polynomial. There is no operator
  here at all. The ban cannot be touched by a routine whose entire output is four float
  arrays and a string.
* **The DSS cheap-entrance ban and the unseeded-global-trawl ban.** No orbit, no fixed point,
  no bifurcation, no search in any flow. No seed is claimed because nothing is searched.
* **The gCLM measurement ban, the 2D-object bans, the GA-on-unvalidated-fitness ban, the
  truncation-gap and weight-exponent bans.** No gCLM, no 2D object, no GA, no weight
  exponent, no domain extension. Disjoint objects.
* **The "no float study upgraded into a certificate after the fact" clause.** Respected in
  the strongest available form: this module's *entire purpose* is to make explicit the
  hypothesis under which sampled floats become a certified statement, and to **refuse** when
  no hypothesis is supplied. It is the opposite of a retroactive upgrade.

**No ban is approached.** The leg is construction on a consumer-side adapter and moves no
`L1 → L4` link; ceiling TIER 2; Clay stays ~0.05%.

## 3. External prior art — and the disclosed hole, partially discharged

**The mathematics is textbook and is claimed as textbook.** The two hypothesis paths are the
two standard ways validated numerics turns discrete data into a set-valued statement:

* *monotonicity* — on a cell whose endpoints are sampled, a monotone function attains its
  extremes at the endpoints, so the sample pair **is** the enclosure;
* *modulus of continuity* — for `r` in a cell of width `h`, some sample is within `h/2`, so
  `|f(r) − f_sample| ≤ ω(h/2)`; the enclosure is the sample hull inflated by `ω(h/2)`.

Neither is new and this leg would be dishonest to present either as new.

**The genuinely relevant literature point is the negative one**, and it is what justifies the
REFUSAL path rather than a default: from finitely many point evaluations *alone*, with no
regularity hypothesis, **no non-trivial enclosure of the range is derivable** — every
superinterval of the range is consistent with the data. Located by title through Crossref
(`10.1023/a:1009977508817`, "Every Superinterval of the Function Range Can Be an
Interval-Computations Enclosure"). **Located, not read**: it is a title-level citation for a
statement this module also proves for itself operationally by refusing, and it is recorded at
that strength and no higher.

**Disclosed hole, inherited and only partly discharged.** Leg 382's pass recorded that arXiv
and Semantic Scholar both returned HTTP 429 for it and left the external question open. This
pass hit **the same HTTP 429 from `export.arxiv.org`** (both through the fetching tool and
through direct `curl`, over http and https, 2026-08-12) — so the arXiv half of leg 382's hole
is **still open and is not closed by this leg**. Crossref *was* reachable and was queried on
three phrasings (`interval enclosure ... grid samples Lipschitz`, `computer assisted proof
decay exponent far field enclosure`, `rigorous bounds from point samples modulus of
continuity`); it returned the classical interval-arithmetic corpus and the Kreinovich-class
result above, and nothing that pre-empts a repository-internal adapter. **This is a weak
search and is labelled weak.** It does not need to be strong: no mathematical novelty is
claimed, so the only thing a stronger search could change is a citation, not a claim.
