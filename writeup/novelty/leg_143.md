## What this module is, and why its audit surface is unlike the previous 23

`solver/viscous_novelty.py` is **not** a certificate module. It has no `Y_0`/`Z_0`/`Z_1`/`Z_2`,
no radii polynomial, and no `closes=True` verdict function — so the fabrication-rejection class
that legs 79/98/116/140 kept hitting **cannot** occur here, and I must not go looking for it.

What it is instead is the executable form of **stage V's novelty gate** (`plan_of_record.py`
records stage `V` as CLOSED BY ITS OWN GATE at leg 48, and the ban on re-opening it lifts only
"if the question is re-posed for a FLUID transport model"). Its load-bearing outputs are:

| entry point | what it underwrites |
|---|---|
| `novelty_verdict()` | the **gate answer itself** — "YES, stage V is pre-empted", computed off `PRECEDENTS` |
| `shoot_newton` / `match_defect` | the claim that DF's published NLS zeros were *re-derived*, not transcribed |
| `branch_in_kappa` / `fold_of` | the claim that DF's fold at `eps* = 0.0606` was *reproduced* |
| `compare_to_published_branch` | the agreement magnitudes quoted in `capabilities.py` (max 3.0e-06 / rms 1.9e-06) |
| `margin_law` | the `||J^-1||` divergence exponent at the fold |
| `read_df_figure` | a published **vector figure read back as data**, self-calibrated against Table 1 |

So the blast radius of a defect here is **a closed stage's gate answer and a live ban**, not a
certificate constant. That is claim-adjacent in a different way, and it is why the gate's
yes-branch says escalate rather than patch.

Two structural facts sharpen the battery before it is written:

* **The module is numerically self-referential.** `capabilities.py` records its validation as
  "their Tables 1/2 reproduced to 1.8e-07", "their Fig. 1a branch to max 3.0e-06", "their fold
  to 3.8e-07". Every one of those is a *difference between a computed number and a transcribed
  constant in the same file*. If the computation silently degrades, the transcribed constants do
  not move — so the failure mode that matters is **a wrong number that still passes the existing
  tests**, not a crash.
* **`test_viscous_novelty.py` gates completeness only** — eight known-answer predicates on
  well-formed input (ledger well-formed, published zeros reproduce, a second row reproduces,
  the defect discriminates, ladders measured, the branch folds, the margin proxy dies at the
  fold, the figure calibration self-checks). Not one of them asks what happens when the input is
  *not* well formed. That is the gap this leg fills, in either direction.

## What is being checked for prior art

* **(N1)** Is a Newton/shooting routine that **returns its last iterate with no convergence
  flag in the return value** a recognised software hazard? `_damped_newton` returns `(x, hist)`
  and `shoot_newton` returns `(mu, kappa), hist` — the *caller* must inspect `hist[-1]`.
* **(N2)** Is **blanket `np.errstate(over/invalid/divide='ignore')`** around a numerical kernel,
  letting `inf`/`nan` propagate quietly, a known, named failure mode? `integrate_from_zero`
  enters exactly that context for the whole integration loop and exits it unconditionally.
* **(N3)** Is **under-resolving an oscillatory far field with a step-capped fixed RK4** a
  standard, documented way to get a plausible-looking wrong answer?
* **(N4)** Is **fold/turning-point location by parameter swap plus a parabolic fit** standard
  numerical continuation (i.e. not this project's idea), and what does the standard literature
  say must be checked that a parabolic fit does not check?

## Queries, verbatim, with the links returned

### Q1 (2026-08-06)
`shooting method Newton iteration returns last iterate without convergence check silent nonconvergence numerical software hazard`

* https://clima.github.io/ClimaTimeSteppers.jl/dev/api/newtons_method/ — ClimaTimeSteppers,
  `NewtonsMethod`: **"Without a convergence checker specified, Newton's method always runs
  `max_iters` iterations"**, and the non-convergence warning is printed *only if* `verbose` is
  set.
* https://acme.byu.edu/00000180-6940-dc15-abb6-f9df79240001/newtons-method — BYU ACME Newton's
  method lab: stopping criteria must combine a tolerance **and** a `max_iters` cap, the latter
  specifically "for detection of nonconvergent iterations".
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.5766 — A Two-Point Newton Method suitable for non-convergent
  cases (the failure of plain Newton to converge is the paper's premise).
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.13221 — Shooting method for two-point BVPs in ODEs numerically.
* https://john-s-butler-dit.github.io/NumericalAnalysisBook/Chapter%2006%20-%20Boundary%20Value%20Problems/602_Non-Linear%20Shooting%20Method.html
  — non-linear shooting: Newton iterations wrapped around a numerical integration.

**Finding (N1): PRIOR ART, and a named precedent for the exact shape.** A production library
(ClimaTimeSteppers) documents precisely this behaviour — iterate to the cap, return the last
iterate, warn only if asked. So "returns a non-converged iterate" is **not a discovery**; it is a
documented design choice with a documented mitigation (an explicit convergence checker). What the
audit can legitimately measure is narrower: whether *this* module's own in-repo callers, and its
own downstream claims, consume that return value **without** checking `hist[-1]`, and by how much
the returned number then differs from the published truth. Note `branch_in_kappa` **does** check
(`hist[-1] < tol`) — that is the module's own positive control and it can come out the other way.

### Q2 (2026-08-06)
`numpy errstate ignore suppressing overflow invalid warnings hides NaN propagation numerical library defect`

* https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.errstate.html — `errstate`, the
  context manager; `'ignore'` means **"Take no action when the exception occurs."**
* https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/routines.err.html — floating-point error handling; the
  default for `overflow`/`invalid` is `warn`, i.e. non-halting.
* https://github.com/numpy/numpy/pull/23634 — numpy PR: ignoring invalid/overflow warnings in
  masked setitem; the discussion states the warning "is useful to know about" even when the
  value is masked.
* https://www.rustcodeweb.com/2025/04/numpy-managing-warnings-and-errors.html — suppressing
  warnings hides problems; only do it when the edge cases are explicitly handled.
* https://runebook.dev/en/docs/numpy/reference/generated/numpy.errstate — guide to managing
  floating-point errors.

**Finding (N2): PRIOR ART, unambiguous, including the mitigation.** NumPy documents that
`'ignore'` takes *no action*, and the community guidance is explicit that suppression is
acceptable **only when the edge cases are explicitly handled downstream**. The module's own
docstring states its intent — "a damped-Newton line search evaluates the map at states that
overflow; those trials are supposed to be rejected on their norm, not to raise." That is a
legitimate, documented reason to suppress. So the suppression itself **must not be reported as a
defect**. The audit question is the conditional the guidance attaches: *is* the edge case
explicitly handled — i.e. does a `nan` produced inside the suppressed region get rejected on its
norm, or can it reach a returned number? A `nan` defect is rejected by `<` comparisons (always
False), which is the mechanism to check. Magnitudes only.

### Q3 (2026-08-06)
`fixed step Runge-Kutta under-resolving oscillatory far field ODE silently wrong solution step size control highly oscillatory`

* https://jonshiach.github.io/ODEs-book/_pages/2.5_Adaptive_step_size_control.html — adaptive
  step size control; embedded RK pairs give automatic error control, which fixed-step RK4 does
  not.
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03107 — WKB-based scheme with adaptive step size control for the
  Schrödinger equation **in the highly oscillatory regime** (the closest published analogue of
  this module's `exp(i kappa xi^2 / 2)` far field).
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08693 — Embedded error estimation and adaptive step-size control
  for optimal explicit SSP Runge–Kutta methods.
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.06836 — Optimized Runge–Kutta methods with automatic step size
  control for compressible CFD.
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5173 — On the parametric instability caused by step size variation
  in Runge–Kutta–Nyström methods.
* https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/69951-runge-kutta-fixed-step-solvers —
  fixed-step RK solvers (the standard "samples per period" heuristic).

**Finding (N3): PRIOR ART, and the module already implements the published remedy.** The
standard heuristic is a step below a fixed fraction of the local period (≥ ~20 samples per
oscillation); `integrate_from_zero` implements exactly that with its `period_step` cap keyed to
`kappa * xi`, and `resolution_ladder` measures the residual effect rather than asserting
convergence. **Under-resolution is therefore not a finding on this module by default.** It
becomes one only if the cap can be *defeated* by an admissible input — e.g. a parameter regime
in which `max(kappa*xi, 1e-12)` floors, or in which the step count explodes without a guard —
and then only with the measured error quoted against a halved-step reference.

### Q4 (2026-08-06)
`numerical continuation fold turning point detection parabolic fit pseudo-arclength Keller standard method`

* https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0905010 — Newton-Like Pseudo-Arclength Methods for
  Computing Simple Turning Points (SIAM J. Sci. Comput.).
* https://ipsen.math.ncsu.edu/ps/sinum65438.pdf and
  https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/060654384 — Condition Estimates for Pseudo-Arclength
  Continuation.
* https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094576523006379 — practical
  implementation of pseudo-arclength continuation, consistent path direction.
* https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042709004622 — numerical continuation
  applied to parameter dependence of solutions of the Schrödinger equation.
* https://grokipedia.com/page/Numerical_continuation — overview: natural parameterisation
  breaks down at simple folds where the Jacobian is singular and Newton fails.

**Finding (N4): PRIOR ART for the technique, and the literature names what a parabolic fit does
NOT do.** Swapping the continuation parameter so the fold becomes an interior point is the
standard fold-crossing trick, and the module says so in `solve_at_kappa`'s own docstring — no
novelty is claimed or available. The useful part for the battery is the *contrast*: the standard
methods **monitor the smallest-magnitude eigenvalue of the Jacobian** to detect a singular point,
whereas `fold_of` locates the fold by `argmax` over a discrete record list plus a 3-point
`np.polyfit`. That is a legitimate cheap estimator, but it inherits two documented sensitivities
— it can return an endpoint (the code flags this honestly with `interior: False`) and a
degenerate/ill-conditioned 3-point fit can produce a vertex far outside the bracket. Whether the
returned `eps_star`/`kappa_star` can land outside its own bracketing records, and by how much, is
a measurable question this leg may legitimately ask. It is **not** a criticism of the method.

## Verdict and the constraints it puts on this leg's prose

**`PROCEED_AS_AUDIT`.** All four axes have prior art; two of them (N3, N4) describe remedies the
module **already implements**, which materially narrows what may honestly be reported. What this
leg can produce that does not exist on main is a **measurement**: which specific inputs to
`solver/viscous_novelty.py` produce a confidently wrong number, by how much, and whether any
in-repo caller can reach them.

Binding constraints, written down before construction so they cannot be relaxed afterwards:

1. **No patch under either branch.** The gate says escalate on yes; the territory says read-only
   on `solver/viscous_novelty.py`. Both hold regardless of what is found.
2. **The `errstate` suppression is not, by itself, a defect** — the module documents a valid
   reason for it (N2). Only a `nan`/`inf` that *reaches a returned number* counts.
3. **Under-resolution is not, by itself, a defect** — the module implements the published
   remedy (N3). Only a defeated cap, measured against a halved-step reference, counts.
4. **The parabolic fold fit is not, by itself, a defect** — it is a cheap standard estimator
   (N4). Only a vertex outside its own bracket, quoted as a magnitude, counts.
5. **Magnitudes, never booleans** (standing discipline). Every case reports the wrong number
   *and* an independently-computed reference, and the ratio.
6. **Lesson 90 — a control that cannot come out differently is not a control.**
   `branch_in_kappa`'s `hist[-1] < tol` / `eps` range check is the module's existing guard and
   is this battery's positive control; every "no guard here" claim must sit beside at least one
   "guard here, and it fired" case from the same module.
7. **Latency is part of the finding.** Legs 79 and 116 both found gaps that were *latent*. This
   leg enumerates every in-repo caller of every audited entry point and says explicitly whether
   the banked numbers in `capabilities.py` are exposed.
8. **No claim that stage V re-opens.** Stage V is closed by its own gate and its ban's lift
   condition is a fluid transport model. Nothing measured here can change that, and a defect in
   this module would bear on *how well the gate's evidence was computed*, not on the gate's
   direction — which rests on DF's published theorems, not on our reproduction of them.
