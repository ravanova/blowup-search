majorant is proposed. The direction under test is the safe one — the leg can only ever
report that the existing bound is weaker than advertised, never that it is stronger.

## 1. What the object is, and where the soundness surface lives

The module's claim (`pointwise_bound`, lines ~120-135) is that for **every** even `h`,

    |H(h)(θ)| ≤ a_sup(θ)·S + a_semi(θ)·T ,   S = sup_j w^{(α)}_j|h_j| ,
                                             T = sup_{j≠k} min(w^{(α-γ)}_j, w^{(α-γ)}_k)·|h_j−h_k| / |θ_j−θ_k|^γ

with `(a_sup, a_semi)` produced by one log-graded trapezoid per side of the folded kernel
`K_θ(φ) = −sin θ / (sin((φ+θ)/2)·sin((φ−θ)/2))` after the global subtraction
`h(φ) − h(θ)`. In the continuum the two envelope routes are elementary — `cS` is the
triangle inequality against the sup part, `cT` is the definition of the Hölder seminorm with
`min(w)` at `min(φ,θ)` — so a soundness failure, if one exists, has to enter through one of
**five discretization/degeneracy channels**, and those are exactly what the battery attacks:

1. **quadrature**: `np.trapezoid` in `log s` over `n_quad=400` nodes on an integrand with an
   integrable endpoint singularity — signed error, not just magnitude;
2. **`eps` truncation**: the inner region `s < eps·L` is dropped outright, cost `~eps^γ`,
   which is *not* small when `γ` is small (`1e-10^0.01 = 0.79`);
3. **the payer rule at extreme `ρ`**: the `cS` route is log-divergent at `s → 0`
   (`|K|·s → 2`, `cS → 2cos^α(θ/2) ≠ 0`), so only `cT → 0` tames the singularity; every `ρ`
   is claimed valid, and `γ → 0` kills the taming term entirely;
4. **interpolation**: `measured_pointwise` consumes the bound by *linear interpolation in
   log X* between `theta_grid` nodes, plus a `np.clip` outside the grid — linear interpolation
   of a concave curve understates it;
5. **the grid seminorm**: `T` is a max over grid *pairs*, so a Nyquist-scale adversary has a
   small grid `T` and a large continuum one.

Channel 5 is the one this repository has already been burned by — JOURNAL §(v5/B1): *"A
discrete Hölder seminorm only looks at pairs of GRID NODES, so duality's extremizer is a
grid-scale sign pattern whose interpolant thrashes in the gaps… its true norm is 3e3× bigger
at J=125 and 5e4× at J=500"*. The banked lesson is that a grid-norm ball is the wrong ball.
This leg therefore measures **both** sides deliberately and reports them separately: a
violation seen only under grid-evaluated norms is the known grid-seminorm artifact and is
reported as such, **not** as a bound-direction failure; a violation that survives
continuum-faithful norms is the gate's yes-branch.

## 4. External prior art

No originality is claimed for the hazard class. Underestimation of an endpoint-singular
integral by a graded trapezoid, `ε`-truncation of a `s^γ` tail, linear interpolation
understating a concave envelope, and IEEE-754 §5.11 NaN comparisons silently selecting a
branch (`take_T = rho*cT <= cS` is **False** for every NaN, so a NaN `cT` silently routes the
whole increment to the `cS` account) are all textbook. The conjugate-function kernel and the
`p.v. ∫K = 0` subtraction are classical (Zygmund, *Trigonometric Series*, ch. III). What is
new here is only the **measurement on this repository's module**, with magnitudes.
