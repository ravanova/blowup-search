## The question the pass had to answer

Leg 127's candidate theorem is: for the assembled bordered `a = 0` CLM linearization `L` in
weighted `l^1_w`, **every bounded approximate inverse `A` — with `A21` free — has
`Z_1 = ||I - A L||_w >= 1`**. The route is the elementary trade-off

    for every x:   ||x||  <=  ||(I - AL)x||  +  ||A|| ||L x||
    hence          Z_1  >=  1 - ||A||_w * sigma_min(L),   sigma_min(L) := 1 / ||L^{-1}||_w

so the theorem reduces to: **is `L` bounded below on `l^1_w`?** Three things had to be checked:
(a) is the inequality itself already in print; (b) is the no-go already in print for operators
whose unbounded part is a shift with zero diagonal; (c) has anything landed on the `a = 0` CLM
linearization since leg 58's pass.

---

## FINDING 1 — the inequality is folklore and IS NOT CLAIMED

The forward direction (`Z_1 < 1` ⟹ the operator is invertible and the radii polynomial closes)
is the standing hypothesis of every paper in the method, stated explicitly in

- Breden–Desvillettes–Lessard, *Rigorous numerics for nonlinear operators with tridiagonal
  dominant linear part*, [arXiv:1503.06315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.06315) — the paper leg 57
  already read in full for a different clause;
- Cadiot, [arXiv:2505.03091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03091) — leg 58's pass resolved this one
  from the full text (standing hypothesis: a Fourier multiplier with `|l(xi)| >= l_min > 0`, an
  infinite **diagonal** tail; this operator's tail diagonal is exactly zero);
- Lessard et al., *Validated matrix multiplication transform for orthogonal polynomials*,
  [arXiv:2411.18361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18361) — the source leg 54 used for the
  block-diagonal convention `A = A^N (+) pi^inf`;
- Whitham / capillary-gravity solitary waves, [arXiv:2403.18718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18718)
  and Swift–Hohenberg, [arXiv:2403.10450](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10450), as two independent
  restatements of the same `Z_1 < 1` hypothesis in current practice.

The **contrapositive with a quantitative remainder** — `Z_1 >= 1 - ||A|| sigma_min` — is one
application of the triangle inequality and is not novel in any form. Targeted searches for it as
a named obstruction (`"approximate inverse" fails operator "not bounded below"
Newton–Kantorovich obstruction`; `radii polynomial ... requires operator bounded below no-go`)
returned only successful applications of the method and standard smallest-singular-value bounds
([arXiv:2108.01221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.01221),
[Rump's verified singular value bounds](https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/paper/rump/Ru10a.pdf)), never
the failure statement. **Reading: the inequality is available to use and may not be claimed.**
What this leg may claim is only which side of it this operator falls on, and the explicit
singular sequence that puts it there.

## FINDING 2 — arXiv:2607.19762 IS ON THIS LEG'S OBJECT, AND IT DID NOT EXIST AT LEG 58

Jie Xu, *The spectral picture of self-similar collapse in the Constantin–Lax–Majda equation*,
[arXiv:2607.19762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19762) (HTML:
[v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19762v1)), ~July 2026 — i.e. **after** leg 58's pass and after
every ban in `plan_of_record.py` was written. It linearizes the `a = 0` CLM equation about the
exact profile `Omega(y) = -y/(y^2 + 1/4)` — the same anchor this repository calls the `a = 0`
CLM linearization — and proves, quoting the abstract:

> "Its essential spectrum meets the closed half-plane `{Re λ ≥ −1/2}` in the single vertical line
> `{Re λ = −1/2}`: the line is placed by a log-widening Weyl sequence, and an explicit
> Hardy–Mellin resolvent bound constructively empties the rest of the half-plane apart from `0`
> and `1`. Its full point spectrum over `C`, on the odd realization, is exactly `{0,1}`, the
> scaling and time-shift symmetry modes, with no embedded eigenvalues; removing these by the
> standard modulation leaves a spectral gap of `1/2` on `X`."

**Why this is a narrowing and not a refutation.** The realization is **origin-`H^2`**, and in it
`spec(L_0) ∩ {Re λ > −1/2} = {0, 1}` — so once the two symmetry modes are removed by modulation
(this repository's gauge row does exactly that: `null_direction_defect` checks the scaling mode
is exactly `e_2`), the operator is **invertible, hence bounded below, in origin-`H^2`.**

The consequence for this leg is not negotiable and is carried into every clause and every line
of prose:

1. **Any no-go proved here is a statement about the `l^1_w` Fourier-coefficient realization, not
   about the `a = 0` CLM linearization as an operator.** A singular sequence in `l^1_w` for
   `s < 1` does not contradict Xu — it separates two realizations of one operator, which is
   lesson 70 ("name the realization") arriving as a literature fact instead of a discipline note.
2. **It supplies the positive control the leg needs, and it can come out the other way**
   (lesson 90): Xu's result predicts that the same construction run in an `H^2`-like weight must
   *fail* to produce a singular sequence. If it does not fail there, the instrument is broken.
3. **It forbids one sentence outright**: no prose in this leg may say the operator "has no
   bounded approximate inverse". The most that is available is "*no bounded approximate inverse
   in `l^1_w` at `s < 1`*", and Xu is the reason.
4. It also means the `yes,(ii)` counterexample branch is *not* a priori absurd: an operator that
   is invertible in one realization is exactly the setting where a clever `A` could exist. The
   branch is kept live and its escalation status (#4) is unchanged.

Xu's own stated route to a computer-assisted proof (uniform large-imaginary-part bounds,
trace-ideal membership, quadrature error in trace norm) is a **different** method from the
radii-polynomial `l^1`-Fourier lane and is not a lift condition for any ban in
`plan_of_record.py`. Nothing here reopens stage `V`, `B`, or any `l^1`-Fourier machinery ban:
this leg builds no new machinery, appends only measurement functions, and quotes Xu as a
constraint on claims, never as a component of a proof.

## ADDENDUM — independent corroboration from leg 157 (Route-CDX), added after this leg's
## result was already derived

Leg 157 re-mined Cadiot [arXiv:2505.03091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03091) and its citation
lineage specifically for an `A21 != 0` construction technique, and landed on main
(`writeup/novelty/leg_157.md`, `experiments/journal/leg_157.md`) while this leg was finishing.
Its two findings are recorded here because they bear directly on this pass's Finding 1 and on
the gate's `yes,(ii)` branch, and because a second independent search is worth more than a
louder claim from the first:

1. **No prior art exists for a working `A21 != 0` shape.** Every preconditioning construction
   in that lineage induces `A21 = 0` (identity-on-the-tail conjugation). This CORROBORATES,
   from a different starting point, the gap leg 58 named and this leg closed — and it means
   the `yes,(ii)` branch would have had to be original rather than borrowed.
2. **The one theorem in the lineage that drops the nonzero-diagonal hypothesis is
   Breden–Payan–Reisch–Tang [arXiv:2504.05066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05066) Thm 2.6**,
   via a COMPACT RESOLVENT rather than a spectral shift. This paper did not surface in the
   present pass's queries, and it is the nearest published statement to this leg's setting.
   It does **not** pre-empt anything here: leg 157 measured its hypothesis failing on this
   operator (eigenvalues inside a fixed disc growing +4.00 per doubling at `r = 10` while
   `min|lambda|` falls `0.539 -> 0.315`, i.e. the spectrum ACCUMULATES at 0 rather than
   staying bounded away from it). That accumulation is the same phenomenon this leg measures
   as `sigma_min -> 0`, reached by a different instrument, so the two results agree.

**Nothing in the addendum changes this leg's gate, its wording, or what may be claimed.** It
strengthens the negative-prior-art record behind Finding 1 and adds one citation
(arXiv:2504.05066) that the original queries missed.
