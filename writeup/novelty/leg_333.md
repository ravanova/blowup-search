## 1. Where 3D NS sits: **α = 2/5**, and that is inside the undecided window

The Katz–Pavlović and Friedlander–Pavlović models both reduce to a single system indexed by one
scalar, the **dissipation degree α**. From the e-print source of

> **A. Cheskidov, *Blow-up in finite time for the dyadic model of the Navier-Stokes equations*,
> [`arXiv:math/0601074`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601074)** — v1 4 Jan 2006, v2 8 Jun 2006;
> Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.

the model is (Introduction, eq. (1.1), verbatim from `dyadic1.tex`)

```
d/dt u_n  +  ν λ^{2αn} u_n  −  λ^n u_{n−1}²  +  λ^{n+1} u_n u_{n+1}  =  g_n ,   n ∈ ℕ,   u_0 = 0
```

with `λ > 1`, `ν ≥ 0`, `α > 0`. **The calibration sentence, verbatim from the source:**

> *"Note that for α = 2/5 the following are sharp estimates on the inertial term:
> |(B(u,u),Au)| ≲ |Au|^{3/2}|A^{1/2}u|^{3/2} … **The best known estimates on the inertial term of
> the 3D NSE are the same**, with (·,·) and |·| being the L²-inner product and norm."*

and, for the dimension axis,

> *"It is remarkable that in the critical case α = 1/3 the following are sharp estimates on the
> nonlinear term: |(B(u,u),Au)| ≲ |Au|²|A^{1/2}u|. Note that **the best known estimates on the
> nonlinear term of the 4D NSE are exactly the same**."*

The blow-up/regularity structure along the same axis, same paper:

| α | status | source |
|---|---|---|
| `α < 1/4` | finite-time blow-up | Katz–Pavlović (original) |
| `α < 1/3` | finite-time blow-up | Cheskidov `math/0601074`, main theorem |
| `α = 1/3` | critical — sharp estimates **= 4D NS**'s | Cheskidov, Introduction |
| **`α = 2/5`** | **3D NS's calibration point** | Cheskidov, Introduction |
| `α > 1/3` | local regularity | Cheskidov |
| `α ≥ 1/2` | **global** regularity; weak global attractor becomes strong | Cheskidov |

**`1/3 < 2/5 < 1/2`.** `1/3 = 0.3333…`, `2/5 = 0.4`, `1/2 = 0.5`. Distance above the proved
blow-up threshold `2/5 − 1/3 = 1/15 ≈ 0.0667`; distance below the proved global-regularity
threshold `1/2 − 2/5 = 1/10 = 0.1`; the undecided window is `1/6 ≈ 0.1667` wide and 3D NS sits
`(1/15)/(1/6) = 2/5 = 40%` of the way across it.

**The answer, in the paper's own words** (Introduction, first paragraph):

> *"In this paper we study a dyadic model, which has similar properties to the 3D NSE, **the same
> estimates on the inertial term, and the same open question concerning the regularity of the
> solutions.**"*

and (Introduction, last paragraph):

> *"Note that there is still a gap between the regions of global regularity and blow-up in finite
> time, which means that the developed technique is not sharp enough to separate these two
> behaviors."*

**The dyadic hierarchy reproduces 3D NS's open problem, at 3D NS's own calibration point, as an
open problem.** That is the placement, it is sharp, it is a rational number, and it is nineteen
years old.

## 2. Which feature flips blow-up on: four named knobs, three with numbers

1. **The dissipation degree α.** Flip lies in `[1/3, 1/2)`. Cheskidov
   [`math/0601074`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601074).

2. **Sign structure / whether backward transfer exists** — the cleanest flip in the hierarchy.
   Forward-cascade-only dyadic models (positive coefficients) blow up; the **sabra** shell model,
   which keeps an NS-like sign structure and a second quadratic invariant, is **globally
   regular** at any positive viscosity:
   **P. Constantin, B. Levant, E. S. Titi, *Analytic Study of Shell Models of Turbulence*,
   [`arXiv:physics/0511075`](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0511075)** (9 Nov 2005, v2 4 Jul 2006)
   — *"We prove the global regularity of solutions and show that the shell model has finitely
   many asymptotic degrees of freedom, specifically: a finite dimensional global attractor and
   globally invariant inertial manifolds."*
   Companion inviscid case, **[`arXiv:physics/0607060`](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0607060)**
   (7 Jul 2006): global weak solutions, short-time uniqueness, a BKM-type blow-up criterion, an
   Onsager analogue under `|u_n| ≤ C k_n^{−1/3}(√n log(n+1))^{−1}`, and **global regularity in
   the "two-dimensional" parameter regime**.
   Direction of the effect made quantitative by **A. Cheskidov & K. Zaya, *Regularizing Effect of
   the Forward Energy Cascade in the Inviscid Dyadic Model*,
   [`arXiv:1310.7612`](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.7612)** (28 Oct 2013): forward cascade
   **regularizes**, forcing `3/5` `L²`-based (or `1/10` `L³`-based) regularity for all positive
   time, conjecturally to Onsager's `5/6` / `1/3`.
   And the inviscid dyadic model **self-dissipates past its own blow-up time**:
   **Cheskidov–Friedlander–Pavlović, [`math/0610814`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610814)** and
   **[`math/0610815`](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610815)** (both 26 Oct 2006) — every forced
   solution blows up in `H^{5/6}`, Onsager's conjecture is confirmed for the model, and
   afterwards solutions return to `H^s`, `s < 5/6`, for almost all time with the unique
   equilibrium an exponential global attractor.

3. **Retention of the enstrophy identity.** **E. Miller, *Finite-time blowup for the
   Fourier-restricted Euler and hypodissipative Navier–Stokes model equations*,
   [`arXiv:2307.03434`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03434)** (v1 7 Jul 2023, v5 28 Aug 2025). The
   most structure-retaining model located: the `(u·∇)u` nonlinearity is **unchanged**, only the
   Helmholtz projection is replaced, and the models *"respect both the energy equality and the
   identity for enstrophy growth from the full Euler and hypodissipative Navier–Stokes equations
   respectively, which is a substantial advance over the previous literature on Euler and
   Navier–Stokes model equations."* **The price of that retention is that blow-up retreats:**
   proved only for `α < log(3)/(6 log 2) ≈ 0.264`, against 3D NS's `α = 1` — **a gap of 0.736,
   opening away from NS.**

4. **The dichotomy itself, stated outright by the newest paper in the field.**
   **S. Palasek, *Finite-time blow-up in an elementary model of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations*,
   [`arXiv:2605.13827`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13827)** (13 May 2026, 20 pp) —
   *"Previously studied models either exhibit a turbulent cascade that **regularizes** the
   three-dimensional viscous dynamics, or rely on **highly artificial interactions** not
   transparently realized in the true Euler nonlinearity."* Palasek claims a model escaping both
   horns (blow-up with full "smooth" viscosity and forcing; plus an inviscid unforced case with
   singularity formation just above the energy level) and closes on *"the prospects for embedding
   the behavior of the dyadic model into the full Euler and Navier-Stokes equations."*

## 3. Independent corroboration in two further currencies

The placement is not one author's calibration. Two papers exist whose entire purpose is this
leg's gate, by a different author pair, using different methods, agreeing in direction:

- **D. Vincenzi & J. D. Gibbon, *How close are shell models to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations?*,
  [`arXiv:2009.01583`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01583)** (3 Sep 2020, 22 pp) — **the title is
  this leg's question verbatim.** *"only the estimate of the mean energy dissipation rate is the
  same in both systems. The estimates of the velocity and its higher-order derivatives display a
  **weaker Reynolds number dependence for shell models than for the 3D NSEs**. Indeed, the
  velocity-derivative estimates for shell models are found to be equivalent to those
  corresponding to a **velocity gradient averaged version of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations
  (VGA-NSEs)**, while the velocity estimates are even milder."*

- **J. D. Gibbon & D. Vincenzi, *Dynamics of vorticity moments in shell models of turbulence: A
  comparison with the Navier-Stokes equations*,
  [`arXiv:2412.07064`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07064)** (10 Dec 2024, 17 pp) — *"**The
  contribution of the nonlinear term turns out to be much weaker than its equivalent for the
  three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations.** … the estimates for shell models formally
  correspond to those for the Navier-Stokes equations **'on a point'**."*

Three methods — sharp nonlinear estimates (2006), Reynolds scaling of derivative estimates
(2020), vorticity moments (2024) — three placements, mutually consistent, none contradicted.

A **dedicated survey of the hierarchy** also exists, by two of the three authors the leg spec
named: **A. Cheskidov, M. Dai, S. Friedlander, *Dyadic models for fluid equations: a survey*,
[`arXiv:2209.10203`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10203)** (21 Sep 2022, v2 12 Apr 2023).

## 5. What this changes for the repository — and the honest Wall-1 reading

**Novel to this repository, not novel to the literature.** The repo has brushed this hierarchy
five times (`writeup/novelty/leg_172.md:69`, `leg_174.md:112` and `:138`, `leg_255.md` §Q2/§Q8,
`experiments/p2_route_vbs_v1_scoping.py:390`) and every one is a **census row** — the hierarchy
entered as candidate objects to screen, and no leg ever asked it the orientation question. That
is exactly the outcome a hard novelty gate exists to produce.

Leg 255's own caveat — a shell model *"discards the geometry of the nonlinearity entirely; it is
fluid-adjacent by **scaling and energy structure, not by transport**"* — was the right instinct,
and §2–§3 above now supply the citations that make it a measured statement.

**Wall 1 reading, stated at full strength and against interest.** Wall 1 holds that a search can
only argue *for* blow-up, never for regularity. This route was flagged as touching it. What the
pass found points the **wrong way** for the programme:

1. At 3D NS's own calibration point `α = 2/5` the hierarchy is **undecided** — it supplies no
   blow-up evidence for 3D NS.
2. Every step toward retaining more genuine NS structure moves the blow-up threshold **away**
   from NS: sabra is globally regular; Miller's enstrophy-respecting model blows up only at
   `α < 0.264` against NS's `α = 1`; forward cascade is *regularizing*; the inviscid dyadic model
   self-dissipates past its own blow-up.
3. Vincenzi–Gibbon and Gibbon–Vincenzi both measure the shell nonlinearity as **weaker** than
   3D NS's, with the shell correspondence being NS *"on a point"*.

**The hierarchy's blow-up results have consistently come from the structure the models *discard*,
not the structure they *keep*.** That is why the hierarchy has never transferred to the real
equations, and it is why Palasek's newest paper must spend its conclusion on the embedding
question. This is genuine evidence about direction (b), banked at full strength per the gate's
no-branch wording.

**Located, not adjudicated, routed to the DM:** both Palasek papers end on open prospects
(embedding into full Euler/NS; convex-integration-shaped construction in the full PDE setting)
stated by their own author. Whether that is a route for this repository is **not this leg's
gate**, and no recommendation is made — see `experiments/journal/leg_333.md` §9 for the three
things that would have to be settled first, including whether it collides with the re-posed
2026-08-06 ban on `ℓ¹`-Fourier/collocation machinery "on **any** model, fluid or otherwise".
