## 1. The load-bearing find: the seeded entrance has been OPERATED — on a neighbour object

* **Hou, *Nearly self-similar blowup of generalized axisymmetric Navier–Stokes and Boussinesq
  equations*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10916>
  (also <https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~hou/papers/Blowup-gNSE-2024.pdf>)

  This is the single most important citation for this leg, and it was **not** in leg 260's
  record. Three things it establishes, each bearing directly on a numbered reason of leg
  260 §3.4:

  1. **It is a seeded search in similarity variables, and it says so.** *"the initial
     condition for the dynamic rescaling formulation is obtained from a late-stage
     adaptive-mesh solution, rescaled via parabolic scaling invariance with a soft far-field
     cut-off."* That is exactly Route-SDSS's entrance: seed from a numerically obtained
     candidate, then run the rescaled flow.
  2. **It does not use a finite-energy box.** *"the computation is performed in a transformed
     domain on a uniform mesh, which maps back to a highly adaptive physical mesh."* An
     unbounded-domain coordinate map, not an `L²` spectral box.
  3. **It uses continuation, with viscosity as the parameter** — the stated plan is to solve
     the self-similar equations directly with background viscosity `ν₀` as the continuation
     parameter, driving `c_l(ν₀) → 1/2`, `n(ν₀) → 3` as `ν₀ → 0`. It also names the
     obstruction the seeding exists to defeat: **scaling instability**, which means a
     traditional time-marching method *"can only get close to the potential blowup without
     reaching arbitrarily near the blowup time."*

  **What it is NOT:** the object is **generalized axisymmetric** NS (solution-dependent
  viscosity, effective dimension ≈ **3.188**, reported as apparently → 3 as background
  viscosity falls), and the profile is **stationary/"nearly" self-similar**, not genuinely
  DSS (time-periodic in `s`). It is a neighbour, not the target. It is on the wrong side of
  the axisymmetric screen that leg 253/260 §2 applies.

## 2. The machinery half — old, published, and not ours

* **Sánchez, Net, García-Archilla & Simó, *Newton–Krylov continuation of periodic orbits for
  Navier–Stokes flows*, J. Comput. Phys. **201** (2004) 13–33** —
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999104001895>,
  full text at <https://chaosbook.org/library/SNGS.pdf>

  Periodic-orbit continuation for NS at discretisation dimension `O(10⁴)`, shooting +
  Newton–Krylov + Arnoldi. Load-bearing sentence for this leg: **good initial conditions
  matter most for periodic-orbit continuation**, the predictor should extrapolate from
  previous solutions or take small parameter / pseudo-arclength steps. This is 22-year-old
  prior art for the *machinery*, exactly the kind of old prior art §0a says to hunt for.
* **Sánchez & Net, *On the multiple shooting continuation of periodic orbits by
  Newton–Krylov methods*** —
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220273291_On_the_Multiple_Shooting_Continuation_of_Periodic_orbits_by_Newton-Krylov_Methods>
* **Knoll & Keyes, Jacobian-free Newton–Krylov survey, J. Comput. Phys. **193** (2004)
  357–397** — the standard background citation, located via the above.
* **Stokes preconditioning in Channelflow/Openpipeflow** —
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05087> — order-of-magnitude speedup for steady states and
  travelling waves in existing 3D NS invariant-solution codes.

## 3. Seed→Newton→continuation as a named recipe elsewhere, and its known hazards

* **gKdV, *Self-similar blow-up solutions in the generalized Korteweg–de Vries equation:
  spectral analysis, normal form and asymptotics*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13770>.
  The explicit recipe: solve the self-similar BVP directly with a pinning/normalisation
  condition, seed from an initial guess or a perturbed travelling wave along the most
  unstable eigenvector, converge by Newton, then continue in the nonlinearity exponent.
* **Huang et al., *Exact self-similar finite-time blowup of the Hou–Luo model with smooth
  profiles*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01528> (CMP 2025,
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-025-05429-9>). Rigorous **algebraic**
  far-field decay rates, and the scaling constant `c_l` is *directly related to* those rates —
  i.e. the far field and the unknown scaling parameter are coupled, the same coupling leg 260
  §3.4 reason 3 identified for `λ`.
* **gCLM, *Self-similar finite-time blowups with singular profiles of the generalized
  Constantin–Lax–Majda model*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25104> — algebraic far-field
  tails in this repository's own model family.
* **Truncation hazard (Bressan–Shen), reported via the above**: distinct self-similar
  profiles can arise from **different truncations of the same initial datum** — the
  truncation choice can select a different branch. Directly relevant to a seeded search,
  where the seed's own far-field cut-off is a modelling choice.
* **PINNs on unbounded domains, high-precision, feeding computer-assisted proofs** — located
  as a live alternative discretisation for exactly the algebraic-far-field profile problem;
  and **neural-network asymptotic self-similar profile for 3D axisymmetric Euler** —
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371651013_Asymptotic_Self-Similar_Blow-Up_Profile_for_Three-Dimensional_Axisymmetric_Euler_Equations_Using_Neural_Networks>.

## 5. The exclusion side (old prior art, re-located independently)

* **Chae & Wolf, *Removing discretely self-similar singularities for the 3D Navier–Stokes
  equations*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.09464>. Thm 1.1 (Type-I bound, leg 253's
  source), single point singularity, and `λ` near 1 removes the singularity.
* **Chae, *Remarks on the asymptotically discretely self-similar solutions of the
  Navier–Stokes and the Euler equations*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0305>. Carries the
  **explicit periodic-orbit definition**: a solenoidal `V̄(y,s)` with `V̄(y,s) = V̄(y,s+S₀)`,
  `S₀ ≠ 0` — DSS **is** time-periodicity of the rescaled field, confirming leg 251's
  equivalence from an independent published source.
* **Hou & Li, *Nonexistence of locally self-similar blow-up for the 3D incompressible
  Navier–Stokes equations*** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0603126>. Excludes local
  self-similar blow-up when the rescaled profile converges in `L^p`, `p ∈ (3,∞)`, by a
  *"Dynamic Singularity Rescaling"* technique — and states that the limiting profile's
  behaviour **can be verified numerically if a locally self-similar blow-up is observed in a
  computation**.
* **Nečas–Růžička–Šverák / Tsai** — the `L³` and `L^p`, `p ∈ (3,∞]` exclusions, re-located
  independently via the above; already this repository's screen.
* **Protas, PDE-optimisation searches for NS singularities** — located via search summary,
  reporting *"No evidence for singularity formation was found in extreme Navier–Stokes flows
  constructed in this manner in three dimensions."* A recorded negative for the
  **unseeded/optimisation** entrance.

## 6. The gap this pass establishes, stated as a negative

Across every query and every spelling variant above, **no source computes a genuinely
discretely self-similar (time-periodic in `s`) blow-up profile for Navier–Stokes
numerically.** The computational literature is stationary or *"nearly"*-stationary profiles in
dynamic-rescaling coordinates; DSS appears almost entirely on the **theoretical exclusion**
side. The genuinely time-periodic computations that do exist are for **forward** DSS (Tsai)
and for **other equations** (NLS log-log, Keller–Segel), where — per the same source —
unstable modes are removed using solution symmetries and spectral analysis of a **compact**
linearised operator about an **explicit ground state**, and the summary states plainly that
this is *"a luxury unavailable for NSE, since there is no explicit ground state and the
linearized operator is not compact."*

**This is a located gap, not a claim of priority, and not a claim that the cell is easy.**
It is recorded because §6's negative is what the leg's (b) answer turns on.
