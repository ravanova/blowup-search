## The determination the gate asks for: bound, or measurement?

The brief offers two readings — leg 163's figure was a looser earlier-stage BOUND, or it was a
wrong MEASUREMENT. **Neither is right, and the third reading is exact and checkable.**

Leg 163's G4 row, verbatim from its own journal:

> bordered-solve residuals ≤ **2.22e−14**, `||u||_X/||f||_X` = **1.3993 / 1.2680 / 1.3769** over
> the three data, so a **lower witness `sigma_min ≥ 0.7147`**

Reciprocate the three ratios: **0.7146430 / 0.7886435 / 0.7262692**. The smallest is
`1/1.3993 = 0.7146430`, and PUB2's quoted **0.71465** is exactly that number rounded up in the
fifth decimal. So the figure is not an independent estimate at all — it is the reciprocal of leg
163's own largest sampled ratio, and its provenance is arithmetically identified, not inferred.

Now the direction. With `f = L u`, each datum gives `||f||_X/||u||_X = 1/r`, and

  `sigma_min = inf_x ||L x||_X / ||x||_X  <=  1/r`  for every sampled `r`.

A finite family of sampled Rayleigh-type ratios can bound the infimum **only from above** — it
can never bound it from below, because the sample may simply have missed the worst direction.
So leg 163's own three data support

  **`sigma_min <= 0.71465`**,

and the sentence in leg 163 has the inequality **inverted**. This is the whole conflict, and once
the sign is corrected **there is no numeric conflict left**: `0.0908 <= 0.71465` holds
comfortably. Leg 176 is not contradicting leg 163's number; it is measuring the quantity that
leg 163's number was only ever an upper bound on.

Three independent confirmations of the reading, all from banked data:

1. **The sample never got near the operator norm.** The true `||R||_X = ||L^{-1}||_X = 11.0127`.
   Leg 163's best datum reached `1.3993`, i.e. **12.71 %** of it. Leg 176's own re-run of leg
   163's construction over four fresh data got ratios near `0.868155`, i.e. **7.88 %** of it —
   *further* from the worst direction, not closer. Random low-mode data does not find the worst
   direction, exactly as leg 176 says.
2. **Reciprocals of both families are upper bounds that bracket correctly.** `1/1.3993 = 0.7146`
   and `1/0.868155 = 1.1519` are both `>= 0.0908`. Under the "≥" reading these two families would
   contradict *each other* (0.71 vs 1.15 as claimed lower bounds on the same quantity); under the
   "≤" reading they are simply two valid, differently loose upper bounds. **Only the corrected
   sign makes leg 163's and leg 176's own numbers mutually consistent.**
3. **Leg 176's near-minimizer closes the loop.** Its ratio `||(u,k)||/||(f,l u)|| = 11.010373`
   reproduces `1/sigma_min = 11.010374` to 1e−6 — a sampled ratio that *did* find the worst
   direction, giving the sharp `sigma_min <= 0.0908234`.

**A second thing the corrected sign exposes, and it must be said rather than quietly inherited.**
Leg 176's `0.0908` is *also* an upper bound: its own words for the domain-only truncation are
*"an upper bound closing down onto `sigma_min`"*, and for the ladder,
*"float64 evidence of a positive limit, not a proof of one."* So **neither leg supplies a lower
bound on `sigma_min` at all.** The claim "bounded away from zero" — which is precisely what the
`≥` sign at the three call-sites was carrying — rests on the *behaviour* of a monotone-decreasing
ladder that flattens (0.0908878 → 0.0908047 over `N = 64 … 512`, **0.0915 %**), plus the two
controls that report the other answer (unbordered collapses to the float floor 1.55e−15 → 6.97e−14;
loose `L^2` decays like `N^{−1.49}`). That is real evidence and it is what distinguishes this space
from `ell^1_w`, where the analogous ladder decays like `M^{−(1−s)}`. It is not a proved inequality,
and after this leg PUB2 does not write it as one.

## What this leg therefore does, and does not, do

- **Does:** correct the inequality *direction* and the *value* at §3.2, §4.5, §5(3); restate
  §3.5's conflict note as a resolved provenance note rather than an open discrepancy.
- **Does:** name the evidential standing of `0.0908` (converged float64 upper bound + controls)
  wherever a `≥` used to assert a lower bound.
- **Does not:** delete either number. `0.71465` is retained at §3.2 with its correct sign, because
  it is a true statement about leg 163's data and its provenance is now fully traced.
- **Does not:** touch any banked JSON, any measurement, any ceiling, any ban, or `plan_of_record.py`.
- **Does not:** change any conclusion of §3–§5. The property those sections use is "there is a
  truncation-independent floor here and there was none in `ell^1_w`", and that is unaffected by
  which of `0.71465` / `0.0908` is quoted, and unaffected by the sign correction — the *contrast*
  with `ell^1_w` is a contrast of ladder behaviour, not of a proved bound, in both spaces.

**Novelty of the finding itself.** The 7.9× discrepancy is leg 176's, not this leg's. What is new
here is (a) the arithmetic identification of `0.71465` as `1/1.3993`, leg 163's own largest
sampled ratio, and (b) the observation that the error is a *sign*, not a magnitude — which is why
the two legs never actually disagreed, and which is the reason the fix is a correction rather than
a retraction.
