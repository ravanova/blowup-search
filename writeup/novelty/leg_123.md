## 3. Why the ten-hour figure is the right one, and its honest limits

The gate asks about the gap **since the last EXT-family window**, not about a fixed calendar
period like the 118–133 "days open" the specific-object watches (74/77/82/90/93) report. Leg 93
is the most recent EXT-family landing, so its close is the correct boundary. Ten hours is short
enough that "nothing new" is close to guaranteed on priors alone (mathematical certificates of
this size do not appear on a ten-hour cadence) — the value of running the channels anyway is
that it (i) confirms arXiv's own daily cycle had not produced a new batch at all as of the pass
time, closing off the one way this could have been wrong cheaply, and (ii) doubles as the
occasion to widen the net past the four specific objects, which is where §4 comes from.

**Honest limit:** a result finished and typeset in the last ten hours but not yet submitted, or
submitted and sitting in arXiv's moderation queue, is invisible to every channel here — it would
also have been invisible to any of legs 74/77/82/90/93 for the same reason, and remains so for
whichever leg re-asks this gate next.

## 4. FF1 — a genuine miss by the narrower family, opened in full, and why it does NOT trigger a YES here

**arXiv:2605.19716** (Feng Shao, Dongyi Wei, Ping Zhang, Zhifei Zhang), *"Self-similar blow-up
solutions of `d`-dimensional incompressible Euler equations with `C^{1,(1-2/d)-}` velocity"*,
v1 **2026-05-19**, v2 **2026-07-22**. Read in full (abstract level, matching the depth legs
74/77/82/90 used for their false friends — this is a context flag, not this leg's certificate
to verify):

> "We investigate self-similar blow-up solutions to the `d`-dimensional axisymmetric
> incompressible Euler equations without swirl for `d ≥ 3`. For any `α ∈ (0, α_d)` with
> `α_d = 1 − 2/d`, we construct a self-similar blow-up solution whose initial velocity field
> satisfies `u_0 ∈ C^{1,α}_loc(R^d) ∩ C^∞(R^d \ {0})`. ... Furthermore, we establish a
> finite-codimensional stability result for the self-similar profiles obtained above."

This **is** a fully analytic (fixed-point, not computer-assisted) certificate of an inviscid
self-similar blow-up profile, by a method different from every one of the four ranked objects.
It genuinely falls inside gate (a)'s broad wording, "by any method." **It does not trigger a YES
for this leg's gate** for one dispositive reason and one supporting one:

1. **Dispositive: it predates the window by more than two months even at v2.** v1 is
   2026-05-19, v2 is 2026-07-22; the window this gate asks about opened at leg 93's close,
   2026-08-06T00:35:44Z. It was not published *since* the last window closed — it was sitting
   in the corpus, unrevised, for 15 days before leg 90 ran and 45 before leg 74 ran, let alone
   before leg 93.
2. **Supporting: it is not one of the four ranked target objects, and none of legs 74/77/82/90's
   narrow, model-specific queries (`"Hou-Luo"`, `"Constantin-Lax-Majda"`, the rank-2/3
   phrasings) were built to catch a different equation family's own construction.** That is a
   real gap in the family's coverage to date, reported here honestly rather than smoothed over,
   but a gap in *prior* coverage of *old* news is not this leg's YES-trigger, which is
   specifically about *new* news since the last close.

**Its own much older sibling** (Ken Abe et al., arXiv:2410.21765, v1 **2024-10-29**, "Stationary
self-similar profiles for the two-dimensional inviscid Boussinesq equations") was found via
open web search to have just appeared in *Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis*
(`doi:10.1007/s00205-026-02195-3`, a 2026-dated ARMA article-in-press). This is the same
phenomenon one level further removed: a **journal typesetting of a 21-month-old preprint**, not
new mathematical content, and not inside the window either way.

**Recorded for whoever re-asks this gate next:** if the window narrows to "since 2026-05-19" on
a future pass, FF1 is the first thing to re-open, and this time with a full-text read against
the exact profile equation, not the abstract.
