## 3. THE STANDARD THE BATTERY IS HELD TO — AND ONE CITATION I REFUSED TO USE

An audit needs a *normative* anchor, not an invented one. Leg 120's precedent (Route-SUA) took
its two anchors from published numerical-analysis sources (Bowman on dealiasing, Johnson on
FFT differentiation) because the module under audit implemented a *published algorithm*.

**This module does not.** The convergence filter, the structural pair, the gauge (N) and the
closed-form continuum are this repository's own construction. So the normative anchor is the
module's own written contract, which is unusually explicit and is therefore auditable:

- `solver/rescaled_spectrum.py`:116–119 — *"THAT IS WHY A CONVERGENCE FILTER IS MANDATORY, not
  decoration: **the discrete eigenvalues are the ones that stop moving under refinement**, and
  the continuum is the part that never does."* This sentence is the specification S3 tests: a
  filter run at `K_fine == K_coarse` refines nothing, so nothing can move, so everything is
  kept.
- `solver/rescaled_spectrum.py`:313–316 — *"Continuation is the honest test for a branch: it
  **FOLLOWS** the object rather than searching for it."* The specification S4 tests.
- `solver/rescaled_spectrum.py`:104–112 — admissibility holds *exactly* for `-1 < Re lambda < 1`.
  The specification S8 tests.
- `test_rescaled_spectrum.py`:26–27 (test 7) — *"Past the end of the branch Newton returns
  `converged=False` rather than handing back its last iterate."* The specification S5 tests, one
  level up: `newton` honours it, and every consumer above `newton` drops the flag.

Two anchors are external and locally verifiable rather than cited from memory:

- **LAPACK/NumPy non-finite rejection.** `numpy.linalg.eigvals` raises
  `LinAlgError("Array must not contain infs or NaNs")` on non-finite input. Verified by direct
  execution in S1, not quoted — the battery records the exception type and message it actually
  received.
- **IEEE-754 propagation.** `nan` and `inf` arithmetic propagates through the residual and gauge
  arithmetic. Verified by execution in S1.

**A citation I considered and did NOT use.** I intended to anchor S3 on Boyd, *Chebyshev and
Fourier Spectral Methods* (2nd ed., Dover 2001), whose eigenvalue chapter carries a
rule-of-thumb that only those eigenvalues agreeing between two *different* resolutions are
trustworthy. I could not locate the passage or verify its wording in this session, so **it is
not cited and no number in this leg rests on it.** Recording the near-miss because leg 57's
BDL episode is the repository's standing lesson that an unverified citation is worse than none.
S3 stands on the module's own line 116 instead, which is stronger anyway: it is the
specification the code was written against.

## 4. WHAT IS GENUINELY NEW HERE

1. **That `converged_spectrum` has no guard on `K_fine` vs `K_coarse`.** Stated nowhere. The
   module's line 116 makes refinement the definition of a discrete eigenvalue; the function
   never checks that refinement occurred.
2. **That `spectrum` and `converged_spectrum` discard `newton`'s `converged` flag.** Leg 70 read
   this file for its *origin condition* and banned itself from calling it; test 7 checks the flag
   at the `newton` level only. That the flag dies one frame up is new.
3. **That `continuation`'s `np.arange` grid goes empty on a sign-mismatched step and the
   fallback silently degrades a followed branch to a cold solve.** Not stated anywhere.
4. **That `continuum_defect` measures only the homogeneous ODE residual and therefore carries no
   admissibility content at all** — it reports a small defect for every `lambda` in `C`,
   including `lambda` far outside the strip the docstring says is exactly the admissible set.
   This is lesson 90's shape (a control that cannot come out differently) in a module that
   predates lesson 90.
5. **The exposure census of items 2–3 against banked Route-E and Route-G rows.** New, and it is
   what decides this leg's escalation branch.

None of these is a re-find of legs 66, 69, 79 or 120: those are all `solver/spectral_utils.py`
and its FFT path, which this module does not import (it is dense linear algebra on a sine
collocation basis, `numpy` only).
