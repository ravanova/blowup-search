## 4. THE RESULT I AM REPRODUCING, chosen and stated BEFORE running

**Theorem 42** (arXiv:2404.04054, §6, p. 31) — the generalised viscous Burgers equation, and
the *only* result in the paper carrying a first-order term `f(x, u, ∇u)` with genuine `∇u`
dependence. It is the result Remark 40 is attached to, so it is the right one for Phase 1;
the four other candidates (Theorems 1, 2, 30, 37, 39) are all `∇u`-free.

The equation. Under `v(t,x) = t^{−1/4} u(x/√t)`, the self-similar profile of
`∂_t v + v²∂_x v = ∂_{xx} v` on `R⁺` with `∂_x v(t,0) = 0` solves

    L u − u/4 + u² ∂_x u = 0,   x ≥ 0,    L = −∂_{xx} − (x/2)∂_x.        [their (54)]

posed on `H = Span{ψ̂_m}^{H²}`, `ψ̂_m = e^{−x²/4}Ĥ_{2m}(x/2)` (Neumann enforced by even
symmetrisation), with `F(u) = u + L^{−1}(−u/4 + u²∂_x u)` [their (55)].

## 5. WHAT THIS LEG'S ARITHMETIC IS — declared before any number is quoted

Standing discipline 75: name what is *not* bounded here, before the numbers, not after.

Breden–Chu's proof is in **interval arithmetic over 16384-bit BigFloat** (their
`quadrature.jl` sets `prec = 16384` to enclose the roots of a degree-4503 Laguerre polynomial
by the intermediate value theorem). This environment has **numpy only — no scipy, no
mpmath** (checked). Reproducing their arithmetic *model* is therefore out of scope at this
leg's size, and I do not pretend otherwise.

**This leg reproduces their certificate's CONSTANTS in float64, not their proof.** It is a
reproduction of the *machinery* — the space, the basis, the operator, the quadrature, the
approximate inverse, all six bounds, the radii polynomial — computed independently from their
published formulas, with my own `ū` from my own Newton solve. It is **not** a rigorous
re-proof, and no sentence of this leg's output may be read as one.

Two things make the float number trustworthy at the resolution the gate needs, and both are
measured rather than asserted:

1. **Their two exact rational quadrature identities are re-checked**, the same two their own
   `quadrature.jl` checks: `√5·π·∫ψ̂₁⁴ψ̂₁′² e^{−r²/4} = 669/31250` and
   `π·∫ψ̂₁³ψ̂₁′ e^{−r²/4} = −29/324`. These calibrate my quadrature against an exact answer.
2. **A resolution sweep in `n`** and **a perturbation of `ū`**, so the reported constants come
   with a measured sensitivity rather than a single unqualified digit string.

## 6. THE WINDOW — pre-committed here, and not moved afterwards

Two readings, both to be reported as magnitudes whichever way they come out.

**READING A (the gate's own words, on the certified SET).** Does my independently-computed
certificate close — `Z₁ < 1` and `P(δ) < 0` for some `δ > 0` — and is Breden–Chu's published
enclosure radius `10^{−3}` a *certified* radius of **my** polynomial, i.e.
`δ_min < 10^{−3} < δ̄_mine`?

**READING B (the stricter pre-commitment, on the CONSTANTS).** Does each of my four
independently-computed constants land within a **factor of 2** of theirs —
`½ ≤ mine/theirs ≤ 2` for each of `Y, Z₁, Z₂, Z₃` — and likewise `δ̄`?

Factor 2 is chosen deliberately, and stated with its reason so it cannot be rationalised
later: `Y` carries a discretisation tail that my quadrature computes differently from theirs,
`Z₂` and `Z₃` are driven by `‖LA_nL^{−1}‖` (an operator norm they bound by
`√(‖·‖₁‖·‖_∞)`, which is itself loose by an unknown factor), and my `ū` is a different
approximate solution. A factor-of-2 agreement across four independently-computed constants of
sizes spanning `10^{−4}` to `10^{3}` would be far beyond coincidence; anything worse is a
finding and gets reported as one.

**Failure attribution, also pre-committed** (the gate's `no` branch): a failure is *ours* if
it is localised at setup (basis/operator/normalisation), at the bounds (a formula I
implemented wrongly), or at verification, and their published number is recoverable once

## 8. An observation about their artifact, recorded before I run anything

Their published constants are internally consistent to high precision: recomputing
`δ̄ = (−Z₂ + √(Z₂² + 2Z₃ − 2Z₁Z₃))/Z₃` from the printed `Z₁, Z₂, Z₃` returns
`0.0027164631369`, matching the printed `0.00271646316` to a relative `8.50e−09`, and
`Q(δ̄) = 1.30e−14`. `Z₃ = 96·‖LA L^{−1}‖` back-solves to `‖LA L^{−1}‖ = 5.7966458`, and
`Z₂ = 2^{15/4}‖LAL^{−1}‖(√2‖ū‖_∞ + ‖∂_xū‖_∞)` back-solves to
`√2‖ū‖_∞ + ‖∂_xū‖_∞ = 4.4030188` — two further quantities I can compare against
independently, and I pre-commit to reporting both.

**One anomaly is already visible and is pinned here before I run**, so that it cannot be
presented later as something my own code discovered by luck. Their `proof.ipynb` cell 35 sets
`δ̲ = Y/(1−Z₁)·1.17163` and validates it by checking `P(δ̲) < 0`. Evaluated at the
**published** constants, `δ̲ = 9.4792e−04` and `P(δ̲) = +2.454e−05 > 0` — the smallest
positive root of `P` is `9.886847e−04`, so the required multiplier is `1.22200`, not
`1.17163` (short by `1.0430×`). Solving for the `Y` at which `1.17163` *would* validate gives
`Y ≤ 6.362e−04`, i.e. `1.189×` below the published `Y`, so print-rounding does not explain it.

**This is explicitly NOT a challenge to Theorem 42, and I pre-commit to not reporting it as
one.** Theorem 42 claims `‖u⋆ − ū‖ ≤ 10^{−3}`, and `10^{−3}` sits strictly inside the
certified interval of the published constants: `P(10^{−3}) = −6.712e−06 < 0`, with
`10^{−3}` exceeding the smallest root by `1.1145×` and below `δ̄` by `2.716×`. The theorem is
carried by its own printed numbers. What the multiplier is, is a **stale constant in the
released notebook**, not a gap in the paper — so under §6's pre-committed attribution rule it
is *not* a "theirs" failure and does *not* trigger the escalation branch.
