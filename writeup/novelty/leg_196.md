**An ingredient for bridging a gap is not the bridge.** This is the same sentence leg 175

## 3. The NEW obstructions the paper itself names

These are additions to leg 175's four open items, not restatements of them.

### N1 — The binding quantity has moved from the residual to λ's error bar

The residual is now at round-off. **λ is not.** Fig. 7(f), the paper's own table of inferred
scaling parameters for IPM (l.714–738), with the accuracies as printed:

| IPM mode | λₛ | stated accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| stable (n=0) | 1.0285722760323 | ± 1e-13 |
| 1st unstable | 0.472129736156 | ± 2e-12 |
| 2nd unstable | 0.3149617817 | ± 3e-10 |
| 3rd unstable | 0.24156641 | ± 1e-8 |
| 4th unstable | 0.1987237 | **± 1e-7** |

The paper's own account of the mechanism, §2.3.3 (l.684–690):
> "…the high-gradient nature of higher-order unstable modes still reduces the convergence rate
> of training. For a fixed computational budget (identical network size, iterations, and two
> training stages), the achievable background PDE residual for the smooth solution increases by
> approximately one order of magnitude for each successive singularity…"

and (l.696):
> "…decreases the achievable λ accuracy for a fixed computational budget by approximately two
> orders of magnitude per model."

**Why this is new and not leg 175's ladder.** Leg 175 recorded the ladder as a *residual*
margin, ~2 decades/mode, measured against the CAP threshold. The full text shows the residual
margin has been paid off — and that what the ladder now degrades is **the enclosure width of
the parameter a CAP would have to enclose.** A self-similar blow-up certificate must enclose λ;
at IPM's 4th unstable, the field's best λ is a **7-digit** number with a ±1e-7 bar, while the
residual beside it is 1e-13. That is a **6-decade** gap between "the equation is satisfied to
round-off" and "the blow-up rate is pinned".

### N2 — The discovery frontier itself stalled: CCF's 3rd unstable was searched for and not found

§2.1.3, *Exploring higher Unstable Solutions* (l.275–281), verbatim:
> "While our gradient-normalization method effectively reduces the residual in the peak region,
> a persistent non-smooth signal at the origin (inset of Fig.3b) remains for all tested λ ∈
> [0.455, 0.4713]. This indicates that if a third unstable solution exists, it is located at a
> λ value corresponding to an even higher-gradient profile. The challenge of exploring the range
> λ < 0.455 and resolving such a feature in the 1D CCF model appears to be even greater than
> finding highly unstable solutions in the 2D IPM equation. A full investigation into the
> existence and properties of this third unstable solution is a compelling direction for future
> research."

**"The full suite of previously discovered CCF solutions" is a suite of three** — stable, 1st,
2nd. The next one was hunted across a λ-window of width 0.0163 and did not appear; the authors
place the remaining search **below** λ = 0.455 and rate it harder than the 2D problem. The
conditional *"if a third unstable solution exists"* is the authors'.

### N3 — The remaining margin is hardware- and arithmetic-bound, not conceptual

The floor is stated as **double-float round-off, O(10⁻¹³)** (§1, l.49–51, quoted in §2c). The
remedies the paper offers for λ accuracy are all budget, not method (l.769–775):
> "…the effectiveness of multistage training can be increased by using larger networks with more
> weights (Fig. 7d), or by introducing a third stage… Both approaches, however, require more
> memory."
> "Figure 6(f) shows the best inferred λ for different singularities of IPM using a multistage
> setup that can be implemented on a single A100 GPU (80GB). To further improve the λ accuracy,
> one could use larger networks or more training stages in parallel on multiple GPUs."

A certificate generally needs its arithmetic to be *tighter* than the object it encloses. This
pipeline is **pinned at double precision** by construction, and its own definition of success
is "residual dominated by double-float round-off". Nothing in the paper addresses what an
interval or higher-precision re-derivation of these profiles would cost.

### N4 — Third-party, post-dating both papers: low residual is not a certificate, demonstrated

Not the authors', recorded because it is directly on the epistemic gap and it cites the target.
Of the **22** papers citing `arXiv:2509.14185` (Semantic Scholar, this pass — see §5), one is:

**`arXiv:2606.25151`** — *Silent Failures in Physics-Informed Neural Networks: Parameter
Poisoning and the Limits of Loss-Based Validation*, D. McShannon, N. Dietrich, June 2026,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25151>. It perturbs PDE parameters before training and obtains
models that **reach loss at or below the clean baseline while differing from the true solution
by large margins**, on Burgers, a Navier–Stokes cavity, and convection–diffusion — i.e. driving
the residual down is not, by itself, evidence of physical accuracy.

**Scope, stated honestly:** its systems are **not** the singularity problems, and it does not
claim any error in `2509.14185` or `2511.22819`. It is cited here for the general point, which
is exactly the gap a CAP is for: **a small residual is evidence; an enclosure is a proof.** It
is the independent, third-party statement of why N1–N3 matter.
