## 1. Is the repair novel? No. It is one clause, specified by the leg that found the defect

Leg 199 did not merely report the defect; it scoped the repair, in
`experiments/journal/leg_199.md`, section *"For whoever lands the repair"*:

> **The repair is one line for M1.** `test_gap_m1_localised_…` is the evidence that the
> `gamma` site is already clean, so an `isinf` test added beside the existing `isnan` one in
> `unit_range_violation` closes M1 without touching the range logic.

**One deliberate departure, and the reason for it.** Leg 199 names
`certificate_guards.unit_range_violation` as the site; this leg's declared territory marks
that file **read-only** (leg 199's closed ground) and confines the repair to
`solver/nk_bounds.py`. The two are reconcilable because leg 199 also *localised* the defect
to an interaction rather than to the guard alone:

> This is not "the guard is careless about infinity"; it is the **interaction** of a missing
> `isinf` test with a caller-supplied infinite endpoint, and `nk_bounds.py:430` is the only
> such caller in the repository.

So the clause is added at the one call site that supplies the infinite endpoint. That is a
strictly narrower change than patching the shared guard: it cannot reach the other four
guards, the other four exported functions, or the three pipelines. What it does **not** do
is closed off explicitly in the runner and the JSON (`C1_at_the_guards_own_surface`): a
caller invoking `unit_range_violation` *directly* with `lo=-inf, lo_open=False` still gets
`None`. There is no such caller in the repository, and closing it belongs to a leg that owns
`certificate_guards.py`. This leg does not claim it.
