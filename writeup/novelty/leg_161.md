## 1. Gate clause (a) — `alpha(1/2) = 3`: **YES, explicitly, in the exact solution itself**

### 1.1 The convention dictionary (this is the only way to misread it)

This repository's own dictionary, `solver/literature_gates.py:32-33`, verbatim:

> `so **alpha_ALS is our beta (the LENGTH exponent), and our alpha is 1/alpha_ALS.**`

LSS uses the same convention as ALS. LSS Eq. (6), p. 3, verbatim:

> "ω = (1/τ) f(ξ) , ξ = x/τ^α , τ = t_c − t,   (6)
> where t_c > 0 is the singularity time and α depends on a, approaching α = 1 in the limit
> a → 0."

So **LSS's `α` is this repository's `1/alpha`**, and `our alpha(1/2) = 3` ⟺ `α_LSS(1/2) = 1/3`.

### 1.2 LSS's exact `a = 1/2` solution produces `1/3` in closed form

**§4, "Exact blow-up solution for a = 1/2", p. 10.** Opening sentence, verbatim:

> "The particular value of the parameter a = 1/2 implies from Eq. (21) that γ = 2."

The ansatz, Eq. (33), p. 10, is the double-pole pair
`ω(x,t) = i ω̃_{-2}(t) ( [x − x_0 − i v_c(t)]^{-2} − [x − x_0 + i v_c(t)]^{-2} )`, and LSS state
of it, verbatim (p. 10):

> "Plugging Eqs. (33) and (34) into Eq. (1), we find the latter equation is identically
> satisfied provided
>   dv_c(t)/dt = − ω̃_{-2}(t) / (4 v_c(t)) ,    (35)
> and
>   dω̃_{-2}(t)/dt = ω̃²_{-2}(t) / (4 v_c²(t)) .   (36)"

Their integration of (35)-(36), Eq. (37), p. 10, verbatim:

> "Solving the system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) (35) and (36) results in
>   v_c(t) = (t_c − t)^{1/3} ṽ_c ,   ω̃_{-2}(t) = 4 ṽ_c² / (3 (t_c − t)^{1/3}) ,   (37)
> where ṽ_c > 0 and t_c are two arbitrary real constants."

The self-similar form, Eqs. (38)-(39), p. 11, verbatim:

> "ω(x,t) = (1/(t_c − t)) · (4 i ṽ_c²/3) ( [ξ − i ṽ_c]^{-2} − [ξ + i ṽ_c]^{-2} )
>          = (1/(t_c − t)) · 16 ṽ_c³ ξ / (3 (ξ² + ṽ_c²)²) ,   (38)
> where
>   ξ := (x − x_0) / (t_c − t)^{1/3}   (39)
> is the self-similar variable."

and **Theorem 2, p. 11**, verbatim:

> "Theorem 2. Eqs. (38) and (39) provide an exact solution of Eq. (1) for a = 1/2 for any
> value of the real constants t_c, ṽ_c > 0 and x_0."

`(39)` is `ξ = (x − x_0)/(t_c − t)^{1/3}`, i.e. **`α_LSS(1/2) = 1/3` exactly**, hence **this
repository's `alpha(1/2) = 3` exactly** — read off an exact closed-form solution, not a fit.

### 1.3 The literal integer 3 is also on the page, twice

* **The far-field decay exponent.** Eq. (38)'s profile is `∝ ξ/(ξ² + ṽ_c²)²`, which decays as
  `|ξ|^{-3}`. LSS say why that number is `1/α`, in §6 after Eq. (49), pp. 13-14, verbatim:
  > "The equation Mf = 0 has a general solution
  >   f ∝ |ξ|^{−1/α}   (49)
  > for α ≠ 0 and f ≡ 0 for α = 0. ... This implies that Eq. (49) describes the decay of f for
  > ξ → ±∞ provided α > 0, in agreement with the exact results of Section 3 (Eq. (30)) and
  > Section 4 (Eq. (38)) for α = 1 and α = 1/3, respectively."

  So `1/α = 3` is LSS's own stated far-field exponent for the `a = 1/2` exact solution, and
  they state `α = 1/3` for Section 4 in so many words.
* **The closed form of the exponent.** Theorem 1 / Eq. (21), p. 8, verbatim: "γ = 1/(1 − a)".
  Eq. (45), p. 12: `α_0 = 2/(γ(γ+1))`. Fig. 1's caption, p. 13, gives it as
  `α_0(a) = 2/(γ(a)(γ(a)+1)) = 2(1−a)²/(2−a)`. At `a = 1/2`: `γ = 2`, `α_0 = 2/6 = 1/3`.
  **Exact, and `1/α_0(1/2) = 3` on the nose.**
* **Table 1, p. 48**, row `a = 0.5`: `α_e = 0.333333333`, `β = −1`, `p = −1.0000007`,
  `α = 0.33333354`, `α_2 = 0.33333340` — the independent numerical eigenvalue solve landing on
  the same `1/3`.

### 1.4 Consequence for this repository's independence framing

`solver/literature_gates.py:88` describes J3 as "`alpha(1/2) = 3` by integrating ALS (49)-(50)
— a system that shares no grid, no basis and no code with our compactified Newton solve."
That statement about *code* is still true and is unaffected. What the full-text read changes is
the **source count**: ALS §5.2's `a = 1/2, σ = 1` system is the **dissipative generalisation of
LSS Eqs. (35)-(36)** (at `ν = 0` the repo's own transcription
`dv_c/dt = ν − Ω/4`, `Ω = w_{-2}/v_c` is LSS (35) verbatim), and **three of ALS's four authors
are LSS's three authors** (Ambrose, Lushnikov, Siegel, Silantyev ⊃ Lushnikov, Silantyev,
Siegel), with LSS (2020) predating ALS (2022). Xu's Table 1 value `c_l(0.5) = 0.3333` is, by
Xu's own statement as transcribed at `solver/literature_gates.py:426`, checked against "the
high-precision branch of [LSS]". **ALS §5.2, Xu Table 1 row `a=0.5`, and LSS §4 are one
ancestor, not three.**

The genuinely independent second source is a *different* paper: **J. Chen,
[arXiv:1908.09385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09385)**, LSS's own reference [6]
(`Nonlinearity` 33(5), 2502 (2020)) — LSS's Note on p. 11, verbatim:

> "Note. After our arXiv preprint submission [35] we learned that the self-similar solution
> (38) was recently discovered by Jiajie Chen in [6]. The result presented here was found
> independently via the complex singularity approach, and has a somewhat more general form by
> including the additional real parameter ṽ_c."

So `alpha(1/2) = 3` has **two independent literature ancestors** (LSS §4 and J. Chen), plus
this repository's own cold integration. It was never novel here, and the current row already
says `CONFIRMED_AND_PRE-EMPTED` — that verdict does not change. Only the *depth* of the
citation does.

## 2. Gate clause (b) — `a_c = 0.6890665`: **YES it is LSS's own primary number, NO it does
not come from the `a = 1/2` exact solution**

### 2.1 LSS is the primary source, and the repo's constant is their value truncated

**Abstract, p. 1**, verbatim:

> "For solutions on the infinite domain we find a new critical value
> a_c = 0.6890665337007457 . . . below which there is finite time singularity formation that has
> a form of self-similar collapse, with the spatial extent of blow-up shrinking to zero."

**§1, Eq. (8), p. 5**, verbatim:

> "The first major result of these simulations is the discovery of a critical value
>   a = a_c = 0.6890665337007457 . . .   (8)
> below which (i.e., for a < a_c) there is finite-time singularity formation, but at which
> point (i.e., for a = a_c) the singularity transitions or changes character."

and, p. 5, the defining condition, verbatim: **"We also find that α = 0 at the critical value
a = a_c."** Repeated in **§12 Conclusions, p. 43**, verbatim: "We identified a new critical
value a = a_c = 0.6890665337007457 . . . such that for a < a_c collapse occurs both on the real
line x ∈ R and for periodic BC."

`solver/literature_gates.py:439`'s `LSS_A_C = 0.6890665` is LSS's `0.6890665337007457`
truncated at the 7th significant figure (`|0.6890665 − 0.6890665337007457| = 3.37e-8`,
`4.89e-8` relative) — consistent, and now sourced first-hand.

### 2.2 It is emphatically **not** implied by the `a = 1/2` exact solution — hypotheses verbatim

The `a = 1/2` closed form's exponent formula is `α_0(a) = 2(1−a)²/(2−a)` (Eq. (45) + Fig. 1
caption, pp. 12-13), which is **strictly positive for every `a < 1`** and therefore has **no
root at `a_c`**. LSS say the leading-order family is not the true branch, p. 12, verbatim:

> "However, we find below in Section 8 (see also Fig. 1) that the numerically computed
> self-similar solution has a different power scaling for ξ = (x−x_0)/(t_c−t)^α than in
> Eq. (45), i.e. α_0 ≠ α for γ ≠ 1, 2. This implies that the l.s.t, neglected in (45), lead to
> a non-trivial modification of α compared with α_0."

and **Theorem 3, §5, p. 11** forbids the exact-solution structure anywhere else, verbatim:

> "Theorem 3. A solution (17) and (21) of Eq. (1) which satisfies assumptions (i) and (ii) of
> Theorem 1 requires l.s.t. which are not identically zero for any a ∈ R" [beyond a = 0, 1/2].

`a_c` is instead **the numerically located root of the nonlinear eigenvalue `α(a)`**, obtained
by two separate numerical routes — §8 time-dependent simulations and §9's generalized
Petviashvili method on Eq. (46)/(67) — and located by a bisection whose ladder is printed in
**Table 1, p. 48**: `α_e = 1.10065641e-06` at `a = 0.689066`, `6.950143e-08` at `0.6890665`,
`1.537e-12` at `0.6890665337`, `6.169686e-17` at `a = 0.6890665337007457`. The **hypotheses
under which that number is stated**, verbatim from Table 1's caption, p. 48:

> "Accuracy of α(a) (for −1 ≤ a ≤ 0.689) and α_2(a) (for −1 ≤ a ≤ 0.689066533) is at least 3-4
> digits of precision, whereas accuracy of α_e(a) is about 3-4 digits of precision for a < 0.3
> and at least 5 digits of precision for a ≥ 0.3, with more precision for 0.3 ≤ a ≤ 0.6890665."

and from Fig. 1's caption, p. 13, verbatim:

> "The green curve terminates at a = a_c since the iteration used to solve the nonlinear
> eigenvalue problem for x ∈ R does not converge for a > a_c."

**So `a_c` is a converged-numerics result of an iterative eigenvalue solve, on the real line,
under decaying BC (7), not a theorem and not a by-product of the `a = 1/2` exact solution.**
The seventeen printed digits are the bisection's residual ladder, not seventeen certified
digits: the paper's own accuracy claim for `α_e` is "at least 5 digits ... more precision for
0.3 ≤ a ≤ 0.6890665". Anyone quoting `a_c` should quote it as **LSS's converged numerical
critical value**, which is what the upgraded row now says.
