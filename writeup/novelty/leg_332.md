## §3 — Decisive prior art, located and named

### PA1 — Gallay & Wayne 2001, **on ℝ³**, in the self-similar variables, in the Gaussian space

> **[arXiv:math/0102198v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0102198) — "Long-time asymptotics of the
> Navier-Stokes and vorticity equations on ℝ³"**
> *"We use the vorticity formulation to study the long-time behavior of solutions to the
> Navier-Stokes equation on ℝ³. … After introducing self-similar variables, we compute the
> long-time asymptotics of the rescaled vorticity equation up to second order. **Each term in the
> asymptotics is a self-similar divergence-free vector field with Gaussian decay at infinity**,
> and the coefficients … can be determined by solving a finite system of ordinary differential
> equations."*

This is **the exact configuration this leg is asked about**, twenty-five years old: the
**vorticity** formulation, in **self-similar variables**, with objects of **Gaussian decay**,
in **d = 3**, with the velocity recovered by Biot–Savart and **never asked to lie in the
weighted space**. The companion [arXiv:math/0102197v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0102197) does
the same on ℝ².

**Consequence for this leg's novelty claim, stated plainly and against my own interest:**
**the mathematics of a NO answer here is not novel and this leg will not claim it is.** If the
Leray obstruction genuinely reached the vorticity formulation on the Gaussian weight, Gallay and
Wayne's ℝ³ programme could not exist. Leg 261 reached this conclusion from Gallay's *Handbook*
chapter (2D); **PA1 is the ℝ³ article and is strictly stronger for this repository's purpose**,
because Clay's object is 3D and the 2D citation left a dimensional gap.

What *is* this leg's own is not the mathematics but the **audit**: which numbered step of legs
257/261's derivation fails to carry over, with a magnitude on each step.

### PA2 — Gallay & Wayne, Burgers vortices, d = 3

[arXiv:math/0503354v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503354) and
[arXiv:math/0503353v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503353) — "Three-Dimensional Stability of
Burgers Vortices". Non-axisymmetric 3D vortices in Gaussian-decay vorticity spaces. Corroborates
PA1: this weight is *the* vorticity weight, and it has carried genuinely 3D, genuinely
non-axisymmetric work.

### PA3 — the forward-self-similar 3D construction, in the vorticity variable

[arXiv:2107.02952v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02952) — "Self-similar source-type solutions to
the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations". Its own abstract: *"In view of the critical
scale-invariance … **we exploit the vorticity curl as the dependent variable** to derive and
analyse the dynamically-scaled Navier-Stokes equations. This formalism offers a viewpoint in
which the problem takes the simplest possible form."* An independent author group choosing the
vorticity formulation **for exactly the reason this leg is measuring** — the scaled problem is
better behaved in it. Note the ansatz is **forward**, not backward, self-similar; this leg does
**not** read it as evidence about a backward-SS blow-up target.

### PA4 — the parent, and its silence

[arXiv:2404.04054v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04054) (Breden–Chu). Leg 257's md5-pinned
full-text census already measured **`vorticity` = 0** in its body against a live control of
124. Q5 returning `n = 1` — the parent alone — reproduces leg 257's own novelty finding on an
independent run. **The vorticity formulation is not merely unaddressed by the parent; the word
does not occur in it.**

## §4 — `capabilities.py` grepped before building (ban compliance)

Grepped for `leray`, `biot`, `vortic`, `projector`, `gaussian`, `weight`, `hermite`.

| row found | verdict |
|---|---|
| `solver/bc_weighted_sobolev.py` — *"Breden–Chu's weighted-Sobolev setting H²(µ), µ = e^{&#124;x&#124;²/4}/Z"* | **The right weight, the wrong dimension.** The module is `d = 1`, even (Neumann), built around `L = −Δ − (x/2)·∇` on the line and Breden–Chu's §6 Burgers example. Its **normalisation constant and weight convention are reused** (`µ = e^{&#124;x&#124;²/4}/Z`); none of its quadrature or eigenbasis machinery is applicable to a 3D vector field. |
| `solver/boussinesq_velocity.py` — *"2D Boussinesq velocity (Biot-Savart)"* | **2D scalar-vorticity Biot–Savart.** Wrong dimension: the 3D law is a vector cross-product kernel, not the 2D `K₂` kernel. Not reusable. |
| `solver/holder_norms.py`, `solver/weight_search.py` | weighted-Hölder and weighted-`ℓ¹` families — **neither is a Hilbert norm** (leg 163's finding, re-confirmed). Not applicable. |

**Conclusion: no existing instrument computes a 3D Biot–Savart velocity or a 3D weighted-`L²`
norm.** The runner therefore builds its own — but **in closed form**, not by grid convolution:
every field in this leg is a Gaussian times a polynomial, or a radial quadrature of one, so the
3D Biot–Savart reconstruction is done by an **exact `ℓ = 1` spherical-harmonic radial ODE** and
the Leray potential by an **exact `ℓ = 0`/`ℓ = 2` radial Green's function**. **No solver module
is added, so no `capabilities.py` row is owed** (leg 257's precedent).
