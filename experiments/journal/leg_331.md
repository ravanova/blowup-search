# Leg 331 — Route-NLH: does Breden–Chu's machinery hold a nonlocal operator?

**Gate.** Does `solver/bc_weighted_sobolev.py`'s machinery, pointed at a named nonlocal
operator on its own weighted space, produce finite, closing bounds of the same kind it
produces for the local operators it was built on?

**Answer: NO — and for the first time the obstruction is MEASURED rather than named.**

Ceiling first: everything below is float64, not interval arithmetic. These are
magnitudes. Nothing here is a rigorous enclosure and nothing here may be cited as one.

---

## 1. What was built

The object is Breden–Chu's own equation (54) with exactly one letter changed:

    L u - u/4 + u^2 W_t u = 0,      W_t = (1-t) d_x + t Lam^{2a},   Lam^{2a} = (-Delta)^a

on their space `H^2(mu)`, `mu = e^{|x|^2/4}/Z`, in their even half-Hermite/Laguerre
basis `psi_m`, `d = 1`. `a = 1/2` is the Hilbert-transform realization `Lam = H d_x`.

`t` is a **homotopy in nonlocality**. `t = 0` is their equation verbatim, so `t = 0` is
a regression control that can come out differently. `t = 1` is the fully nonlocal
object. The gate then stops being a boolean at one endpoint and becomes a ladder: the
question is not *whether* it fails but *where along the ladder, and by which bound*.

### The primitive, and the identity that is FALSE

`psi_m` is **not** an eigenbasis of the Fourier transform. The tempting identity
`FT[H_n(x/2) e^{-x^2/4}] ~ H_n` is false — Hermite *functions* carry `e^{-y^2/2}`,
these carry `e^{-y^2}`. The generating function `e^{2zt-t^2}` gives a **monomial**:

    FT[H_n(x/2) e^{-x^2/4}](xi) = 2 sqrt(pi) (-i)^n (2 xi)^n e^{-xi^2}

hence the exact primitive actually used (no scipy, no mpmath, no new special functions):

    Lam^{2a} psi_m (x) = (2 / (sqrt(pi) m! Zeta_m)) Int_0^inf xi^{2m+2a} e^{-xi^2} cos(xi x) dxi

I asserted the false version first. The pre-committed `a = 0` known-answer gate caught
it: `m = 0` exact to 1.2e-15 and every `m >= 1` at ~100% relative error, identical at
286 and 960 panels and at `x_max` 14 and 20 — resolution-independent, therefore
structural, not numerical. That is the whole value of pre-committing the gate.

## 3. The measured obstruction

### 3a. `Lam^{2a} psi_m` is not in the space at all

The symbol's non-smooth factor `|xi|^{2a}` multiplies `xi^{2m}`, forcing an **algebraic**
tail where the local control has a Gaussian one. Pre-registered prediction
`x^{-(1+2a+2m)}`, measured at `m = 0`:

| alpha | predicted | fitted |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 1.5 | **1.507674** |
| 0.50 | 2.0 | **2.012245** |
| 0.75 | 2.5 | **2.517908** |

Against the weight `e^{x^2/4}` an algebraic tail is fatal. Truncated
`||Op psi_0||^2_{L^2(mu), |x|<R}` at `alpha = 0.5`:

| R | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Lam^{2a}psi_0` | 0.9655 | 8.713 | 1196 | 2.651e6 | 5.872e10 | 1.147e16 | **1.869e22** |
| control `d_x psi_0` | 0.95399 | 0.99956 | 0.99999948 | 0.99999999992 | 1.000000000 | 1.000000000 | 1.000000000 |

The control saturates at 1 to twelve digits; the nonlocal one has no limit.
**The operator does not map the machinery's space into itself.** This is
resolution-independent: no `n` fixes it.

### 3b. The machinery's finiteness is an artefact of where its rule stops looking

Their six-product rule has a largest node. Beyond it nothing is sampled, so a divergent
integral still returns a finite number. `log10` of the truncated weighted norm² at each
rule's own horizon, `alpha = 0.5`, `m = 0`:

| n | six-rule largest node | log10 nonlocal | log10 local control |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 30.668 | **95.151** | 0.0 |
| 200 | 43.501 | **197.728** | -3.9e-16 |
| **1500** (Breden–Chu's published resolution) | 119.726 | **1546.389** | -3.9e-16 |

Taken in logs because at `n = 1500` the quantity overflows float64 outright; an overflow
is not a measurement. Refining `n` makes this **worse, monotonically**, because a larger
`n` pushes the horizon further out into the divergence.

### 3c. The mechanism inside their bounds: quadrature exactness is lost

Their K-product Gauss–Laguerre rules are exact for polynomial×Gaussian. `Lam^{2a}psi` is
not in that class. Relative error of their rule on the nonlinearity's own norm, against a
refined reference carrying the *same* `t`-mixture (so the diagnostic contains its own
control at `t = 0`), `n = 100`, `alpha = 0.5`:

| t | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.4 | 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule error, nonlocal | 1.617e-12 | **0.3393** | 0.1463 | **0.7639** |
| local control, same reference | 1.62e-12 | 1.59e-12 | 1.51e-12 | 5.76e-13 |

O(1) error at 20% nonlocality, against a control exact to 1e-12 at every t. This matters
because `Y` and `Zbar21` compute their tail terms as a **difference** of rule-evaluated
quantities, so lost exactness contaminates the difference directly.

### 3d. The gate quantity: Z1 leaves the admissible region before t = 1

Breden–Chu need `Z1 < 1`; above 1 the radii polynomial has no positive root at all,
whatever `Y` does. `Z1` climbs monotonically with nonlocality and crosses 1 at a
**finite** `t`, bisected to a bracket of width 6.1e-06:

| | alpha = 0.25 | alpha = 0.5 | alpha = 0.75 |
|---|---|---|---|
| n = 100 (Z1(0) = 0.359321) | **t\* = 0.30000** | **0.40000** | **0.45586** |
| n = 200 (Z1(0) = 0.246098) | branch lost first; Z1 <= 0.96098 where tracked | **0.47050** | **0.51560** |

So the machinery tolerates roughly 30–52% of one nonlocal operator and no more. The
crossing does move outward with `n` (0.40000 -> 0.47050 at alpha = 0.5), which is honest
to report and which alone would leave the endpoint open — but 3a and 3b are
`n`-independent and get *worse* with `n`, so the two ladders point the same way.

### 3e. Which bound fails, and it is not an accident

At `n = 200`, `alpha = 0.5`, along the tracked branch:

- `Zbar11` stays at ~1e-15 (derivation-free) — never a problem;
- `Zbar21` rises only from 0.06706 to 0.31568 (derivation-free);
- **`Zbar12`** goes 0.10175 -> 0.72524 -> **62.349**;
- **`Zbar22`** goes 0.21560 -> 1.16982 -> **30.585**.

The two that blow up are exactly the two graded **TRANSCRIBED** at the start of the
construction — the two whose Breden–Chu derivations used locality. That grading was
written down before any of these numbers existed.

## 4. Two things that did NOT fail, reported because they didn't

- **The sup-norm route survives.** `sum_m sup|Lam^{2a}psi_m|^2 / lam_m^2` is finite and
  comparable to the local control: 1.362 (a=0.25), 1.235 (a=0.5), 1.871 (a=0.75) versus
  0.857 local. Fitted sup growth exponents 0.003, 0.265, 0.522. So the obstruction is
  **not** in the sup/embedding route; it is in the `L^2(mu)` norm of the image and in
  the quadrature. A future attempt should not waste effort re-deriving `Zbar22`.
- **`Y` never blew up on the tracked branch** and `tail_sq` never went negative — the
  failure mode I predicted in advance for the tail estimator did *not* occur. Recorded
  as a refuted prediction of my own.

## 5. Lesson-90 hazard, handled

`u == 0` solves the equation at every `t`, and its radii polynomial closes with
`Y ~ 1e-29`, `closes = True`. Newton falls into it once the nontrivial branch folds.
A verdict that cannot come out differently is not a verdict, so every step carries
`collapsed_to_trivial_solution` and `verdict_is_meaningful`, and no `closes = True` in
this leg's data is a certification of anything. All six (n, alpha) runs end in that
trivial state by `t = 1`; the fold is located by bisection to width 2.4e-05.

## 6. Bearing on other legs — routed, not adjudicated

Screen (iv_a) killed 18/18 fluid rows (leg 261) and 3/3 non-fluid rows (leg 311) on the
two nonlocality reasons Remark 40 *names*. Those 21 kills now have measured footing
rather than inferred: the machinery does fail on a nonlocal operator, and the reason it
fails is **not** the reason Remark 40 gives. Remark 40 talks about what the method is
for; what actually breaks is that `Lam^{2a}` leaves `L^2(mu)`, i.e. it is the **weight**
`e^{|x|^2/4}` versus an algebraic tail, not nonlocality as such. That distinction is
load-bearing for leg 334 and is a matter for the DM, not for this leg: it suggests the
re-screen question is "which weight tolerates an algebraic tail", and a Gaussian one
provably does not.

**No ban is lifted here.** This is not a fourth space/basis; it is Breden–Chu's own
space, and the `ell^1`-Fourier/radii-polynomial ban is untouched — the norm here is the
Hilbert `H^2(mu)` norm, not `ell^1_w`, which is the same argument leg 256 banked.
