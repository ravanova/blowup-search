## 1. The question

Leg 374 built a per-basis spectral inventory for route 4's Galerkin-plus-tail bridge (leg 348's
own four named asks: R³ dimension, vector/Leray-projected state space, CAP-grade rigor, and
geometric/exponential-in-truncation tail decay). Its single closest-weight-match candidate —
generalized/weighted Hermite functions, weight `(1+x²)^{-γ}` (Xiang-Wang 2010, Zhang-Guo 2014,
Guo-Zhang 2015, Guo 1999) — was flagged with one open, unresolved concern sourced to Boyd (1980):
does the coefficient-decay rate for this family, under an algebraic weight, come out only
algebraic (polynomial-in-N) rather than geometric/exponential, which would fail ask (iv) and be a
structural mismatch with every compact-domain CAP closure route 4's bridge needs to mirror?

This leg's job: answer that question, definitively, one way or the other.

Semantic Scholar's API (rate-limited on first two tries, succeeded on a third after backoff)
surfaced two directly on-topic, fully open-access, and much more current papers than leg 374's own
table had: Hu & Yu, arXiv:2412.08044v3 (Oct 2025) and arXiv:2602.03083v1 (Feb 2026), both
specifically about resolving "the confusing impasse" of Hermite/generalized-Hermite convergence-
rate theory (their own words). Both PDFs were fetched from arxiv.org directly (no paywall) and read
in full via the Read tool's PDF support (not just abstract-snippet).

**Read closely, these turned out NOT to be the same basis as leg 374's candidate**, despite
sharing the name "generalized Hermite functions": arXiv:2602.03083 §2.2 defines its GHFs with
weight `|x|^{2μ} e^{-x²}` — still Gaussian-enveloped, with an added power singularity at the
origin — while Zhang-Guo/Guo-Zhang's family (leg 374's actual candidate) has weight `(1+x²)^{-γ}`,
no Gaussian factor, algebraically enveloped throughout. Hu-Yu's headline finding — algebraic-decay
targets get only algebraic convergence from Hermite-family bases even under optimal scaling,
because Gaussian-enveloped Hermite functions are approximately Fourier self-dual (can't localize in
both space and frequency at once) — is real, current, and directly explains WHY plain/scaled
Hermite struggles with algebraic decay, but the mechanism is specific to the Gaussian envelope and
does not transfer automatically to Zhang-Guo's non-Gaussian, algebraically-enveloped construction.
Recorded as a citation-tracking trap correctly avoided, not a wasted read: it clarified precisely
what needed to be measured directly, and why.

## 3. Measurement — `experiments/p2_route_hcdx_v1.py`

Reading could not settle this for the actual candidate basis, so this leg builds and runs a small,
independently-checkable numerical instrument rather than guessing.

**Construction (elementary, not invented for convenience).** Orthogonal polynomials on `R` w.r.t.
weight `(1+x²)^{-γ}` are, under the algebraic bijection `t = x/√(1+x²)` mapping `R` onto `(-1,1)`,
exactly Gegenbauer/ultraspherical polynomials `C_n^{(γ-1)}(t)`, orthogonal w.r.t. the classical
weight `(1-t²)^{α-1/2}`, `α=γ-1`. Derivation (`x=tan θ`, `t=sin θ`, `dx=(1+x²)dθ`) is in the
script's own module docstring; this is the actual mathematical content of a "generalized-Hermite-
type" basis matched to route 4's own algebraic weight family, up to a normalization constant that
does not affect the decay RATE this leg measures (only the amplitude).

**Target.** `u(x) = 1/(4+x²)^(s/2)` — analytic on all of `R` (poles at `x=±2i`, off the real axis),
decaying at exactly route 4's own algebraic class `|x|^{-s}`, `s>1` (leg 260's own sharp
threshold).

**Instrument.** Expand `g(t) = u(x(t))·(1+x(t)²)^{γ_w/2}` (the generalized-Hermite-function
coefficient integrand) in the Gegenbauer system via `scipy.integrate.quad` (tight tolerance
`1e-13`) for even `n=0..60` (odd coefficients vanish by symmetry — `g` is even in `t` for this
target). Fit `log|c_n|` against BOTH a geometric model (linear in `n`) and an algebraic model
(linear in `log n`) by least squares, score each by `R²` — an instrument built so it CAN report
either regime, not rigged toward one answer.

**Two regimes tested, both predicted analytically before running:**

* **Matched** (`γ_w = s`): `g(t)` is then analytic on the *closed* interval `[-1,1]` (no branch
  point at the mapped-infinity points `t=±1`) — predicts geometric decay.
* **Mismatched** (`γ_w = s+δ`, `δ≠0`): `g(t)` then carries an explicit
  `(1-t²)^{(s-γ_w)/2}` factor — a genuine non-integer-power branch point at `t=±1` unless `δ` is an
  even integer — predicts only algebraic decay, worsening with `|δ|`.

**Results (full numbers, `writeup/data/p2_route_hcdx_v1.json`'s `measurement` block):**

| case | param | geo_R² | alg_R² | dominates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| matched | s=1.1 | 0.9754 | 0.8630 | GEOMETRIC |
| matched | s=1.5 | 0.9998 | 0.8393 | GEOMETRIC |
| matched | s=2.0 | 1.0000 | 0.8477 | GEOMETRIC |
| matched | s=3.0 | 0.9998 | 0.8583 | GEOMETRIC |
| mismatched | δ=0.25 | 0.8101 | 0.9957 | ALGEBRAIC |
| mismatched | δ=0.5 | 0.8521 | 0.9985 | ALGEBRAIC |
| mismatched | δ=1.0 | 0.8836 | 0.9951 | ALGEBRAIC |
| mismatched | δ=2.0 | 0.9056 | 0.9893 | ALGEBRAIC |

Both predictions confirmed cleanly and robustly (four independent parameter values each, not one
lucky case): matched case is geometric in all four; mismatched case is algebraic in all four, with
the fitted algebraic power growing steeper (`-1.39` to `-2.44`) as the mismatch `δ` grows —
consistent with the named branch-point-order mechanism, not a coincidence of the fit.

## 4. Classification: **ADEQUATE**

Boyd (1980)'s concern is real (corroborated directly by this leg's own mismatched-case
measurement: getting the weight wrong genuinely does degrade decay from geometric to algebraic).
But it does not kill leg 374's candidate for route 4 specifically, because route 4's target decay
exponent is not an unknown to be guessed at — leg 260 already measured it sharply (`s>1`).
Choosing the generalized-Hermite basis weight to match (`γ=s`) is an available, known choice, not
speculative tuning, and this leg's own measurement shows that choice yields genuine geometric
coefficient decay for an analytic target in route 4's own decay class, across four tested values
of `s` spanning that class.

This resolves leg 374's specific open question and closes the basis-inventory thread on THIS
question, in the ADEQUATE direction. It does **not** resolve leg 374's other three named gaps for
this same row (fails (i) R³/dimension — this measurement is 1D; ambiguous-to-unaddressed on (ii)
vector/Leray-projected state space; fails (iii) CAP-grade validated rigor vs. asymptotic
numerical-analysis convergence) — those remain open, per leg 374's own table, and this leg's gate
was narrower by design (Boyd's concern specifically, not the full candidate certification).
