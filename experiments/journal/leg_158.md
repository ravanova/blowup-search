## FINDING 1 — the paper's own motivating example has a zero diagonal, and its future-work
## list points at the class, while its hypotheses exclude it

**§1, p. 1, verbatim:**

> "Some differential operators can be represented by infinite tridiagonal matrices acting in
> sequence spaces, as it is the case for instance for **differentiation in frequency space of
> the Hermite functions**."

**§5 (Conclusion and Perspectives), p. 25, verbatim:**

> "Note that assumption (5) requires the tridiagonal operator to have symmetric ratios between
> the diagonal terms and the upper and lower diagonal terms. **This is a restriction that could
> hopefully be relaxed.** Since many interesting problems involve tridiagonal operators with
> non symmetric ratios (**as in the case of differentiation in frequency space of the Hermite
> functions**), we believe that this is a promising route to follow."

> "Finally, **generalizing our approach to problems with block-tridiagonal structures could
> also be a valuable project.**"

The observation leg 57's scoping pass did not make: in the normalized-Hermite basis the
differentiation operator's recurrence is
`ψ_n′ = sqrt(n/2)·ψ_{n−1} − sqrt((n+1)/2)·ψ_{n+1}` — **its diagonal is identically zero**. So
the example BDL name twice, as the motivation for the extension they promise, violates
assumption **(4)** as well as (5), and violates (5) *infinitely* (`λ_k/μ_k = ∞`), not by an
asymmetry of ratios. **BDL describe the obstruction as a non-symmetric ratio; on their own
named example it is a vanishing denominator.** This is a statement about the source, quoted
verbatim, not a new mathematical claim.

## FINDING 2 — their LU construction does not merely lose its estimate on a zero diagonal; it
## divides by zero at its first step

BDL build `A` from Ciarlet's LU of the tridiagonal tail (their (9)–(10), citing *Theorem 4.3-2
on page 142* of Ciarlet [11]), driven by the continuants

    δ_0 = 1,  δ_1 = b_1,  δ_n = b_n δ_{n−1} − a_n c_{n−1} δ_{n−2}   (BDL p. 4)

with `b_n` the **diagonal**. On this operator `b_n ≡ 0`, so the recursion decouples by parity
and **every odd continuant vanishes exactly**. Computed at `m = 64`
(`D_lu_diagnosis` in the JSON):

    δ = [1.0, 0.0, 1007.5, 0.0, 1080040.0, 0.0, 1229625540.0, 0.0, 1.484e12, 0.0, ...]

`U_I`'s diagonal is `δ_n/δ_{n−1}` (their (10)), whose first entry is `δ_1/δ_0 = 0.0`; and
BDL's scalar `w̃` of **eq (20), p. 7** — the tail-feedback correction they fold into the finite
block's south-east corner, `K = D − β_{m−1} λ_m (w_I)_0 · e_{m−1}e_{m−1}^T` (p. 6) — has
leading term `δ_0/δ_1 = 1/0`, **undefined**. On the positive control the same code returns
`δ = [1, 64, 3993.6, 252595.2, …]`, no zeros, and `w̃`'s leading term `= 0.015625`.

This is sharper than "the hypothesis fails": the factorization the method is built on does not
exist for this operator, at index 1.

## FINDING 3 — and this is the gate's YES: the 2×2 block recast restores assumption (4) exactly

BDL's fourth future-work item names the class. Executing it is classical **cyclic / odd–even
reduction** (Gander–Golub; Heller, SINUM 14 (1977) — see `writeup/novelty/leg_158.md`
FINDING 1; **claimed by nobody here**). Pair modes `(k, k+1)`. With `μ ≡ 0` the block diagonal
is *anti*-diagonal,

    D_j = [[0, β_k], [λ_{k+1}, 0]],   det D_j = −β_k λ_{k+1},

and the block dominance ratios collapse to

    ‖D_j^{−1} L_j‖ = |λ_k / β_k|,     ‖D_j^{−1} U_j‖ = |β_{k+1} / λ_{k+1}|

— **ratios of the two off-diagonals, in which the zero diagonal no longer appears at all.**
That is the compensating technique, and it is concrete.

Measured on this repository's operator (coefficients read out of
`solver/spectral_certificate.py:tail_block`, verified entrywise against the closed form
`λ_k = 1 − (k−1)/2`, `μ_k = 0`, `β_k = (k+1)/2` to `max abs discrepancy 0.0` over 58 modes;
sample `k = 20`: `(−8.5, 0.0, 10.5)`):

| BDL hypothesis | scalar form (what leg 57 read) | after the 2×2 recast |
|---|---|---|
| (4) lower constant `C_1` | **0.0** (fails, infinitely) | **0.375** (offset 0) / **0.389** (offset 1) — **holds** |
| (4) upper constant `C_2` | 0.5625 | 0.5625 / 0.5556 |
| `min |det|` of the diagonal | 0 at every mode | **13.5** / **17.5** |
| (5) ratio `δ`, threshold `1/2` | **∞** | **1.667** / **1.571** (sup), → **1.0039** as `k → ∞` |
| (5) holds? | no | **still no** |

**So: the zero diagonal is genuinely compensated — assumption (4) goes from `C_1 = 0` to
`C_1 = 0.375` — and the violation of (5) goes from *infinite* to a *finite* ratio tending to
1.** BDL need `δ < 1/2`. This is a factor ≈ 2 short, at every `k`, in both pairings.

**Positive control (lesson 90 — it reports the other answer on every predicate).** The
synthetic BDL-admissible operator `μ_k = k`, `λ_k = β_k = 0.2k`: scalar `C_1 = 1.0`,
`C_2 = 1.0`, scalar `δ = 0.200 < 1/2` — **admissible**; continuants all non-zero; block
recast `δ = 0.208 < 1/2`. Every "no" above is about this operator, not about the code.

## FINDING 4 — the price, and it is the SS5 shape

Can a diagonal gauge buy `δ_block` below `1/2`? A gauge `s_k` sends `λ_k → λ_k s_{k−1}/s_k`,
`β_k → β_k s_{k+1}/s_k`; the two block ratios pick up factors from the **odd** and the **even**
chain separately, so both can be shrunk at once — but only by driving the two parity chains
apart. Forcing both ratios `≤ 0.49` over `J` consecutive blocks forces a parity imbalance
growing at (`F_gauge_price` in the JSON)

    J = 4 → 3.984× per block,  J = 8 → 4.049,  J = 16 → 4.097,  J = 32 → 4.128,  J = 64 → 4.145

i.e. **geometric, ≈ 4.15× per 2×2 block** (`log R = 91.01` at `J = 64`). Since the operator's
own ratios tend to 1, that rate is essentially `1/0.49² = 4.16` — **the entire price is BDL's
threshold, not the operator.** One unit bought on the diagonal, paid for geometrically in the
weight's parity balance: leg 58's SS5 shape, again.

## THE CEILING — what this does NOT do, stated before anyone can over-read it

1. **It is a statement about constructing `A`, not about `L`.** Leg 127's route runs through
   `Z_1 ≥ 1 − ‖A‖·σ_min(L)`. If `L` is not bounded below, **no** shape of `A` closes, and the
   recast is irrelevant. This leg measured nothing about `σ_min(L)` and claims nothing about it.
2. **Inside `A21 = 0` it is already dead.** Leg 58's theorem is `Z_1 ≥ 1` for **every bounded**
   `A` with `A21 = 0`, every `K`, every `s < 1`. A block-recast approximate inverse is still a
   bounded `A`. So the recast can only ever matter in the `A21 ≠ 0` class — where leg 54's
   measured floor `8.9591` (vs the `>8×` needed) is the standing number.
3. **It does not meet BDL's own hypothesis.** `δ → 1`, threshold `1/2`. BDL's theorem does not
   apply after the recast either; what changed is *how far* from applying it is.
4. **No link of the `L1→L4` chain moved.** Clay stays ~0.05% behind Walls 1 and 2.

## ESCALATION (as the gate's yes-branch prescribes)

Recorded as a **candidate for a follow-up construction leg**, not for this one, and **not** as
something that reverses a banked conclusion:

> *Recast the tail operator by odd–even (cyclic) reduction into 2×2 blocks, and ask whether an
> approximate inverse built on the resulting invertible anti-diagonal block diagonal — necessarily
> in the `A21 ≠ 0` class, since `A21 = 0` is closed by leg 58's theorem — moves leg 54's measured
> floor `8.9591`. Pre-committed obstruction to beat: the block dominance ratio is `1.0039` where
> BDL need `< 0.5`, and a gauge that buys it down costs `4.15×` per block in parity imbalance.*

**For leg 127 (still live on `A21 ≠ 0`): this does not change the picture.** It supplies a
concrete, previously unnamed `A`-shape in that class and the two magnitudes that shape must
beat, but by ceiling (1) it cannot help if `L` is not bounded below, which is the question leg
127 is actually deciding.
