## What the one real candidate actually does

**Cao-Labora, Gómez-Serrano, Shi, Staffilani, `arXiv:2310.05325`, *Non-radial implosion for
compressible Euler and Navier–Stokes in T³ and R³*, Cambridge J. Math. 13(4) 753–885** —
published, v2 2025-04-21, two of the parent's three authors. It is a genuinely stronger
*theorem* than the parent (non-radial, periodic **and** whole-space). It is **not** a stronger
*certificate*, in three measured ways:

**1. It has no certification apparatus of its own.** Case-insensitive census over the complete
6642-line extraction: `interval arithmetic` **0**, `computer-assisted` **0**, `enclosure` **0**,
`validated numerics` **0**, `certif` **0**, `rigorous` **0**. The parent, for contrast:
`interval arithmetic` **2**, `computer-assisted` **34** over 10108 lines. This upgrades leg 172's
abstract-depth reading ("the fetch found none") to a full-text measurement.

**2. The certified object it imports is explicitly the ν = 0 system.** Line 251, verbatim:

> *"From [8, 51], we know that there exist radially symmetric profiles (U, S) that solve (1.5)
> for **ν = 0**, with r in the range (1.6)."*

`[8]` is the parent. The inherited object is inviscid, and the paper says so.

**3. The viscous term is DOMINATED, by the authors' own account.** It enters (1.5) as the only
ν-dependent term, carrying the prefactor `e^{−δ_dis s}`, and Remark 1.5 states the mechanism
outright:

> *"condition (1.8) guarantees that δ_dis > 0, hence the term ν C_dis e^{−δ_dis s} ΔU/S^{1/α}
> in (1.5) has an exponential decay, which is crucial in the proof of stability. If ν = 0, this
> term vanishes and thus the condition is not needed. The proof is analogous to the
> Navier–Stokes one, **except that one does not need to bound the dissipation term** when doing
> energy estimates."*

The Euler and Navier–Stokes proofs differ by *one bounded error term*. And §3 line 2363 prices
it: *"Because F_dis has decay not faster than e^{−δ_dis s}, our bootstrap estimate can only have
a lower decay."* The viscous term is an entry in the **forcing vector** of the perturbation
equation (1.15) — the textbook shape of DOMINATED, in leg 174's own terms.

## The sharpest form of the result: the inequality did not move

The parent's viscous condition, `2208.09445` (1.20)/(1.21), line 636–639:

> *"The last term can be treated as an error so long as −δ_dis = 2 − r + (1/α)(1−r) < 0, or
> equivalently r > 2γ/(γ+1)."*

The follow-up's, `2310.05325` (1.8), line 249: `δ_dis = (r−1)/α + r − 2 > 0`.

**These are the same inequality.** Not paraphrased — the runner transcribes each in its own
paper's notation and evaluates both (plus the different-group paper's `δ_dis := ℓ(r−1) + r − 2`,
`ℓ = 2/(γ−1)`) over a 39×19 (γ, r) grid: **max absolute deviation 1.78e-15**, i.e. bit-level
agreement. Fourteen months and a much harder theorem later, the viscous term is handled by the
identical scalar inequality on the scaling exponent.

**The magnitudes, since a boolean is not a result.** On the parent's own case γ = 7/5
(α = 0.2, so δ_dis collapses to the line `6r − 7`), the viscous theorem exists only on
`r ∈ (1.1666667, 1.1909830)` — width **2.43e-02** — and across that entire window
**δ_dis ≤ 0.1458980**. That number *is* the domination margin: the whole viscous term is carried
by a prefactor decaying no faster than `e^{−0.146 s}` in self-similar time. It is positive, and
it is all there is.

**Three published identities re-derived rather than quoted** (each could have failed, and the
r\* formula had to be recovered from a mangled PDF extraction): (1.21)'s threshold is a root of
δ_dis to 2.2e-15; the two branches of r\*(γ) agree at γ = 5/3 exactly, to absolute deviation 0.0 (1.2679491924), which is
what the authors assert in words and is what pins the correct parse; r\*(γ) → √3 as γ → ∞,
matching (1.7); and the ceiling γ < 1 + 2/√3, bisected from `r*(γ) = 2γ/(γ+1)` at
**2.154700538379** against the published **2.154700538379**.

## The control, and why it is the strongest part of the leg

**Shao, Wei, Wang, Zhang, `arXiv:2501.15701`** (γ = 5/3, monatomic — the degenerate case) is a
**different** author group, so it is out of the gate's scope. It is in the record because it is
the sharpest available control on the *mechanism*: an independent group, on the same object,
reaches the viscous theorem through the **identical** `δ_dis > 0` inequality (their (1.4)) —
with **0** occurrences of `interval arithmetic` over 4146 lines, having replaced the
computer-assisted profile step by an **analytic** one.

So the domination is **not an artifact of the parent's computer assistance**. It is how this
entire literature transfers an Euler profile to Navier–Stokes, and removing the computer from
the argument does not change it. That is a stronger statement than the gate asked for, and it
is the one that should be banked.

**A misreading this leg caught in itself, recorded so it is not re-made.** γ = 5/3 is *not*
excluded because its viscous window is empty — the runner computes that window as **nonempty**,
`r ∈ (1.2500000, 1.2679492)`, width 1.79e-02. CGSS exclude it because the *profiles* they import
are MRRS's "almost every γ" result, from which the degenerate γ = 5/3 is excluded. The control
paper is exactly what fills that hole, analytically.

reason: the viscous term is admitted as an exponentially decaying error under δ_dis > 0, never
enclosed, and the certified object remains the ν = 0 system.**

The negative holds in this realization and **nowhere wider**: the enumerated publication record
