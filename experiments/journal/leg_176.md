## The construction — and the one piece of mathematics that made it tractable

Leg 163 scoped the split (Xu's Hardy block-diagonalization), the shape (the Hardy–Mellin
resolvent) and the bordered rows (`m = y b⁻²`, `ℓ(f) = i f(0) − f′(0)/4`). What it did not have
was a **discretization**, and it said so: *"a discretization that enforces the origin condition.
And the repository currently has the opposite."*

The realization this leg adds comes from pushing Xu's operator through the Fourier transform.
Writing `φ(y) = ∫₀^∞ φ̂(ξ)e^{iyξ}dξ` (so `H₊² ≅ L²(0,∞)`), `d/dy → iξ` and `y → i d/dξ`, hence
`−1 − y d/dy → ξ d/dξ` — **the Mellin generator**, which is why Mellin appears in Xu at all — and
`i/b(y)` has transform `e^{−ξ/2}`, so multiplication by it becomes a Volterra convolution:

```
    L₀⁺ = ξ ∂_ξ + V ,   (Vg)(ξ) = ∫₀^ξ e^{−(ξ−η)/2} g(η) dη ,   ‖φ‖²_X ∝ ∫₀^∞ (1+ξ⁴)|φ̂|² dξ
```

In the **Laguerre basis** `ℓ_n(ξ) = L_n(ξ)e^{−ξ/2}` both pieces are exact and banded, by two
classical identities (`∫₀^x L_n = L_n − L_{n+1}`, and the `ξL_n` / `ξL_n′` recurrences — this leg
claims neither; see the novelty log). Combining them:

```
    L₀⁺ ℓ_n = −(n/2) ℓ_{n−1} + (1/2) ℓ_n + ((n−1)/2) ℓ_{n+1}
```

**Exactly tridiagonal, rational entries.** And Xu's two symmetry modes then occupy *exactly*
`span{ℓ₀, ℓ₁}`: `b⁻² ↔ −(ℓ₀−ℓ₁)` (eigenvalue 1) and `m = yb⁻² ↔ −(i/2)(ℓ₀+ℓ₁)` (eigenvalue 0).
Modulating out the symmetry modes is **literally truncating two coefficients**. The border row
becomes `ℓ_n = i(−1)ⁿ(1−2n)`.

### These are exact zeros, not tolerances

| identity | value |
|---|---|
| `L₀⁺ b⁻² − b⁻²` | **0.0** |
| `L₀⁺ m` | **0.0** |
| `ℓ · L₀⁺` (untruncated columns) — `ℓ` is the **left null vector** | **0.0** |
| `ℓ(m)` | **1** exactly |
| `ℓ(b⁻²)` | **0.0** |
| tridiagonal entries vs an independent Laguerre quadrature | 1.608e−13 |
| `ℓ` in Laguerre coords vs `i f(0) − f′(0)/4` in y-space | 4.6e−16 |

## Gate conjunct 1 — does it close? **YES on leg 163's own diagnostic**

Leg 163's scoped closing quantity is its gate G4, `‖u‖_X/‖f‖_X` — *"the `X`-realization analogue
of the quantity leg 127 drove to ZERO in `ℓ¹_w`"* — i.e. `σ_min` of the bordered operator in the
`X` metric.

**The shape of the section is the whole methodological point.** Truncating the **domain only**
(leaving the range untruncated) gives a quantity that is non-increasing in `N` and converges to
the infimum over the union of the subspaces — an **upper bound closing down onto** `σ_min`.
Truncating the range as well (a square Galerkin section) gives a number that is neither, and it
reports something different — see C7.

| `N` | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 | 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `σ_min` | 0.0927566 | 0.0911590 | 0.0909310 | 0.0908878 | 0.0908506 | 0.0908234 | **0.0908047** | *0.0909363* |

**`σ_min = 0.0908`, `‖R‖_X = 11.0127`**, monotone decreasing through `N = 512` and varying by
**0.139% over a 16-fold truncation range**. The `N = 1024` row *rises* instead of falling: that
is the float floor of an `X` Gram whose entries reach ~1e12, not a mathematical statement, and it
is reported as the reason the reliable window stops at 512. **This is float64 evidence of a
positive limit, not a proof of one** — a decreasing positive sequence is reported as what it is.

**The tail block closes too, and this is the direct `ℓ¹_w` comparison.** On modes `≥ 2`:
0.250374 / 0.249248 / 0.248673 / 0.248377 over `N = 64…512`, i.e. `‖T⁻¹‖_X = 4.026`,

## Two corrections to leg 163, both of which a construction leg is *for*

**1. Leg 163's `σ_min` witness was optimistic by 7.9×.** It inferred `σ_min ≥ 0.7147` from three
test data (ratios 1.3993 / 1.2680 / 1.3769). That is a **witness, not a bound**. The measured
value is **0.0908**. Reproducing G4's own quantity from the closed form here gives
0.868155 (converged over `M = 1024…8192`, spread 6e−6) and 0.868 / 0.840 / 0.786 / 0.690 over
four data — all near 0.87 while the operator norm is 11.0. **Random low-mode data does not find
the worst direction**, and three data are not a battery.

**2. The naive Galerkin section is inconsistent, and the mechanism is exact.** `κ` must be 0
(`ℓ(f) = 0` was imposed and `ℓ·L₀⁺ = 0` exactly), yet the square truncation returns

| `N` | 60 | 120 | 240 | 480 | 960 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `|κ|` | 5.8489 | 5.8735 | 5.8857 | 5.8919 | **5.8950** |
| `N²|u_{N−1}|` | — | — | — | 5.9227 | 5.9103 |

The truncated `ℓ` row misses exactly the term `ℓ_N·((N−2)/2)·u_{N−1}`, and `N²|u_{N−1}|` is
**constant**, so the defect does not vanish. Underneath it is a structural fact worth banking on
its own: **the exact solution's Laguerre coefficients decay only like `C/n`** (`n|c_n|` =
5.9615 → 5.1162 over `n = 32…1024`), because the resolvent maps analytic data to `u ~ log(y)/y`
— a **log singularity at the Mellin origin `ξ = 0`**. No basis smooth at `ξ = 0` converges
geometrically on this operator. That is why C1 uses a range-untruncated section.

## A floating-point defect that was real, was caught, and is kept (lessons 76, 86)

Two, in fact, both in the same family leg 163 flagged.

1. **`G₂ = G − G(0) − G′(0)t` by literal subtraction.** `G₂` is `O(t²)` while `G(0)` is `O(1)`
   and Xu's integrand multiplies the difference by `s^{−2}`. Built here from **exact Taylor
   coefficients by algebra** — `a = −(i/2)(1+2it)`, `b = (i/2)(1−2it)`, integer convolution —
   so nothing is ever subtracted. An FFT recovery of those coefficients was tried first and is
   **not good enough**: `G`'s radius of convergence is ½, so the `ρ^{−m}` amplification destroys
   them beyond `m ≈ 26` (measured: exact to 1e−16 at `t = 0.2`, 0.178 error at `t = 0.45`).
2. **Grid-dependent quadrature, caught by the test suite and not by the runner.** The first
   cumulative integrator put one Gauss panel across each caller-supplied interval, so the
   residual depended on how finely the *caller* happened to sample: **2.533e−06** on the test's
   sparse grid against 3e−15 on the runner's dense one. The tell was that two grids disagreed by
   nine orders on the same identity. Fixed with geometric sub-panelling (`_PAN_RATIO = 1.6`);
   now sparse / dense / single-far-point give 2.566e−15 / 4.767e−15 / 1.137e−15. **The accuracy
   is a property of the operator again, not of the caller.**
