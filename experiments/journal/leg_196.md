## 2. The certificate is not there, and that is a measurement

The runner's discriminators, all computed from a term census over the full extraction:

* **E1 — apparatus.** `interval arithmetic` **0**, `enclosure` **0**, `validated numerics`
  **0**, `certif*` **0**. Also `eigen*` **0**.
* **E2 — claim.** Every one of the **4** occurrences of `computer-assisted` is classified
  individually and exhaustively: `DEFINITION` (§1 l.49, defines the accuracy target),
  `PREREQUISITE` (abstract l.41; Conclusion l.1438), `ASPIRATION` (§3 Gross–Pitaevskii l.907).
  **`ACHIEVED`: 0.**
* **E3 — located.** Yes, so the third branch (`no later work locatable`) does not apply.

The paper's own two self-descriptions decide it:

> "…providing an important ingredient for bridging the gap between numerical discovery and
> computer-assisted proofs…" (Abstract)

> "Beyond numerical discovery, high accuracy is a **prerequisite** for rigorous mathematical
> verification via computer-assisted proofs." (§5 Conclusion — the paper's last word)

**An ingredient is not the bridge; a prerequisite is not the proof.** The runner requires
apparatus **and** claim together, and its self-tests show it returns `CERTIFICATE_ACHIEVED` when
both are present, `STILL_SHORT` when either is missing alone, and `NOT_LOCATABLE` on an empty
search — five cases, all passing, so this NO is a control that could have come out otherwise
(lesson 90).

## 3. The new obstructions

**N1 — the binding quantity moved from the residual to λ's error bar.** The residual is now at
double-float round-off. λ is not. Fig. 7(f), the authors' own table for IPM:

| mode | λₛ | accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| stable | 1.0285722760323 | 1e-13 |
| 1st unstable | 0.472129736156 | 2e-12 |
| 2nd unstable | 0.3149617817 | 3e-10 |
| 3rd unstable | 0.24156641 | 1e-8 |
| **4th unstable** | **0.1987237** | **1e-7** |

A self-similar blow-up certificate must **enclose λ**. At IPM's 4th unstable the equation is
satisfied to `1e-13` while the blow-up rate is pinned only to `1e-7` — a **6.0-decade** gap
between "the residual is at round-off" and "the rate is enclosed". Fitted degradation
**1.570 decades/mode** over the five rows, against the **2.0 decades/mode** the paper states in
a different sentence (§2.3.3 l.696, *"decreases the achievable lambda accuracy for a fixed
computational budget by approximately two orders of magnitude per model"*) — two independent
sources, agreeing in order of magnitude, so it is a real cross-check. They are not identical
quantities (fitted = best achieved; stated = at fixed budget), and the runner says so.

**This is not a restatement of leg 175's ladder.** Leg 175 measured that ladder in **residual**
margin against the CAP threshold. The follow-up paid that margin off — and what the ladder now
degrades is the **enclosure width of the parameter a CAP would have to enclose.**

**N2 — the discovery frontier itself stalled.** §2.1.3: CCF's **third** unstable solution was
searched for across λ ∈ [0.455, 0.4713] and **not found** — *"a persistent non-smooth signal at
the origin remains for all tested λ"*, and *"if a third unstable solution exists, it is located
at a λ value corresponding to an even higher-gradient profile."* The authors put the remaining
search **below** λ = 0.455 and rate it *"even greater than finding highly unstable solutions in
the 2D IPM equation."* So *"the full suite of previously discovered CCF solutions"* is a suite
of **three**, and the 1D model is now rated harder than the 2D one.

**N3 — the remaining margin is hardware- and arithmetic-bound.** The floor is **double-float
round-off**, by the pipeline's own definition of success (§1 l.49). Every remedy offered for λ
accuracy is budget, not method: larger networks, a third stage, *"more training stages in
parallel on multiple GPUs"*; the reported results fit *"a single A100 GPU (80GB)"*. A
certificate generally needs arithmetic **tighter** than the object it encloses, and nothing in
the paper addresses what an interval or extended-precision re-derivation would cost.

**N4 — third party, recorded honestly as such.** Of the **22** papers citing `2509.14185`
(Semantic Scholar, this pass), one is `arXiv:2606.25151` (McShannon–Dietrich, Jun 2026): PINNs
perturbed in their PDE parameters **reach loss at or below the clean baseline while differing
from the true solution by large margins**. Its systems are Burgers, an NS cavity and
convection–diffusion — **not** the singularity problems — and it claims no error in either
paper. It is cited for the general point, which is exactly what a CAP is for: **a small
residual is evidence; an enclosure is a proof.**
