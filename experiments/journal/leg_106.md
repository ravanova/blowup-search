# Leg 106 — Route-HPA: adversarial audit of `hilbert_pointwise.py`'s bound direction

**Branch** `leg/hpa-v1`. **Agent** LEG-G. **Date** 2026-08-06.
**Gate (verbatim).** *Under an adversarial battery of degenerate or NaN-poisoned inputs,
does `solver/hilbert_pointwise.py`'s bound ever fail to dominate the true |H(h)| value on a
case where both are computable?*

## GATE ANSWER: **YES.** Escalated, not patched.

`solver/hilbert_pointwise.py` is untouched by this leg, under the gate's own instruction.
Two configurations produce a finite, plausible-looking `(a_sup, a_semi)` that an explicit,
computable `h` exceeds.

| | configuration | returned pair at θ=1.0 | first violation | worst measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | `rho = NaN` (bit-identically `+inf`, `1e300`) | `(24.092158, 0.0)` | step width `1e-20`, ratio **1.0269** | **2.5276** at width `1e-50` |
| **V2** | `gamma = 0` — **no NaN anywhere** | `(0.0, 12.229355)` | step width `1e-10`, ratio **1.0377** | **4.9794** at width `1e-50` |

Neither raises, neither warns. Six of seven degenerate arguments return a finite pair in
complete silence; only `gamma = +inf` emits a `RuntimeWarning`, and it returns anyway.

## The mechanism, in one line of the module's own construction

At offset `s = |phi - theta|` the increment is charged to the seminorm account
(`cT = s^gamma cos^{alpha-gamma}`) or the sup account (`cS = cos^alpha(phi/2) +
cos^alpha(theta/2)`) by `take_T = rho*cT <= cS`. As `s -> 0` the kernel weight `|K|*s -> 2`
while `cS -> 2 cos^alpha(theta/2) != 0`, so **the sup account's integrand in `d(log s)`
tends to a nonzero constant and the sup-route integral is logarithmically divergent.** Only
`cT -> 0` tames the singularity, which needs `gamma > 0` *and* needs the rule to select it.

* **V1** is IEEE-754 §5.11: every comparison against NaN is False, so `rho*cT <= cS` is
  False *everywhere* and the singularity is charged to the divergent account. `rho = +inf`
  and `rho >= 1e300` take the identical branch — a caller writing "always charge the sup
  account" gets an unsound bound, and the docstring's claim that *"any FIXED rule for
  choosing gives a valid linear bound"* is false exactly there.
* **V2** needs no NaN: at `gamma = 0` the `cT` route no longer vanishes either.

**The number returned is finite only because the quadrature starts at `s = eps*L`.** It is
the ε-truncation of a divergent integral, not a bound, and it moves accordingly:

| eps | 1e-6 | 1e-10 | 1e-14 | 1e-20 | 1e-30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a_sup` at `rho=NaN` | 14.45 | 24.09 | 33.73 | 48.19 | **72.30** |

Growing without limit. Meanwhile the adversary's `|H(h)|` diverges at a measured
**0.5234 per ln(1/δ)** against the analytic prediction `(2/π)cos^α(θ/2) = 0.5234` — a 0.0%
discrepancy, so the divergence is the real thing and not quadrature noise. For any fixed
`eps`, a thin enough step breaks the bound; there is no threshold that rescues it.

## The adversary, and why its |H(h)| is trustworthy

The extremiser is forced by the kernel's sign structure — `sign K_θ(φ) = sign(θ-φ)` — so the
increment must be positive below θ and negative above, at the largest size the sup account
allows: a **step of width δ inside the decay envelope**,
`h(φ) = cos^α(φ/2)·clip(-(φ-θ)/δ, -1, 1)`, with `S = 1` exactly. Everything is parameterised
by the *exact signed offset* `u = φ - θ` — the module's own offset trick — which is what makes
`δ = 1e-50` meaningful rather than cancellation noise. Gate A1 known-answer-checks the whole
quadrature against `solver.hilbert_holder.conjugate` to **2.1e-09** over 18 cases (and the
p.v. of the constant function to exactly 0.0) *before* any verdict is read from it.

## Blast radius: the shipped configuration is NOT affected

α=1.5, γ=0.5, ρ∈{1,6,25}, hunted with the same adversary over 10 angles × 3 ρ:

| | worst true/bound |
|---|---|
| against the exact bound | **0.999950** |
| through `measured_pointwise`'s interpolated path | **0.999789** |

Domination holds everywhere. But the margin is **2.11e-04** and closing: the ratio rises
monotonically toward the far field — 0.62 at θ=0.5, 0.82 at 3.0, 0.94 at π−1e-2, 0.9999 at
π−1e-8. **The bound is asymptotically saturated as θ→π, not violated.** That is the sharpest
*positive* result here: v9's headline of "nearly attained, ratio ~0.98" understates it, and
it means the far field has no headroom to absorb any further approximation.

Which matters, because three approximations are already eating it, all in the unsafe direction:

| approximation | magnitude |
|---|---|
| default `n_quad=400` vs its own majorant, at small θ | **−2.68%** (understates) |
| `measured_pointwise`'s linear interpolation in log X, ρ=1 | returns **0.9233** of the exact coefficient |
| `weighted_sups` sup over 140 θ-nodes vs 2240 | understates by **0.43%** / **1.99%** |

None is large enough to break the shipped configuration — the far-field margin survives
because the interpolation deficit happens to be small exactly where the ratio is near 1 —
but that is luck, not design, and it is now an executable gate rather than a memory.

## What is NOT a finding (pre-declared before construction)

A smoothed-subgradient search over the polyhedral norm ball produces an `h` with
**grid-norm ratio 1.0321 > 1** — but its *true* norms are **8.0×** (sup) and **4.8×**
(seminorm) larger than the grid pairs can see, and its refined-norm ratio is **0.1437**.
This is JOURNAL §B1's grid-seminorm lesson re-measured on this module, exactly as the
novelty pass pre-committed to reporting it: a caller who feeds grid-evaluated `S` and `T`
into the bound is not using the norms the theorem is stated in. It is a **usage hazard, not
a bound-direction defect**, and it is banked as gate 8 with the extremiser frozen in the JSON
so the replay is deterministic.

## Disposition

**ESCALATE. Do not patch under this leg's authority.** The repair, when authorised, is a
*guard* — reject non-finite `rho` and `gamma <= 0` — **not** a change to the majorant, which
is correct wherever the payer rule selects the seminorm account on a neighbourhood of the
singularity. The soundness predicate is exact and analytic: **`gamma > 0` and `rho`
finite-and-not-NaN**. Note for whoever implements it: the natural runtime *diagnostic* (does
the pair move with `eps`?) separates the regimes only for `gamma >= 0.1`; at `gamma = 0.01` a
formally sound configuration still drifts 2.00× over 8 decades of `eps`, because `eps^gamma`
converges too slowly to tell them apart. Guard on the predicate, not on the symptom.

Gates 2, 3, 4 and 9 of `test_hilbert_pointwise_adversarial.py` are **GAP-PINS**: they assert
the current unsound behaviour so it cannot decay at the rate of memory, and **they must be
INVERTED, not weakened, the day a guard lands** (leg 84's precedent, its own words).
