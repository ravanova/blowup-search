## 2. The full-text read — and it changes the object

**Theorem 1.1 (p.3), verbatim:**

> "Consider (1.1) with `L ω = -∂_xx ω`. There exists `δ > 0` such that for
> `a ∈ (1/2 - δ, 1/2 + δ)`, `0 ≤ ν ≤ 1`, (2.1) develops a self-similar singularity in
> finite time for some `C_c^∞` initial data."

So the blow-up at γ=2 is real. But **the profile Chen supplies at γ=2 is the INVISCID
profile.** Section 2, p.4, opens *"Firstly, we study the inviscid problem, i.e. ν = 0"*, and
gives, as eq (2.2):

> `Ω = -2bx/(x²+b²)²`, `U_x = (b²-x²)/(b²+x²)²`, `U = x/(b²+x²)`, `c_l = 1/3`,
> `c_ω = -1`, where `b = √(3/8)`

and immediately after, the sentence that decides this leg:

> "In this self-similar blowup, the spatial blowup scaling is `c_l = 1/3`, if we add the
> diffusion term, such term is asymptotically small compared to the nonlinear term in the
> equation of the self-similar variables. In our later analysis, we will treat the diffusion
> term as a small perturbation to the nonlinear part, especially the vortex stretch term
> `u_x ω`."

**Everywhere `ν` appears in Chen's argument it appears as the time-dependent `ν(t)`** — in
the approximate steady state (2.8), in the error term (2.10) `F(ω̄,t) = … + ν(t)ω̄_xx`, and in
the normalisation (2.11) — **and `ν(t) → 0`.** The two that fix the steady object are on p.5:
`ν(t) = exp(∫(c_ω + 2c_l))·…·ν` with `c_ω + 2c_l = -1 + 2/3 = -1/3 < 0`, so `ν(t) → 0`
exponentially; and `c̄_ω(t) = -1 - ν(t)·ω̄_xxx(0)/ω̄_x(0)`, whose correction vanishes with it
(its constant is exactly `-12/b² = -32`).
**There is no ν-dependent steady profile in the paper to certify.** The approximate steady
state (2.8) *is* the inviscid profile (2.2).

### The γ tension, resolved from the located text

Three findings, one of them a correction to this repository:

1. **The criticality formula is `γ = |a| - 1`, not `γ = |a|^{-1}`** (p.2, §1.2). The
   `|a|^{-1}` form carried in from a prior verifier's leg-64 review is a **transcription
   slip**, corrected here against the source.
2. It applies **only for `a ≤ -1`**, where the `L^{|a|}` a-priori estimate exists. At
   `a = 1/2` it does not apply at all; there criticality is `γ = 1`, from `L¹` conservation.
3. **No contradiction, and the reason is the scaling, not criticality.** `γ = 2` at `a = 1/2`
   is *stronger* than critical, yet blow-up happens, because Chen's `c_l = 1/3` is below the
   diffusion-balanced `c_l = 1/γ = 1/2`. Theorem 1.3's global well-posedness covers Classes 1
   and 2; Chen's blow-up data is Class 3, and Remark 1.4 says so — *"(1.4) is sharp due to
   the blowup result in Theorem 1.1."*

**`δ` is UNQUANTIFIED in the source.** Theorem 1.1 asserts "There exists `δ > 0`" and no
numerical value appears anywhere in the text. That is now known-absent, not merely unread.

**Corroboration, unplanned.** Lushnikov–Silantyev–Siegel arXiv:2207.07548 (fetched and read)
list their exact real-line solutions as `a=1/2, σ=1`; `a=0, σ=1`; `a=0, σ=0`; plus Schochet's
`a=0, σ=2`. **No `a=1/2, σ=2` exact solution.** Their `a=1/2, σ=1` solution (their eq (55)) is
explicitly *"an exact solution of the inviscid problem ν = 0"*, with the same `α=1/3, β=1`
double-pole shape. Two independent sources, same conclusion: at `a = 1/2` the profile is the
inviscid one whatever the dissipation. Their §2 also notes `ν` is a pure gauge — *"By
rescaling each of t and ω, we can eliminate ν from the problem"* — so a `ν` sweep would be
vacuous even if it were legal.
