# Leg 289 — ROUTE-D1X: is D1's exact law a usable predictive shortcut?

**Gate: YES.** D1's law (`D1(κ) = C·κ^{-1/2}`, leg 288) predicts a held-out banked `κ` point
to `0.0` relative error — exactly leg 281's own reported precision floor
(`fit_max_relative_residual = 1.11e-16`). Bank it as a validated predictive shortcut, with one
load-bearing scope limitation attached (§3): it is `N`-fixed, not `N`-free.

## 1. Which held-out points exist, and which don't

Checked before running anything (novelty pass §1): **leg 249 has no `D1` data at any `κ`** —
its sweep is over the *border weight* for `σ_min` (the `A1` quantity), not the dual norm. So
the only sources of held-out `D1(κ)` points are leg 281's own `N=512` sweep (11 points,
`κ ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁴]`, of which only `κ=1` pins the law's coefficient — see leg 281's own
`"pinned_by": "kappa = 1"` annotation, and its analytic mechanism statement that the `−1/2`
exponent is not fit at all, only the multiplicative constant is) and leg 288's `N=256` probe
(4 points, `κ ∈ [10², 10⁸]`, of which `κ=100` pins a *second*, independent coefficient at that
different discretization).

## 2. P1 — same-N holdout confirmed, both parts of the prediction correct

Anchoring the law's coefficient at leg 281's `κ=1` point (`C = 0.8873620215869099`, an
independently certificate-measured number, not curve-fit) and predicting the other 10 sweep
points:

- **Headline held-out point: `κ=10⁴`.** Measured `D1 = 0.008873620215869099`; predicted
  `0.008873620215869099`. **Relative error: `0.0`** (exact to float64's own resolution — the
  measured and predicted values are bit-identical).
- Max relative error across **all 10** held-out `N=512` points: `6.53e-11`. The two non-zero
  entries (`κ=π`, `κ=2π`, at `6.53e-11` and `1.43e-11`) are an artifact predicted in advance:
  those `κ` keys are stored in the JSON truncated to 10 significant figures, not full-precision
  `π`/`2π`, which sets a visible `~1e-10` floor unrelated to the law's actual accuracy — exactly
  the falsification threshold (`1e-6`) the novelty pass set aside for this. **P1 not falsified.**

## 3. P2 — cross-N check: the law is N-fixed, not N-free (this is the leverage boundary)

- **P2a (same-`N` holdout, leg 288's probe).** Anchoring a *second, independent* coefficient at
  `κ=100` from leg 288's `N=256` probe (`C₂₅₆ = 0.8873451208021278`) and predicting `κ ∈ {10⁴,
  10⁶, 10⁸}`: max relative error `1.53e-16` — machine precision, at a **6-decade** extrapolation
  from the anchor. **P2a not falsified.**
- **P2b (cross-`N` transfer — the boundary case).** Using the `N=512`-anchored coefficient to
  predict the `N=256` probe points directly, with **no `N=256` reference point at all**: max
  relative error `1.90e-05` — three orders of magnitude the wrong side of the P1/P2a
  machine-precision floor, and this equals (to 10 digits) the coefficients' own relative
  difference between `N=256` and `N=512` (`1.905e-5` both ways). **Predicted outcome confirmed
  exactly**: the `−1/2` homogeneity in `κ` is exact *at fixed `N`*; the multiplicative constant
  is itself a finite-`N` discretization artifact and does **not** transfer across `N` for free.

## 4. What this licenses, precisely (the flag, not an application)

**Flag, do not apply**, per the task brief: a future leg that already holds one certificate-
measured `D1(κ₀, N)` at some discretization `N` can get `D1(κ, N)` at **any other `κ`, same
`N`**, to machine precision, with **zero fresh solve** — verified here across 4 decades (P1) and
6 decades (P2a). It **cannot** be used to skip a certificate compute at a **new `N`** — the
coefficient must be re-anchored once per `N` (one point), after which the whole `κ`-sweep at
that `N` is free. This is a real, bounded shortcut, not a license to treat `D1` as
convention-free or `N`-free — the "exact law" qualifier attaches to the `κ`-exponent only.
