## §0 — GATE ANSWER, in its pre-committed wording

> **Gate.** Does a full-text read yield a definite answer — (i) the theorem's hypotheses DO
> reach the screened object (the theorem bears on the packet), or (ii) they do NOT (the λ

## **YES — and the definite answer is (ii). PINEAU–VICOL DOES NOT REACH THE SCREENED OBJECT.**

**The deciding clause, verbatim** (Theorem "Main result for DSS solutions", `Liouvillev2.tex`
l.296–299, the α = 0 theorem — this and not the RDSS theorem is the one that bears on the
screened object; see §5 for why the dispatch's pointer needed correcting):

> *"There exists $\underline{\lambda} = \underline{\lambda} (C_{U,0}) >1$, such that if
> $1 < \lambda < \underline{\lambda}$, then $U\equiv 0$."*

The screened object is specified with **λ significantly larger than 1** — leg 251's clause, in
leg 313's own words on `leg/313-sdss-v1` (§4 below). The hypothesis is a **λ-near-1** clause and
the object is defined by its negation. The theorem is **silent** on
`NS3D-DSS-NONAXI-LAMBDA-LARGE`.

**Three magnitudes attached to "near 1", from the paper's own text, so this is not a word
game:**

| # | what | value | locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | the authors' own WLOG ceiling on λ̲ | **λ̲ ≤ e^{1/2} = 1.6487212707001282**, equivalently **S = 2 log λ ≤ 1** | l.1382 |
| M2 | the smallness the proof actually needs | *"(1+α²) S < 2 log λ̲ ≪ 1"* — the paper's own `≪` | l.1607–1611, eq. `eq:moral:of:the:story` |
| M3 | how λ̲ is finally chosen | *"choose $\underline{\lambda}=\underline{\lambda}(C_{U,0})$ sufficiently close to $1$"* | l.1715 |

M1 verbatim (l.1382): *"Without loss of generality, we let $\underline{\alpha} \leq 1$ and
$\underline{\lambda} \leq e^{\frac 12}$, so that $|\alpha| \leq 1$ and $S \leq 1$."* So the
theorem's entire λ-reach is **contained in the interval (1, 1.6487…)** — a hard, quotable
ceiling — and inside that interval it is further cut down to a non-explicit λ̲(C_{U,0}) that
the paper itself calls "sufficiently close to 1".

**The gate's own "and/or" needed splitting, and one half of it is false** — see §5, GAP-330-C.
The **Type I clause does NOT exclude the object**: the object *satisfies* it. Only the λ clause
decides. That distinction is recorded because a packet that says "Type I excludes it" would be
wrong.

**Branch taken:** yes-(ii). Per the dispatch, branch (i) — and only branch (i) — required an
immediate out-of-turn flag to the orchestrator. **The answer is (ii), so no immediate flag is
raised**, and this leg does not describe its own finding as movement on the L1→L4 chain,
because nothing moved: see §9.

## §3 — The four theorems in the paper, and which one can possibly bear

The paper's own hierarchy, l.331–334, verbatim:

> *"$\text{SS}\subseteq \text{RSS}\subseteq \text{DSS}\subseteq \text{RDSS}$, and hence
> Liouville-type theorems should be hardest to establish in the setting of rotated discretely
> self-similar solutions."*

| # | theorem | class | hypothesis on the free parameters | locator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Main result for **RSS** | rotated SS, time-**independent** profile | `either \|α\| < α̲ or \|α\| > ᾱ`, with `0 < α̲ ≪ 1`, `1 ≪ ᾱ < ∞` | l.229–232 |
| **T2** | **Main result for DSS** | **backward globally DSS, α = 0** | **`1 < λ < λ̲(C_{U,0})`** | **l.296–299** |
| T3 | Main result for **RDSS** | rotated DSS | `(\|α\| ≤ α̲ and 1 < λ < λ̲)` **or** `(\|α\| ≥ ᾱ and 1 < λ < λ̲^{1/(1+α²)})` | l.349–352 |
| T4 | Approximately SS Type I solutions are regular | **no** self-similarity assumed, local | Type I in `B_1`, bounded pressure on an annulus, and `‖∂_sU(·,s̄)‖_{L^∞(B_{e^{s̄/2}})} ≤ δ_0` at **one** time | l.379–400 |

**The screened object is a DSS object with α = 0**, so **T2 is the theorem that bears**, and T3
is its α ≠ 0 generalisation. T1 bears only on the strict sub-locus described in §7. T4 is
adjudicated in §6.

**T2 verbatim, in full** (l.296–299):

> *"Let $u$ be a solution of the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on
> $\RR^3 \times [-1,0)$, which satisfies the Type~I upper bound~(1.7) for some $C_{U,0}>0$.
> Assume that there exists $\lambda >1$ and $U\in C^2(\RR^3 \times [0,S])$ with
> $S= 2 \log(\lambda)$, such that $u$ is a backwards globally discretely self-similar solution
> of~(1.1) with profile $U$ (the DSS ansatz~(1.12a) holds). There exists
> $\underline{\lambda} = \underline{\lambda} (C_{U,0}) >1$, such that if
> $1 < \lambda < \underline{\lambda}$, then $U\equiv 0$."*

**T3 verbatim, in full** (l.349–352), because the dispatch pointed at this one:

> *"… There exist $\underline{\alpha} = \underline{\alpha}(C_{U,0})>0$ and
> $\underline{\lambda} = \underline{\lambda} (C_{U,0}) >1$, such that if
> $|\alpha|\leq \underline{\alpha}$ and $1 < \lambda < \underline{\lambda}$, then $U\equiv 0$.
> Moreover, there exist $\overline{\alpha} = \overline{\alpha}(C_{U,0})>0$ and
> $\underline{\lambda} = \underline{\lambda} (C_{U,0}) >1$, such that if
> $|\alpha|\geq \overline{\alpha}$ and $1< \lambda< \underline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha^2}}$,
> then $U\equiv 0$."*

**Both T3 clauses are strictly narrower in λ than T2**, and the second is narrower still:
`λ̲^{1/(1+α²)} < λ̲` for every α ≠ 0, and → 1 as `|α| → ∞`. So no route through T3 reaches a
λ that T2 does not already reach. **There is no clause anywhere in the paper whose λ-window is
not a subset of (1, λ̲).** Both DSS/RDSS section headings say so in their own titles: *"DSS and
RDSS: proof of the main result for α small **and λ close to 1**"* (l.1378) and *"… for α large
**and λ close to 1**"* (l.1602).

**Whose theorem T2 is.** PV state, l.293 and l.295, that T2 is a restatement of Chae–Wolf:

> *"Not much seems to be known about the existence of solutions to (1.13), other than the
> following Liouville-type theorem of Chae and Wolf [ChaeWolf17], which concerns the case of
> discrete self-similarity factors $\lambda$ such that $0< \lambda - 1 \ll 1$."*
>
> *"In fact, a slight modification of the proof of Theorem~1.4 immediately recovers the main
> result in~[ChaeWolf17], which we re-state here for convenience."*

This is load-bearing for the packet in a way that goes beyond "does not bite": **T2 is the very
theorem the screened object was already screened against.** Leg 253's `λ_*` — carried into leg
260's specification as one of the three structural conditions — *is* Chae–Wolf's `λ_*`. So the
packet does not merely fail to gain an obstruction from PV; it **cannot** gain one from T2,
because T2 is already inside the screen that defined the object.

## §6 — v2's new local theorem (T4), adjudicated separately. It does not reach the object either — and it yields a screen.

T4 is the one part of v2 that no prior leg has read (§2: 0 occurrences in v1). It is *not* a
self-similarity theorem, so the λ argument above does not automatically dispose of it, and it
was checked on its own terms.

**T4's hypotheses, verbatim** (l.381–399), all three of them:

> *(i)* *"$|u(x,t)|\leq \frac{C_u }{\sqrt{-t}+|x|}, \qquad (x,t)\in B_1 \times [-1,0)$"*
> *(ii)* *"$|p(x,t)|\leq C_p,\qquad (x,t)\in A\times [-1,0), \qquad A:= \{\tfrac{1}{2}< |x|< \tfrac{3}{4}\}$"*
> *(iii)* *"There exist $\delta_0=\delta_0(C_u )\in(0,1]$ and $s_0=s_0(C_u ,C_p)\geq 1$ such that if, at a single time $\bar t\in(-e^{-s_0},0)$ we have the bound … $\leq \delta_0$, then $(0,0)$ is a regular point."*

and its self-similar restatement, which the authors say is the form they actually use
(l.412–419):

> *"$\|\partial_sU(\cdot,\bar s)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{e^{\bar s/2}})} \leq \delta_0$, for some
> $\bar s\geq s_0$. In the proof of Theorem~1.9 we work with the restatement (1.24) of
> assumption (1.23)."*

**Verdict: T4 does not reach the screened object either, because its hypothesis (iii) is not
implied by the object's specification** — a DSS profile is a genuinely time-periodic orbit and
nothing in the specification makes `∂_s U` small at any phase. (H2/H3 of §4 supply (i);
(ii) is discussed below.) **This leg does not claim T4 excludes anything.**

**But T4 does yield a necessary condition on the object, and it is new to this repository.**
This is **this leg's own inference from PV's statement, not a statement PV make**, and is
labelled as such:

> **Derived screen (leg 330's inference).** Let `U` be a nontrivial profile of the screened
> object, so `(0,0)` is a singular point. `U` is `S`-periodic in `s` with `S = 2 log λ`, and
> `B_{e^{s̄/2}} ↑ R³` as `s̄ → ∞`. Applying T4's contrapositive at `s̄ = σ + kS` for every
> `k ∈ N` and every phase `σ ∈ [0,S]` gives
> **`‖∂_s U(·,σ)‖_{L^∞(R³)} ≥ δ_0(C_{U,0})` for EVERY phase `σ ∈ [0,S]`** —
> the object must be uniformly non-self-similar, at every point of its orbit, not merely on
> average.

**Two conditions on that inference, both stated rather than buried:**

1. **The pressure hypothesis (ii) is NOT verified for a globally DSS solution from the paper's
   own decay estimates.** PV's pressure bound is `|∇^k_y P| ≤ C_{P,k}/(1+|y|)^{2-σ+k}` with
   *"$C_{P,k}$ depends on $C_{U,0}$, $k$, and $\sigma >0$"* (l.1414–1420), i.e. with an
   arbitrarily small but **strictly positive** loss σ. For a DSS solution
   `p(x,t) = (-t)^{-1}P(y,s)`, that gives `|p| ≲ C_{P,0}(-t)^{-σ/2}|x|^{σ-2}` on the annulus
   `1/2<|x|<3/4`, which **diverges as `t → 0⁻` for every σ > 0** and is bounded only at the
   unattained value σ = 0. So the derived screen is **conditional** on closing that σ-loss.
   Recorded, not laundered.
2. **`δ_0 = δ_0(C_u)` is non-explicit**, so the screen cannot be evaluated numerically as it
   stands — the same wall leg 260 recorded for `λ_*`, `λ̄`, `ᾱ`: *"there is no number to
   clear."*

**Why it is worth recording anyway.** It is a *falsifiable structural check on a future
numerical candidate*: a constructed DSS profile that turns out to have a nearly-stationary
phase is, conditionally on (1), trivial. That is a test the Phase-1/Phase-2 construction can
carry, and it did not exist in this repository before v2 was read.

## §7 — The one corner where PV's theorems DO touch the object's class, with its magnitude

Stated because a clause-by-clause adjudication that reports only "no" and never looks for the
"yes" is not an adjudication. **This corner does not change the gate answer** and is not a
reach on the object *as specified*.

PV's Remark 1.5 (l.272–279), verbatim:

> *"rotated self-similar (RSS) solutions … for a given $\alpha \in \RR$ are automatically
> discretely self-similar ($\lambda$-DSS) for any factor $\lambda> 1$ such that
> $2\alpha\log(\lambda)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$. … the discrete similarity factor $\lambda$ and the
> rotation strength $\alpha$ are related via $\lambda = e^{\frac{\pi}{|\alpha|}}$. Importantly,
> we note that **the regime $|\alpha|\ll 1$ corresponds to $\lambda\gg 1$**, while the regime
> $|\alpha| \to \infty$ corresponds to $\lambda \to 1^+$."*

So T1's **small-α** clause, read through this embedding, *is* a statement about **large λ** —
the only place in the paper where a large-λ DSS object is touched at all. Its magnitude,
computed from PV's own constant chain (α̲ defined at l.676, formula **unchanged from v1**, §2)
using leg 262's most-favourable evaluation `α̲ ≤ 6.22e−5` at `C_{U,0}=C_{U,1}=1`:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| threshold above which the RSS sub-locus is reached | `λ ≥ e^{π/α̲}`, i.e. **`λ ≥ e^{50507.9} = 10^{21935.3}`** |
| for comparison, T2's ceiling on the same axis | **λ < e^{1/2} = 1.6487** |
