## §1 The ban, and why this leg is outside it

The re-posed ban forbids the ℓ¹-Fourier/radii-polynomial machinery dead in three
realizations. **None is touched.** The norm here is the *Hilbert*
`‖u‖_{H²(µ)} = ‖Lu‖_{L²(µ)}`; the basis is the even half-Hermite family
`ψ̂_m = L_m^{(−1/2)}(x²/4)e^{−x²/4}/ζ_m`; the nonlinearity is tamed by the Sobolev
embedding `H²(µ) ↪ C⁰₀` plus a quadrature rule, not by an ℓ¹ Banach-algebra property;
and the tail is tamed by the genuine Poincaré inequality `L`'s spectral gap supplies on
the **unbounded** domain — no truncation, no compactification. Full table in
`writeup/novelty/leg_256.md` §1.

**This leg does not lift the ban and no Phase-1 construction runs under its authority.**
Leg 257 is the leg that argues distinctness; this one supplies reproduction evidence.

## §2 What the arithmetic is — stated before the numbers (discipline 75)

Breden–Chu prove Theorem 42 in **interval arithmetic over 16384-bit BigFloat** (their
`quadrature.jl` needs it to enclose the roots of a degree-4503 Laguerre polynomial by
the IVT). This environment has **numpy only — no scipy, no mpmath**.

**Everything here is float64. This reproduces their certificate's CONSTANTS, not their
proof.** Nothing below is a rigorous enclosure and nothing below may be cited as one.

The reproduction's own resolution is **measured, not assumed**, and reported before any
constant:

| gate | magnitude |
|---|---|
| `√5·π·∫ψ̂₁⁴ψ̂₁′² = 669/31250` (their own test) | rel err **1.287e−09** |
| `π·∫ψ̂₁³ψ̂₁′ = −29/324` (their own test) | rel err **8.605e−10** |
| `⟨ψ̂_a, ψ̂_b⟩ = δ_ab`, **all** `a,b ≤ 1500` | max defect **1.604e−09** |
| `Σᵢwᵢ = Γ(α+1)`, three rules | ≤ 4.5e−10 |

**Reproduction resolution: 1.60e−09 relative.** Every ratio below is read against it.

Two things were genuinely hard and are worth recording. (a) **Range**: at `n = 1500` the
quadrature nodes reach `s ≈ 1.8e4`, where the weight is `~e^{−1.8e4}` and the Laguerre
value `~1e1220` — neither is representable in float64, only their *product* is, so every
Vandermonde entry is assembled as `value · exp(running_log_scale + log_weight − log_ζ)`
with the recurrence carrying a per-node log scale renormalised every step. Breden–Chu
never meet this; BigFloat's exponent range makes the factors representable directly.
(b) **Nodes**: with no scipy there is no Gauss–Laguerre rule to call, so nodes come from
Sturm bisection on the Jacobi matrix (the Sturm count fixes each root's index, so a root
cannot be missed or doubled) followed by Newton on the scaled recurrence.

**Their own exact rational identity earned its keep during construction.** My first
change-of-variables Jacobian was `c^{−1}` where it should be `c^{−(α+1)}`; the
`669/31250` test failed by a factor of exactly `√5` and localised it in one step. The
orthonormality gate could not have seen it — `c = 1` for the two-product rule.

## §3 The approximate solution was found independently, and it lands on theirs

Their `ubar` is never used to produce a headline number. Eq. (54) was shot as an ODE,
`u'' + (x/2)u' + u/4 − u²u' = 0`, `u'(0) = 0`, bisecting `u(0)` on the sign change of
`u(9)` — the generic ODE solution decays only like `x^{−1/2}` and is not in `H²(µ)`,
the admissible one like `e^{−x²/4}`, so the branch is isolated by plain bisection. The
profile was projected onto the basis and Newton run up a continuation ladder
`n = 100 → 200 → 400 → 800 → 1500`.

| | |
|---|---|
| `u(0)` from shooting alone, before any spectral machinery | **1.16537736** |
| `ū(0)` implied by their released coefficients | **1.16537510** |

So the two approximate solutions are the same one, and ours is the better-converged

## §5 The two constants that differ are the two that could — and the ablation says which

`Y`, `Z₃`, `‖LA L^{−1}‖` and `ū` itself reproduce essentially exactly. `Z₁` and `Z₂` do

`‖∂_xψ̂_m‖_∞` are bounded** — and arXiv:2404.04054v2 contains more than one answer to

Read across: **no single family reproduces both published constants.** Their `Z₁` is
matched to `0.969` by Remark 41's sharp bound; their `Z₂` is matched to `0.948` by the
alternative that is *commented out* in the released notebook. The family the notebook
actually computes today gives neither.

The looseness is large and worth recording on its own: the released cell-16 bound
overshoots the measured `‖ψ̂_m‖_∞` by up to **136.3×** (its max over `m` is 12.357 where
the true max is `π^{−1/4} = 0.75113`, attained at `m = 0`), and cell 17 overshoots

paragraph threatens Theorem 42; it says the released artifact and the published

## §7 Two mechanisms the ablations could have exposed, and what they actually showed

* **"Our `ū` differs from theirs, and that is where the gap is."** *Killed.* Rerunning
  every bound on **their** `ū` returns `Y = 7.564094e−04`, `Z₁ = 8.518342e−02`,
  `Z₂ = 276.4989`, `Z₃ = 556.4780` — identical to ours to 9 significant figures. The
  gap is not in the solution, which is what sent §5 to look at the sup bounds.
* **"`Y` is set by how well Newton converged."** *False, and it matters.* `Y`'s finite
  part is **1.06e−15**; `Y` is **100% the discarded truncation tail**
  `‖(I−P_n)(ū²∂_xū)‖`. So `Y` is resolution-set, not iterate-set — the same shape of
  finding leg 61 reported when its `Y₀` turned out to be float-residual-set rather than
  resolution-set. The ladder confirms it: `Y ∝ n^{−0.75}` across `n = 100…1500`
  (`5.757e−03 → 7.564e−04`), and `Z₁ ∝ n^{−0.53}`. **The certificate does not close
  below `n ≈ 800`**, so Breden–Chu's `n = 1500` is not conservatism — the problem needs
  it, and a reproduction at a convenient smaller `n` would have reported a false NO.

**Poisoning.** Displacing the first 20 coefficients: at `1e−10` and `1e−6` the total `Y`
does not move (`1.00×`) while its finite part moves `3.87e+06×` and `4.85e+10×` — again
because `Y` is tail-dominated. At `1e−03` the total moves `74.4×` and the certificate
**stops closing**. So the defect bound does track the iterate; reporting only the total
`Y` would have hidden by how much.

## §8 The caveat to carry forward, and it is not a trophy

This gate localises the pipeline to **~1.6e−09 relative** (§2), which is far finer than
leg 61's `~0.5 decade` Kawahara gate — but the comparison against `Z₃` is limited by
**their print granularity**, not by our arithmetic: `556.478` is six digits, so `9.0e−07`
relative is all that pair can ever resolve. Our `Z₃` agrees to `7.4e−10`, i.e. it rounds
to their printed digits, and that is the strongest statement available. It must not be
cited as a `1e−10` agreement of two independently known numbers.

**And the load-bearing caveat: this is float64.** A YES here says the machinery is
*usable* by this repository, not that this repository can produce a rigorous enclosure in
this setting. Doing that needs interval arithmetic over a big-float type with an exponent
range this environment does not currently have — §2(a) is the concrete obstruction, and
it is a tooling gap, not a mathematical one.
