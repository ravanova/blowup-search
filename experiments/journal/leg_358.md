## 2. What the source papers actually say (see JSON for full citations/quotes)

**Lucas & Kerswell 2015 (1406.1820v2)**, the paper leg 353's Table IV target came from:
- DNS extended to T=5e6 ("50 times longer than each of the 3 runs analysed in [Chandler &
  Kerswell 2013]")
- Rthres=0.3 for recording near-recurrences; only guesses with R<0.25 and 0.5<T<60 were actually
  fed to Newton (5120 of 11934 total guesses)
- Newton-GMRES-hookstep, same algorithm as Chandler & Kerswell 2013, GPU-accelerated time-stepping
- Fig. 3 + text: **"the chances of converging a guess settle to about 10% for R >~ 0.2"** -- this
  is the SAME residual regime leg 353's 5 attempts sat in (R=0.177-0.263). Leg 353's 0/5 is NOT
  anomalous relative to the source's own reported success curve: P(0 successes in 5 trials at
  p=0.10) = 0.9^5 = 59%.
- 81 unique converged recurrent flows from 5120 attempted guesses (heavy duplication into the
  same few dozen distinct solutions)

**Chandler & Kerswell 2013 (1207.4682)**, origin of both the Re=60 search and the hookstep code:
- Table 1: DNS T=1e5 per run at Re=60 (3 runs, series A), 3e5 for the nonzero-shift series B run
- max Newton=75, max GMRES=500, max Hookstep=50 iterations (vs leg 353's max_newton=10,
  max_gmres=20, plain line search -- not hookstep at all)
- **Success rate SPLITS SHARPLY by orbit class at Re=60**: plain periodic orbits (s=m=0,
  series A) 62.7%/64.4%/74.4% across the 3 runs; genuine RPOs with nonzero shift (series B, run
  p) only **7/163 = 4.3%**. UPO37 (leg 353's primary target, s=0.375) is in this harder,
  4.3%-success class even in the source's own hands.
- "typically lead to run times of a couple of months for each of the Re=60, 80 and 100 runs" on a
  single pre-GPU CPU core -- these are not cheap runs even for the original authors.

**Viswanath 2007 (physics/0604062)**, the founding hookstep paper both above cite:
- Locally-constrained optimal hookstep = SVD-based trust-region step on the GMRES/Arnoldi
  Hessenberg system, radius adapted step-to-step (Dennis & Schnabel 1996 machinery)
- Load-bearing quote, directly confirming leg 353's globalization diagnosis independent of sample
  size: **"the Newton step by itself never leads to convergence. The widely used expedient of
  damping the Newton step is also ineffective."** Plain-Newton-with-line-search IS a form of step
  damping. The founding paper of this entire methodology states, in its own words, that this
  approach is expected to fail on this problem class -- this is not something leg 353 discovered
  by accident, it is a documented, decades-old fact about the algorithm class.

**Also found (not requested but relevant to residual risk)**: 1308.3356 reports the SAME
Newton-GMRES-hookstep code FAILING to converge at the two endpoints of a continuation branch
(Re=15.03, Re=26.68) -- a correctly implemented hookstep is a substantially better globalization,
not a convergence guarantee.

## 3. Costed retry spec (full numbers in JSON)

Two axes, each costed against leg 353's own measured rates:

**Globalization**: implement genuine locally-constrained optimal hookstep (SVD-trust-region on
the Krylov Hessenberg system). Self-contained numerical-methods task, unit-testable and
validatable against leg 353's own control case (the perturbed laminar fixed point, which
line-search-Newton already handles) before pointing at the hard targets. **1 leg, negligible
compute** (control-case validation only, no long DNS needed for this leg).

**DNS length**: leg 353 measured 352.3256s wall for T_total=2000 (200000 steps, dt=0.01) => 1.7616
ms/step, pure numpy CPU, N=24. Linear extrapolation:
- T=1e5 (Chandler & Kerswell's own shortest sourced length, Re=60): ~4.9 hours
- T=5e6 (Lucas & Kerswell's full scale): ~10.2 days

No GPU path exists in this leg's solver stack; the literature itself needed GPU acceleration
(1406.1820) or CPU-months (1207.4682) to reach these lengths at their own resolution.

**Attempt count**: at the source's own 4.3%-10% per-attempt RPO success rate, 5 attempts have a
59-77% chance of zero successes REGARDLESS of whether DNS/globalization are fixed. Need ~50-100
independent attempts (matching the order of magnitude the source papers themselves used per Re)
for a null result to be informative. At leg 353's measured per-attempt cost (90-380s, avg ~196s)
scaled 3-5x for the source's own higher iteration caps (max_newton=75 vs 10, max_gmres=500 vs 20):
~600-1000s/attempt, ~17-28 hours for 100 attempts.

**Totals**:
- **Minimum-viable spec-compliant retry** (T=1e5, genuine hookstep, ~100 attempts, SAME N=24
  resolution as leg 353 -- resolution was never one of leg 353's declared gaps): **2 legs, ~22-33
  hours (roughly 1-1.5 days) wall-clock**, single CPU core, current solver as-is.
- **Full literature-scale retry** (T=5e6, matching resolution unclear): DNS alone is ~10.2 days --
  NOT a single-leg-session budget, a multi-week background compute commitment or a GPU port,
  neither of which is priced here beyond flagging it.

**Residual risk, named** (4 items, full text in JSON):
1. Sample-size risk -- 4-10% success rate means even a spec-compliant retry at modest N can come
   up empty by chance.
2. Hookstep is not universally convergent even in the source's own hands (branch-endpoint
   failures in 1308.3356).
3. Resolution gap not costed here -- leg 353's N=24 is coarser than what 1406.1820's text implies
   for their "full" recurrence check (Nx=Ny=128); this was never one of leg 353's two declared
   simplifications, so it's out of THIS retry spec's scope, but flagged as a plausible third gap.
4. GPU dependency -- full literature-scale retry is not affordable on this leg's CPU-only pure-numpy
   solver within normal leg-session budgets; only the minimum-viable version is.
