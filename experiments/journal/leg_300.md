## 3. The failing magnitude, named

Leg 266 quotes the target window as `6.855×` wider than BCG's dominance window. Evaluated
from BCG's own `(eq:rstar)` and `(eq:r:restriction)` at `γ = 7/5` in 60-digit arithmetic:

```
width ratio = (1/6) / (r* − 7/6) = 2/(7 − 3√5) = (7 + 3√5)/2 = 6.8541019662496845446…
```

which is **`6.854`** at the four significant figures leg 266 quoted. Leg 266 wrote
**`6.855`**. Absolute error `0.00090`; relative error `1.310e-04` (**0.0131%**).

The other four magnitudes reproduce exactly at their quoted 7 d.p.: `1.1666667`,
`1.1909830`, `0.0243163`, `0.1666667`. Two closed forms fell out that the repository had
never written down: `r* − 7/6 = (7 − 3√5)/12` and the ratio `(7 + 3√5)/2`.

**Defect localised.** Re-dividing leg 266's *own* rounded endpoints gives `6.8541143…` →
`6.854`, the same 4-s.f. value the exact derivation gives. So the formula and the inputs
are sound and only the transcribed digit is wrong: **a slipped digit, not a misread of
the papers.** Clause (a)'s reading and clause (b)'s untouchedness are unaffected, and
nothing downstream (the re-posed obligation, the `r ≤ 7/6` coverage requirement, leg 275's
`r^(3)` re-naming) depends on the ratio's fourth digit — those rest on the endpoints,
which are exact.

The gate was pre-committed and clause (c) names the `6.855x` figure and the words *"at the

be small is exactly the failure mode pre-commitment exists to prevent. So: NO, with the
magnitude reported so the reader can see it is 0.013% and arithmetic, not architectural.
