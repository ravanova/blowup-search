## Why the leg existed

Leg 241 reported, in one paragraph, that the positional read *"flips the published verdict
on the one ladder carrying Route-L's headline"* at **94.56×** (9.7245 bending → 0.1028
flat) — and concluded, two paragraphs later, that it is **not claim-adjacent**. Both
sentences are true, but the second rests in leg 241's own report on **one cited source
line** (`DIMS = (10,20,40,80,160)`, `p2_route_l_v1_precond.py:49`), produced by the same leg
that found the defect. This leg re-checks that half only. Leg 241's characterisation of the
mechanism is **accepted as given and not re-derived**.

## The five footings, each able to come out the other way

**1 — order audit, 11 arrays (leg 241 audited 5).** Every banked array that reaches
`stall_verdict` is stored **strictly ascending in `m`**: 0 of 11 non-ascending. The six
`L1_attribution` ladders, which reach it through `attribution_summary` and carry the
published §1 table, are audited here for the first time.

**2 — the decisive one: order-immune re-derivation vs what was PUBLISHED.** Every stall
number re-derived from the banked ladders keyed on `m` (`min`/`max` by key, never by
position), in code written here, then compared under `==` on raw float64 — never
`allclose`. **All four L3 stall verdicts bit-identical on all seven keys, every difference
exactly `0.0`**; all six `L1` ranked rows bit-identical, and the published ranking
`[pure dilation, angular transport OFF, velocity in s_rho OFF, reaction terms OFF, velocity
feedback OFF, full]` reproduced in exactly that order. This gate reads **no source line of
Route-L's runner and takes nothing from leg 241**: what Route-L published simply *is* what a
repaired, order-immune `stall_verdict` emits, so the defect cannot have touched it.

**3 — AST enumeration closes leg 241's cited-line gap.** All **6** `krylov_ladder` call
sites (lines 133, 176–180) enumerated by parse, not grep; every one ascending. **Line 180 is
the deep rung's own inline `dims=(240, 320)` and is NOT reached by the line-49 literal leg
241 cites** — yet leg 241 quotes that rung's `61.6× → 0.0162×` inversion. Mechanically
enumerating settles it: ascending there too.

**4 — the headline decomposed, number by number.** *"an `O(N)` exact sweep takes the Krylov
stall from 0.6623 (flat) to 3.3e−6."*

| component | traces to | order-sensitive? |
|---|---|---|
| `0.6623` | `ladders/none`, the `m=160` row | **no** — a single measured row |
| `(flat)` | `stalls/none → flat` | yes in principle, **but 1.0487 ascending vs 0.9536 reversed, both < 2.0 — cannot flip** |
| `3.3e−6` | `line_sweep_deeper`, the `m=320` row | **no** — a single measured row; Route-L never publishes a stall verdict for the deep rung, the `61.6×` is a quantity **leg 241 formed**, not one Route-L banked |
| gain `9.72`, *"the ladder stops being flat"* (§3, what the headline rests on) | `stalls/full transport line sweep` | **yes, and it would not survive reversal** |

So leg 241 is right that the headline's load-bearing verdict rides the flipping ladder —
and right, separately, that the banked configuration never presents it in the flipping order.

**5 — control both ways (lesson 90).** A deliberately descending copy of the same ladder is
flagged, and the two reads separate by **94.565×** with `flat` True against False. A
**shuffled** copy `[40, 10, 160, 20, 80]` — neither ascending nor descending, so the
predicate is not merely a reversal detector — is also flagged, positional gain **1.4163
(flat)** against the true **9.7245 (bending)**, a **6.87×** error and a second verdict flip.
The live banked array comes out **clean**: predicate silent, the two reads differing by
exactly `0.0`.

## What this leg does NOT say

It does **not** clear the defect. **The margin is 0.0**, exactly as leg 241 said, and this
leg sharpens *why that matters*: the single order-sensitive, load-bearing component of
Route-L's headline **is** the ladder that flips. Nothing in `krylov_ladder`, `stall_verdict`
or any caller enforces the ordering that protects it — and gate 5 shows the exposure is
wider than reversal, since an arbitrary permutation flips the verdict too. A repair keying
the verdict on `m` remains warranted. What this leg establishes is only that **no banked
number is currently wrong**.

Leg 241's gate (a), its five stale bank rows, R1, R3, and leg 217's parked state are outside
this leg's territory and are untouched. No banked number moves. No link of the L1→L4 chain
moves; Clay odds unchanged at ~0.05%.
