## 1. CSD1 — THE OPERATOR UNDER TEST IS THE BANKED ONE (exact arithmetic, three paths)

Discipline 85 says re-measure the headline object before building on it. The pointwise operator

    L h  =  cos(theta) h  -  (H h) sin(theta)  -  sin(theta) h_theta

is assembled here **only** from product-to-sum identities and the module's own exact Hilbert
identity `H(sin k theta) = -cos k theta + (-1)^k` — nothing about the banked matrix is used in
building it. It is then checked against **two** independent banked constructions:

| path | agreement |
|---|---|
| `spectral_certificate.bordered_linearization(K, exact=True)`, columns 1..12 | **12/12 exact** |
| exact central difference `(R(b0+e_k) − R(b0−e_k))/2` of `spectral_certificate.clm_residual` at `clm_anchor` (`R` is quadratic in `b`, so this is the derivative with **no** error term) | **12/12 exact** |

All in `fractions.Fraction`; there is no float anywhere in CSD1. Column `k` comes out
`row k+1 : 1 − k/2`, `row k−1 : k/2`, `row 1 : −(−1)^k` — the banked SHIFT with **zero
diagonal**. The leg is measuring the repository's own object, not a lookalike.

## 2. CSD2 — (E) EXISTENCE: THE DOMAIN *DOES* CONTAIN COMPACTLY SUPPORTED ELEMENTS

This is the half that goes **against** the expected verdict, and reporting it first is the
point. The domain is the odd sine system `{sin k theta}, k >= 1` on `theta ∈ (−pi, pi)` with
`X = tan(theta/2)` — a **complete** system for odd `L^2(−pi, pi)`. So it plainly contains
nonzero elements vanishing on a neighbourhood of `theta = ±pi`, i.e. compactly supported in `X`.
Leg 162's phrasing — the `a = 0` linearization has "no compact-support interval at all" — is the
fact about the **anchor** read as a fact about the **domain**. They are different statements.

An **exact** family is exhibited: `m`-fold box convolutions supported on `theta ∈ [0.4, 2.0]`
(i.e. `X ∈ [0.2027, 1.5574]`), whose sine coefficients are closed-form,
`b_k = (2 sin(k h/m)/k)^m sin(k theta_c)`. **There is no quadrature and no noise floor anywhere
in this section.**

| `m` | smoothness | measured decay exponent | predicted | flat `l^1` at `K = 2048` | `max_k log10(1.05^k |b_k|)` at `K = 2048` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | discontinuous (box) | −1.0009 | −1 | 5.43657 (log-divergent) | **+40.14** |
| 2 | `C^0` | −2.0075 | −2 | 2.78272 (convergent) | **+37.58** |
| 4 | `C^2` | −4.0492 | −4 | 3.64149 | **+32.75** |
| 8 | `C^6` | −8.1185 | −8 | 5.19040 | **+24.46** |
| 16 | `C^14` | −15.4226 | −16 | 7.37777 | **+11.13** |

**The weight-class dichotomy, which no prior leg states.** `spectral_certificate.weight_vector`
defines three classes and its own docstring calls the geometric one *"the literature's default:
analytic profiles"*.

* **flat** `w_k = 1` and **algebraic** `w_k = (1+k)^s`: **contain** nonzero compactly supported
  elements (any `m >= 2 + s` above; the `m = 1` box is the boundary case that does *not* make
  flat `l^1`, and `m = 2` with `s = 1` is the log-divergent boundary of the algebraic class).
* **geometric** `w_k = nu^k`, `nu > 1`: **contains none at all.** A `nu^k`-summable coefficient
  sequence extends holomorphically to an annulus, i.e. is real-analytic on the circle, and a
  real-analytic function vanishing on an open arc vanishes identically. The measured
  `max_k log10(nu^k |b_k|)` column above is the numerical face of that theorem: it grows without
  bound for **every** `m`, by `+11.13` to `+40.14` decades already at `K = 2048`.

So *"the domain is unbounded"* is **not** by itself a reason for the gate to answer NO, and any
report that leans on it is leaning on the wrong fact. The invariance half is what decides.

## 3. CSD3 / CSD3b — (I) INVARIANCE: THE LINEARIZATION EJECTS EVERY COMPACTLY SUPPORTED `h`

Two of `L`'s three terms — `cos(theta) h` and `sin(theta) h_theta` — are pointwise multiples of
`h` and `h_theta`, so they vanish identically off `supp h`. **The entire escape is the single
term `(H h) · Omega_0`**, in which the anchor `Omega_0 = −sin(theta)` vanishes only at
`theta = 0, pi`.

**Escape fraction** = mass of `|L h|` lying outside `supp h`, over total, with the local terms'
outside contribution measured separately as the finite-`K` truncation artifact it is:

| `m` | escape fraction | Hilbert term / truncation artifact | resolved at `K = 2048`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | **0.094421** | 86.77× | **no** — excluded from the gate |
| 4 | **0.049271** | 6.069e+06× | yes |
| 8 | **0.027562** | 4.499e+13× | yes |
| 16 | **0.017151** | 1.735e+13× | yes |

Truncation-stable: over `K = 256 → 2048` (an 8× refinement) the `m = 8` escape fraction is
`0.027562052910117 → 0.027562052909941`, i.e. **stable to 13 significant figures**.

**The `m = 2` exclusion, and why the bar was not moved.** The 1000× dominance bar was set before
the data existed. The `m = 2` row does not clear it — its `C^0` coefficients decay only like
`k^-2`, so the truncation artifact falls only like `1/K` and is still `1/86.8` of the Hilbert
term at `K = 2048`. Over the same `K = 256 → 2048` refinement:

| `K` | 256 | 512 | 1024 | 2048 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hilbert / artifact | 13.007 | 21.866 | 35.657 | **86.770** |
| escape fraction | 0.0943488 | 0.0943819 | 0.0944057 | **0.0944210** |

**The artifact converges away (6.67× over an 8× refinement) while the escape fraction moves by
0.076%.** The row is nevertheless **excluded from the gate rather than admitted by lowering the
bar**, and the exclusion is conservative: `m = 2` carries the *largest* escape fraction of the
four.

**CSD3b — the escape is everywhere off the support, not a boundary artifact.** Off `supp h` the
kernel of `H` is smooth, so `H h` — and hence `−(H h) sin(theta)` — is **real-analytic** on the
complementary arc. If it vanished on any sub-interval it would vanish on all of it by analytic
continuation, and then `h` and `H h` both vanish on an arc, which forces `h = 0` (below).
Measured at `m = 8`, `K = 2048`: of **3,865** outside quadrature nodes, **100.0% (fraction 1.0)**
of those away from the two forced zeros `theta = 0, pi` carry escape density above `1000×` the
truncation leakage (`3.997e−15`). The escape density ranges from `1.8005e−05` at its smallest,
away from the forced zeros, to `0.51345` at its largest.

**The exact statement behind the numbers — Luzin–Privalov.** Write `F = sum_k b_k e^{ik theta}`,
so `Im F = h` and `Re F = −(H h) + c`. If `h` and `H h` both vanished on an arc then `F ≡ c`
there, and a Hardy-class function with constant boundary values on a set of positive linear
measure is constant, so every `b_k = 0` and `h ≡ 0`. **Hence: no nonzero compactly supported
subspace of the domain is invariant under `L`, in any of the three weight classes.**

**CSD3 minimized — the SHAPE of the ejection, and an honest resolution limit (lesson 86).**
Minimizing `||(H h) sin(theta)||^2_{L2(off supp)} / ||h||^2_{L2(supp)}` over `K`-dimensional
families of compactly supported `h`:

| `K` | min Rayleigh | float64 floor | resolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.030185e−02 | 6.73e−18 | yes |
| 2 | 1.164218e−02 | 7.03e−18 | yes |
| 4 | 6.698670e−05 | 1.15e−17 | yes |
| 8 | 1.256757e−09 | 1.78e−17 | yes |
| 16 | −1.641271e−18 | 2.59e−17 | **no** |
| 32 | −1.000213e−17 | 3.49e−17 | **no** |

Strictly positive across the whole resolved range, falling **7.382 decades over `K = 1 → 8`**. At
`K = 16` and `32` the computed eigenvalue goes **negative**, which a positive semi-definite form
cannot: that is a statement about float64 and the Gram conditioning, not about the operator, so
**this ladder is deliberately not what the gate is read off** (`not_used_for_the_gate: true` in
the JSON). Its content is the *shape*: the ejection is **exact but not uniform**. Approximately
invariant compactly supported subspaces exist and get rapidly better with dimension; an exactly
invariant one does not exist at any dimension. That non-uniformity is the same severe
ill-posedness that makes analytic continuation off an arc unstable, and stating it is what keeps
this leg from over-claiming.

## 7. LESSONS THIS LEG WOULD ADD, IF THE LEDGER TOOK THEM

**"Compact support" is a property of a SUBSPACE, and its two halves — membership and invariance
— can answer differently.** This leg's domain *contains* compactly supported elements (in 2 of 3
weight classes) and *preserves* none of them. A report that measured only the first half would
have answered YES; a report that measured only the anchor's support would have answered NO for
the wrong reason. Both halves were needed, and the gate turns entirely on the second. This is
lesson 89's shape again — the term that decides is the one you have not written down yet — and
lesson 127's — *"the operator is bad"* and *"this SPACE is bad for the operator"* are different
claims, and so are *"this space has no compactly supported vectors"* and *"this operator
preserves none of them."*

**And a lesson-86 instance worth banking on its own:** the first draft of this leg read its gate
off the minimized Rayleigh ladder, which goes negative at `K = 16` from float64 roundoff on a
provably positive semi-definite form — and would have flipped the gate to YES on an artifact.
The repair was not a tighter tolerance but a different gate predicate, built from quantities
above their own evaluation floors, with the fragile ladder demoted to reporting the *shape*. The
same discipline then cost the leg its largest single escape measurement (`m = 2`, 0.0944), which
sits below a **pre-set** dominance bar and was excluded rather than accommodated. **A threshold
moved after seeing the data is not a threshold** — and the two places this leg had to apply that
rule both went against its own preferred answer, which is the only evidence that it was applied
honestly.
