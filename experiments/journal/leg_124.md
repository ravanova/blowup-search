## §0. The positive control comes first, because it is what makes the rest readable

The module's own docstring (lines 85–89) specifies the check that "pins the quadrature, the
subtraction and the sign convention at once":

```
(1/pi) p.v. INT_{-1}^{1} sqrt(1-y^2) U_{n-1}(y) / (x - y) dy = T_n(x)
```

**It was never implemented.** This leg implements it (G1 / S1). At `p = 1/2` the basis is
`phi_k = sqrt(1-v^2) T_{2k}`, and since `T_0 = U_0` and `T_m = (U_m - U_{m-2})/2` for
`m >= 2`, the exact answer is `Htilde_0 = T_1` and `Htilde_k = (T_{2k+1} - T_{2k-1})/2`.

| N | max abs error vs the closed form |
|---|---|
| 500 | 3.625e-06 |
| 1000 | 9.063e-07 |
| 2000 | 2.266e-07 |
| 4000 | 5.664e-08 |
| 8000 | **1.416e-08** |

A clean second-order rate (**16.0×** per 4× refinement) onto **1.416e-08**. This is a
control that could have come out the other way (lesson 90), and it did not: **the
finite-support Hilbert transform, the principal-value subtraction and the sign convention
are correct.** Everything below is therefore about *domain hygiene*, not about a broken
quadrature — and the 1.4e-08 figure is the noise floor against which §3's O(1) equation
violations are measured.

## §1. FINDING A — the `converged` flag is decided by a measurement too coarse to support it

**This is the sharpest of the five, and it is not the one the novelty pass expected.**

`FiniteSupportProfile.solve` reports `converged` from the residual at the `K - 1`
collocation nodes only. Measured off that grid (200 points at `v ∈ [0.013, 0.987]`, none of
them a collocation node, at 20× the quadrature resolution the solve used, `a = 0.5`):

| K | collocation rows | reports converged | reported residual | independent off-grid residual | amplification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **0** | **True** | **exactly 0.0** | 3.308e-01 | ∞ |
| 2 | 1 | **True** | 0.0 | 1.906e-01 | ∞ |
| 3 | 2 | False | 2.437e-02 | 6.178e-01 | 2.54e+01 |
| 4 | 3 | **True** | 5.551e-17 | 1.897e-01 | **3.42e+15** |
| 6 | 5 | **True** | 2.775e-13 | 3.271e-02 | 1.18e+11 |
| 8 | 7 | **True** | 6.719e-13 | 1.315e-02 | 1.96e+10 |
| 12 | 11 | **True** | 3.331e-15 | **1.217e+00** | **3.66e+14** |
| 16 | 15 | False | 6.359e-01 | 5.277e+00 | 8.30e+00 |
| 24 | 23 | False | 6.436e-01 | 1.042e+00 | 1.62e+00 |

**Six of nine values of `K` self-report `converged = True`.** At `K = 12` the module reports
a residual of **3.331e-15** on a state whose profile-equation residual between the
collocation nodes is **1.217** — a factor of **3.66e14**, and six orders of magnitude above
G1's 1.4e-08 quadrature floor, so this is collocation aliasing and not numerical noise.

**The degenerate end is unambiguous.** At `K = 1` there are `K - 1 = 0` collocation rows:
the profile equation is evaluated at **zero points**. The square system is the amplitude
gauge plus the free-boundary row, so the reported residual is **exactly 0.0** and
`converged` is `True` **by construction**. Nothing in the module guards `K`. `K = 0`
constructs too (and then raises inside `solve`).

**The spread is the second half of the finding.** Across the six `K` that self-report
converged, `X_c` ranges **2.0409 to 2.6112** — a **24.2 %** relative spread, with no
monotone trend (2.041, 2.209, 2.611, 2.416, 2.423, 2.440). Each individual call presents
one of these as a converged answer at machine precision.

> **What is deliberately NOT claimed here.** Not that the module should converge — its own
> docstring says it does not — and **no value of `X_c` is asserted, endorsed or compared to
> any other build.** The claim is exactly one thing: the flag is decided by a measurement
> that cannot support it, and the module says `converged = True` while the equation it
> exists to solve is violated by O(1) a few thousandths of a unit away from a node.

**Rider (H5, minor).** `hist` is appended at the *top* of the Newton loop, so on a
non-breaking exit the last recorded entry is the *previous* iterate's residual while the
returned `residual` field is recomputed at the final one. Measured gap at `a = 0.3`,
`K = 16`: **1.497e-06**; with `max_iter = 1`: **2.352e-02**. `converged` is derived from the
stale entry at threshold `1e-8`, while the loop's own break uses `tol = 1e-12` — two
thresholds, two iterates, one flag.

## §2. FINDING B — out-of-support values are fabricated **exactly when `1/a` is an integer**

`even_cheb` (line 115) evaluates `np.arccos(np.clip(v, -1, 1))`, so every `|v| > 1` is
silently evaluated **at `v = 1`**: max deviation from the `v = 1` row over
`v ∈ {1.01, 1.5, 3, 50, −7}` is **exactly 0.0**, from **0 warnings** and 0 exceptions. At
`v = 50` the returned `T_10` is **1** against an exact **4.995e+19**.

Meanwhile the algebraic prefactor `(1 − v²)^p` uses the **unclipped** `v`. The two halves of
the basis function disagree about which point they are at, and the consequence has a shape:

| a | p = 1/a | integer? | max returned \|Ω\| on `v ∈ {1.01, 1.5, 2, 5}` (truth **0**) | warnings from `fields()` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1 | yes | 24 (and the **sign is flipped**) | **0** |
| 0.5 | 2 | yes | **576** | **0** |
| 1/3 | 3 | yes | 13 824 (sign flipped) | **0** |
| 0.25 | 4 | yes | 331 776 | **0** |
| 0.2 | 5 | yes | **7 962 624** (sign flipped) | **0** |
| 0.3, 0.35, 0.7, 0.9, 1.2 | non-integer | no | `nan` — **flagged** | — |

**5 of 10 values of `a` fabricate silently; 5 of 10 are caught by NaN.** The split is
*exactly* whether `1/a` is an integer: for non-integer `p` the negative base sends the
fractional power complex and NumPy returns `nan`, which is a flag; for integer `p` it
returns a finite, sign-plausible, arbitrarily large number where the true value is **exactly
0** (the module's own docstring, lines 50–53: perturbations supported in `[0, X_c]` have
residuals supported there too).

The silent set is `a = 1/n`. **It contains `a = 0.5`, one of the module's two documented
smoke runs, and `a = 0.2`, the bottom of its documented range 0.2–1.2.** The values are
grid-independent: relative spread **exactly 0.0** across `K ∈ {6, 12, 24}` × `N ∈ {400,
1600}`, so this is structural, not a discretization artifact.

This is the same line leg 107's bench repair found in the superseding module and explicitly
declined to widen into: `solver/first_integral.py:328` now carries a justification comment
and a companion guard at `:375`; `finite_support.py:115` carries neither. **Of the 10
`np.clip` sites in the 45 modules under `solver/`, these two are the only ones clipping the
argument of an inverse trigonometric function** — and only one of them is now guarded.

## §3. FINDING C — a quadrature-node collision is floored, not flagged

`_hilbert_matrix` line 178: `d = np.where(np.abs(d) < 1e-300, 1e-300, d)`. When an
evaluation point coincides with a quadrature node, the divided difference is exactly
`0 / 1e-300 = 0`, so the term is **dropped** rather than replaced by the derivative it
stands in for.

| evaluation | relative error vs the same point under a non-colliding rule (`N + 1`) |
|---|---|
| the colliding point | **8.944e-03** |
| a point **1e-7** away, same rule | 2.122e-06 |

**4215× amplification, from 0 warnings from the evaluation itself.** (Construction emits 2
warnings from the `v = 1` row of the internal velocity grid — benign, see §5 — which is why
the measurement is scoped to the colliding call and not to the constructor. That correction
was made *after* an earlier draft asserted an unmeasured zero.)

**Scope, measured rather than assumed:** across `K ∈ {8, 16, 24, 32}` × `N ∈ {800, 2000}`,
the module's own default collocation node sets collide with the quadrature **0** times. The
defect is reachable only through the public `FiniteSupportOps(v_eval=...)` entry point.

## §4. FINDING D — degenerate `a` is accepted without a word

No guard on `a`, `K`, `N`, `c` or `n_int` exists anywhere in the module.

| a | outcome |
|---|---|
| `0.0` | **`ZeroDivisionError`** — flagged (the one soundness case) |
| `nan` | constructs, `p = nan`, **0 warnings** |
| `inf` | constructs, **`p = 0.0`**, **0 warnings** |
| `-inf` | constructs, `p = -0.0`, **0 warnings** |
| `-0.5` | constructs, `p = -2.0`, 2 warnings |
| `-1e-13` | constructs, `p = -1e+13`, 15 warnings |
| `1e-13` | constructs, `p = 1e+13`, 1 warning |

**3 of 7 construct with zero exceptions and zero warnings.** The worst is `a = inf`:
`p = 0.0` replaces the algebraic edge zero `(1 − v²)^p` by the **constant 1**, discarding the
ansatz the entire module is built on, in silence. All four non-finite cases do eventually
surface as a `nan` residual from `solve` — the *construction* is silent, the *solve* is not,
which is why this is listed fourth and not first.

## §5. What did NOT fire, and it matters as much

**H4 — the log endpoints are LOUD.** `np.log(v / (1 − v))` at `v = 0` returns `-inf` and the
returned row is non-finite: wrong-but-loud, not silent. (It carries **no warning**, because
`log(0)` under the `errstate('divide')` suppression is a *valid* infinity rather than an
invalid operation — worth knowing, but a non-finite return is not a fabricated plausible
number.) At `v = 1` the reverse: the multiply warns (`0 * inf → nan`) and the
`phi_v != 0` mask then discards the nan and returns the **correct** finite value. Neither
contributes to the gate answer.

**H9 — poisoned coefficients propagate, 8/8.** `nan` or `inf` in the coefficient vector
comes back non-finite from `system`, `system_jacobian`, `operator_norm` and
`profile_values`, every time, with a clean all-finite control on the same inputs. Nothing
absorbs a poison. Over the wider battery, **18 of 42** poisoned (case × entry point) pairs
return a finite number — but the finite ones are almost entirely the *radius* cases, not the
coefficient cases.

**H9 rider, which IS open.** A **negative** support radius — physically impossible for a
free boundary — is accepted by **4 of 4** entry points and every one returns a finite
number, including `operator_norm`, the module's declared kill switch (**34.84** at
`X_c = −1`). At `X_c = 1e-14` the kill switch returns **4.08e+14** with **0 warnings**, from
a Jacobian whose condition number is only **2556** — so the blow-up is the `/X_c` in the
norm's own definition, not ill-conditioning.

**H8 — `np.linalg.inv` as a rank test.** On clean states the Jacobian's condition number
runs **6.31e+02 → 4.62e+04 → 8.15e+05 → 3.59e+10** at `K = 4, 8, 12, 16`. `inv` never
raised. *No value of `‖A‖` is reported by this leg* — only whether a number comes back and
how conditioned the matrix it came from was.

**Dead code, recorded and not counted as a finding.** Line 179 computes `reg` and line 181
immediately overwrites it with a differently-spelled equivalent. Harmless.

## §7. Recommended repair — for whoever holds the authority this leg does not

Not applied. `solver/finite_support.py` is byte-identical to `origin/main`.

1. **The `converged` flag (§1) is the one that matters** and it is not a domain guard.
   Either evaluate the residual off the collocation grid before setting the flag, or refuse
   `K` below the point where the system has equation rows at all (`K >= 3`), or — simplest,
   and consistent with the module's status — have `solve` refuse to set `converged` on a
   module whose banner says DO NOT USE. Note that the leg-107 template does **not** apply
   here: this is not extrapolation, it is under-measurement.
2. **§2 is exactly leg 107's repaired defect**, one module over. The merged template is
   directly transferable: a named `Warning` subclass, a dynamic threshold (`|v| > 1 + 4ε`,
   where `1` is the *definition* of the scaled support and not a magic number), an
   `on_outside=` argument defaulting to the **known true value 0.0**, and warn-not-raise so
   the audit that measures the guard stays runnable.
3. **§3** wants the collision replaced by the analytic derivative limit, or rejected.
4. **§4** wants three lines: `a` finite and positive, `K >= 3`, `X_c > 0`.

**Because the module is superseded with zero importers, "delete it" is also a legitimate
answer, and is cheaper than four repairs.** That is a decision for the DM, not for this leg.
If it is kept, the four gaps above should be closed or the file should keep this battery
next to it — the battery is what makes the banner enforceable instead of advisory.
