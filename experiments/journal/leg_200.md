# Leg 200 — Route-PCA: the adversarial audit of `solver/port_certification.py`

**Branch:** `leg/200-pca-v1`. **Date:** 2026-08-06. **Role:** LEG.
**Module under test:** `solver/port_certification.py` — **read-only, byte-identical to
what this leg inherited, under both branches of the gate.**

## The gate, verbatim and pre-committed on both branches

> Under adversarial and degenerate inputs, does `port_certification.py` ever silently
> return a wrong value rather than reject or visibly propagate the defect?

**Answer: YES.** Four independent mechanisms, seven of the battery's twelve gates SILENT. Per the
yes-branch this leg has no patch authority and did not patch; the finding is **escalated
and parked**, and the branch is pushed on its own.

## Why this module had never been audited, despite seven prior legs having touched it

Leg 79 ran a 39-case battery here, leg 86 regression-checked it, leg 128 used this file as
its **bit-identical comparison target**, and leg 195 re-ran PORT's evidence scripts. Every
one of those aimed at **one of sixteen public entry points** (10 module-level functions +
6 `ProfileResidual` methods) — `radii_polynomial_status` — and that one is now the
best-guarded thing in the file. The other fifteen had never been handed a malformed input;
`test_port_certification.py` exercises most of them
and every case in it is well-formed. The prior-art table is `writeup/novelty/leg_200.md`,
committed before a line of the battery was written.

## The four mechanisms, as magnitudes

### 1. `line_sweep_solve` silently inverts a *different operator* — 18820x

Docstring: *"EXACT O(N) inverse of `(-s_rho d_rho - s_beta d_beta + c_diag)`, first-order
upwind. Requires `s_rho > 0`."* It never checks. The backward radial difference is
hard-coded, so where `s_rho < 0` it is the **anti-upwind** stencil and the routine returns
the exact inverse of a matrix that is not the one it names — finite, correctly shaped,
plausible, no exception, no NaN, no flag.

| `min(s_rho)` | rel. error vs the **advertised** operator | residual / ‖rhs‖ | rel. error vs the operator it **assembles** |
|---|---|---|---|
| +0.3896 (the live run) | 5.09e-16 | 5.76e-16 | 5.09e-16 |
| +0.001 | 3.81e-16 | 1.96e-15 | 3.81e-16 |
| 0.0 | 9.31e-16 | 1.94e-15 | 9.31e-16 |
| −0.001 | **9.69e-03** | 2.79e-03 | 4.2e-16 |
| −0.1 | **5.95** | 1.81 | 4.4e-15 |
| **−0.4** | **1.88e+04** | **2.07e+04** | 7.9e-13 |
| −1.0 | 1.21 | 0.45 | 7.3e-16 |
| −3.0 | 1.12 | 0.50 | 7.6e-16 |

Three things in that table are the finding rather than the number:

* **The last column is ~1e-16 everywhere.** The sweep is doing exactly what it says
  arithmetically. All of the wrongness is in *which operator*, none of it in the solve.
  That column is the control that can report the other answer, and it does not.
* **The curve is not monotone.** The worst error is at a *marginal* breach (−0.4), not a
  gross one (1.12x at −3.0). Banked lesson 88 again, from the other side: the maximum of a
  failure curve is no more where you expect it than the minimum is.
* **A breach 390x smaller than the live run's own margin already costs ~1%.** The margin
  is not comfortable; it is simply unmeasured after the first print.

### 2. The sign-awareness is present on one axis and absent four lines away

This is what makes (1) a finding rather than an omission of its era. The **same loop body**
selects the angular difference direction pointwise by the sign of `s_beta`
(`pos = sb > 0`; `a[pos] = sb[pos]/dbeta` vs `c[~pos] = -sb[~pos]/dbeta`) and selects the
radial direction not at all. Measured behaviourally, not by reading the source: over three
`s_beta` sign patterns (all-positive, all-negative, mixed) the answer tracks the
correctly-upwinded reference to ≤1.7e-16; flip the sign of `s_rho` instead and it is
1.88e+04 out.

### 3. `radii_polynomial_status` issues a closing certificate for a ball of radius 0

**The sharpest one, because the guard exists and is never asked.** The function returns
`closes = bool(disc >= 0)` and never forms `r_min = ((1-Z_1) - sqrt(disc)) / (2 Z_2)`.
`solver/certificate_guards.py::radius_violation` was written verbatim for this class —
*"a non-positive or non-finite `r_min` is not a conservative verdict — it asserts a zero
inside a degenerate or empty set. Leg 116 measured 14 such 'certificates' in
`nk_bounds.budget` alone"* — and `solver/nk_bounds.py:561` calls it.
`solver/port_certification.py` imports `hypothesis_violations` and `NAN_HINT_GE_ONE` from
that module and nothing else.

So **legs 79/128 repaired the hypothesis half of this defect here and left the radius
half** — in the module that was leg 128's own comparison target.

And `Y_0 = 0` is not a synthetic input in this repository. **Leg 51 measured `Y_0` exactly
zero on the a=0 CLM profile** (it is one basis mode), and `plan_of_record.py` carries a
standing ban on how to read that zero. Fed the banked value:

| input | status | `closes` | `r_min` (never computed) | what `radius_violation` would say |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Y_0=0, Z_1=0.5, Z_2=1e4` | EVALUATED | **True** | 0.0 | rejects |
| `Y_0=0, Z_1=0.9, Z_2=1.0` | EVALUATED | **True** | 0.0 | rejects |
| `Y_0=-0.0, Z_1=0.5, Z_2=1.0` | EVALUATED | **True** | 0.0 | rejects |
| `Y_0=Z_1=Z_2=0` | EVALUATED | **True** | 0.0 | rejects |
| `Y_0=1e-8, Z_1=0.5, Z_2=1.0` (control) | EVALUATED | True | 2.00e-08 | accepts |

### 4. `leading_order_solve` truncates an integer right-hand side — rel. error 1.000

`out = np.zeros_like(rhs)`, no cast. Its sibling twenty lines away opens with
`rhs = np.asarray(rhs, float)`. An integer rhs therefore gets an integer output buffer and
every `out[i] = prev` truncates: a canonical basis vector whose exact answer is `0.5` comes
back as `0`. Worst relative error **1.000**; `[[1,2],[3,4]]` gives 0.333. The truncation is
documented NumPy behaviour (numpy#7730, numpy#8733); the missing cast is the defect, and
the sibling asymmetry is the evidence it was not deliberate. The same routine is sign-blind
in `c_l` for mechanism (1)'s reason: **26.0x** at `c_l = -0.5`.

### 5. The verdict layer: a NaN gets a confident reading, and so does a reversed list

`stall_verdict`'s `flat = bool(gain < 2.0)` — and `NaN < 2.0` is `False`. This is
byte-for-byte the mechanism `certificate_guards.NAN_HINT_GE_ONE` exists to warn about
(*"note that `NaN >= 1.0` is False, so an unguarded NaN would slip past the contraction
test"*), moved from a certificate verdict to an attribution verdict. The harm is not the
lost information, it is the **prose**:

| ladder | reported | prose emitted |
|---|---|---|
| both residuals NaN | `flat=False`, gain NaN | *"bending => finite ill-conditioning, and more Krylov work would help"* |
| NaN at either end | `flat=False`, gain NaN | same |
| `+inf` residual | `flat=False`, gain inf | same |
| **negative** residual (an impossible norm) | `flat=True`, gain **−25.0** | *"flat … => a continuum in the spectrum"* |

Which is the exact opposite of this module's entire finding about the operator. It
propagates: `attribution_summary` **ranks** the NaN ablation into the published
attribution table at `gain=NaN, flat=False`, i.e. presents it as an ablation that
un-flattened the ladder.

Separately, `stall_verdict` reads the ladder's ends **positionally** (`rows[0]`,
`rows[-1]`), never by `m`, and `krylov_ladder` preserves the caller's `dims` order. The
same two measurements therefore give **opposite published verdicts** depending on the
order of the tuple: work ratio 16.0 → 0.0625, `flat` False → True.

## What came out clean — including the mechanism that was likeliest a priori

* **`gmres`'s projected-vs-true residual is a NULL.** The function returns the Arnoldi
  least-squares residual and never forms `‖Ax−b‖/‖b‖`; `gmres_controls` attaches a
  `rel_true` to the well-conditioned case and **not** to the `cond ~ 1e8` case whose number
  (0.0864) the module's docstring uses to argue the observed Jacobian stall is worse than
  ill-conditioning alone. Under modified Gram-Schmidt these can drift
  (Greenbaum–Rozložník–Strakoš, BIT 37 (1997); Paige–Rozložník–Strakoš, SIMAX). Across
  cond 1e0..1e16 they do not: worst deviation **8.1e-06** relative, at cond 1e16. The
  missing `rel_true` is a reporting asymmetry, not a wrong number — supplied here, the
  module's own 0.08640395900178 agrees with the true 0.08640395900307 to 1.5e-11.
* **Every non-finite input to `gmres` raises** `LinAlgError` — in the rhs, or injected by
  the operator. Visible, not silent.
* **Every degenerate port PARAMETER is visible**: `drho=0` → `ZeroDivisionError`; exactly
  singular diagonal, NaN `c_diag`, infinite `c_l`, NaN in `rhs` → non-finite entries carried
  into the returned array. The one finite case is 1 ULP off singular, and there the
  amplification (5.07e+30) is itself the signal.
* **The hypothesis guard holds**: 10 of 10 hypothesis-violating inputs return
  `INVALID_INPUT`/`closes=False`; the kill-switch returns `BLOCKED_AT_STEP_ONE` even with a
  poisoned `Z_2`, and leaks no value into the `Y0`/`Z1` slots. Legs 79/128 have not
  regressed.
* **`s_rho ≡ 0` is a guard false-positive, and that is informative.** The predicate is
  `min > 0`, so it reports `holds=False`, but the sweep is exact there — there is no radial
  coupling to upwind. It is also the configuration the module's own ADI negative control
  runs at (`p2_route_l_v1_precond.py:172` passes `0 * one`), which is direct evidence
  nobody consults the guard: a caller that did would have refused its own control.

One minor extra, reported for completeness: `ProfileResidual.pack`/`unpack` validate only
the **total** size, so a field handed in the transposed layout round-trips into a
different, finite, plausible field at 1.32 relative error. A wrong total length is caught.

## NO BANKED NUMBER MOVES — and the distinction is the point

Every PORT-family reach-table value was produced with `min(s_rho) = 0.38963488051119644 > 0`
(`writeup/data/p2_route_l_v1_precond.json` → `outward_upwinding`, read, not re-derived) and
float64 fields — i.e. inside the corner where all four mechanisms are dormant. **In leg
116's sense these are NOT load-bearing**: the hypothesis-satisfying counterpart returns the
same number, so nothing in the banked record is wrong.

They are **latent**. They fire on the next profile, grid, ablation or caller that leaves
that corner, and nothing in the module or in its pipeline would say so. Two of the four are
a guard *that already exists in this repository and is never called*:

| defect | the guard that would catch it | who calls it |
|---|---|---|
| `line_sweep_solve` at `s_rho < 0` | `port_certification.outward_upwinding_holds` — same file | `p2_route_l_v1_precond.py:113`, which formats it into a `print()` and never branches on `holds` |
| `radii_polynomial_status` radius-0 certificate | `certificate_guards.radius_violation` | `nk_bounds.py:561` only |

## Escalation

Per the gate's yes-branch, **priority finding, parked, not patched, branch pushed alone.**
The recommended repair is one line of policy, not new mathematics, and it should be a
leg with its own gate:

1. `line_sweep_solve` calls `outward_upwinding_holds` and refuses (or upwinds by sign) —
   and the runner branches on the result instead of printing it.
2. `leading_order_solve` gains `rhs = np.asarray(rhs, float)`, matching its sibling, plus
   the same sign check.
3. `radii_polynomial_status` computes `r_min` and routes it through
   `certificate_guards.radius_violation`, completing legs 79/128's repair.
4. `stall_verdict` rejects non-finite and negative residuals, and sorts by `m`.

Every one of those is a **rejection layer** in leg 128's sense — it may turn something
previously accepted into a refusal and must never move a clean-input number. This leg's
battery is the instrument for checking that: gates 0, 8 and 9 of
`test_port_certification_adversarial.py` are the soundness half, and gates 1–7 and 10 are
GAP-PINs written to be **inverted** by the repair, with leg 200's pre-repair magnitudes
kept in their docstrings.

## One correction this leg made to itself

The first draft of the runner reported the GMRES residual gap as `max(ratio)` rather than
`max|ratio − 1|`, which under-reported the worst deviation by **14x** (5.8e-07 against the
true 8.1e-06) because the largest deviation happened to sit *below* unity. Caught by the
pinned test disagreeing with the runner's own headline, fixed in both, and the runner now
carries a comment saying why. It changes no conclusion — the mechanism is a null either
way — but a battery that reports its own null loosely has no standing to report anyone
else's magnitude tightly.
