## The read, and why the finding is a compound one, not a single "no"

Tsai 1998's paper is not one theorem, it is two, proved by the **same mechanism** with **two
different finishing moves**:

- **Theorem 1** (generalizing NRS 1996): `U ∈ L^q(R³)`, `q ∈ (3,∞]` ⟹ `U` constant. This is the
  route this repo's every prior citation of "NRS/Tsai" has used, and it is the route leg
  357's `ledger_nrs_tsai()` implements (test `L³` convergence, done).
- **Theorem 2**: `u` satisfies the **local energy estimates** (1.4) in a parabolic cylinder around
  the singularity, and is of the exact self-similar form (1.2) ⟹ `u ≡ 0`. **No `L^q` membership
  anywhere in its hypothesis.**

Both are proved (Section 5, p.48–49, read at primary text, independently re-verified) by the SAME
two-step chain: (1) establish `U(y) = o(|y|)`, a **sub-linear growth** bound, vastly weaker than
`L^q` membership — Theorem 1 gets this from `L^q`-based harmonic-analysis estimates (Section 3),
Theorem 2 gets it from the local energy estimates via `A_p`-weight/CKN-type partial regularity
(Section 4); (2) plug into the Liouville-type comparison Lemma 5.1, which needs only
`|U(y)| ≤ b|y|` for some `b < a`, to force the Bernoulli pressure `Π` constant; then finish via
the classical Liouville theorem — **Theorem 1 finishes using `U ∈ L^q`, Theorem 2 finishes using
`U → 0 at infinity`** (quoted verbatim in the JSON). A profile decaying like `1/|y|` trivially
satisfies "`U → 0` at infinity" — Theorem 2's finish needs no integrability at all.

**And the paper's own headline motivating example (eq. 1.5, p.31) is essentially this repo's
exact target's decay rate**: `U(y) = A(y/|y|)·1/|y| + o(1/|y|)`, i.e. exponent `-1` — matching
leg 357's measured `-0.9971` almost exactly. Tsai states as a corollary: *"A particular corollary
of these results is that a weak solution U of (1.3) with the decay (1.5) must be zero."* This
decay rate is the EXACT log-divergent `L³` boundary case (`|u|³r² ~ r^{-1}`) — I did not have to
search for a paper that reaches this case; Tsai 1998 was WRITTEN around this exact motivating
example, using Theorem 2 to close it after Theorem 1/NRS's route (which needs `U(y)=O(|y|^{-3})`,
p.32) provably cannot reach it (Tsai says so himself, p.32: "This seems to be a serious obstacle
for generalizing the method used in [NRS] to `q > 3`").

**So sub-question 2 (does the technique reach the log-divergent boundary) is a clean, decisive
class (i) — REACHES — and it is not a subtle inference, it is the paper's own stated point.**

## Where the object actually escapes — confirmed, not merely re-cited

Both Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are stated `"If u is of the form (1.2)_1"` — Leray's EXACT
(continuous) backward self-similar ansatz, a single stationary profile `U` under one parameter
`a`. The screened object is DISCRETELY self-similar: a periodic-in-log-time orbit, period
`T = 2 log λ`, not a fixed point of the rescaled flow. This is leg 253's ansatz finding
(confirmed independently, at leg 253's own quoted locators) and leg 341's second, independent
ground — but I confirm here, by reading Theorem 2 at primary text myself, that the SAME ansatz
gate blocks the local-energy-estimates route, not just the `L^q` route. Nothing in Section 4 or 5
of Tsai's proof relaxes this to DSS; the whole machinery (Lemma 3.2/3.3 for Theorem 1, the
`A_p`-weight argument of Section 4 for Theorem 2) is built around `U` solving the single
time-independent elliptic system (1.3), not a periodic family.

**Consequence:** the decay boundary is NOT what protects this repo's object — Tsai's own paper
would kill it in one line if it were exactly self-similar with this decay. What protects it is
being DSS, not SS. This is a genuine sharpening of leg 341's S2 finding: leg 341 concluded the
L³-route "dissolves" for the target's own decay rate and reported this as one of "two independent
grounds" alongside leg 253's ansatz finding. Having now read Theorem 2, I find the decay-based
ground is **incomplete as stated** — a fuller reading of Tsai 1998 shows the paper's OTHER theorem
does reach exactly this decay rate. The ansatz ground is therefore not merely "a second,
independent" reason; it is the ONLY one that actually survives a full read of the primary source.
This does not change leg 341's bottom line (`NOT EXCLUDED` stands) but it changes WHY, and that
matters for anyone tempted to lean on "the decay rate saves it" as a repeatable argument.
