## §1 — The argument, and what it actually proves

The structure is clean and I could not break it. Both halves of Theorem 1.4 run through **one
identity**, `l.573–580`:

```
    ∫ |Ω|² w dy  =  α ∫ E w dy
```

where `w > 0` solves `L*w = 0` with `L* = −Δ − (U + y/2)·∇ − 3/2` and has two-sided Gaussian
bounds (Prop. 4.2, `l.553–568`), and `E` is the rotation error term (`l.489–494`). The whole paper
is the construction of `w` plus two different ways to make the right-hand side small:

| regime | mechanism | locator |
|---|---|---|
| `\|α\| ≪ 1` | `\|E\| ≤ C_E` crudely; RHS is `O(α)` | `l.582–590` |
| `\|α\| ≫ 1` | `E = ½∂_θ(\|U\|²+U·y)` is a **pure angular derivative** (`l.507`), and `\|α\|·‖RU‖_{L²_μ} ≤ ε` (Prop. 6.6) | `l.1224–1276` |

Then Prop. 3.1 (`l.394–410`): small enstrophy in a fixed ball `B_R̄`, `R̄ = √(8C_{U,1})`, forces
`U ≡ 0`. The rotation never enters Prop. 3.1 — it is an `α`-independent statement, which is why
the whole scheme works.

**The Bernoulli bypass is genuine, and it is not an artifact of a silently relaxed hypothesis.**
I checked this specifically because it is what the dispatch asked. `L` is the *same* operator that
appears in NRS's maximum-principle identity (`l.154–158`); PV do not modify it. What they do is
stop asking it for a maximum principle and instead ask its **adjoint** for a positive kernel
element to use as a weight. That is a real change of technique, not a change of hypothesis: the
hypothesis set is exactly the Type-I bound `|u| ≤ C_{U,0}/(|x|+√(−t))` plus `U ∈ C²`, and PV are
careful (Remark 1.3, `l.218–221`) to insist `C_{U,0}` is **independent of `α`** — the one place
where a hidden `α`-dependence would have made the theorem vacuous. Lemma 2.1's proof
(`l.375–389`) is written twice, and the *reason* it is written twice is to make the
`α`-independence of `C_{U,1}, C_{U,2}, C_{P,0}` visible. That is the opposite of a silent relaxation.

## §3 — THE MAGNITUDE FINDING: "α ≈ 1" understates the open window by about five million decades

This answers leg 253's forward point (6), and it answers it with a number rather than with the
boolean the question offered.

PV say only: *"leaves open the case `α ≈ 1`"* (`l.232`). I unwound their own constant chain:

```
  R̄        = √(8 C_{U,1})                                             l.396
  C_E       = C_{U,0} + C_{U,1} + 2 C_{U,0} C_{U,1}                    l.502
  α_lo      = (1/200) m e^{-(3/8)R̄²} C_E⁻¹ M⁻¹ C_Ω⁻²                   l.593
  M′        = 49.4187 · M · (1 + 3 C_{U,0})                            l.1240–1253
```

**So leg 253's point (6) has an answer that is neither of the two it offered.** PV's thresholds

**G1 is the only real rigour gap and the repair is routine.** `w` solves `L*w = 0` with drift
growing like `|y|/2`; interior Schauder on unit balls upgrades the Gaussian sup bound to a
Gaussian gradient bound with at most polynomial loss, which still beats `e^{-(1-ε)|y|²/4}`. I
checked all three boundary terms — `w ∂_nΠ`, `Π ∂_n w`, `Π w (U+y/2)·n` — and each is

**G4 is where "explicit in principle" is doing the most work**, and it matters for §3: a *larger*

cannot close it.** A Phase-1 costing must not assume `C_{U,1}` is small.

**None of the six breaks the window.** That is not a surprising outcome once stated plainly: the
openness claim is a claim about the *absence* of a theorem, and an error in PV could only make the

reached `α ≈ 1`. I checked the four candidates and none does:

* **ESS / `L³`** — `l.223`: Type I gives `U ∈ L^{3,∞}` only, never `L³`. No.

* **The axisymmetric Type-I exclusion** — *cannot* reach this class, and the reason is derived

* **PV's own Thm 1.7 via `RSS ⊆ RDSS`** — an RSS solution at `α` is `(α,λ)`-RDSS for every

**And leg 253's own axisymmetric-DSS composition does not reach here either.** Under

## §5 — THE CROSS-LEG FINDING: PV answer leg 255's open question, positively, at a stated price

This is the finding I did not expect and it is the most useful one for this repository, because it
is about **our** machinery, not about Navier–Stokes.

`writeup/novelty/verify_255.md` §1 handed leg 257 a prediction:

> *"the Leray projector is built from Riesz transforms (Calderón–Zygmund operators), and CZ
> operators are bounded on a weighted `L²(w)` essentially only when `w` is a Muckenhoupt `A₂`
> weight. Breden–Chu's weight is `μ = e^{|x|²/4}` … emphatically not `A₂` … the expectation is
> that the Leray projector is **genuinely unbounded on `L²(μ)`** … if it holds, the 'argue Leray
> into the reach' lane is **dead on arrival**."*

**Pineau–Vicol hit exactly this obstruction and say so in their own words**, `l.1074`:

> *"Note that we are seeking a bound in the Gaussian weighted `L²_μ` norm, and so we do not have
> access to usual Calderon–Zygmund bounds. This is compensated for by the `ε`-factor present on
> the right side of (6.3)."*

Their workaround (`l.1098–1118`) is **not** to claim boundedness. It is to split at radius `L_ε`:
inside, use `μ ≤ 1` to fall back to *unweighted* `L²` where CZ **is** bounded, paying `e^{L_ε²/8}`;
outside, use the pointwise decay `|V| ≤ 2C_{U,0}C_{U,1}(1+|y|³)^{-1}` that the Type-I bound
supplies. The output is not a bounded operator but the loss estimate

```
    ‖(∇P)_a‖_{L²_μ}  ≤  ε  +  C_ε(‖(U)_a‖_{L²_μ} + ‖∇(U)_a‖_{L²_μ}),    C_ε ~ 9 e^{L_ε²/8},  L_ε ~ ε^{-2/3}
```

**Two consequences, both actionable:**

1. **verify_255's assessment should be narrowed, not overturned.** It is *correct* about
   boundedness — the Leray projector is not bounded on `L²(μ)`, and PV implicitly agree. But
   "dead on arrival" is too strong: the lane is **alive at a stated and enormous price**, and the
   price is buyable **only for fields with a priori pointwise decay**. That is a much more precise
   thing to hand leg 257 than either "open" or "dead".
2. **This is the mechanism behind §3's double exponential.** `α_hi` is astronomical *precisely
   because* the Gaussian weight is not `A₂`. The two findings are one fact seen from two sides —
   and the honest reading is that the non-`A₂` weight is not a technicality, it is the thing that
   sets the scale of every constant in the paper.

## §6 — The certificate target (clause b), and the two things it would NOT prove

**Object.** A nontrivial RSS profile `(U, P)` at angular speed `α` in the open window, on `ℝ³`,
non-axisymmetric (**forced**, see D1), with `|U(y)| ≤ C_{U,0}/(1+|y|)`.

**Profile equation — named.** PV eq. (2.4), `l.185–193`:

```
    α(JU − (Jy·∇)U) + ½U + ½(y·∇)U − ΔU + (U·∇)U + ∇P = 0,    ∇·U = 0    on ℝ³
```

and its **pressure-free** form, PV eq. (5.2), `l.417–420`, which is the working form:

```
    α(JΩ − Jy·∇Ω) + Ω + ½y·∇Ω − ΔΩ + U·∇Ω = Ω·∇U,    Ω = ∇×U,  U = BiotSavart(Ω)
```

The vorticity form **carries no pressure**, so §5's Calderón–Zygmund price enters only through
Biot–Savart, which is applied in *unweighted* `L^p` where it is bounded — PV do exactly this at
`l.430–438`, naming `C′` as the `L⁴` operator norm of `∇∇×(−Δ)^{-1}`.

**Space — named.** A **two-norm pair**, both halves already used by PV:

* **outer:** unweighted `H¹(ℝ³)³ ∩ L²`, divergence-free — where Biot–Savart and the
  vortex-stretching estimate live (`l.421–438`), and where Prop. 3.1's small-enstrophy criterion
  is stated;
* **inner:** Gaussian-weighted `L²_μ`, `μ = e^{-|y|²/4}` (`l.926–944`), in which
  `−Δ + ½y·∇` is **self-adjoint** with discrete spectrum `{n/2}` on the vector Hermite basis, and
  `R = J − (Jy)·∇` is **skew-adjoint**, acting as `−∂_θ`, i.e. multiplication by `−ik` on the
  angular Fourier index. **They commute** (`l.1148–1152`), so the linear part is **normal** and
  simultaneously diagonalisable.

**The diagonal, measured not asserted.** In the joint `(n, k)` eigenbasis the linear part of the
vorticity equation, `αR + 1 + (−Δ + ½y·∇)`, is **diagonal with entries `1 + n/2 − iαk`**, of
modulus `≥ 1` for every `(n,k)` and growing like `n/2`. The runner builds the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
matrix on the Hermite basis and confirms the spectrum `{n/2}` to **max abs error 0**, and confirms
`R` skew-adjoint and commuting to machine zero.

**Why that number matters here specifically.** Leg 57 established, from Breden–Desvillettes–Lessard
`arXiv:1503.06315` assumptions (4)–(5) read at full PDF, that the radii-polynomial machinery
**requires a diagonal bounded away from zero**; leg 158 recorded that this repository's Hermite
**differentiation** matrix has **zero diagonal**. Those are *different operators*. The object here
is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator itself, which is exactly diagonal in its own eigenbasis and,
once the vorticity equation's zeroth-order term is added, bounded below by 1 in modulus. **That is
the precise structural difference**, and it is the reason this space is a *namable candidate* for
the fourth space the re-posed stage-V ban asks for.

**Enclosure meaning — named.** A **bordered** radii-polynomial / Newton–Kantorovich enclosure: a

## §7 — Derived facts (this leg's inferences, labelled as such — none is a quotation)

**D1 — nontrivial RSS at `α ≠ 0` is *necessarily* non-axisymmetric, and `RU ≠ 0`.**
`RU = 0` iff `U` is axisymmetric (`l.882–892`, `l.897`). If `U` is axisymmetric then
`R(αs)U(R(−αs)y) = U(y)`, so the RSS ansatz collapses to the plain SS ansatz — the rotation does
nothing — and `U` solves the Leray system, where NRS/Tsai/Chae–Wolf kill it. **Consequence:** leg
253's forward item (2) ("if the candidate is axisymmetric it cannot be self-similar or DSS") is
*automatically satisfied* here. Non-axisymmetry is **not an extra hypothesis to impose; it is
forced.** The axisymmetric Type-I exclusion therefore cannot reach this class, and neither can
leg 253's axisymmetric-DSS composition.

**D2 — every nontrivial candidate in this class has *infinite kinetic energy*, necessarily.**
Suppose `U ∈ L²(ℝ³)`. Type I gives `U ∈ L^∞`, so
`‖U‖³_{L³} ≤ ‖U‖_{L^∞}‖U‖²_{L²} < ∞`, i.e. `U ∈ L³`. But the RSS ansatz gives
`‖u(·,t)‖_{L³} = ‖U‖_{L³}` for every `t` and every `α` (PV state exactly this, `l.223`), so
`u ∈ L^∞_t L³` and Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák forces regularity, hence `U ≡ 0`. Contrapositive:
**`U` nontrivial ⇒ `U ∉ L²(ℝ³)`.**

> **This is the single most consequential line in this leg.** A certified nontrivial RSS profile
> would **not** be a Clay counterexample. Clay asks for blow-up from smooth, finite-energy,
> rapidly decaying data; this class **structurally cannot supply it**. The lane's *maximum* payoff
> is resolving **Perelman's conjecture** negatively — a genuine, expert-flagged open problem, but
> **not the Clay problem**. The step between them is the classical localisation problem, untouched
> by PV and untouched here.

**D3 — the linearisation has a guaranteed nontrivial kernel element `RU`.** Derived in §6.
