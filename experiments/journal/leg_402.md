### 0.2.1 The test, stated as the predicate I will apply

**W2's 2D-LIFT PREDICATE (as banked).** A certified profile on `ℝ³` (or `T³`) is a **2D LIFT** when,
in the certificate's own Cartesian coordinates:

- (L1) it has **no dependence on one Cartesian coordinate** — zero modes in `x₃`, extent 1 in `x₃`; and
- (L2) the **corresponding velocity component vanishes identically**, `u⁽³⁾ ≡ 0`; and
- (L3) consequently **two of the three vorticity components vanish identically**, `ω⁽¹⁾ ≡ ω⁽²⁾ ≡ 0`,
  with only `ω⁽³⁾` nonzero.

It is **genuinely 3D** when the conjunction (L1) ∧ (L2) ∧ (L3) **fails**.

**PRE-COMMITTED, BEFORE READING — the four adjudication rules that will decide clause 2.** These are
fixed now precisely because I do not yet know which case the paper falls into.

- **R2-a.** If the paper ships decodable certified data (coefficient arrays / `.mat`-equivalent), I

- **R2-b.** If the paper ships no decodable arrays, I apply the **same predicate** to the paper's own

- **R2-c — THE AXISYMMETRIC CASE, PRE-COMMITTED NOW SO IT CANNOT BE DECIDED AFTER THE FACT.**
  An **axisymmetric** profile reduces to two *independent variables* `(r, z)`, and it is tempting to
  call that a 2D object. **Under W2's test as banked it is NOT a 2D lift**, and I pre-commit to that
  reading now:
  - axisymmetric **with swirl** (`u_θ ≢ 0`): in Cartesian, `u⁽³⁾ = u_z ≢ 0`, the field depends on
    `x₃ = z`, and all three `ω` components are generically nonzero. (L1), (L2), (L3) all fail ⇒ **3D**.
  - axisymmetric **without swirl** (`u_θ ≡ 0`): in Cartesian, `u⁽³⁾ = u_z ≢ 0`, the field depends on
    `x₃ = z`, `ω = ω_θ e_θ` has `ω⁽¹⁾, ω⁽²⁾ ≢ 0` and `ω⁽³⁾ ≡ 0`. This is the **mirror image** of the
    `1902.00384` pattern, not the pattern itself: (L1) and (L2) both fail ⇒ **3D under W2's test**.
  - **In both sub-cases I will ALSO report, as a flagged OBSERVATION that is NOT folded into the
