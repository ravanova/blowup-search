## R1 — the a-sign. **CONFIRMED**, with one qualifier §24 did not carry.

§24's reading, verbatim: *"the two-scale scenario is described as the **a <= 0** case, with
**a > 0 giving ONE-scale self-similar blowups**."*

**p.1, Abstract, verbatim:**

> our numerical study reveals distinct self-similar blowup behaviors depending on the sign of
> 𝑎. For 𝑎 > 0, we observe one-scale self-similar blowups with regular profiles that have not
> been found in previous studies. In contrast, for 𝑎 ≤ 0, we discover a novel two-scale
> self-similar blowup scenario where the outer profile converges to a singular function at the
> blowup time while the inner profile remains regular on a much smaller scale.

Restated independently in the body, **p.3**: *"For 𝑎 > 0, we observe that smooth degenerate
initial data could lead to asymptotically self-similar finite-time blowups with undiscovered
regular profiles…"*

**The qualifier.** The dichotomy is **conditional on degenerate initial data**. **p.3**,
verbatim: *"we call an initial datum **degenerate** if 𝜔 has zero first derivative at the
eventual blowup point. **The main purpose of this paper is to find out whether and how
self-similar blowups can develop from degenerate initial data** and in what ways they are
different from those in the non-degenerate case."* It is not a general statement about gCLM at
`a > 0`, and §24 — reading a search summary — carried the dichotomy without the condition.

**What that does to §24's inference.** §24 concluded: *"If that sign is right the question is
not whether our object is novel but whether it is the **RIGHT OBJECT**."* The sign is right and
**the inference does not follow**. Our `a > 0` traveling wave is a real object:

* **p.15, Theorem 2.7** (proved as Theorem 7.9, p.44): *"For any 𝑎 ∈ (−∞, 1), the generalized
  Constantin–Lax–Majda model admits a traveling wave solution of the form (2.16) (up to
  rescaling)…"*
* **p.45, Theorem 7.10(3):** *"0 < 𝑎 < 1: 𝜔 is compactly supported on [−𝐿𝜔, 𝐿𝜔]…"* — which is
  exactly what §21/§23 measured.

The a-sign dichotomy governs **which blowup scenario degenerate initial data produce**. Whether
the traveling wave exists at `a > 0` is a different question, and its published answer is *yes*.
So the object is right — **and it is somebody else's.** Which is R2.

## R2 — is their fixed point our first integral? **CONFIRMED, in the strongest available form.**

Ours, `PHASE2_P2_NOTES.md` §23, verbatim:

> **Omega(X) = -( E(X)/c )^{1/a},   E = c + aU,   U_X = H(Omega).**      (FI)

Theirs, **p.38**, the *definition* of the map:

> `R_a(ω)(x) = ( 1 − a T(ω)(x) / r(ω) )^{1/a}_+ , ω ∈ 𝔻_a`,  with
> `T(ω)(x) = (1/π) ∫ ln|(x−y)/y| ω(y) dy` and `r(ω) = (1/π) ∫₀^∞ (ω(0)−ω(y))/y² dy`  (7.2)

Under `(ω, u, r) = (−Ω, −U, c)` these are the same function. **It is not "related to" or "an
analogue of" — it is the same closed form, including the `(·)₊` truncation that §23 derived
separately as "the profile ENDS".**

And **p.39**, their proof of the converse half of Proposition 7.1, is our derivation verbatim:

> if (𝜔(𝑥), 𝑟) is a solution to (7.1) … then for 𝑥 such that 𝜔(𝑥) ≠ 0, we have
> **𝜔′/𝜔 = 𝑢′/(𝑎𝑢 − 𝑟). Solving this ODE and writing 𝑢 = 𝑻(𝜔) gives** 𝜔(𝑥) = (1 − 𝑎𝑻(𝜔)(𝑥)/𝑟(𝜔))^{1/𝑎}₊

against §23's:

> `R = Omega H(Omega) - E Omega_X = 0  <=>  (log|Omega|)_X = (1/a)(log E)_X  =>  |Omega| = C E^{1/a}`

Same equation, same substitution, same order of operations, published first.

**The correspondence fixes the constant, not just the form.** **p.37**, their `a = 0` case:
*"which admits an explicit solution 𝜔(𝑥) = 1/(1+𝑥²), 𝑢(𝑥) = ½ ln(1+𝑥²), 𝑟 = ½."* §23's anchor:
*"on the anchor U = -(1/2)log(1+X^2), c = 1/2, so Omega = -1/(1+X^2) **exactly** — measured

### Three of §23's "free" consequences are their theorems

§23 listed three things the first integral gave "for free". Two are proved in this paper and one
is an immediate corollary of their formula:

* §23: *"The profile ENDS, forced not discovered … **CAVEAT SAID OUT LOUD: needs H(Omega)<0 for
  X>0 — true on every solution found, not proved.**"* → **p.45, Theorem 7.10(3)** proves compact
  support for `0 < a < 1`, and the p.46 proof supplies **exactly the caveated ingredient**
  (monotonicity of `T(ω)` with `T(ω)(0)=0`, Lemma 7.4).
* §23: *"Only finitely smooth … classical (C^1) exactly while a<1."* → **p.47, Theorem 7.11(2):**
  *"0 < 𝑎 < 1: 𝜔 is compactly supported … and 𝜔 is smooth in the interior of (−𝐿𝜔, 𝐿𝜔)."*
* §23's **edge exponent** `Ω ~ −A(X_c−X)^{1/a}`, fitted to rel err ≤ **1.4e−5** — **not** stated
  as a theorem there. It is an immediate corollary of their own formula (`g` is `C¹` and vanishes
  linearly at `L_ω`), so it is not available as a claim either.

### Where the priority actually lies — §24 guessed the wrong paper

**p.37, §7.1, first sentence:** *"we follow the idea of **[HQWW24]**…"*, and **p.15, §2.5:**
*"Using a similar fixed-point method as in [HQWW24]…"*. Their bibliography (p.50) resolves
`[HQWW24]` to **Huang–Qin–Wang–Wei, ARMA 248(2):22, 2024 = arXiv:2305.05895** — a paper §24
already had, at abstract level.

So I read that one too. **p.8**, its map:

> `R_a(f)(x) := ( T_a(f)(x)^{1/a} · exp( ((1−a)/a) ∫₀^x (T_a(f)(y) − T_a(f)(0))/(y T_a(f)(y)) dy ) )`

**Same trick, different algebra.** The extra exponential factor is forced by their ansatz
`Ω(x) = −x f(x)` for the **self-similar** profile equation, whose `c_l X Ω_X` term contributes a
`1/y` kernel the **traveling-wave** equation does not have. Corroborated independently by term
extraction: `"traveling wave"` occurs **0** times in 2305.05895 against **37** in 2603.25104.

**Conclusion.** (FI) in our exact algebraic form is 2603.25104's traveling-wave specialization
