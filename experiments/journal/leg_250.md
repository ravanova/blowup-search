# Leg 250 — ROUTE-PUB2FIX — the three `σ_min` citation sites in PUB2

**Gate.** *Do all three `σ_min` citation sites in PUB2 now state the relationship between leg
163's witness and leg 176's true value accurately, with §3.5's own conflict note updated to match
rather than left redundant?*

**Answer: YES.** All three sites fixed, §3.5 rewritten from an open flag into a resolved
provenance note, and the blog's companion paragraph brought into line. Ruling 1(b) closes: PUB2
carries no internally unreconciled numeric conflict.

## The determination, made before any prose was edited

The brief asked which framing is correct — leg 163's `0.71465` as a looser earlier BOUND, or as a
wrong MEASUREMENT. **It is neither, and the true reading is checkable arithmetic.**

`0.71465` is exactly `1/1.3993` — the reciprocal of leg 163's own largest sampled ratio
(`1/1.3993 = 0.7146430`, rounded up in the fifth decimal). Leg 163's G4 row reads
`‖u‖_X/‖f‖_X = 1.3993 / 1.2680 / 1.3769` *"so a lower witness `σ_min ≥ 0.7147`"*. With `f = L u`
each datum gives `‖f‖_X/‖u‖_X = 1/r ≥ σ_min`, so a finite family of sampled ratios bounds the
infimum **only from above**. The inequality is **inverted**: leg 163's own data support
`σ_min ≤ 0.71465`.

**So the error is a sign, not a magnitude, and the two legs never actually disagreed** —
`0.0908 ≤ 0.71465` holds comfortably. Leg 176 was not contradicting leg 163's number; it measured
the quantity leg 163's number was only ever an upper bound on.

Magnitudes supporting the reading, all from banked data:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| leg 163's reciprocals, three data | 0.7146430 / 0.7886435 / 0.7262692 |
| sharpest (the quoted figure) | **0.7146430** → `0.71465` |
| true `‖R‖_X = ‖L⁻¹‖_X` | **11.0127** |
| fraction of `‖R‖_X` leg 163's best datum reached | **12.71 %** |
| fraction leg 176's four-datum re-run reached (0.868155) | **7.88 %** |
| reciprocal of that re-run family | **1.1519** |
| leg 176 near-minimizer ratio vs `1/σ_min` | 11.010373 vs 11.010374 (**1e−6**) |
| discrepancy as quoted by leg 176 | **7.87×** (`0.71465 / 0.0908`) |

Row 6 is the decisive consistency check: under the erroneous `≥` reading, leg 163's `0.71465` and
leg 176's re-run `1.1519` would both be claimed *lower* bounds on the same quantity and would
contradict each other. Under the corrected `≤` reading they are simply two valid, differently
loose upper bounds. **Only the corrected sign makes the repository's own numbers self-consistent.**

## A second thing the corrected sign exposed, and it is now written down

The `≥` at the three sites was carrying the claim "**bounded away from zero**", i.e. a lower
bound — and **neither leg proves one**. Leg 176's domain-only truncation is in its own words
*"an upper bound closing down onto `σ_min`"*, and its ladder is *"float64 evidence of a positive
limit, not a proof of one."* So `0.0908` is a converged upper bound too.

What is actually established is a monotone-decreasing ladder that **flattens** — 0.0908878 →
0.0908047 over `N = 64 … 512`, a **0.0915 %** move (0.139 % over the full 16-fold range) — plus
two controls reporting the other answer (unbordered collapses to the float floor, 1.55e−15 →
6.97e−14; loose `L²` decays like `N^{−1.49}`). Against `ℓ¹_w`, where the analogous ladder *decays*
like `M^{−(1−s)}`. The `ℓ¹_w`-vs-origin-`H²` contrast §3–§5 rests on is a contrast of **ladder
behaviour in both spaces**, not of a proved bound in either, so it survives the correction intact.
PUB2 now says exactly that, and claims no proved floor anywhere.
