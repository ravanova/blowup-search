## The answer, and the line

**Gate branch (i): the hypotheses exclude a zero/vanishing diagonal.** The line, page 6:

> **Assumption 1.** *Let l be defined in (2). Assume that there exists ρ > 0 such that l is
> analytic on the strip I_ρ … Moreover, assume that there exists* **l_min > 0** *such that*
> **|l(ξ) | ≥ l_min for all ξ ∈ ℝᵐ** *and lim_{|ξ|₂→+∞} |l(ξ)| = +∞.*

`L` is the linear part and, by the class definition (1)–(2), it is a **Fourier multiplier**:
`ℱ(Lu)(ξ) = l(ξ)ℱ(u)(ξ)`. So **l *is* the diagonal**, and a zero/vanishing diagonal is
exactly the case `l_min = 0` that Assumption 1 excludes — by hypothesis, on the paper's
first page of setup, before anything is constructed.

**8 of 12 located clauses are decisive**, i.e. the exclusion is load-bearing in eight
independent places, not one:

| clause | where | what fails at a zero diagonal |
|---|---|---|
| `A1_LMIN` | Assumption 1, p.6 | the hypothesis itself |
| `SPACE_H` | (9), p.6 | Assumption 1 *"allows to define"* `H`; `L : H → L²` is an isometric isomorphism only if `1/l` is bounded |
| `SPACE_XQ` | §2.2, p.6 | the Fourier-side norm is weighted by `|l(n/2q)|²` — a vanishing mode has zero norm |
| `SIGMA_DELTA` | (14), p.8 | `σ_δ = {λ : |l(ξ)−λ| > δ ∀ξ}`; at `λ=0` this **is** the non-vanishing condition, and Lemma 2.2 puts the complement in the **essential** spectrum |

| `TAIL_DIAGONAL_IS_THE_SYMBOL` | §3, p.10 | `λ_n = l(ñ) + (DG(U₀))_{n,n}` — the Gershgorin **centres** are literally the symbol |
| `SYSTEMS_KAPPA` | §5.3, p.26 | the systems form carries `κ = sup_ξ ‖l(ξ)^{-1}‖₂`, `+∞` the moment the matrix symbol is singular |

## The distinction the question turns on — and it is not pedantry

**A zero *eigenvalue* is covered. A zero *diagonal* is not.** §5.2 encloses the Whitham
translation kernel to `[−0.0011, 0.0011]` and concludes `ν₂ = 0` exactly. So "zero is a
problem for this framework" is **false** about eigenvalues and **true** about the diagonal.
Read the ban's question the other way and one would answer *"yes, covered"* — the wrong
answer to the right words. This is recorded as a `COUNTER-DIRECTION` clause in the JSON
because a scope answer that suppresses the one clause pointing the other way is not a
measurement.

## Magnitudes, not booleans

* **`l_min` on the paper's own four examples:** `0.2` (Whitham, `1 − c`), `0.28` and `0.32`
  (Swift-Hohenberg square / hexagonal, `= µ`), `0.99994` (Gray-Scott, `σ_min` of the 2×2
  matrix symbol). Range `[0.2, 0.99994]`, **worst reproduction error against what the
  author states: 1.416e-07**. Confirmation of leg 62's numbers at a second run, reported as
  confirmation and never as new.
* **The dial from covered to uncovered is the paper's own parameter.** On Swift-Hohenberg,
  `l(ξ) = −(1−|2πξ|₂²)² − µ`, so `l_min = µ` **exactly**: measured on a grid dense at the
  unit circle, `max |l_min − µ| = 0.0e+00` across `µ ∈ {0, 1e-6, …, 1.0}`, and at `µ = 0`
  the measured `l_min` is `0.0` attained on the whole circle `|2πξ| = 1`. **Cadiot's leading
  example is a one-parameter family that crosses the boundary of its own hypothesis, and
  his two published runs sit 0.28 and 0.32 from a genuine zero diagonal.**
* **The method dies at a *finite* gap, not at zero.** Remark 5.2, p.24: against an
  essential spectrum starting at `0.2`, eigenvalues are enclosed out to `0.16`
  (**gap 0.04**) and the eigenvalue near `0.19` (**gap 0.01**) *"we were not able to
  enclose"*, because the `Z_{u,i}` blow up as the eigenvector delocalises. This is
  **stronger than the hypothesis alone gives**, and it is the author's own statement.

## Two internal inconsistencies in the source, recorded not smoothed

Both **verified against the rendered page image of p.24**, so neither is an extraction
artefact, and neither is load-bearing for this leg's answer:

1. Lemma 5.4's proof prints *"we fix δ = 0.4"* and one sentence later
   *"σ_δ = (−∞, 0.16)"*. With `l_min = 0.2` the second forces `δ = 0.04`; Remark 5.2's
   *"δ < 0.1"* likewise has to be `δ < 0.01` to reach `0.19`. A factor-of-ten slip in the
   δ's.
2. Lemma 5.4 states *"exactly three eigenvalues in (−∞, 0.16]"* and then lists
   `ν₁ ∈ [0.2691, 0.2704]`, which is not in that interval (and would be embedded in the
   essential spectrum, where this machinery cannot reach). Most plausibly a sign slip,
   `ν₁ ∈ [−0.2704, −0.2691]`. **Recorded, not resolved.**

# BLOG — the sentence a ban was waiting on

For a while this project has been carrying a ban with a hole in it. The ban says: don't
re-claim leg 51's methodological finding at full strength. The reason attached to the ban
kept changing. First it was Breden–Desvillettes–Lessard — until leg 57 read their PDF and
found their assumptions (4)–(5) need a diagonal bounded away from zero, so BDL never
covered our case and that reason was discharged. A stronger reason took its place: Cadiot's
2025 paper says the same thing, in full text, in §2 and §3. And then the ban was left with
an explicit, honest note that the *actual* open question had moved: **does Cadiot's
construction cover a zero diagonal?** Nobody had answered it. Leg 57 said so. Leg 62 read
the paper carefully but was asking a different question — about off-diagonal unbounded
parts, which is our operator's problem, not the ban's wording.

This leg went and read the sentence.

It is on page 6, and it is Assumption 1. *"assume that there exists l_min > 0 such that
|l(ξ)| ≥ l_min for all ξ ∈ ℝᵐ."* That is the whole answer. The linear part of Cadiot's
equations is a Fourier multiplier, which means it *is* a diagonal — the symbol `l` is
literally the list of diagonal entries. Requiring `l_min > 0` is requiring the diagonal to
be bounded away from zero. A zero diagonal is not something the paper fails to handle; it
is something the paper's first hypothesis rules out before the machinery starts.

What makes it more than a quote is how many other things break with it. The Hilbert space
the whole argument runs in is *defined* by that hypothesis — the paper says so, in those
words: Assumption 1 "allows to define" the space `H`, in which `L` is an isometric
isomorphism. The Fourier-side space is weighted by `|l|²`, so it degenerates too. The
region where eigenvalues can be enclosed at all is `{λ : |l(ξ) − λ| > δ}` — put `λ = 0` in
that and you get back the non-vanishing condition, and outside it you are in the
*essential* spectrum, the one part of the picture this framework computes rather than
encloses. Eight places, one hypothesis.

The nicest number is the one the paper hands over without meaning to. Its leading example
is planar Swift-Hohenberg, `l(ξ) = −(1−|2πξ|²)² − µ`, and for that symbol `l_min` is
exactly `µ`. We measured it on a grid across five orders of magnitude and the error is
zero. At `µ = 0` — classical Swift-Hohenberg at onset — the symbol vanishes on an entire
circle. So the paper's own leading model is a one-parameter family that walks right up to
the excluded case, and the two published runs sit `0.28` and `0.32` away from it. The
exclusion has a *distance*, in the author's own units.

And the paper is more candid than its hypothesis. In Remark 5.2 Cadiot reports an
eigenvalue near `0.19` that he could **not** enclose, with the essential spectrum starting
at `0.2` — a gap of `0.01`, where a gap of `0.04` had worked. The constants blow up as the
eigenvector spreads out. So it is not a knife edge at zero: the method is already in
trouble at a finite distance from a vanishing diagonal.

One thing had to be got right or the answer would have been confidently wrong. Zero as an
*eigenvalue* is completely fine here — Cadiot encloses one, the translation kernel of the
Whitham solitary wave, and pins it to exactly `0`. If you read "does it cover a zero?" as a
question about eigenvalues, the answer is yes. The ban's question is about the *diagonal*.
Those are different objects and the distinction is the whole leg.

So: the ban keeps its footing, and for the first time that footing is a quoted line with a
sha256 next to it rather than an inference. What the ban does *next* is not this leg's
call. Its lift condition routes through the user, and answering a question is not the same
act as changing a rule.

# TECHNICAL — Route-CADX v1

**Object.** `arXiv:2505.03091v1`, sha256 `0f1bc618…1f8081d`, read at source at full text.
**Question.** The lift condition of the standing ban on re-claiming leg 51's methodological
finding at full strength: does Cadiot's construction cover a zero diagonal?
**Answer.** No — branch (i). Excluded by hypothesis, quoted, with eight decisive use sites.

**§1 The class, and why `l` is the diagonal.** Cadiot studies `F(u) = Lu + G(u) = 0` on
`ℝᵐ` with `L` a **Fourier multiplier**, `ℱ(Lu)(ξ) = l(ξ)ℱ(u)(ξ)` (eq. (2)). In the Fourier
coefficient representation `L_q U = (l(n/2q) u_n)_n`: the linear part is diagonal in the
Fourier index **by construction**, and the symbol is the diagonal. §3's identity for
`n ∉ I^N` makes this explicit at the level of the Gershgorin centres:
`λ_n = l(ñ) + (DG(U₀))_{n,n}`.

**§2 The hypothesis.** Assumption 1 (p.6) requires (a) analyticity of `l` on a strip `I_ρ`,
(b) `|l(ξ)| ≥ l_min` for some `l_min > 0` and all `ξ ∈ ℝᵐ`, and (c) `|l(ξ)| → +∞`.
Assumption 2 constrains the nonlinear multipliers through the **ratio** `g^p_{j,k}/l`,
requiring it analytic on `I_ρ` with `g^p_{j,k}(·+is)/l(·+is) ∈ L¹` — i.e. Assumption 2 is
*also* stated in terms of division by `l`, and is meaningless where `l` vanishes. The two
assumptions together give Remark 2.1's exponential decay
`ℱ^{-1}(g^p_{i,k}/l)(x) = O(e^{−2πρ|x|₁})`, which the paper calls *"one of the main
features of the framework."*

**§3 Where non-vanishing is consumed.** Four structural sites and four proof sites:

1. `H ≝ {u ∈ L² : ‖u‖_H ≝ ‖Lu‖₂ < ∞}` (eq. (9)), introduced by *"Assumption 1 allows to
   define the Hilbert space H"*, with *"By construction L : H → L² is an isometric
   isomorphism."* Surjectivity with bounded inverse is `sup 1/|l| < ∞`.
2. `X_q` with `(U,V)_{X_q} = Σ_n u_n v_n |l(n/2q)|²`: not an inner product if `l` vanishes
   on the lattice.
3. `σ_δ ≝ {λ ∈ ℂ : |l(ξ) − λ| > δ ∀ξ}` (eq. (14)) with Lemma 2.2's identification
   `σ_ess(DF(ũ)) = ℝᵐ \ σ₀ = range(l)`. `0 ∈ σ_δ ⟺ l_min > δ`.
4. Proposition 2.1's shift `t`: built from `|l(ξ)| → ∞` and boundedness of `DG(U₀)L^{-1}`,
   the latter finite only for `l` bounded below.
5. Lemma 3.1: *"(L + tI)^{-1} : ℓ² → ℓ² is compact **thanks to Assumption 1**"* — the
   discreteness of `σ(DF(U₀))` is charged to it by name.
6. Lemma 3.2's generalized-Gershgorin shift: needs one `s ∈ ℂ` with `|λ_n + s| > r_n/2` at
   **every** `n`, obtained from `|l(ñ)| → ∞`.
7. Lemma 4.1's proof: *"because λ ∈ σ_δ, we know that L − λI is invertible"*, and every
   constant `Z_{u,1..3}, C_{1,2}, Z_{1,1..4}` is a norm of an operator containing
   `(L − µI)^{-1}` uniformly over `µ ∈ J ⊂ σ_δ`.
8. §5.3's systems constant `κ = sup_{ξ∈ℝ²} ‖l(ξ)^{-1}‖₂`, appearing in every bound of the
   only multi-component example. The systems case therefore **restates** the hypothesis in
   matrix form (invertibility of the matrix symbol, uniformly) rather than weakening it.

**§4 The one clause pointing the other way.** §5.2 encloses `ν₂ ∈ [−0.0011, 0.0011]` and,
using the 1-D translation kernel, concludes `ν₂ = 0`. The construction covers a **zero
eigenvalue**. It does not cover a **zero diagonal**. The ban's wording is the latter.

**§5 Measured magnitudes.**

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| `l_min`, Whitham (`1−c`, `T=0.5`, `c=0.8`) | `0.19999999999999996` (author states `0.2`) |
| `l_min`, SH square / hexagonal | `0.28` / `0.32` (`= µ`) |
| `σ_min`, Gray-Scott matrix symbol (`λ₁=1/9, λ₂=10`) | `0.999937653995348` |
| worst reproduction error vs author-stated | `1.416e-07` |
| `max |l_min − µ|`, SH `µ`-dial over `{0,1e-6,…,1.0}` | `0.0e+00` |
| `l_min` at `µ = 0` | `0.0` (attained on `|2πξ| = 1`) |
| Whitham gap that enclosed | `0.04` (out to `λ = 0.16`) |
| Whitham gap that failed (Remark 5.2) | `0.01` (eigenvalue `≈ 0.19`) |
| decisive exclusions / located clauses | `8 / 12` |
| quote probes verified against the pinned PDF | `11 / 11` |
| forward citations relaxing the hypothesis | `0 / 2` (this leg) `0 / 5` (with leg 62's) |

**§6 Controls (lesson 90).** `covers_zero_diagonal` returns *covers* on a synthetic scope
with Assumption 1 deleted and on the real scope plus a hypothesis-relaxing forward
citation; it returns *does not cover* on the located clauses. The negative is a property of
the clauses.

**§7 Scope, and what this does not establish.** This is a statement about the coverage of
one published construction. It is **not** a claim that leg 51's methodological finding is
true; **not** a novelty claim (the observation is folklore in print, leg 57, and `A1_LMIN`
was already located by leg 62); **not** a deficiency claim against Cadiot; and **not** a
lift, narrowing or discharge of the ban, which is integration-owned and the user's call.
**Clay odds ~0.05%, unmoved** — no link of the L1→L4 chain is touched by a literature scope
answer.
