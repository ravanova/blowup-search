## 3. The discriminators — and the one that turned out not to discriminate

Leg 175's own two, restated: **D1** — was the technique *applied* to the resisting object and
did it still resist? **D2** — does the paper name a **different model class** in the passage
where the obstruction is stated, and rank difficulty by model? Two more were needed and were
fixed in the novelty pass before the evidence was applied: **D3** — in how many *distinct
model classes* does the paper itself exhibit the same obstruction? **D4** — are all remedies
offered apparatus-side?

**Rule, pre-committed:** `MODEL_SPECIFIC` iff D1 ∧ D2; `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC` iff D3 ≥ 2 ∧ D4 ∧
¬(D1 ∧ D2); else `UNDETERMINED`.

**`D1` is TRUE on all three, and therefore discriminates nothing here.** That is not a defect
in the evidence — it is structural: all three obstructions are, by construction, *what is left
after* the loss-reweighting fix leg 175 identified, so "the technique was applied and it still
resisted" is true of each by definition. **A control whose value is identical on every input is
not a control (lesson 90).** It is recorded and then set aside as non-decisive. **D2 does the
work**, and the difference between this leg's answer and leg 175's is entirely a D2 difference:
leg 175 measured D1 = *No* (0 of 4) and got `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`; here D1 = *Yes* everywhere and
only obstruction B also has D2.

## 4. Obstruction A (N1) — λ's error bar. **TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC**, a *precision* gap

**D2 = no.** §2.3.3 is internal to IPM throughout and attributes the loss to **mode order** and
to **"a fixed computational budget"** (l.683–687). No alternative model class appears in it.
The string *"per model"* at l.769 is, in context, *"per successive singularity"* — l.686 states
the same quantity as *"for each successive singularity"*. Recorded because it is the one string
in the passage that could be misread as a cross-model claim; it is not one.

**D3 = 3 classes**, and the third is the load-bearing one, new to this leg: the identical
structure — *a parameter of interest pinned far worse than the residual, because the loss
cannot see it* — occurs for **λ in IPM** (§2.3.3, Fig. 7f), for **λ in CCF** (§2.3.1
l.466–475: the second-stage origin signal *"is spurious… prevents us from finding a more
accurate smooth λs"*, sign flipping between random seeds), **and for the core coefficient
`a_n` in Gross–Pitaevskii vortices** (§3.2.1 l.941–944: *"this plateau is a spurious artifact
caused by the insensitivity of the standard loss function to the extremely small value of `U_n`
around the origin"*) — an equation with **no fluid transport, no self-similarity and no λ at
all**, fixed by the same normalization. **D4 = yes**: larger networks, a third stage, more
memory, more GPUs (l.770–775).

**Magnitude.** Residual `1e-13` against λ accuracy `1e-7` at IPM's 4th unstable = **6.0
decades**; degradation **1.570 decades/mode** fitted over the five Fig. 7(f) rows against
**2.0** stated (ratio **1.274×**). Both reproduce legs 196 and 212 exactly. Fitted and stated
are **not the same quantity** (best-achieved vs at-fixed-budget) — they agree in order of
magnitude, which is what makes this a cross-check rather than a tautology.

## 5. Obstruction B (N2) — CCF's 3rd unstable. **MODEL_SPECIFIC**, a *scope* gap

**D2 = yes, and the paper names the class itself**, §2.1.3 l.278–280:

> *"The challenge of exploring the range λ < 0.455 and resolving such a feature in the 1D CCF
> model appears to be even greater than finding highly unstable solutions in the 2D IPM
> equation."*

This is the **only** sentence in 27 pages that ranks difficulty between two named models, and
its direction is the surprising one: **the 1D model is rated harder than the 2D one.**

**The paper's own reasoning**, which the gate asks for precisely, is three strands:
1. **Profile geometry, as a function of the model's own parameter.** *"as λ decreases, the
   solution's peak amplitude increases dramatically, reaching nearly 40 for λ ≈ 0.455"*
   (l.271–272) ⇒ a third solution *"would possess even more extreme gradients than the second"*.
2. **The residual fix worked and was not the binding constraint.** *"While our
   gradient-normalization method effectively reduces the residual in the peak region, a
   persistent non-smooth signal at the origin… remains for all tested λ ∈ [0.455, 0.4713]"*
   (l.275–277).
3. **Existence is conditional.** *"**if** a third unstable solution exists"* (l.277); *"A full
   investigation into the **existence** and properties…"* (l.280). **No amount of precision
   decides an existence question** — this is the one strand among all three obstructions that
   is not a numerics statement at all.

**D3 = 1**, and the counter-evidence is explicit: on IPM the frontier **advanced** (+1 mode,
the 4th unstable newly confirmed); on NLS excited states the analogous "cannot reach the target
solution" problem was **solved** by targeted inequality constraints (§3.3.3 l.1187–1212). CCF
is the only class where the frontier moved **0 modes**.

**Magnitude, and it is deliberately not a decade count (lesson 73).** Searched window
λ ∈ [0.455, 0.4713], **width 0.0163**; peak amplitude at the low end **≈ 40**; modes gained
this paper: **CCF 0** (suite stays at three — stable, 1st, 2nd, l.157) vs **IPM +1**. The
authors place the remaining search on the **open half-line λ < 0.455**, so there is **no finite
gap to quote** — when a quantity has no referent, say so rather than bound it.

## 6. Obstruction C (N3) — the double-float floor. **TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC**, an *infrastructure* gap

**D3 = 5 settings across 4 model classes**, and this is the sharpest cross-model control in the
paper: the floor is named as **double-float round-off** in every one — CCF `O(10⁻¹³)`
(l.616–620), IPM `O(10⁻¹¹)`–`O(10⁻¹³)` (l.1423–1424), NLS double-well (loss plateau `10⁻²⁹`,
l.860–862), GP vortices `O(10⁻¹⁵)` (l.948, l.1004–1005), NLS excited states (l.1159–1161).
**Three of the five settings are not fluid equations.** The paper's own definition of success
*is* the floor (§1 l.50–51). **D4 = yes**, same budget list.

**Magnitude is a zero, not a distance.** `extended precision` **0**, `quadruple` **0**,
`float64` **0**, `interval arithmetic` **0**, `enclos` **0**, `certif` **0** over all 1593
lines — the first three are this leg's addition to the census. The paper never once
contemplates arithmetic tighter than double, and its entire discussion of doing better is
`memory` (**1** occurrence) and `GPU` (**2**, audited: the raw count of 4 includes two base64
payloads inside `<latexit>` blobs, which would have overstated the hardware discussion 2×).

A certificate needs arithmetic **tighter** than the object it encloses. **The distance from
this floor to a CAP is not measurable from this paper, because the paper contains no quantity
of that kind** — the same shape of negative leg 173 recorded for Xu's gap (2): not *far from*
usable, but **undefined** off its own construction.
