## §1. GATE (a) — DOES IT BUILD?

## **NOT-ESTABLISHED — AND THE BLOCKER IS MEASURED AND NAMED, not guessed at.**

**`mathlib4.blob.core.windows.net:443` is DENIED by this environment's egress policy** — the
agent proxy's own `recentRelayFailures` records `connect_rejected`, *"gateway answered 502 to
CONNECT (policy denial or upstream failure)"*, at `2026-09-09T16:10:55.443Z`. **That is the
mathlib olean cache.** `lake exe cache get` therefore has nothing to get, and **mathlib itself
must be compiled from source before one line of this project is elaborated.** The blocked host
is reported, not routed around.

**A second, different failure is banked beside it so the two are not collapsed.** The first
`lake exe cache get` also died mid-clone with `error: RPC failed; curl 56 Recv failure:
Connection reset by peer`. The proxy log shows this one is **not** a policy denial:
`ws_closed_mid_exchange — tunnel closed (code 1006) after 748s; 461,265,558 B received`. A relay
timeout on a long transfer. **The clone succeeded on the next attempt.** One line of log would
have merged a permanent denial and a transient reset into *"the build did not work"*.

**The build was started anyway and was left running** — `lake build`, compiling mathlib from
source, **~385 mathlib modules in the first ~10 minutes on 4 cores.** Early mathlib modules are
the cheap ones; **no total is extrapolated from a ten-minute sample and none is written here.**

**What is established.** `elan` installs; **`leanprover/lean4:v4.34.0-rc2` installs** and reports
`Lean (version 4.34.0-rc2, x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu, commit 6a10ac8c22beadecabdbb0919c2b50214762f91d, Release)`;
`Lake version 5.0.0-src+6a10ac8`. `lake exe cache get` resolves the manifest and begins
fetching — **Comparator cloned and checked out at `19e111e2141cf333c7daff0f64c5f24acc91dd2e`,
the pinned revision, confirmed in lake's own log.** Declared mathlib rev
`85e3a25e006c35636f0e53b0e9296caca2685bc0`. **No configuration error, no manifest error, no
toolchain mismatch, and no egress refusal at any point.**

**Why it did not finish.** Host: **4 cores, 15 GB RAM, 30 GB free disk.** Dependency fetch
measured at **~30 MB/min**. With the cache host denied, the work is **mathlib4 from source
(~6,500 modules) *and then* this project's 2,486 modules** — for which no cache exists anywhere
in any case, since `lake exe cache get` serves mathlib only. **590k lines of proof term on 4
cores is hours to days, and the reason that compute is needed at all is the denied host.**

**What would settle it:** a completed `lake build` with no error, then the two `#print axioms`
lines **already present at the foot of `NavierStokes/ComparatorSolution.lean`**. Both are
mechanical once the build lands. **The build was left running; its final state is banked by U6,
and if it contradicts anything here, as a `CORRECTIONS.md` entry.**

**Not claimed:** that the project builds. That it does not build. Neither.

## §2. GATE (b) — IS THE TOP-LEVEL STATEMENT THE PAPER'S THEOREM?

## **IT IS FEFFERMAN'S (C) AND (D) EXACTLY, AND IT IS STRICTLY WEAKER THAN THE PAPER'S THEOREM 1.1.** Both halves are measured.

```lean
theorem navier_stokes_breakdown_R3 (nu : ℝ) (hnu : nu > 0) :
    ∃ (u₀ : ℝ³ → ℝ³) (f : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ³),
    InitialVelocityConditionDecay u₀ ∧ ForceConditionDecay f ∧
    ¬ (∃ v p, NavierStokesExistenceAndSmoothnessRn nu u₀ f v p)
```

### 2.1 The finding that matters: **the definitions are not OpenAI's**

The most common way a formalisation is *true but not the theorem* is a self-authored encoding
that quietly weakens a hypothesis. **That failure mode is excluded here, and I checked the
exclusion at the upstream source rather than trusting the header comment that asserts it.**

I fetched Google DeepMind's *Formal Conjectures* file at the exact commit the project pins —
`.../formal-conjectures/8bf45ed70d48b2b2a501de9c00b26bfa38c573ee/FormalConjectures/Millenium/NavierStokes.lean`,
296 lines, sha256 `f446284f2aa54375f558c263a580b34e2e7bc9f44a28829637199cc72257d25d` — and diffed
it against `ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean` from `open ContDiff` to end of file.

**Every difference is non-mathematical:** the namespace, two inlined `local notation` lines
replacing an upstream import, one `local` keyword, removal of upstream's
`@[category …]`/`@[AMS 35]` attributes, and deletion of upstream's statements **(A)** and **(B)**.
**Every definition and both breakdown theorem statements are character-for-character identical.**

Second diff: `NavierStokes/ComparatorDefinitions.lean` — the module the proof adapters import —
differs from the challenge file by **exactly the two theorem statements and nothing else.**

### 2.2 Quantifier by quantifier, against Fefferman at primary

| Fefferman (C) | Lean | |

| *"Take `ν > 0` and `n = 3`"* | `(nu : ℝ) (hnu : nu > 0)`, ambient `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)` | **MATCH** |

| **(4)** `\|∂ₓ^α u°\| ≤ C(1+\|x\|)^{−K}` | `∀ m K, ∃ C, ∀ x, ‖iteratedFDeriv ℝ m u₀ x‖ ≤ C/(1+‖x‖)^K` | **MATCH.** Order-`m` Fréchet derivative dominates every partial of that order; `C` is existential |

### 2.3 In what sense it is **weaker** than Theorem 1.1

The manuscript's Theorem 1.1 asserts **six** things (`arc6_acquire_v1.json` ::
`theorem_1_1_assertions_enumerated`). **The top-level Lean statement asserts only the sixth —
the non-existence clause. It says nothing about the constructed field at all.** The other five
are, measured:

| | manuscript assertion | in the Lean |

| A2 | compact `K ⊇ supp u(·,t) ∪ supp p(·,t)`, all `t < 1` | **proved**, `…Properties.{velocity_support,pressure_support}` |

| A4 | `sup_{0≤t<1}‖u(t)‖_{L²} < ∞` **for the constructed field** | **proved, but elsewhere** — `NavierStokesR3.CompactEnergy.uniform_finite_energy`, a separate theorem, **not** a field of `Properties`. That module **is** in the top-level import closure. |
| A5 | `limsup_{t↑1}‖u(t)‖_{L∞} = ∞` | **proved**, `…Properties.speed_unbounded` = `∀ M>0, ∀ δ>0, ∃ t ∈ Ioo 0 1, ∃ x, 1−δ < t ∧ M < ‖u(t,x)‖` — a **limsup**, matching the manuscript's own wording |

**And one thing that is defined and never proved.**
`NavierStokesR3.ProblemStatement.breakdownStatement` — **the project's own transcription of the
manuscript's Theorem 1.1, bundling every clause including `energy_bounded`** — appears in

name, and it is **not a theorem**.

### 2.4 A structural difference from the manuscript

**In the Lean, the PERIODIC object is primary and `ℝ³` is derived from it.**
`NavierStokes.ProblemStatement.candidateStatement` is the **torus** statement — its
`CandidateProperties` carry `velocity_periodic`, `pressure_periodic`, `force_periodic`.
`ActualCandidateAssembly.selected_candidate` proves that; `R3CompactCandidate.of_localized_fields`
/ `of_periodic_local_model` then localise it to the whole space.
**In the manuscript the order is reversed:** Theorem 1.1 is the `ℝ³` statement, and Corollary
10.6 periodises it. Not a defect; a measured statement that the Lean is not a transcription of
the manuscript's proof order — which is gate (d)'s business.

## §4. GATE (d) — WHAT FRACTION OF THE PAPER'S ARGUMENT IS FORMALISED AT ALL?

## **THE THEOREM IS FULLY FORMALISED. THE PAPER'S ARGUMENT IS NOT MEASURABLY FORMALISED, AND THE PROJECT DOES NOT CLAIM IT IS.**

`formalization.yaml`'s alignment has **four rows** — the two Navier–Stokes statements and the two
Euler ones. **No lemma, proposition or section of either manuscript is mapped to a Lean
declaration anywhere in the repository.**

Below that level I measured the only thing there is to measure — the Lean sources' own
cross-references to numbered results:

| | |
|---|---|
| distinct `Theorem/Lemma/Proposition/Corollary/Remark n.m` labels cited in the 2,486 files | **25** |
| present in the published Navier–Stokes manuscript | **9** |
| present only in the published Euler manuscript | 1 |
| **present in NEITHER published manuscript** | **15** |
| numbered results in the published Navier–Stokes manuscript | **73** |

**And the detail that settles it.** The published Navier–Stokes manuscript has **ten** numbered
sections plus Appendices A, B, C. **There is no Section 11.** The Lean cites **`Lemma 11.3`**,
**`Proposition 11.4`** and **`Proposition 11.7`**, and `NavierStokes/R3/ProblemStatement.lean` is
titled *"The whole-space assertion of **Part II**, Theorem 1.1"*. **The published manuscript is
not in parts.** The sources refer throughout to *"the candidate manuscript"* and *"the supplied
manuscript"*.

**Reading: the Lean was developed against a different draft from the one published on
2026-09-08** — one with at least eleven sections and at least two parts. **That is unremarkable
for a formalisation built alongside a paper, and it is not an accusation of anything.** What it
means for this gate is exact: **a lemma-by-lemma alignment between the published 166 pages and
the 2,486 Lean files cannot be read off the project, and this unit does not manufacture one.**

**The honest fraction, in two numbers because one number would be a lie:**

- **Of the paper's THEOREM: 100%**, in the only sense that is checkable — the top-level statement
  is Fefferman (C), and (D), with definitions byte-identical to an independent third party's.
  **Entirely subject to gate (a) and the kernel half of gate (c).**
- **Of the paper's ARGUMENT: not measurable from the artefact.** At most **9 of 73** numbered
  results (**12.3%**) are even *named* in the Lean, and for none of those nine did this unit
  check that the Lean statement is the manuscript's statement.

**Why both numbers are needed.** A formalised theorem and a formalised argument are different
objects. If the build passes with the three standard axioms, **the theorem is proved and the
manuscript's 166 pages stop being load-bearing for the truth of the statement.** But a reader who
wants to know whether **the paper's proof** is correct learns almost nothing from the Lean,
because the Lean does not say which of its 23,604 declarations is which page.
