## §2. WHAT TSAI ACTUALLY SAYS — VERBATIM, LOCATED

All from `Papers/TSAI1998.pdf`, `pdftotext -layout`, line numbers of that extraction.

**Theorem 1** (p.30, extraction line 128):
> "**Theorem 1.** If a weak solution `U` of (1.3) belongs to `L^q(R³)`, for some `q ∈ (3, ∞]`,
> then it must be constant (and hence identically zero if `q < ∞`)."

**Theorem 2** (p.30, lines 131–133):
> "**Theorem 2.** Suppose `u` is a weak solution of (1.1) satisfying the local energy estimates
> (1.4) in the cylinder `Q₁(0,T) = B₁(0) × (T−1, T)`. If `u` is of the form (1.2)₁, then `u` is
> identically zero."

**And, immediately after (p.30, lines 134–136) — the sentence that decides this leg:**
> "A particular corollary of these results is that **a weak solution `U` of (1.3) with the decay
> (1.5) must be zero**."

where **(1.5) is exactly `α = 1`** (p.30, lines 103–105): `U(y) = A(y/|y|)/|y| + o(1/|y|)` as
`y → ∞`, `A : S² → R³` smooth. And two lines earlier (p.30, line 107), in Tsai's own words about
that same class:
> "the function `u` given by (1.2)₁ **satisfies the local energy estimates**"

**Theorem 2's hypothesis list, stated by Tsai himself** (§2, p.34, lines 234–236):
> "We remark that, in Theorem 2, **we do not require the weak solution `u` to be a Leray-Hopf
> weak solution. Our only requirements (apart from self-similarity) are (i) and (ii): the
> Navier-Stokes equations and the local energy estimates.**"

and (p.32, line 183): *"It should be emphasized that **Theorem 2 is purely local** in the sense
that we do not impose any boundary condition on `u`."* **`SOURCES.md` row 2's record — "Thm 2 …
no `L^q` in the hypothesis" — is CORRECT and is confirmed at primary.**

`(1.4)` itself (p.30–31, lines 92–96): `ess sup_{t₃<t<T} ∫_B ½|u(x,t)|² dx + ∫_{t₃}^{T}∫_B
ν|∇u|² dx dt < ∞` for **some** ball `B` and **some** `t₃ < T`.

**Two more facts, both load-bearing.** (a) §2, line 246: *"By standard regularity theory of
stationary Navier-Stokes equations, **every weak solution `U` of (1.3) is actually smooth**"* —
so a weak solution of (1.3) is locally bounded, and `L^q` membership is decided by the tail alone.
(b) **Remark 5.4** (p.49, lines 1127–1131) — Tsai's own warning that the hypotheses are not
decoration: *"To conclude that a solution of (1.3) is zero, **certain assumptions on the growth of
`U` are necessary**, as can be seen from the following example. Let `Φ` be an arbitrary harmonic
function on `R³`. Let `U = ∇Φ` and `P = −½|U|² − ay·U` … Then `(U,P)` satisfies Leray's equations
(1.3)."* **There ARE nontrivial solutions of (1.3) without a growth/integrability hypothesis.**
This is precisely why "which theorem, and are its hypotheses met" is not a pedantic question.

**A third, weaker sufficient condition, recorded because it exists** — **Remark 5.3** (p.49,
lines 1120–1122): *"By Lemma 5.1, it is sufficient to have `|U(y)| ≤ b|y|` for some `b < a` and
for `|y| > r₀` (and `P(y) = O(|y|^N)` for some `N > 0`), for the triviality of `U`."* Our object
**decays**, so it satisfies this for every `b ∈ (0, a)`. **It is a Remark, not a Theorem, and the
record should lean on Theorem 2, not on this.** Recorded so it is not re-discovered as new.

**Tsai's own bibliography, `[NRS]`** (p.50, extraction line 1211), quoted because the record has
it wrong (§6):
> "`[NRS]` J. Nečas, M. Růžička & V. Šverák, On Leray's self-similar solutions of the
