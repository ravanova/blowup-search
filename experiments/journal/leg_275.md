## 1. The defect, and why it is a defect

Leg 251 §3's ansatz named `r^{(n)} ∈ (r_n(γ), r_{n+1}(γ))` with **`n` odd and large**, citing
BCG `arXiv:2208.09445` Thm 1.2 and Thm 1.3.

BCG's **Lemma `k`** (l.579–586) makes `k` a **monotone bijection `[1, r*) → [1, ∞)`**, with
`r_j` defined by `k(r_j) = j`. So `n → ∞` forces `r_n → r*` and `r^{(n)} → r*`. Since
`7/6 < r* = 1.1909830`, "large `n`" places the target **strictly inside** BCG's dominance window
`(7/6, r*)`.

That is the region leg 251's **own obligation 4** forbids — *"show it is not contained in BCG's
dominance regime"* — glossed under its obligation 1 (as re-posed by leg 266) as `r ≤ 7/6`. The
ansatz and obligation 4 pointed in **opposite directions**.

A second, independent route to the same contradiction: Thm 1.3, which leg 251 cited for its
Navier–Stokes conclusion, requires `δ_dis > 0`, i.e. `r > 7/6` — inside the window again.

## 3. The repair, and why `n = 3` rather than `n = 15`

**`n = 3` is unconditional.** BCG **Theorem 1.1** (l.214), verbatim: *"Let `γ ∈ (1, +∞)`. There
exists `r^{(3)}(γ) ∈ (r_3(γ), r_4(γ))` …"* — no *"large enough"*, no `γ`-restriction, and
`7/5 ∈ (1, ∞)`. Its `δ_dis ∈ (−0.5778, −0.4302)` is strictly negative, so `r^{(3)}` sits
**outside** the dominance window, on obligation 4's side.

**`n = 15` is arithmetically cheaper and was not taken.** Its `|δ_dis| ≤ 0.0141` is **31–41×**
smaller, so the forcing it leaves behind is far milder. But it rests on BCG Thm 1.2's
*"odd and large enough"*, which BCG never quantify; whether `15` qualifies is **open** in the
paper and is not settled by leg 265, `verify_265`, or this leg. Reported open, and the
unconditional branch taken instead.

**The price of `n = 3` is stated, not hidden.** On `r^{(3)}`, `F_dis` **grows** like `e^{+0.5 s}`
against an architecture BCG calibrated for decay at rate `≤ 0.1459`. That is a real cost — and
it is the *same* cost obligation 1 already accepted when it asked for the stability step with
`F_dis` retained at `r ≤ 7/6`. The correction makes the ansatz consistent with the obligation;
it does not make the obligation easier.
