## The gate, and its answer in its pre-committed wording

> **Gate.** Does LSS arXiv:2010.01201's own exact `a = 1/2` solution (read at full text, not
> via ALS's or Xu's secondary citation) explicitly contain, derive, or trivially imply (a) the
> `alpha(1/2) = 3` scaling exponent, and/or (b) the `a_c = 0.6890665` boundary value, with
> hypotheses recorded verbatim?

**(a) YES — explicitly, and exactly.** LSS Eq. (39), p. 11, *is*
`ξ = (x − x_0)/(t_c − t)^{1/3}`: `α_LSS(1/2) = 1/3`, hence this repository's
`alpha(1/2) = 3` under `literature_gates.py`'s own dictionary (`our alpha = 1/alpha_ALS`,
lines 32-33). It is stated in words at pp. 13-14 ("*...in agreement with the exact results of
Section 3 (Eq. (30)) and Section 4 (Eq. (38)) for α = 1 and α = 1/3, respectively*"), it is the
far-field exponent `1/α = 3` of Eq. (38), it is the closed form `α_0(1/2) = 1/3` of Eq. (45),
and Table 1 p. 48 lists `α_e = 0.333333333` at `a = 0.5`.

**(b) YES as primary source, NO from the `a = 1/2` exact solution.**
`a_c = 0.6890665337007457…` is LSS's own number (abstract p. 1; §1 Eq. (8) p. 5; §12 p. 43),
and this repository's `LSS_A_C = 0.6890665` is that value truncated (4.89e-08 relative). But it
is **not** implied by the exact solution: LSS define it by `α(a_c) = 0` and locate it
numerically (§8 time-dependent simulations, §9 generalized Petviashvili method; the bisection
ladder is Table 1 p. 48), while the exact-solution family's closed-form exponent
`α_0(a) = 2(1−a)²/(2−a)` is strictly positive for every `a < 1` and has no root at all.

Per the gate's yes-branch: the derivation and locators are recorded, the citation row is
upgraded from secondary-source to primary-source-read, and the one framing consequence is
**flagged, not applied**, for the leg-58-bundle writeup (`DIRECTION.md` action item #6). No
other file was reworded.

## Plain language: what actually changed

Two numbers this project has been carrying around had never been checked against the paper
they supposedly came from. Both were traced through intermediaries. Reading the paper settles
both, and it moves them in opposite directions.

`alpha(1/2) = 3` turns out to be **more** firmly the literature's than we recorded: it is not a
by-product of somebody's later dissipative system, it is an exact closed-form solution LSS
write down and prove as their Theorem 2, and the number 3 is on the page three separate ways.
Nothing this project claimed about it was wrong — the ledger already said
`CONFIRMED_AND_PRE-EMPTED` — but the *count of independent sources* was inflated. ALS §5.2, Xu's
Table 1 row at `a = 0.5`, and LSS §4 are **one ancestor**, not three: three of ALS's four authors
are LSS's three, LSS came first, and Xu's own text says the branch was checked against LSS's.
The genuinely independent second discoverer is a different paper, J. Chen arXiv:1908.09385,
which LSS themselves credit in a Note on p. 11.

`a_c` moves the other way, slightly. It is entirely LSS's, we never claimed otherwise, and the
useful new fact is about *what kind of number it is*: converged numerics from an iterative
eigenvalue solve that does not converge past `a_c` at all, with the paper's own stated accuracy
being "at least 5 digits" in that range — not seventeen certified digits, whatever the printed
tail of digits looks like. Anyone quoting it should quote it as that.

And the two findings are **not** the same fact wearing two hats. They come from one paper but
from disjoint halves of it: one analytic and exact, one numerical. The "shared ancestor"
worry that motivated this leg is real for `alpha(1/2) = 3`'s *source count* and false for the
relationship between the two findings.

## Technical: what the driver measures, and the controls that can fail

Everything below is re-derived from LSS's own printed equations, in float64, with analytic
derivatives. `experiments/p2_route_lss_v1_lit.py`, ~10 s, deterministic.

| id | what | result | control that can report otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Theorem 2 verified as a **PDE residual** of LSS Eq. (1) on Eqs. (33), (34), (37), on a τ-ladder to τ = 1e-3 | worst relative residual at `a = 1/2`: **7.92e-16** | same evaluation at `a = 0.45 / 0.55 / 0`: best **6.05e-02**, i.e. **13.9 decades** apart |
| L2 | `alpha(1/2) = 3` as the **root of a scalar equation**: eliminate `w_{-2}` between Eqs. (35) and (36) with `v_c = ṽ_c τ^p` and the system collapses to `3p² − p = 0` | bisected `p = 0.333333333333333` (`p − 1/3 = 5.55e-17`), `alpha_ours = 3.000000000000000` | the same code path on LSS's general-γ Eqs. (42)-(45) returns `alpha_ours = 1` at `γ=1` (`a=0`) and `6` at `γ=3` (`a=2/3`) — three different numbers from one path |
| L3 | far-field exponent of Eq. (38) against Eq. (49)'s `f ∝ |ξ|^{−1/α}` | `d log|ω| / d log ξ → −3.000000` | — (and see the sign finding below) |
| L4 | Eq. (34)'s `u_x = Hω` for the double-pole pair, against `solver/line_hilbert.py` on a sinh grid (2401 nodes, half-width 400), same PV convention as LSS Eq. (2) | relative **2.48e-05** on `|x| ≤ 20` (the module's own validated accuracy is 1.6e-4 on the CLM pair) | a 3%-perturbed right-hand side reports **3.00e-02** |
| L5 | `α_0(a) = 2(1−a)²/(2−a)` scanned over `a ∈ [−1, 0.999]` | **no root below `a = 1`**; `α_0(a_c) = 0.147497`; repo `LSS_A_C` off LSS's 16 digits by **4.89e-08** | the scan reports the minimum it finds (2e-06, at the `a → 1` end), so a root would have been seen |

**Lesson 90 compliance.** L2's control is the point: a check that returns `3` no matter what is
not a check. The general-γ path returns `1`, `3`, `6`, `10` at `γ = 1, 2, 3, 4`, off the same
five lines of code, so the `3` is a measurement of `a = 1/2` and not of the code.

**A sign finding, verified on the rendered page and not on a text dump.** LSS Eq. (38) as
printed is internally inconsistent by an overall sign: its left expression
`(1/(t_c−t))·(4iṽ_c²/3)·([ξ−iṽ_c]^{−2} − [ξ+iṽ_c]^{−2})` equals **minus** its own right
expression `(1/(t_c−t))·16ṽ_c³ξ/(3(ξ²+ṽ_c²)²)`, because
`[ξ−iṽ_c]^{−2} − [ξ+iṽ_c]^{−2} = 4iṽ_cξ/(ξ²+ṽ_c²)²` and `i·4i = −4`. I rendered PDF page 11 at
160 dpi and read the equation off the image before writing this down, because a dropped minus
sign is exactly what `pdftotext` does. Which branch is correct is settled by **measurement, not
algebra**: L1's residual is 7.9e-16 for the ansatz whose closed form carries the minus, and
Eq. (1) is *not* invariant under `ω → −ω` (that flips the whole right-hand side), so the sign is
not free. It is also the sign convention of the `a = 0` CLM profile `−4X/(1+4X²)` that
`solver/line_hilbert.py` already uses. **Consequence for anything banked here: none** — the
exponent, Theorem 2's content, and `alpha(1/2) = 3` are all sign-independent. Recorded as
`L3.sign_finding` in the JSON, claimed as a typo in one printed equality and nothing more.

* `experiments/p2_route_lss_v1_lit.py`, `writeup/data/p2_route_lss_v1_lit.json`,
  `writeup/novelty/leg_161.md`, `experiments/journal/leg_161.md`.

**Restraint 1 — the `alpha(1/2) = 3` ledger row was NOT touched.** That row
(`"source": "2207.07548 §5.2 + XU Table 1"`) is the **ALS** row, not the LSS row, and the gate's
yes-branch says in terms: *flag for the leg-58-bundle writeup, do not silently reword any other
file*. The independence correction is therefore recorded in `LSS_PRIMARY_READ`
(`independence_correction`) and in the JSON's `gate.consequence`, and left for the bundle
writeup to apply where it belongs.

**Restraint 2 — the arXiv id is deliberately kept out of the ledger row's `source` field.**
`cited_ids` in `experiments/p2_route_lga_v1_ledger.py` scans that field with
`ARXIV_RE = \b(\d{4}\.\d{4,5})\b` and cross-checks every hit against `ARXIV_METADATA`, which
carries no `2010.01201` entry. Putting the id there would make `test_2_no_phantom_citations`
report a real, fetched, fully-read paper as a **phantom citation**, and fixing that properly
means editing `p2_route_lga_v1_ledger.py` — outside this leg's territory. The id lives in
`LSS_PRIMARY_READ["arxiv"]` and in the row's `note` instead, and the reason is written into the
module next to the edit so the next reader does not "fix" it by hand. **This is a real, small
gap in the ledger's provenance graph and it is named here rather than papered over:** LSS is now
a primary source that `PRIMARY_SOURCES` does not list, because listing it would break
`test_1_source_resolution`'s and `test_2`'s counted assertions in a file this leg may not edit.
A follow-up with `experiments/p2_route_lga_v1_ledger.py` in territory can close it in one edit
(add the `2010.01201` metadata row, add the `PRIMARY_SOURCES` entry, bump the two counts).

**No quartet BLOG/TECHNICAL/figure.** `DIRECTION.md` §161's territory lists neither a writeup
pair nor a figure, and this leg has no new curve to register — its deliverable is a provenance
upgrade plus five residual numbers. The BLOG-grade and TECHNICAL-grade content is the two
sections above, in-territory. Flagged for integration in case the orchestrator wants it
