**The tolerance rule was fixed before the comparison and is leg 60's, verbatim.** Every quoted
value is passed to the ledger as *the string that appears in the document*, and the tolerance
is **half a unit in that string's own last printed digit**. A document that prints `1.28` claims
three significant figures, so the check accepts exactly what rounds to `1.28`. There is no
global relative tolerance anywhere in the file, which is what stops the check from being tuned
until it passes. The novelty pass (`writeup/novelty/leg_110.md`, `PROCEED_AS_BOOKKEEPING`) found
two on-topic methodological references — arXiv:2607.25589 and arXiv:2606.09500 — that
independently say the tolerance rule must be fixed *before* the comparison, which is why the
rule was inherited rather than invented.

**Rows are of two kinds, declared per row and never re-labelled after seeing the result:**

* **STRICT** — one printed number against one stored quantity. **These decide the gate.**
* **RANGE** — one printed number asserted to hold across a *set* of stored values ("flat at `X`
  across the whole range", "floors at `~X`", "every class drops to `0.000`"). Reported with
  magnitudes alongside, but they do **not** decide the gate: a range claim failing at one end is
  a different defect from a wrong number, and lesson 75 says not to merge them.

**Derived quantities are re-derived, not transcribed twice** (leg 60's discipline). Every
divergence exponent is **re-fit** by log-log least squares over the stored `M`-ladder; every
per-mode growth factor is recomputed from the ladder endpoints; every convergence order is
recomputed as `log₂` of consecutive-rung factors; `τ` is recomputed as `budget / ‖A‖_w`; every
`defect/τ` headline is recomputed from those two; the endpoint-zeroing share, both extrapolated
`n`s, and `MM`'s improvement factor are all recomputed. Each re-derivation is then checked
against the corresponding stored field as its **own separate row**, so the load-bearing numbers
are genuinely reproduced.

## The four STRICT discrepancies, with magnitudes

**All four are in Arm A, and all four are in the *periphery* of the coefficient-basis writeup,
not in the numbers the death rests on.**

**(1) and (2) — `TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` §9 and `PHASE2_P2_NOTES.md` (L1v2-4): the
divergence exponent at `s = 2` is quoted `+1.27`; the stored curve gives `1.264554318528`.**
Magnitude: `5.446e−03` absolute, **relative `4.29e−03`**, **1.09 half-ulps** out at the two
decimals quoted. `1.2646` rounds to `1.26`, not `1.27`. The re-fit reproduces the stored
`divergence_exponent` field to `0.66` half-ulps at twelve digits, so the *measurement* is
internally consistent — this is a rounding slip in transcription, the same shape as leg 60's
finding (3). The three other points of the same sentence (`+1.28` at `s = 0`, `+0.639` at
`s = 1.00`, `+1.00` at the object's boundary) all re-derive. **Direction: the slip makes the
divergence at that one endpoint look marginally *worse* than measured**, i.e. it overstates the
negative by 0.43%. The sentence's actual claim — *"no point of the curve touches zero"* — is
untouched: the re-derived minimum over all nine `s` values is `+0.639`, comfortably positive.

**(3) and (4) — `TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` §4 and `PHASE2_P2_NOTES.md` (L1v2-3): "The
geometric growth factor **is `ν`**, to three digits, in both cases."** It is not, in either
case, at three decimal places:

| `ν` | measured per-mode growth (re-derived) | absolute | relative | half-ulps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1.05` | `1.045022` | `4.978e−03` | `4.74e−03` | 9.96 |
| `1.20` | `1.194556` | `5.444e−03` | `4.54e−03` | 10.89 |

Read instead at three *significant* figures the `ν = 1.05` case holds (`1.05` vs `1.05`) and the
`ν = 1.20` case still fails (`1.19` vs `1.20`), so at least one half of the sentence fails under
either reading. The growth is **0.47% / 0.45% BELOW `ν`**, so the accompanying claim — *"every
neglected mode costs the full weight of the mode"* — is 99.5% true rather than exactly true.
**Direction: the sentence overstates the geometric class's badness, i.e. it overstates the
negative.** The table's own printed entries `×1.045` and `×1.195` both re-derive to under one
half-ulp; it is only the prose's "is `ν`" that does not.

## The five RANGE discrepancies, with magnitudes

**(R1) — the largest by magnitude, and the one worth a reader's attention.**
`BLOG_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md`: *"Every weight class saturates immediately. Flat, algebraic, geometric:
the divergence exponent drops to `0.000` and stays there."* At `μ = 0.1` the geometric `ν = 1.2`
class has a re-fit exponent of **`33.96`**, not `0.000`, and a per-mode growth of `1.0807`. It
only reaches `2.92e−10` at `μ = 0.5`. **`TECHNICAL` §7 and `PHASE2_P2_NOTES.md` (L1v2-5) both
state this correctly** — "the geometric class needs `μ = 0.5`, five times more" re-derives
exactly (`0.5 / 0.1 = 5`), as does the `×1.081` per-mode figure at `μ = 0.1`. So the finding is
right everywhere it is stated technically; the blog's one sentence is wrong. **Direction: it
makes the positive control — the thing that makes the inviscid divergence a *measurement* rather
than a broken instrument — look cleaner than it is.** The control still does its job: two of
three classes saturate at `μ = 0.1` and all three at `μ = 0.5`, so the instrument demonstrably
can report "bounded".

**(R2)** `TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTETN_V1.md` §7: *"`H` is flat at `4.704e−03` across the whole range."*
It is, for `a ∈ {0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 8}` — six of seven scale-curve points. At `a = 32` it is
`4.695418e−03`: `8.582e−06` absolute, **relative `1.82e−03`**, 17.16 half-ulps. The qualitative
claim (`H`'s defect does not move with the profile's scale) holds; the four-figure number does
not hold at the last point.

**(R3)** same section: *"`≥ 1` floors at `≈ 7.13e−08`."* True at `a = 1, 2, 8`
(`7.1317e−08`, `7.1315e−08`, `7.1268e−08`); at `a = 32` it is `7.0533e−08` — **relative
`1.08e−02`**, 15.35 half-ulps. Again the floor exists; the printed figure drifts 1.1% at the end.

**(R4)** `BLOG_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md`: *"Agreement: 15 digits."* The stored max relative gap is
`8.771e−15`, i.e. **14.06 digits**. `0.94` digits optimistic, 1.89 half-ulps. `TECHNICAL` §8's
`8.8e−15` re-derives exactly as the max over the five stored rows.

**(R5)** `TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` §2: *"the recursion's constant terms are `c_k = (−1)^k k`
exactly (defect `0.0`, `k = 1..16`)."* The defect is exactly `0.0` and re-derives — but the bank
stores **12** constants, `k = 1..12`, not 16. This is an **under-banking**, not a wrong number:
`k = 13..16` cannot be checked from the curated JSON at all. Recomputing `(−1)^k k` against all
twelve stored constants gives a max defect of exactly `0.0`.

## Why this lands rather than parking

The gate's no-branch says *report the exact discrepancy and escalate to the user; change no
conclusion, lift no ban, do not re-measure.* Checked against `ORCHESTRATION.md` §8's four true
escalations: this leg changes nothing in `plan_of_record.py`, lifts and weakens no ban, claims
no movement on the Clay chain, and deletes and rewrites no banked result — it only **adds** a
script and a JSON. **None of the four applies**, so per this leg's own brief it lands with the
discrepancies reported prominently rather than pushing a parked branch.

**And the substantive reason is stronger than the procedural one.** Read with magnitudes rather
than as a boolean:

* **No banked *finding* failed to reproduce.** The gate's own four named quantities — `+0.639`,
  `0.606`, the per-class exponents, and the collocation realization's failing quantities — are
  exact, in every document that prints them.
* **The collocation death is 103 / 103 exact**, including all three re-derived headline ratios.
* **The ban-bearing numbers are exact.** Stage `V`'s ban rests on "`L1` first", and `L1`'s death
  rests on `0.639` / `0.606` (coefficient basis) and `2.04e+11×` / `1.85e+07×` (collocation) —
  all four re-derived from the ladders rather than read.
* **Every one of the four STRICT discrepancies points the same way: the prose overstates its own
  negative.** Nothing here makes a dead lane look more alive. The one that goes the other way
  (R1) makes a *positive control* look cleaner, and the control's real magnitudes still support
  the finding it was built for.

What the user actually faces is narrow, and a leg may not make the edit under this protocol
because the numbers are banked: **`TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` §9 and `PHASE2_P2_NOTES.md`
(L1v2-4) say `+1.27` where the data says `+1.26`; §4 and (L1v2-3) say the geometric growth "is
`ν` to three digits" where it is 0.45–0.47% below `ν`; and `BLOG_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` says every
class saturates at `μ = 0.1` where the geometric class needs `μ = 0.5`.** Either correct those
three sentences or record the disagreement. **No quoted number was changed by this leg.**

## Banking the script as a regression check

The gate's yes-branch asked for the script to be banked as a permanent regression check. The
no-branch does not withdraw that, and the script is more useful with four known-failing rows
than with none: it is now a **falsifiable** ledger. Re-running
`python experiments/p2_route_l1r_v1_repro.py` re-derives all 209 rows from committed data in
under a second and fails loudly if any banked JSON is edited under a document that quotes it.
The four STRICT and five RANGE discrepancies are recorded in the JSON's `discrepancies` block
with file, section, quoted string, reproduced value, and magnitude in three forms
(absolute, relative, half-ulps), so a future fix to any of those sentences flips the
corresponding row to PASS without any change to this script.
