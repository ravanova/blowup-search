# Leg 201 — Route-ICA2: the degenerate-band audit of `solver/interval_certificate.py`

**Branch:** `leg/201-ica2-v1`. **Role:** LEG. **Date:** 2026-08-06.
**Family:** audit (the convention of legs 66, 69, 79, 98 — measure, pin, do not patch).

## GATE — answered in its pre-committed wording

> *"Under adversarial and degenerate inputs — including inputs near `interval.py`'s own known
> failure bands (subnormal range, above 2^997), even though no live operator's range reaches
> them — does `interval_certificate.py` ever silently return a wrong value?"*

## **YES.** 11 of 41 cases. The mechanism, the magnitude, and the attribution:

`solver.interval_certificate.matmul_point_interval` returns, **normally and with no warning**,
an interval that **does not contain** the value it encloses, once the accumulated products are
subnormal.

| accumulation length `m` | containment escape | as a fraction of the returned magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0 η | 0 % |
| 16 | 6 η | 17.65 % |
| 64 | 30 η | 23.08 % |
| **103** (shipped `BorderedCLM` `N`) | **50 η** | **24.04 %** |
| **405** (shipped `BorderedHL` `N`) | **200 η** | **24.63 %** |

η = 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴ = 4.94e-324, so 200 η = 9.88e-322 absolute. **The relative column is the one that
matters**: this is not a last-ulp curiosity, it is a quarter of the answer.

**It reaches the certificate's own returned constant.** `interval_constants` documents `Y_0` as
a *rigorous upper bound* on `max_i w_i |(A F(z))_i|`. On a synthetic **linear** system (identity
Jacobian — the most favourable case there is) whose `A @ F` products are subnormal, it returns

* `m = 103`: `Y_0 = 1.032597e-321` against an exact `1.274689e-321` — **18.99 % too small**
* `m = 405`: `Y_0 = 4.016754e-321` against an exact `4.999944e-321` — **19.66 % too small**

with no exception and the result still flagged `rigorous: True`.

## The mechanism, derived before it was measured

`matmul_point_interval` bounds its `m`-term float accumulation by `γ_m · Σ|M_ij B_jk|` and adds
one `nextafter` push. Both are **relative**. Once the products are subnormal that guard term
underflows to **exactly zero**, while the true accumulation error is **absolute** — up to
`m/2 · η`. The outward push is worth 1 η. The gap is the rest.

The probe is deterministic, not searched. With every `M` entry `2⁻⁵³⁷` and every `v` entry
`k·2⁻⁵³⁷`, each product is exactly `k·η`; at `k = 1.5` and `k = 2.5` that is a forced
round-to-even tie, so every product errs by exactly ±0.5 η and subnormal addition accumulates
them without further rounding. `k = 1.5` walks the **lower** endpoint above the truth;
`k = 2.5` walks the **upper** endpoint below it — the direction that understates a *magnitude*
and therefore the one that reaches `Y_0`.

**The analytic value is `(m/2 − 1) η` and the measurement is 1–1.5 η inside it, at every `m`.**
That discrepancy is recorded per case (`analytic_escape_bound_eta`, `measured_minus_analytic`)
rather than the analysis being retuned to match; the missing η is one rounding inside the BLAS
`Mp @ Blo`, which this module does not own. **What is claimed is the shape** — the escape grows
linearly in the accumulation length with slope ½ η per term (discipline 72) — and analysis and
measurement agree on the shape exactly.

A randomised sweep confirms the deterministic case is the worst case of one mechanism and not a
separate special point: **54 of 240** random trials at input scales 1e-165…1e-150 escape
containment (22.5 %), worst 7 η.

## Attribution — required explicitly by the gate's YES branch (discipline 75)

**It is the SAME mechanism as leg 69's defect 1, and an INDEPENDENT UNREPAIRED INSTANCE.**

Leg 69 (Route-IA) found exactly this underflow gap in the primitive `solver/interval.py`
(62 false negatives in 1680 cases, worst escape 6.58 η) and **repaired it there**, adding Rump's
absolute η term (BIT Numer. Math. 52:201–220, 2012) to `isum`, `matvec` and `dot2_matvec` —
`_ETA_TERMS_PLAIN = 2`, `_ETA_TERMS_DOT2 = 8`.

`interval_certificate.matmul_point_interval` is a **clone of `interval.matvec`** — same monotone
`M⁺/M⁻` split, same `γ_m` term, same outward push — living in a different file. **It never
received the fix.** The literature name for this is a *missing bug-fix in a code clone*
(Poehlmann et al., ECEASST; Islam et al., SANER 2016/2019), which is why the novelty pass ruled
it bookkeeping and not a discovery.

So: **not a new hazard class**, and **not a re-report of leg 69** — leg 69's finding is about
`interval.py` and is closed there. This is a *second, still-open instance* of it, one level up,
in the module leg 69's own scoping note did not examine.

**The control is what makes that attribution stick (discipline 90).** `interval.matvec` — the
repaired sibling — is run on **byte-identical input** and encloses correctly at every `m` and
both rounding directions. If the data were simply too hard for interval arithmetic, the control
would fail too. It does not. The difference is the clone, not the input.

## What HELD — and it is most of the module

* **The 2^997 band holds, one level up.** An oversized operand reaching `interval_constants`
  through the compensated residual path — how every shipped enclosure class builds `F` — raises
  `OverflowError` out of the certificate entry point naming `2^997`, rather than returning
  `[nan, nan]`. Leg 69's defect-2 repair is confirmed at the level above the one it landed at.
* **Overflow never produces a *finite* wrong endpoint.** 200 trials at operand scales 2⁹⁹⁰…2¹⁰²³:
  every non-containing outcome is `inf` or `nan` (from `inf − inf`), and those reach
  `radii_verdict`, which rejects them by hypothesis (leg 98's repair). **0** finite-but-wrong.
* **The module's other helpers are sound in the same band**: `weighted_rowsum_bound` (all terms
  same sign — no cancellation, the single push covers it), `_scale_rows` (one product per entry,
  no accumulation), and `dot2_matvec` on the residual path (it carries the η term). **That is
  why the escape enters through `A @ F` and not through `F`.**
* **Seven degenerate `radii_verdict` shapes leg 98 did not reach** — `Z₁` one ulp below 1,
  subnormal `Z₂` (budget overflows to `+inf`), subnormal `Y₀`, both, `−0.0` constants (a
  legitimate bound, correctly *not* a violation), `Z₂ = 1e300`, and `N = 1` — all sound.
* **Both live controls hold.** Honest `BorderedCLM` `n=101`: `Y₀ = 1.396e-11`, `Z₁ = 2.754e-09`,
  `Z₂ = 7.993e+05`, **closes**, `r ∈ [1.396e-11, 1.251e-06]`. Displaced by `1e-3`: **does not
  close**, separation **4.23e+08×**. The module that produced every number above still certifies
  correctly.

## Severity: LATENT, and the scoping is MEASURED, not asserted

The gate anticipated this and it is confirmed with numbers rather than reasoning:

* The subnormal band is entered only when an **entire row's** `|A| @ |F|` mass is of order
  `m·η ≈ 2e-321`. On the live `BorderedCLM n=101` substrate the minimum row mass is
  **1.630e-28** — **292.9 decades above** the band.
* The largest live operand is **4.184e+03**, against the `2^997 = 1.339e+300` threshold —
  **296.5 decades below** it.

**No banked number in this repository is shown to be wrong.** What the gap removes is the
*guarantee* — the same scoping legs 69 and 98 both carried. And it is the informative
confirmation the NO-branch would have banked, arriving on the YES branch instead: **leg 69's
"no live operator affected" scoping does hold one level up — for reachability. It does not hold
for coverage**, because the repair itself did not propagate to the clone.

## Escalation, and what this leg deliberately did NOT do

Per the gate's YES branch: **escalated, parked, branch pushed only, `main` untouched, no entry
appended to `capabilities.py`** (that was the NO-branch's deliverable and this gate did not
answer NO).

`solver/interval_certificate.py` and `solver/interval.py` were **not modified**, under either
outcome, per this leg's declared territory and the precedent of legs 66, 69, 79 and 98.

**Recommended repair (NOT performed under this leg's authority).** One line, already written and
tested twelve metres away: add `_eta_floor(m, _ETA_TERMS_PLAIN)` to both error terms of
`matmul_point_interval`, exactly as `interval.matvec` does —

```
e_lo = _up(_up(g * a_lo) + _eta_floor(m, _ETA_TERMS_PLAIN))
```

As `interval.py`'s own comment records, `m·η ≲ 1e-321` for every accumulation length this
repository uses, which is **below half an ulp of any normal double**: adding it changes **no
radius in the certificate's operating range bit-for-bit**. It only lifts the radius floor where
the relative terms have vanished. The repair is therefore expected to be a **zero-diff** change
to every banked number — which is exactly what makes it cheap and what makes leaving it out
indefensible.

**A second, structural recommendation, which is the more valuable one.** The Rule of Three that
leg 128 applied to `_hypothesis_violations` — three copies became one shared
`solver/certificate_guards.hypothesis_violations`, so drift became *impossible rather than
merely intended against* — was never applied to the **arithmetic**. `matmul_point_interval` is
`interval.matvec` with a different signature. This leg found the exact failure mode leg 128's
abstraction was built to prevent, in the routine that abstraction did not cover. **Whoever takes
the repair should consider deleting the clone rather than patching it.**

**When the repair lands:** invert the two GAP-PIN gates in
`test_interval_certificate_adversarial.py` (each names its own inversion in its docstring) —
**do not weaken or delete them**, and keep the measured magnitudes in the docstrings as the
record of what they protect against.

## Premise correction, recorded because it changed what this leg built

This leg's dispatch stated that `interval_certificate.py` *"has never itself been adversarially
audited"* and listed `test_interval_certificate_adversarial.py` as a **new** file. Both are
false: **leg 98 (Route-ICA) audited exactly this module** (12/36 false accepts, 8 load-bearing,
repaired — the guards cite leg 98 by number in the shipped source), and its 464-line, 14-gate
battery already occupies that filename. This leg therefore **appended** to it and reordered,
weakened or deleted nothing; leg 98's 14 gates still pass unchanged alongside leg 201's 6.

The gate survived the correction **intact**, because the gate never asked "is this module
audited" — it asked about the **two bands leg 98 never entered**, and that is exactly the gap
that was still open. Recorded in `writeup/novelty/leg_201.md`, committed before construction.
