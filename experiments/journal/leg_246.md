## The two candidates, and why each fails

**`arXiv:2411.01891` — Silantyev, Lushnikov, Siegel, Ambrose, *Stud. Appl. Math.* 155, e70115
(2025).** All four parent authors, same object, published. It is an **exact pole-dynamics** paper:
closed-form periodic solutions at **σ ∈ {0, 1}** and **a ∈ {0, 1/2}** only. Where closed form runs
out they reach for phase-plane analysis, l.2309: *"When σ = 1 closed form analytical solutions are
not available, and we resort to a phase-plane analysis using special variables."* Apparatus census
over 2704 lines: **0** on all ten terms except a single `computer-aided` at **l.54**, which cites
**Chen and Hou's** proof, not theirs. Recorded rather than rounded to zero.

**`arXiv:2504.14346` — Ambrose, Lopes Filho, Nussenzveig Lopes (2025).** §5 is the dissipative CLM
equation at **general σ > 0** (so σ = 2 is in scope), §6 adds advection. Apparatus census over 1632
lines: **0/10**. But the direction is *global existence* (l.693: *"for any σ > 0, sufficiently
small periodic data in Y^{−σ/2} leads to existence of a global solution"*) and analyticity for
σ ≥ 1 — and `self-similar` = **0** in the whole paper. **There is no profile in it to certify.**

## What the γ = 2 line should take from this, stated narrowly

This repository has spent six legs (63, 125, 174, 185, 187, 193) on the γ = 2 dissipative gCLM
object. The most directly relevant precedent's authors published, **640 days ago**, a paper whose
conclusion says, verbatim (l.2358–2360):

> *"When a = 0 and σ = 2, a pole dynamics solution for the problem on the real line was found by
> Schochet [6]. … **Despite significant effort, we have been unable to generalize this solution to
> the periodic domain.**"*

and whose stated next step is (l.2364):

> *"In future work, we intend to **numerically investigate** finite-time singularity formation in
> the gCLM equation with dissipation over the full range of a and for more general values of σ…"*

Their Table 1 puts every **σ ∈ (1, ∞)** cell — which is where σ = 2 lives — at **`GEIVP`**, small-data
global existence: the *opposite* side of the question from a blow-up profile.

**This is confirming context, not a result.** It does not make anything in legs 125/187/193 correct.
It means only that the frontier those legs are measured against **has not moved**.

## Two methodological findings worth more than the verdict

**(1) The cross-check net *corrected* the primary net, it did not merely confirm it.**
`au:"David M Ambrose"` (38 entries) does **not** return `arXiv:2504.14346`, because that paper is
filed as plain *"David Ambrose"*. Only `cat:math.AP AND au:"Ambrose"` returns it — and it is the
candidate **closest to this repository's own γ = 2 object**. Leg 242 learned this lesson from the
confirming direction; here the broad net caught a real miss. **A full-name author net silently
narrows.**

**(1b) And the union count had to be enumerated, not added.** The first pass hand-wrote the
author-line total as 17 by summing per-author counts that had silently included the **parent
itself** (dated exactly on the cut-off). Listing the distinct arXiv ids and taking a set gives
**19** against a naive sum of 23. Recorded because it is the same class of error as everything
else on this page: a count that no code recomputed.

**(2) Three initial-form nets returned 334 entries between them and contained neither the parent
nor the follow-up.** `au:"Lushnikov_P"` (14 entries, newest 2020-03-11), `au:"Ambrose_D"` (164,
zero papers by David M. Ambrose — it is a particle physicist), `au:"Siegel_M"` (156, dominated by
Daniel M. Siegel, astrophysics). Verified by direct substring test on the raw XML: `False`/`False`
in all three. This is leg 240's `au:"Buckmaster_T"` failure and leg 242's `au:"Figueras_J"` failure
**recurring on three author lines at once**. Had any one been the only net for its author, this leg
would have reported NO for entirely the wrong reason and looked identical doing it.

**And the one line that explains the negative rather than reporting it.** Silantyev's stated
computational interests (UCCS profile) are *"scientific computing, high-performance computing
(HPC), and multi-precision computations"* and spectral methods. This group's numerical culture is
**high-precision floating point**, not **validated numerics** — the same finding leg 242 made on
`arXiv:2607.03498`, now on a second author line.
