### 2. The predictions, derived in closed form — NOT imported

#### 2.1 The width law

Let `t = log r`, `h = log(1+δ)` the half-height of the multiplicative tube in log space. For an
**exact** power law `g(t) = c₀ − p₀t`, the affine functions lying within `h` of `g` on
`[t₀, t₁]` are exactly those with

  `|p − p₀| ≤ 2h/(t₁ − t₀)`,

(the extreme tilt pivots about the window midpoint and uses the full tube at both ends), so

> **PREDICTION W.** `width(δ) = 4·log(1+δ) / log(R1/R0)`, and for small δ
> `width ≈ (4/log(R1/R0))·δ`.
>
> On `[10, 1000]`: coefficient `4/log(100) = 0.8685889638065035`.
> On `[10, 100]`: coefficient `4/log(10) = 1.737177927613007` — **exactly 2× the primary
> window's**, and this window-dependence is a prediction leg 382 never made or tested.

Note this derivation *explains* leg 382's `0.8686` rather than borrowing it: `0.8686` is
`4/log(100)`, a property of the **window**, not of the instrument.

**Pass band for W:** `measured/predicted ∈ [0.95, 1.05]` at each `δ ∈ {1e-9, 1e-6, 1e-3, 1e-2,
1e-1}` on the primary window, and the secondary-window coefficient within `[0.95, 1.05]` of
`2×` the primary one.

#### 2.2 The critical tolerances `δ*`

Write `g(t) = c − p₀t + φ(t)`, `φ` the profile's **departure** from an exact power law. A tube
of half-height `h` admits some affine function iff `h ≥ E∞(φ)`, the Chebyshev best-affine
approximation error of `φ` on `[t₀, t₁]`. Hence

> **PREDICTION D.** `δ* = exp(E∞(φ)) − 1`, and the exponent at the transition is
> `p_c = p₀ − b*`, `b*` the best-affine slope of `φ`.

Computed (numerically, to `1e-13`, on the closed-form `φ`s — this is arithmetic on the
*profile definitions*, not a run of the instrument):

| case | `φ(t)` | `E∞` | **`δ*` predicted** | `p_c` predicted | 382's lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | `log(1 + 0.01 e^t)` | `0.276728` | **`0.318807`** | `1.500000` | `0.315697` |
| C2 | `−log(1 + e^{4(t − log 300)})` | `1.493887` | **`3.454378`** | `3.047509` | `3.352868` |
| C3 | `log t` | `0.074203` | **`0.077025`** | `1.761439` | `0.069739` |
| K2 | `0` | `0` | **`0`** | `2` | `0` |

**Pass band for D:** each measured `δ*` within **±20 %** of the prediction. The predictions sit
**above** every one of 382's leads (by `+1.0 %`, `+3.0 %`, `+10.4 %`), and that direction is
itself predicted: the *cell* enclosure is slightly wider than the exact profile tube, so the
tube reaches feasibility at a slightly **smaller** δ than the continuum theory gives.
**Recorded in advance: if the fresh measurement lands on 382's leads rather than on the
closed-form values, the closed form is the thing that is wrong, and Part II says so.**

#### 2.3 The exact local-slope ranges (what "contained truth" can mean for a non-power law)

A profile that is not a power law **has no true exponent**, so demanding that the certified
interval contain the nominal `p₀` would be demanding the instrument certify something false.
The truth a non-power profile *does* have is its **exact local log–log slope range**
`[s_min, s_max] = [min_t (−g'(t)), max_t (−g'(t))]`, in closed form:

| case | `s_min` | `s_max` | nominal `p₀` |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | `1.090909…` (`2 − 10/11`) | `1.909090…` (`2 − 0.1/1.1`) | `2` |
| C2 | `2.000005…` | `5.967747…` (`2 + 4x/(1+x)`, `x = (10/3)^4`) | `2` |
| C3 | `1.565702…` (`2 − 1/log 10`) | `1.855285…` (`2 − 1/log 1000`) | `2` |

> **PREDICTION S.** At `δ = δ*`, the certified interval is a near-point at `p_c` and is
> **contained in `[s_min, s_max]`**, and — stated in advance so it is not read later as a
> failure — it does **NOT** contain the nominal `p₀ = 2` for C1, C2 or C3. Nominal containment
> is recorded as a magnitude, not as a pass condition.

### 3. GATE CLAUSE 1 — *the δ mode is an instrument, not a fit with extra steps*

**Wording, fixed:** *At the freshly-measured `δ*`: do the three non-power planted cases certify
with contained truth and reported widths, does the exact power law still certify at `δ = 0`
with 382's widths reproduced, AND does a sub-`δ*` control remain EMPTY?*

**YES iff all four sub-conditions hold:**

* **(1a) Width law.** PREDICTION W holds inside its pass band, on both windows.
* **(1b) The three non-power cases certify at their own freshly-measured `δ*`.** At
  `δ = δ*_hi` (the upper bisection bracket — the first tolerance at which EMPTY fails), each of
  C1, C2, C3 returns verdict `INTERVAL` (**not** `EMPTY`, **not** `INCAPACITY`), with a finite
  reported `width`, and `[p_lo, p_hi] ⊆ [s_min, s_max]` of §2.3.
* **(1c) The exact power law still certifies at `δ = 0`, with 382's widths reproduced.**
  `p₀ ∈ {1, 2, 2.5, 3}` each returns `INTERVAL` at `δ = 0` containing its truth, with
  `width ≤ 1e-13`, and each width **within a factor 10** of 382's corresponding lead
  (that factor-10 band is the pre-registered meaning of "reproduced": these are
  rounding-floor quantities and demanding bit-equality across a code change would be theatre).
* **(1d) A sub-`δ*` control remains EMPTY.** At `δ = 0.9·δ*_lo` **and** at `δ = 0.5·δ*_lo`,
  each of C1, C2, C3 returns `EMPTY`. *This is the clause that stops the mode being a fit: if
  a profile that should be excluded stops being excluded below its threshold, δ has become a
  knob.*

* **YES →** the instrument gains a usable mode whose looseness is a declared magnitude, never
  silent; certified-vs-fitted remains a distinction with teeth. **CEILING TIER 2;
  `CLAY_OBLIGATIONS.md` §6 items 1 and 2 OPEN.**
* **NO →** report the measured width law verbatim and which sub-condition failed. **A δ mode
  that cannot be pre-registered is not an instrument and is not shipped** — the argument is
  reverted to leg 382's landed state and the leg reports NO.

### 4. GATE CLAUSE 2 — *does the admissible-cutoff analysis tolerate δ > 0 AT ALL?* (co-equal, not a footnote)

**The mechanism, engaged with rather than restated.** `CLAY_OBLIGATIONS.md` §4's admissible
cutoff radius is a function of the **certified** exponent, and every bound in leg 381's priced
bill depends on the enclosure's **lower** endpoint `α_lo`, not its centre. Leg 381 composed
this as `α_lo = α_centre − 0.434·δ`, giving `δ < (α_centre − 1)/0.434` (fixed-ball energy and
critical `L³`) and `δ < (α_centre − 3/2)/0.434` (global `L²`). Meanwhile δ must **exceed** the
profile's own departure `δ*`, or the verdict is EMPTY and there is no `α_lo` at all.

Two observations this leg makes *before* running, because they determine what to measure:

1. **`0.434` is `2/log(R1/R0)`** (§2.1) — a property of the **window**, not a universal
   constant. Leg 381's threshold is therefore window-dependent, and a longer window relaxes it.
2. **Leg 381's `α_lo = α_centre − 0.434·δ` may be conservative.** If the half-width vanishes at
   `δ = δ*` rather than at `δ = 0` (call it **H-shift**: `half-width ≈ a·(δ − δ*)`), then
   `α_lo → α_centre` as `δ ↓ δ*`, and the admissibility condition collapses from
   `α_centre > 1 + 0.434·δ*` to the far weaker `α_centre > 1`. **Between those two readings
   lies the entire difference between a usable instrument and a relabelled one**, so this leg
   discriminates them by measurement:
   * **H-381:** `half-width(δ) = 0.434·δ`.
   * **H-shift:** `half-width(δ) = a·(δ − δ*)`, i.e. the intercept is at `δ*`.
   Discriminant, fixed now: measure `half-width(1.1·δ*)` for C1/C2/C3 and compare with
   `0.434·1.1·δ*`. H-shift is favoured if the measured half-width is below **half** of the
   H-381 value; H-381 is favoured if it is within `±10 %` of it. Any other outcome is reported
   as "neither law fits" with the numbers.

**The primary clause-2 measurement is DIRECT, not algebraic.** For each mismatch shape
`S ∈ {two-power, rational cutoff, log-corrected}` and each planted nominal exponent
`p₀ ∈ {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0}`, locate by bisection the δ set

  `D₁(S, p₀) = { δ ≥ 0 : verdict = INTERVAL and p_lo > 1 }` — fixed-ball energy / critical `L³`
  `D_{L²}(S, p₀) = { δ ≥ 0 : verdict = INTERVAL and p_lo > 3/2 }` — global `L²`

and report `[δ_min, δ_max]` and its width, or **EMPTY**. `δ_min` is the profile's own departure
threshold; `δ_max` is where the enclosure's lower endpoint drops through the physical threshold.
This measures the composed condition itself, with no law interposed.

**The α value in play is pre-registered as `α_centre = 1`**, because that is the value
`CLAY_OBLIGATIONS.md` §4's third amendment names for the banked object: *"`L²` needs
`α > 3/2`; the banked Type-I object gives `α = 1` — deficit 0.5, ratio 1.5×"*. The measured
`α_centre = (p_lo + p_hi)/2` of each row is recorded alongside, so the clause is decided at the
α actually realised and not at a nominal one.

**Decision rule, fixed:**

* **ADMISSIBLE →** `D₁` is non-empty at the α in play. Name the measured window
  `[δ_min, δ_max]`, the `α_centre` it is computed at, and state its width as a magnitude.
* **EMPTY →** `D₁` is empty at the α values in play. **Say so plainly**: it would mean the
  certified-exponent route cannot feed §4's cutoff analysis at any tolerance. No post-hoc
  widening, no rescue reported as gate-deciding.

**α-sensitivity is reported in BOTH branches**, as §4's consumers need to know whether a window
that exists at one α vanishes at another: the full `(S, p₀)` table above, plus the crossover
`α_centre` at which `D₁` first becomes non-empty for each shape.

**Prediction (recorded, may be wrong).** `D₁` is **EMPTY at `α_centre = 1`** for the two shapes
whose local slopes run *below* `p₀` (two-power, log-corrected), since `p_lo ≤ s_min < 1` there
and no tolerance can lift a lower endpoint. For the rational-cutoff shape, whose local slopes
run *above* `p₀` (the cutoff steepens the decay), `D₁` may be **non-empty even at `p₀ = 1`** —
and if so, that is a shape-dependence finding, not a rescue, and it will be reported with the
warning that a profile only decays that fast *because the window contains the cutoff*.

**If the two clauses answer in opposite directions, both verdicts are reported side by side and
are NOT netted into one word.**
