## 2. The result that changed this leg's answer

**The plan of record's own survivor list was wrong on one of its three classes.**
`CLAY_ROADMAP.md` §7.5 and `CONTINUATION_PROMPT.md` both say the target must be *"discretely
self-similar, unstable-self-similar with a finite unstable spectrum, or non-self-similar."*

The middle class is **excluded**, and the exclusion is **stability-blind**. Tsai 1998
Theorem 1 constrains the *profile* through its integrability; the finite-unstable-spectrum
condition constrains the *linearisation*. They never meet. Under merely the Type I bound the
profile lies in `L^p` for every `p > 3` — Pineau–Vicol state it in as many words
(`2607.09619` §1.2: the borderline decay *"guarantees `U ∈ L^p(R³)` for every `p > 3`, but
not membership in the scaling-critical space `L³`"*) — so Theorem 1 applies **however
unstable that profile would have been**.

This matters because it is the class with numerical anchors: `arXiv:2509.14185` supplies
unstable self-similar profiles at CAP-adjacent precision, and it would have been the natural
Phase-1 candidate. Leg 253 found this first, at full text; I re-derived it independently
from Tsai 1998, Bradshaw–Tsai and Pineau–Vicol, and the two reads agree. Leg 253's own gate
answered NO and it is parked on `leg/253-nrsx-v1`.

**A second correction to the repository's own paraphrase, found at source.** Nineteen
in-repo sites call Tsai's hypothesis a *decay* condition. It is not — it is a **growth**
condition. Tsai's own §1 (`TSAI1998.txt` l.193–196): *"even a much weaker condition
`U(y) = o(|y|)`, `P(y) = O(|y|^N)` … is sufficient to imply that Π is constant."* And Remark
5.4 gives the escape hatch explicitly: for **any** harmonic `Φ` on `R³`, `U = ∇Φ` with
`P = −½|U|² − a y·U` solves Leray's system. So the exclusion is a statement about a growth
class, not about the equation — which is why every surviving candidate below has to name
*which hypothesis it fails*, not merely assert that it is different.

### What a certificate for it would need to show

The gap is precise, and the authors state it themselves. **BCG §7, opening sentence, read at
full text:**

> *"The stability for the Euler equation will follow in general, while in the Navier-Stokes
> case we need to restrict the parameter `r` to a regime where the self-similar profile
> **dominates the dissipation**."*

So the computer-assisted part encloses the **inviscid** ODE profile — per leg 174, the first
10 000 `(W_j, Z_j)` Taylor coefficient pairs at `r = r*`, ≈14 h single CPU, `γ = 7/5` — and
the viscous term is then handled analytically, by a parameter restriction, in the stability
step. CGSS §3.4.2 is a *"Dissipation term"* energy estimate: the same architecture. A live
novelty check (§3 of `writeup/novelty/leg_251.md`) confirms the state of the art is still
*"viscosity treated as a perturbation"*. **Nobody has enclosed it.**

A certificate must therefore show:

1. **A rigorous enclosure, in interval arithmetic, of the *stability step with the
   dissipative forcing `F_dis` retained*, at a similarity exponent `r` outside BCG's
   dominance regime.** [RE-POSED 2026-08-07 by leg 266 — see the correction note below.]
   The enclosed object itself carries the viscous term, rather than the viscous term being
   estimated against an enclosed inviscid object. That is exactly leg 174's Grade-A
   definition, and it is the one thing that moves the object out of the Grade-B cell.

   The concrete locus, from BCG's own text: dissipation enters the **dynamically rescaled**
   `(W, Z)` system as a non-autonomous forcing `F_dis` — defined at l.2130, on the
   right-hand sides at l.2138–2139, and named *"the dissipative forcing"* in the paper's own
   words at l.2142 — carrying the explicit prefactor `e^{(2 − r + (1−r)/α)s}`. BCG's
   `(eq:delta:dis)`, l.489–491, sets `−δ_dis = 2 − r + (1−r)/α` and requires `δ_dis > 0`,
   equivalently their `(eq:r:restriction)`
   > `r > 2γ/(γ+1)`

   precisely so that this forcing decays in self-similar time and, l.489, *"can be treated
   as an error"*. Combined with their standing `1 < r < r*`, `(eq:rstar)`, the **dominance
   window at `γ = 7/5` is `r ∈ (7/6, r*) = (1.1666667, 1.1909830)`, width `0.0243163`** —
   and the certificate's `r`-coverage must lie outside it, i.e. at `r ≤ 7/6`, where `F_dis`
   no longer decays and cannot be treated as an error. The interval `(1, 7/6]` is non-empty,
   so this obligation is statable as a certificate target; the requirement that the coverage
   land there is obligation 4, stated below and unchanged.
2. **A named function space**, with the profile inside it and the linearised operator
   boundedly invertible in it, `σ_min` bounded **below** by a rigorous enclosure — *not* by a
   sampled Rayleigh ratio. Leg 250 established that sampled ratios bound `σ_min` only from
   **above** (`0.0908 ≤ σ_min ≤ 0.71465` was that correction's entire content).
3. **A radii-polynomial margin with `Z₁ < 1` on the *assembled* system**, not term by term —
   lesson 89. The numbers to beat are on the record: leg 54 reached `Z₁ = 8.9591`, leg 176
   `140.72`, where `< 1` was needed.
4. **Explicit `r`-coverage.** The certificate must state the interval of similarity exponents
   it covers and show it is *not* contained in BCG's dominance regime. A Grade-A certificate
   that only re-covers the `r` where domination already works certifies an already-certified
   object — screen 2 would kill it. This is the Chen–Hou lesson applied to ourselves in
   advance.
5. **A negative control that can fail** (lesson 90): the same machinery, at a viscosity where
   the profile is known not to exist, must report failure.

## 5. A survivor I am reporting and not naming

**`NS3D-RSS-ALPHA-1`** — 3D incompressible NS, **rotated** backward globally self-similar at
rotation speed `α ≈ 1`. It survives **both** screens and it is **not** DSS-dependent, so I am
not entitled to hide it.

It is the narrowest genuinely open window, and it is expert-flagged rather than unexamined:
Perelman's question, Tsai's book Conjecture 8.9, Bradshaw–Tsai Open Problem 5.2. Pineau–Vicol
Theorem 1.4 kills `|α| ≪ 1` and `|α| ≫ 1` and says so: *"resolves Conjecture 1.1 for
`|α| ≪ 1` and `|α| ≫ 1`, but **leaves open the case `α ≈ 1`**."* Their method is explicitly
built to survive the loss of the Bernoulli head-pressure maximum principle that NRS's and
Tsai's whole method rests on. And the ansatz is **stationary**, which is the shape a
certificate needs — Bradshaw–Tsai call the asymmetry with the SS case *"surprising"* for
exactly that reason.

**Three reasons it is not the Phase-1 candidate:**

1. **No numerical anchor.** A radii-polynomial certificate is an existence argument in a ball
   around an approximate solution. No approximate nontrivial RSS profile has ever been
   computed, by anyone. There is nothing to put in the ball.
2. **The expected answer is nonexistence.** Conjecture 1.1 is that RSS solutions are
   *trivial*, and Thm 1.4 proves it at both ends. A computer-assisted **existence** proof is
   the wrong *shape* for a conjecture whose expected answer is zero — lesson 87 applied to a
   target instead of an operator.
3. Wrong side of Wall 2 (a rotated profile on `R³` has no reduction to a 1D ODE, so its
   3D-ness would have to come from the certificate itself), and it needs the Leray projector
   §4 just measured out of reach.

**`NS3D-NON-SELF-SIMILAR`** also survives, and is now *forced* in the axisymmetric setting
(Pineau–Vicol footnote 6: axisymmetric Type I is ruled out, so *"any putative singularity
must be of Type II"*). It is recorded and not proposed: a non-self-similar blow-up has no
stationary profile equation, hence nothing for interval arithmetic to enclose. Naming it
would be naming a class, not a target.
