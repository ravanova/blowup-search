## The question is not the question it looks like, and that is finding zero

`DIRECTION.md` asks for a space "interpolating between `ℓ¹_w` and origin-`H²`". **Those two
spaces are not two points on one scale.** They differ in three independent coordinates:

| | `ℓ¹_w` (leg 127) | origin-`H²` (leg 163) |
|---|---|---|
| domain | periodic circle, Fourier-coefficient truncation | the line `ℝ` |
| index | `ℓ¹` on the coefficient side (`p = 1`) | `L²` (`p = 2`) |
| side condition | algebraic weight `w_k = (1+k)^s` | origin regularity (`φ'' ∈ L²` at 0) |

So "interpolate" is not one move, it is two, and this leg checks both:

* **MOVE A — the Fourier-coefficient scale.** Hold the domain and realization, vary the index:
  `ℓ^p_w`, `1 ≤ p ≤ 2`. This is the Fourier–Lebesgue / Besov-coefficient scale and it contains
  `ℓ¹_w` at `p = 1`. **Measured here**, G1–G5.
* **MOVE B — the origin-regularity index on the line.** Hold `p = 2`, vary the Sobolev index
  between `L²` and `H²`. The only scale that reaches origin-`H²`. **Checked against leg 163's
  own census**, not re-measured — this leg builds nothing on the line.

Saying this precisely matters, because a leg that reported "no interpolant works" without
noticing that the two endpoints are not on a common scale would be reporting a confusion, not
a result.

# MOVE A — the `ℓ^p_w` scale. Check (a) fails, and the mechanism is exact.

## G1 — the positive control: reproduce leg 127 before generalizing it

Leg 127's witness is the tail operator's analytic kernel `h` (`tail_right_null`), truncated at
`M`. Local slopes converge **monotonically from above** to `1−s`, exactly as leg 127 reported.
Reading the endpoint as the answer would have reported a 0.044 deviation at `s = 0.7` as a fact
about the operator when it is a fact about where the ladder stopped, so the instrument is the
**Aitken limit** of the local slopes (discipline 72):

| `s` | slopes at `M = 512…8192` | endpoint | **Aitken limit** | predicted `1−s` | leg 127 banked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.0231 → 1.0014 | 1.0014 | **1.0000** | 1.00 | 0.9925 |
| 0.3 | 0.7501 → 0.7065 | 0.7065 | **0.7002** | 0.70 | 0.6985 |
| 0.7 | 0.4305 → 0.3442 | 0.3442 | **0.3090** | 0.30 | 0.3202 |

Max deviation of the Aitken limit from `1−s`: **0.0090**. Monotone from above at all three `s`.

**Negative control that can fail (lesson 90).** The identical code path, fed the dissipative
operator at `μ = 1`, returns exponent **−0.2904** — the ratio *grows* — against **+0.7002** at
`μ = 0`, same `s`. So the `μ = 0` decay is a property of the operator, not of the arithmetic.
Stated honestly in the JSON: this is **not** leg 127's own `μ > 0` control (that one saturates
at `|exponent| ≤ 2.62e−03` on the full bordered object using the `μ`-dependent near-null
direction); here the `μ = 0` kernel is deliberately fed to the dissipative operator, which is a
different and weaker control. Not conflated.

## G2 — THE MECHANISM, and it is the whole of check (a)

Because `h` solves the two-term recursion exactly, `T h^(M)` vanishes in every row except the
truncation edge, where the dropped term `((M+1)/2)·h_{M+1}` survives. Measured at `M = 4096`:

* single-row share of `‖T h‖₁`: **0.9999999999998843** (everything else is float noise at
  1e−16 … 1e−18);
* rows above 1e−12 relative: **1**;
* **negative control**: the same counter fed `h + 1e−3·N(0,1)` (seed 0) reports **4088** rows.
  So "one row" is a property of the witness, not a tautology of the code.

**And therefore the numerator is `p`-blind.** The norm of a one-entry vector is the same real
number in every `ℓ^p`. Measured at fixed `(K, M, s) = (8, 4096, 0.3)`:

| | `p=1.0` | `p=1.25` | `p=1.5` | `p=1.75` | `p=2.0` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| numerator `‖T h‖_{ℓ^p_w}` | 0.0932816967029332 | 0.09328169670292792 | 0.09328169670292795 | 0.09328169670292795 | 0.09328169670292794 |
| denominator `‖h‖_{ℓ^p_w}` | 11.7877 | 5.9580 | 4.1585 | 3.3600 | 2.9304 |

Numerator relative spread across `p`: **5.653e−14** — the same float to 14 digits.
Denominator relative spread: **1.5707**, i.e. **157%**.

**That is check (a), in two numbers.** Moving along the interpolation scale changes the
quantity leg 127's proof *divides by* by 157%, and the quantity it divides by 6e−12 %. The
index `p` cannot reach the numerator, because the numerator is one entry.

## G3 — the sweep: leg 127's exponent over the whole `(p, s)` grid

Aitken limit of `σ_min ∼ M^{−e}` at each `(p, s)`:

| | `s=0.0` | `s=0.3` | `s=0.7` | `s=0.9` | `s=1.0` | `s=1.2` | `s=1.4` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `p = 1.0` | 1.0000 | 0.7002 | 0.3090 | 0.1436 | 0.0836 | 0.0202 | 0.0038 |
| `p = 2.0` | 1.0000 | 0.7000 | 0.3000 | 0.1000 | 0.0000 | −0.1996 | −0.3898 |
| predicted `1−s` | 1.00 | 0.70 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.00 | −0.20 | −0.40 |

* max deviation from `1−s` over `s ≤ 0.7`: **0.00905**
* max spread across `p` at fixed `s ≤ 0.7`: **0.00904**
* **rows where `p > 1` rescues an exponent that is dead at `p = 1`: 0**

**The exponent tracks `1−s` and is `p`-blind where it is `O(1)`.** Leg 127's technique does not
weaken along the interpolation scale at all. It weakens only as `s → 1` — and `s` is a degree
of freedom that was already available at `p = 1`, and was already swept there by legs 51/55/127.

**One thing reported rather than trimmed:** the `p`-spread *grows* with `s` (0.0000 at `s=0`,
0.0090 at `s=0.7`, 0.0836 at `s=1.0`, 0.3936 at `s=1.4`). That is not a `p`-dependence of the
mechanism — it is the Aitken limit becoming ill-conditioned exactly where the exponent it
extrapolates passes through zero. It is in the JSON, and the conclusion deliberately rests on
the `s ≤ 0.7` block where the exponent is `O(1)`.

## G4 — THE INVARIANT: the whole two-parameter scale collapses to one parameter

Three membership thresholds decide the scale. With the kernel `h_m ∼ m^{−2}` (measured
exponent **−2.00236**), the cokernel functional `u_m ∼ m^{+1}` (measured **+1.00118**), both
from leg 51/127's own `fredholm_sides`, and the target's coefficients `∼ k^{−(1+α)}` with
`α = 0.3973531116778231` (leg 55's banked value):

| in `ℓ^p_w`, `w = (1+k)^s`, dual `ℓ^q_{w^{-1}}` | condition | in terms of `σ := s + 1/p` |
|---|---|---|
| kernel `h` **in** the space | `p(2−s) > 1` | `σ < 2` |
| cokernel `u` **bounded** on it | `q(s−1) > 1` | `σ > 2` |
| target **in** the space | `p(1+α−s) > 1` | `σ < 1+α` |

**All three are functions of the single combination `σ = s + 1/p`** — the Sobolev/Besov scaling
index, which is precisely the invariant of interpolation: real or complex interpolation of a
couple at equal `σ` produces spaces of the same `σ`.

Measured, as a growth exponent of the partial-sum norm rather than a convergent/divergent
boolean (report a magnitude, never a boolean), over 45 `(p, s)` points × 3 quantities:

* quantities scored: **114**; in the marginal band (`|σ − threshold| ≤ 0.15`): **21**
* worst deviation from the `σ`-prediction outside the band: **0.01605**
* mismatches above 0.02 outside the band: **0**

The marginal band is excluded from scoring because *at* a threshold the true behaviour is
logarithmic and a power-law fit correctly reads a small spurious exponent — scoring there
would be scoring the instrument. The band is reported, not hidden, and its log behaviour is
**demonstrated**: at `p=1, s=1` (`σ=2`) the partial sums at `N = 10³…10⁶` add a *constant* per
decade, max/min increment ratio **1.01114** — the signature of `log N`.

**The one row nearest the band edge was chased rather than tolerated.** At `p=1, s=1.2`
(`σ=2.2`) the deviation is 0.01605 at `N = 8e5`; over the ladder `N = 5e4 → 2e5 → 8e5 → 3e6` it
falls monotonically **0.02955 → 0.02167 → 0.01605 → 0.01213**. It is the truncation of the
partial sum, not a failure of the `σ` prediction. Banked as a ladder rather than absorbed by
widening the band until it passed.

## G5 — the window, and why its width is exactly zero

The kernel leaves the space at `σ ≥ 2`; the cokernel enters the dual at `σ ≤ 2`. **The only
`σ` at which neither `ℓ¹_w` obstruction is present is `σ = 2` exactly — a single point, at
every `p`.** At `p = 1` that point is `s = 1`, which is exactly leg 51's divergence-curve
minimum and exactly the exponent the standing ban calls "the ONE exponent at which bordering
cannot help." The interpolation scale does not widen it; it translates it.

**And at that point the target is out of the space.** The target's ceiling is `σ < 1+α =
1.397353`. The crossing is at `σ = 2`. The margin is

> **`α − 1 = −0.602647` exponent units — the SAME number at every `p`.**

| | `p=1` | `p=1.25` | `p=1.5` | `p=1.75` | `p=2` | `p=3` | `p=10` | `p=∞` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `s` at the crossing | 1.0000 | 1.2000 | 1.3333 | 1.4286 | 1.5000 | 1.6667 | 1.9000 | 2.0000 |
| `s` ceiling for the target | 0.3974 | 0.5974 | 0.7307 | 0.8259 | 0.8974 | 1.0640 | 1.2974 | 1.3974 |
| **margin** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** | **−0.602647** |

**Both walls translate by exactly `−1/p`, so the gap between them is an invariant of the whole
scale.** Interpolation moves the picture; it does not change any relative position in it.

**Crosscheck against the banked `p = 1` measurement**, which is the point of quoting a
prediction: leg 55 measured the `ℓ¹_w` margin at `s = 1` as **−0.6062554687114012**
(`p2_route_nb_v1_targetnorm.json`, `NB5_norms/classes[3]`). This leg's asymptotic prediction is
**−0.602647**. Difference **0.003609** — leg 55's is the measured norm, this leg's is the
exponent-level asymptote, and they agree to 0.6%.

This is the `p`-independent generalization of leg 51's own sentence: *"the class where the
operator is least bad is the class where the target has infinite norm."* It is true on the
whole scale, with the same 0.60-exponent-unit gap.

## O3 — what actually forces `a = 0` exactness, and why it is not a property of the space

Leg 163's O3 names it: the **single-simple-pole identity**

```
    H(Ω) − iΩ  =  i / (y + i/2)
```

which holds because `Ω(y) = −y/(y² + ¼)` is the *exact* `a = 0` CLM profile. That identity is
what collapses the nonlocal linearization to a scalar first-order ODE on each Hardy block, and
it is what produces the closed-form kernel and the exact Mellin norm `1/α`.

**It is an equation satisfied by the PROFILE. It contains no norm, no weight, and no index.**
Every space — interpolated or not, on the line or on the circle — inherits it if and only if
the profile is the exact `a = 0` one. No choice of scale can supply it and none can remove it.

So check (b) is not a question an interpolation scale is *able* to answer. That is a stronger
statement than "the candidate scales fail check (b)", and it is the honest one.

## O4 — and the one interpolation that could even be attempted is ruled out in Xu's own text

Moving the Sobolev index between `L²` and `H²` *is* the MOVE-B interpolation. Leg 163 read Xu
arXiv:2607.19762 §4.6 at primary source and quotes it:

> "One cannot use the `H²` metric to empty the strip and the `L²` metric to close the origin
> channel."

The origin-regularity index and the positions of the essential lines are **coupled**. On the
maximal `L²` realization every point of the open strip `−½ < Re λ < 3/2` is a genuine
eigenvalue, so there is no `L²` spectral gap at all; requiring `φ'' ∈ L²` near the origin is the
whole difference; and a *stronger* realization shifts the origin line off instead. An
intermediate index gets neither.

**This was pre-registered.** `writeup/novelty/leg_182.md` pass 3c named this sentence as the
candidate obstruction for check (b) *before* the check was run, so the NO is not a discovery
this leg's own construction conveniently arrived at.

**Attribution, stated rather than implied: this leg did NOT re-read Xu at primary source.**
Every Xu sentence here is quoted from leg 163's journal, which did. Recorded in the runner
docstring, the JSON and the novelty log.

## The Banach-algebra constraint, pre-registered and reported as NOT the reason

The novelty pass (3a) surfaced why the CAP literature is bimodal — `ℓ¹_ν` **or** Hilbert `H^l`,
nothing between: `ℓ¹_ν` is a Banach algebra under convolution (which is what `Z₂` needs) and
`ℓ^p` is **not** an algebra for `p > 1`. On the `ℓ^p_w` scale the algebra property is recovered
only when the weight embeds the space in `ℓ¹`, i.e. `σ > 1`.

`σ > 1` holds throughout the region of interest (the crossing is at `σ = 2`), so **this
constraint does not bind, and it is not the reason the gate answers NO.** Recorded explicitly
so it cannot later be mistaken for the reason — the reasons are G2/G5 and O3/O4.
