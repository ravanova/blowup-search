## What "independent" means here, and where it honestly stops

The exact algebra shares **no code** with `solver/chen_inviscid_certificate.py`. Leg 187 works
in univariate `Q[X]` at five pinned rational values of `g`; this leg works in **bivariate
`Q[X, b]` with `g = b²` left symbolic**, so the orbit identity is established for *every* `b`
at once. The kernel claim is likewise upgraded from prose-plus-float to an exact construction:
`DF[φ]` is assembled term by term in the same arithmetic — using that `H` and `∫₀^X` act in `X`
only and therefore commute with `∂_b`, so `Hφ = ∂_b(HΨ)` and `VHφ = ∂_b U` exactly — and its
numerator is the zero polynomial.

The tangent, in exact form: `∂_b Ψ = (16/3 · X b⁴ − 16 X³ b²)/D³`, nonzero, and dividing by
`2b` reproduces leg 187's `orbit_generator` **exactly** — checked as a polynomial identity, not
numerically.

Where independence stops, stated rather than glossed: **the float columns reproduce bitwise**
(relative difference `0.0` on all 20), because an independently written re-implementation still
drives the same deterministic NumPy code path on the same grid. That agreement is a check on
transcription and on the JSON, not on the numerics. The genuinely independent content of this
leg is the exact algebra and the second resolution ladder.

### The one thing the exact algebra could not have caught by itself, and was checked

An exact rational identity is only about the certificate's equation if the closed forms it
starts from are the repository's own operators. `H Ψ = κ(b² − X²)/D²` and `U = κX/D` are
therefore **measured** against `dp.H` and `dp.VH`, not assumed:

| n | rel err vs `dp.H` | rel err vs `dp.VH` | grid residual on the centre |
|---|---|---|---|
| 201 | 3.900e-06 | 7.187e-06 | 2.923e-04 |
| 401 | 2.146e-07 | 9.328e-07 | 1.889e-05 |
| 801 | 1.283e-08 | 9.031e-07 | 1.187e-06 |

All shrinking with resolution, i.e. discretisation error, which is what ties the exact
statement to the equation the certificate is about. (`Ψ` and `HΨ` are the real and imaginary
parts of `κ/(X + ib)²`, analytic and decaying in the upper half plane — a Hilbert pair.)

## Finding 1 (PRECISION, does not change the verdict): `n^2.36` is an average, not an exponent

`Z₂ ~ n^2.36` is a least-squares slope over a **curving** ladder. The local pairwise slopes on
leg 187's own ladder climb monotonically:

| n range | 201→301 | 301→401 | 401→601 | 601→801 | 801→1201 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| local slope | 2.129 | 2.299 | 2.391 | 2.455 | 2.495 |

and refitting on a **different** ladder (`n = 151…901`, local slopes 1.876 → 2.474) gives a
global slope of **2.2466**, i.e. the headline moves by **0.113 (4.8%)** with the choice of
resolutions. Leg 187's prose quotes `2.36` without that caveat.

This does **not** weaken H7 — it slightly strengthens it. Every local slope is ≥ 1.88 and
*increasing*, so `Z₂` diverges at least as fast as claimed; what is unsupported is the
particular decimal, not the divergence. The negative control behaves the other way and is
stable across both ladders: `σ_min/σ_max`'s slope is **−6.6751** vs **−6.6548** (0.3% apart).

Where the growth lives, decomposed (leg 187 states the mechanism; this quantifies it):
`‖B‖ = 2(‖H‖ + a‖VH‖‖D‖)` with slopes `‖H‖ 0.0004`, `‖VH‖ 0.0000`, `‖D‖ 1.0033`, `‖A‖ 1.3562`.
Every power of `n` in `Z₂` is the **derivative operator's** — exactly the structural reason leg
187 gives (`B` contains `v_X`, which the weighted sup norm does not control). Also worth
recording, as a construction note rather than a discrepancy: `‖B‖` is a **product bound**, not
the induced norm of the bilinear map, so `Z₂`'s absolute size (`2.30e12` at `n = 801`) is an
over-estimate. It does not soften the divergence, for the reason just decomposed.
