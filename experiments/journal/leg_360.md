## §2 — Condition 2 first: δ₀'s explicitness

The proof's very last step (l.2072-2075, end of §9.2, "Proof of Theorem 1.9") gives δ₀ an
**explicit formula**, quoted verbatim:

> *"Upon letting $\delta_0 = K_H^{-1} K_2^{-1} \cdot \frac 18 \theta_*^2$ (a constant that only
> depends on $C_u$) ..."*

So leg 330's "non-explicit constant" is **not a bare existence statement** — the paper does not
merely assert `δ₀ > 0` exists; it writes down the formula. That matters, because it changes the
question from "is there any route to a number" (leg 330's honest open framing) to "does the
formula bottom out in things that are themselves numbers". I traced every symbol in the
formula back to its definition:

| symbol | where it comes from | is it a number? |
|---|---|---|
| `ε_*` | *"there exists a universal constant $\eps_*>0$ such that if $\limsup_{r\to 0^+} \frac 1r \int_{Q_r}\|\nabla u\|^2 \leq \eps_*$, then $(0,0)$ is a regular point"* (l.1994-1995, citing Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg `[CKN82]`, `[Lin98]`, `[LadySeregin99]`, `[Kukavica09]`) | **NO.** Classical ε-regularity threshold, cited generically, never assigned a value in this paper. |
| `θ_*` | *"we pick $\theta_* \in (0,1]$ to be small enough to ensure $10\,\theta_*^2 \leq \eps_*$"* (l.2075 region) | formula-level (`θ_* ~ √(ε_*/10)`), inherits `ε_*`'s non-explicitness |
| `K_H = K_H(R_{**}, C_u)` | *"By Harnack's inequality for the uniformly elliptic operator $\bar L^*-\lambda_{\bar R}$ (see e.g. [Corollary 9.25]{GilbargTrudinger98}), there exists a constant $K_H = K_H(R_{**},C_u)>0$"* (l.2063-2065) | **NO.** Cited generically to Gilbarg-Trudinger's general elliptic PDE textbook; no formula or bound derived here. |
| `K_2 = K_2(C_u)` | from the enstrophy bound `∫|Ω|²w ≤ K_1 R̄⁻² + K_2 δ₀` (l.2054-2058), itself built on the Gaussian eigenfunction bound `M(ε,C_u)` from a barrier-argument construction (Lemma `Gaussian:upper`) | traceable but not reduced to closed form in the paper |
| `R_*` | *"$R_* := \min\{R\geq 2 : K^2R^{-5/2}+8C_\mathcal{E}K^3R^{-1}\leq \frac14\theta_*^2\}$"* (l.1893-1895) | **this one IS genuinely explicit** — a computable inverse-function definition given `θ_*`, `K`, `C_E` |

**Conclusion, definite:** `δ₀` is boundable **as a formula**, one link short of a number. Two
of its five ingredients (`ε_*` from CKN, `K_H` from Harnack/Gilbarg-Trudinger) are classical
"exists but not computed" citations — producing an explicit numeric `ε_*` or `K_H` is a
well-known, nontrivial undertaking in its own right (effective versions of CKN's constant and
of elliptic Harnack constants both exist in the literature in *some* contexts, but PV neither
cite nor derive one here, and doing so is well outside a read-and-report leg). This is not "no
route to a number" (leg 330's most cautious phrasing) — there is a route, laid out precisely —
but it does not terminate in this paper.

## §3 — Condition 1: does the pressure-decay loss close for the screened class?

This is where I found something sharper than "still open" — a **computed** answer, using only
two of the paper's own displayed estimates.

**T4's own pressure hypothesis**, quoted (l.396-399, `eq:local:pbound`):

> *"assume there exists a constant $C_p>0$ such that the pressure satisfies the bound
> $|p(x,t)|\leq C_p,\qquad (x,t)\in A\times [-1,0)$"*, `A = {1/2<|x|<3/4}`.

**The paper's own global DSS/RDSS pressure-decay estimate**, first proved for RSS (l.447-449,
`eq:decay:pressure`) and then explicitly extended **uniformly in time over the whole DSS/RDSS
period** (l.1389-1392, Lemma `many:gradients`, exactly the lemma governing the screened
object's class):

> *"the pressure $P(\cdot,s)$ obeys estimate~\eqref{eq:decay:pressure} for every $s\in[0,S]$,
> with the same $(\alpha,S)$-independent constants ... $C_{P,0}$"* — and, spelling out the
> `k`-derivative form leg 330 already quoted (l.1416-1420, `eq:decay:pressure:k`):
>
> *"$|\nabla_y^k P(y,s)| \leq \frac{C_{P,k}}{(1+|y|)^{2-\sigma+k}}$ ... where $C_{P,k}$ depends
> on $C_{U,0}$, $k$, and $\sigma >0$"*

**The computation** (mine, using only the change of variables the paper itself defines at
l.1730-1733, `p(x,t) = (-t)^{-1}P(y,s)`, `y = x/√(-t)`, `s = -log(-t)`):

For `x` fixed in the annulus `A`, as `t → 0⁻`, `|y| = |x|/√(-t) → ∞`, comparable to `1/√(-t)`.
Substituting:

```
|p(x,t)| = (-t)^{-1}|P(y,s)|
         ≤ (-t)^{-1} · C_{P,0}(σ)/(1+|y|)^{2-σ}
         ~ (-t)^{-1} · C_{P,0}(σ) · (-t)^{(2-σ)/2}
         = C_{P,0}(σ) · (-t)^{-σ/2}
```

**This diverges as `t → 0⁻` for every fixed `σ > 0`** — and the paper's decay lemma is
quantified over "any `σ ∈ (0,1)`" (a strict open interval): **`σ = 0` is never attained.** So
the general, class-wide estimate the paper proves for exactly the DSS/RDSS Type-I profiles the
screen targets **does not establish** the annulus-boundedness hypothesis `eq:local:pbound` — it
produces a bound that blows up there.

**What this is and is not a proof of.** It is *not* a proof that every conceivable DSS/RDSS
candidate's pressure must diverge on the annulus — the estimate is an upper bound from a
singular-integral/Riesz-transform representation (*"The bound~\eqref{eq:decay:pressure} follows
[from the] singular integral kernel representation $P = R_i R_j (U^i U^j)$"*, l.468), not
claimed sharp, and a specific candidate could in principle have extra cancellation. But it *is*
a definite, computed answer to the question asked: **the paper's own machinery, applied
directly to its own screened class, does not close the gap — it actively points at failure.**
This sharpens leg 330's "not verified from the paper's own decay estimates" (a negative,
absence-shaped finding) into a positive, computed divergence.

## §4 — Gate verdict: class (ii) NON-OPERATIONAL

Both sub-questions were read to a **definite, quoted, computed** conclusion — not to
"could go either way" readings. That is why this lands at (ii) and not (iii): nothing here was
ambiguous once the formula and the two estimates were located and combined; the arithmetic in
§3 is a two-line substitution, not a judgment call.

- **δ₀:** formula-explicit, numerically non-explicit. Bottleneck: CKN's `ε_*`, Harnack's `K_H`.
- **Pressure decay:** does not close; the paper's own class-wide estimate diverges at `t→0⁻`
  for every attainable `σ`.
