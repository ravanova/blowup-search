Leg 248 wrote the patch and its own "independent" reachability re-run **in the same commit**

# BLOG — what an independent re-run of a repair is actually for

Leg 248 fixed a real bug. Its own report said so, with numbers. This leg's whole job was to
not take that on trust, and the honest headline is that **leg 248 was right** — but the two
things worth writing down are *how close* the check came to the numbers, and the one place
where independence actually changed something.

The bug was a nice one. `ACollocation.newton` decided whether it had converged by looking at a
*ratio*: the size of the residual divided by the size of the term the residual is supposed to
cancel. Both are quadratic in the unknown, so the ratio is **exactly** blind to the equation's
own scaling symmetry — multiply the profile and the wave speed by the same λ and you get
another exact solution, and the verdict cannot tell you which one you got. The two gauge rows
that pin *which* solution you wanted were computed by the method and then thrown away.

You can watch that happen. Feed the module the family (λΩ\*, λc\*) for λ from 0.5 to 1000 and
its relative residual sits at **8.78e-14 and does not move** — total spread across the whole
family **6.92e-14**, a dynamic range of **1.79×** — while the reported wave speed sweeps
**2000×** (0.248 → 496.1) and the *absolute* residual sweeps **6.72e+06×**. The verdict said
"converged" to all of them. That is not a small error in a number; it is a number that was
never looking.

The fix was to AND in the thing that *is* affine: the gauge defect. And it is affine, precisely.
Divide the measured gauge defect by |λ−1| along the family and you get 0.9999999999999993,
1.0000000000000013, 1.0000000000000002, 1.0000000000000002, 0.9999999999999996,
1.0000000000000009 — six values, all 1.0 to within 1.3e-15. The repair's own justification is
not an argument, it is a measured slope.

So: does it bite? Pre-repair, **6 of 6** off-gauge members were accepted. Post-repair, **0 of
6**. Does it overcorrect — does it start rejecting solves that were genuinely fine? **0 of 8**
clean solves suppressed, and the closest one sits **7.18 decades** below the threshold. Does
it move anything? **0 floats moved out of 12,356 bitwise comparisons** against the genuine
pre-repair source read out of git.

The place independence earned its keep is smaller and more interesting than any of those. The
verification recomputes the gauge defect *itself*, from the returned profile, using the formula
in the module's docstring — rather than reading the field the module returns. Those could have
disagreed: a repair can compute the right verdict from a wrong number, or return a number that
is not the one the gate used. They agree bitwise, 41/41, relative error exactly 0.0. That is
now a fact somebody other than the patch's author has checked, which is the entire point of the
228/231–234 discipline.

And one thing did come out differently from leg 248's report, at the fifteenth digit. Leg 248
banked the worst gauge defect among converged cases as **8.882e-16**; this re-run measures
**1.110e-15**. Same decade, 1.25× apart, both about seven decades below the 1e-8 threshold —
a float-reproduction difference between machines, not a disagreement. It is reported here rather
than smoothed over, because a verification leg that only ever reproduces numbers exactly is a
verification leg that is not really re-running anything.

**Clay odds: ~0.05%, unmoved.** This is solver hygiene on a latent defect in a module with no
claim-bearing call site. No link of the L1→L4 chain is touched.
