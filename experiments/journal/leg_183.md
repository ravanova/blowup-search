## The answer, and it does not rest on comparing headlines

Both statements are about the same operator — Xu linearizes about `Ω(y) = −y/(y²+1/4)`,
`c_l = 1`, this repository's `clm_one_scale` normalisation. **So the gate was never "is it the
same operator". The operator is shared; the space and the class are where the answer lives.**

### Xu §8's no-go, verbatim and COMPLETE

Quoted whole rather than clipped at its memorable clause, because the clipped form is what
makes it look broader than it is (p. 33–34):

> *"Likewise a naive interval-arithmetic resolvent enclosure is unavailable: a weight is a
> change of norm, eigenvalues are norm-invariant, and the truncated `L_a` genuinely carries
> essential-smear eigenvalues inside the strip (the compactified grid mis-renders the
> continuous line at the wrong real part), so no weighted enclosure can exclude them. The
> consequence is that the rigorous discrete exclusion must be analytical (the `a = 0` anchor
> plus continuation) rather than a black-box computer-assisted enclosure; where a
> computer-assisted proof does enter, it must first be given a spectrally correct rendering of
> the essential spectrum (a non-periodic log-Mellin operator at high resolution feeding an
> argument-principle count), not applied to the raw grid."*

**Its five hypotheses, each located in the paper** (full detail in `writeup/novelty/leg_183.md`
§2 and in the JSON's `XU8_HYPOTHESES`):

| | hypothesis | where |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | the object is the **truncated** `L_a` — the §7(i) dense finite-difference Jacobian on the compactified grid, `N = 1024`–`8192`, **at sampled advections `a = 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.65`, all `a > 0`**. At `a = 0` there is nothing to enclose: Theorem 2 excludes discrete spectrum in closed form, and §8 puts `a = 0` on the **analytic** side of its own dichotomy | §7 l. 1834–1839; §8 l. 1925 |
| H2 | the task is **spectral exclusion from a region** — "them" = essential-smear eigenvalues inside Proposition 2's strip `{−1/2 ≤ Re λ ≤ 3/2}`, serving the winding count `n_disc(a)` | eq (3.5); §3.2, §7 |
| H3 | **load-bearing:** the discretization imposes **no origin condition**, so it renders the *wrong realization*. The smear is not numerical error — it is the faithful spectrum of the maximal `L²` realization | abstract l. 30–31; §7(ii)'s `Re = +2.4931` vs `+5/2` |
| H4 | the quantified class is **weights (norms)** and the mechanism is **norm-INVARIANCE**. Valid exactly and only for similarity-invariant quantities | §8 l. 1922–1923 |
| H5 | it is about a **black-box** route, with the prerequisite named — not that CAP is impossible on the object | §8 l. 1927–1929 |

**Stated positively, so the question closes:** *for the `a > 0` continuation task, applying a
weighted interval-arithmetic resolvent enclosure to the raw compactified-grid truncation of
`L_a` — a discretization imposing no origin condition, hence faithfully rendering the maximal-`L²`
realization in which the strip genuinely IS spectrum — cannot exclude the strip eigenvalues,
because a weight is a similarity and eigenvalues are similarity-invariant.*

Xu's own novelty sentence names nothing adjacent: *"The novelty lies in the completeness of the
point spectrum, the realization dichotomy, and the constructive resolvent."*

## Axis C is the decisive one, and it is a proved impossibility rather than a difference

Xu §8's argument, in its entirety, is: *the quantity is invariant under change of norm,
therefore no choice of norm helps.* Apply that template to `Z₁` and it is **false on Xu's own
results** — the same operator has `σ_min = 0` in `ℓ¹_w` at `s < 1` and a spectral gap of `1/2`
after modulation on origin-`H²` (Xu Theorems 1–3). An argument whose whole content is
"changing the norm cannot move this" cannot reach a quantity the norm demonstrably moves from
`0` to `1/2` on the same operator.

**Made falsifiable on this repository's own matrix**, pre-registered in the novelty log before
it was coded, in three parts:

| part | result |
|---|---|
| **premise (exact, rational)** — is the weight really applied as the two-sided similarity `D L D⁻¹`? Falsifiable: a one-sided row scaling is *not* a similarity and would change `tr(Mʲ)` | **YES.** `tr(Mʲ)`, `j = 1..4`, **identical** for `p = 1` and `p = 2` against unweighted `(1, −353, −5, 23205)`, exact `Fraction` arithmetic, `K = 14` |
| **Xu's quantity** — eigenvalues under that similarity | **exactly invariant. It is a theorem, not a measurement**, and is stated as one |
| **NGX's quantity** — `σ_min = 1/‖L⁻¹‖_w` under the *same* weights, *same* matrix, *same* module | **NOT invariant.** Spread **31.02× / 50.94× / 83.40×** at `K = 32/64/128` over `s ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.7, 0.9, 1.5}` |

**How it could have come out the other way** (lesson 90): if `σ_min` had come back invariant
alongside the eigenvalues, Xu §8's mechanism *would* transfer to `Z₁` and the gate answer would
have had to be *partially* or *fully*. It did not.

**Reported as a shape, not an endpoint** (discipline 72): the spread **grows** with truncation
— `31.02 → 50.94 → 83.40` — because `σ_min` falls faster at small `s`. And leg 127's landed
ladder is sharper still: the **exponent** `p` in `σ_min ∼ M^{−p}` tracks `1 − s`
(`0.9925 / 0.6985 / 0.3202`). **A similarity-invariant quantity cannot have a weight-dependent
exponent.**

## One of my own checks failed first, and the failure is kept (lesson 76)

`writeup/novelty/leg_183.md` §4 pre-registered that the eigenvalues *"must be invariant in `s`
to machine precision"*. The first draft of axis C measured exactly that, **in float64**, and it
**failed, by `1.907` relative** at `K = 64` — worst per truncation `1.7274 / 1.9073 / 1.8574`.
**The novelty log is left exactly as committed, wrong prediction included** — that is the point
of committing it first (leg 181's precedent). Interrogated rather than banked:

* The invariance is a **tautology of linear algebra** for a diagonal weight. Measuring a
  tautology in floating point is **lesson 86** arriving on schedule — the result was a
  statement about conditioning, not about mathematics, and it should never have been the
  control.
* The float deviation has a named cause, and **Xu states it himself** (§8 p. 33): *"`L_a` is
  strongly non-normal (the eigenvector matrix is severely ill-conditioned, its condition number
  growing with resolution)"*. So the failed check is **kept in the runner** as
  `axis_c_float_eigenvalue_census`, relabelled: it is an **independent corroboration of Xu's own
  non-normality caveat on this repository's matrix**, not a refutation of the exact result.
* The exact check that replaced it (`axis_c_premise_check`) is aimed at the one thing that is
  **not** a tautology and **can** come out the other way: whether the weight is applied as a
  two-sided similarity at all.

**This leg's verdict rests on the exact premise check and the `σ_min` spread, not on the float
census.**

Refusing to overstate the disjointness is as much a duty as refusing to overstate the novelty.

(`origin/main` `2d89233`, flagged to me mid-leg by the coordinator) measured that `v′(0)` — half
of Xu's own modulation projection, eq (A.13) — has divergent dual norm on `ℓ¹_w` **exactly for

mechanistically, *"the origin condition is not boundedly imposable here"*.

**Not a pre-emption, for three checkable reasons:** (i) it is a kinship with **Prop. 2, not
§8**, and the gate asks about §8; (ii) Prop. 2's conclusion is `σ_ess ⊇` strip **in `L²`**, and
no derivation of an `ℓ¹_w` statement from it exists in the paper or anywhere else; (iii) leg 181
is **this repository's** observation, not Xu's — Xu never connects his origin condition to a
weighted sequence space, because he never writes one down (negatives N1/N2).

Leg 181 also **sharpens axis E in this leg's favour**: leg 127's object is not the "raw grid"
§8's no-go is about — it carries an origin condition that is Xu's own functional.
