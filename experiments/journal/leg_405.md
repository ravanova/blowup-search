## §1 METHOD

### §1.1 What was measured against (§3k discharged BEFORE dispatch, not by me)

I did not hand-roll a benchmark and call it a reference. A self-authored comparison measures
its author's own assumptions.

- **Solver level:** JAX-CFD `jax_cfd.spectral.equations.ForcedNavierStokes2D` with
  `forcings.kolmogorov_forcing` — Kochkov, Smith, Alieva, Wang, Brenner & Hoyer, PNAS
  118(21) e2101784118 (2021), arXiv:2102.01010. Pinned `jax 0.11.1`, `jax_cfd 0.2.1`, x64
  enabled. Verified constructed and stepped at `N = 24`; state `(24,13) complex128`; stepper
  `time_stepping.crank_nicolson_rk4`. Timed **in the library's own idiom**: `jax.lax.scan`
  over 100 steps under `jit`, so that JAX is not charged per-call dispatch it never pays in
  practice. A `reference_sanity` check confirms the reference state actually advances and
  stays finite — a reference that is fast because it is doing nothing is not a reference.
- **Transform level:** FFTW3 — Frigo & Johnson, Proc. IEEE 93(2):216–231 (2005) — via
  `pyfftw 0.15.1`, planned with `FFTW_MEASURE` on aligned buffers. Supplies the
  20-transforms-per-RK4-step **algorithmic floor** for this scheme.
- Chandler & Kerswell, JFM 722:554–595 (2013): **citation only, UNREAD, depth not upgraded,
  nothing in this leg rests on it.**

The reference did **not** drift; the fallback to the FFTW3 floor alone was not needed.
Both instruments are reported anyway, because two independent yardsticks that agree is worth
more than one.

### §1.2 Environment discipline

The reference stack was installed into a **throwaway venv in session scratch**, never into
`/home/andy/projects/Unsolved/.venv` — that venv carries live campaign runs. `rerun.sh`
builds the throwaway venv itself and asserts it is not the repo venv. Thread counts pinned
explicitly (`OMP/MKL/OPENBLAS/NUMEXPR/VECLIB_NUM_THREADS=1`, plus
`XLA_FLAGS=--xla_cpu_multi_thread_eigen=false intra_op_parallelism_threads=1`) so that a
ratio is not secretly a ratio of thread counts.

### §1.3 The machine was NOT quiet, and that shaped the whole instrument

Three sibling units ran concurrently in this working tree throughout. Load average moved
between ~4.2 and ~13.5 during the leg. **I could not get a quiet machine, so I report
ratios, not absolutes**, and I built the instrument to make that honest:

1. **Interleaved rounds with rotating order.** Contestants are timed inside the same round
   and the ratio is formed *within* the round, so a load excursion moves numerator and
   denominator together.
2. **Two clocks.** `time.perf_counter_ns` (wall) and `time.process_time_ns` (CPU time of
   this process). The CPU clock does not charge us for time another process held the core;
   agreement between the two is the check that the answer is not a scheduling artefact.
3. **Medians over many repeats**, with an inner loop sized to ~0.02 s (or ~0.3 s for the
   duel) so the timer itself is amortised. Warm-up: 3 discarded calls for numpy; for JAX,
   the `jit`/`scan` compile is triggered and `block_until_ready()` called before timing, so
   compile time is never counted as run time.
4. **A reproducibility re-run** of the whole `ours` part at ~2x the load, banked as
   `raw/ours_venv_r2.json`, precisely to show *which* numbers survive load and which do not.

### §1.4 Controls, including one that FAILED

Every part carries three controls: a **positive** (busy loop of 4x the work must come back
~4x), a **negative** (no-op floor: what the harness charges for nothing), and a **planted**
(`fft2` at 128 vs 64 must come back heavier).

**The `--part ref` sequential run's positive control FAILED** — busy-loop ratio 7.15 against
an expected ~4 — because load drifted from 4.35 to 7.69 *during* the run; "ours" measured
2519 µs in that process against 1072 µs twenty minutes earlier. A ratio built from two blocks
measured at different loads is not a measurement of two implementations. **That run is banked
but answers nothing.** It is the reason `--part paired` and then `--part duel` exist. The
failure is recorded in the artefact rather than hidden; it is also the single most useful
thing this leg learned about its own instrument.

### §1.5 Two independent attributions for (i)

- **(A) IN-SITU:** `np.fft.fft2`/`np.fft.ifft2` monkeypatched with timing wrappers while a
  *real* `_rk4_step` runs 300 times. Wrapper overhead measured separately (0.49 µs/call) and
  subtracted.
- **(B) LINE-LEVEL RECONSTRUCTION:** every array expression in `_rhs_hat` and `_rk4_step`
  re-created with identical operands and shapes and timed individually.

The two disagree, and the disagreement is reported as
`UNATTRIBUTED_python_frames_and_attribution_error` so that **the table sums to 100% by
construction**. It is a breakdown, not a list of hot spots.

### §2.2 GATE (ii) — fixed per-call overhead, ESTABLISHED BY A SIZE SWEEP

Sweep over N = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80, 96, 128, 160, 192, 256,
384, 512, three curves each: `np.fft.fft2`, a bare elementwise complex multiply, and the full
`_rk4_step`.

- `np.fft.fft2` on a **4x4** complex array: **35.0 µs**. On 8x8: 34.0 µs. On 24x24: 42.3 µs.
  A 4x4 transform is 36 complex operations. **The per-call price is essentially independent
  of the array** in this regime.
- **Flatness check:** `t(32)/t(24) = 1.129` for **1.939x** the `N²log N²` work.
  `_rk4_step`: `t(32)/t(24) = 1.182`. **The Conductor's overhead-bound hypothesis is
  CONFIRMED as a regime and its magnitude is now measured rather than asserted.**
- **ANSWER: fixed per-call overhead = 59.6% of the step.** Direct floor method:
  20 transforms x 34.04 µs + 78 elementwise x 0.801 µs = 743.3 µs of a 1246.9 µs sweep-context
  step.
- **Cross-check, two-point extrapolation from N=384 and N=512 back to N=24: 64.6%.** Marked
  `TRUSTED`.
- **A third estimator was computed and is marked `TRUSTED: False` with its reason recorded.**
  An OLS intercept fit over N≥128 returns a fixed fraction of 1.86 — greater than 100%, i.e.
  nonsense — because numpy's `fft2` is radix- and cache-sensitive and is *not* monotone in N:
  N=128 measures 471.7 µs, **slower** than N=160's 381.2 µs. It is banked because it was
  computed, not because it is used.

So: **~60% of every step is dispatch, not floating point.**

### §2.3 GATE (iii) — within 3x of the reference at N=24? **NO.**

Instrument: `--part duel`, 3 contestants only, ~0.3 s rounds, rotating order, 60 rounds,
under **both** clocks.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| ratio ours / JAX-CFD, **CPU clock** | **4.341** median (p10 3.578, p90 5.429, **worst round 3.372**) |
| ratio ours / JAX-CFD, **wall clock** | 4.212 median (p10 3.446, p90 5.236) |
| ours, per step | 3027.3 µs (duel context, high load) |
| reference, per step | 721.1 µs (same context, same rounds) |
| ours / 20-transform FFTW3 floor | **16.88x** (p10 14.58) |
| reference / same floor | **3.97x** |
| `numpy.fft.fft2` / FFTW3 `fft2` at 24x24 | **8.85x** |

**On the CPU clock every one of the 60 rounds exceeds 3x — the worst is 3.372.** On the wall
clock one round dips to 1.998; that round is a scheduling artefact (the CPU clock for the
same round does not dip), and it is recorded in the artefact rather than dropped. The `NO` is
carried by the CPU clock, which is the load-immune instrument, and corroborated by the wall
clock median.

**Difference (a), stepper — MEASURED, NOT NORMALISED AWAY.** Ours: classical RK4, 4 explicit
stages, then the exact integrating factor applied to the *result* (a Lie–Trotter split,
globally first order in the splitting). Reference: Carpenter–Kennedy low-storage RK4, **5**
explicit stages, Crank–Nicolson on the linear term *inside* the stage loop (IMEX). **The
reference does 25% MORE explicit stages and is still 4.3x faster.** Per stage the gap is
5.43x. **Difference (a) makes our position worse, not better.**

**Difference (b), spectrum — MEASURED, NOT NORMALISED AWAY.** Ours: full complex `fft2`,
state (24,24), 20 transforms/step. Reference: half-spectrum `rfft2`/`irfft2`, state (24,13),
25 transforms/step. Measured at N=24, an `rfft2` costs **0.763** of a complex `fft2` through
FFTW3 and **0.943** through numpy. So the reference's 25 half-transforms are **19.09
full-transform equivalents against our 20** — a transform-work ratio of 1.048, i.e.
essentially the same work. **Work-normalised ratio: 4.143. Still NO.**

**THE CANDIDATE FINDING, WRITTEN DOWN NOW SO NOBODY LATER PRESENTS IT AS A DISCOVERY:**
moving to `rfft2` is **published practice that the reference already makes** (row 27); it is
not ours to discover. And at N=24 it is **not worth 2x**. The factor-of-two "half the
spectrum, half the work" argument is an *asymptotic FLOP* argument, and this regime is
overhead-bound: measured, the saving is 0.76x (FFTW3) or 0.94x (numpy), because a call costs
what a call costs. Anyone proposing `rfft2` as a speedup at N=24 must argue against 0.94, not
against 0.50.

**Which number answers the gate:** the raw CPU-clock ratio, **4.34**. The work-normalised
4.14 is reported because I was told to report both, and it does not change the answer.

### §2.4 The single change that recovers most of it (PRICED, NOT LANDED)

Prototypes built **beside** the solver in `r_prof_candidates_v1.py`, never inside it. Each is
checked against `Kolmogorov2D._rk4_step` on the same input at 1 step and after 200 steps in
relative sup norm, plus a bitwise flag. Timed in interleaved rounds under both clocks.

| candidate | what it is | speedup (CPU, within-round median) | equivalence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 batched numpy** | the 4 inverse transforms of a stage issued as ONE `np.fft.ifft2(stack, axes=(1,2))` on a (4,N,N) array; 5 transform *calls* per stage become 2; **no new dependency** | **1.62x** | **BITWISE IDENTICAL at 1 step and at 200 steps** |
| **V2 planned FFTW3** | the same 5 transforms through pre-planned `pyfftw.FFTW` objects on aligned buffers | **3.41x** | agrees to ~6.4e-17 (≈1 ulp) |
| **V3 both** | batched AND planned: 2 planned calls per stage | **3.87x** | ~1 ulp |

**THE SINGLE CHANGE: replace the 20 per-call `numpy.fft` invocations with pre-planned FFTW3
transforms. 3.41x.** That alone would put us at ~1.27x the reference, inside 3x.

The honest sub-finding: **V1 is free of every risk.** It uses `numpy.fft`'s own `axes=`
argument, adds no dependency, and is bitwise identical at 200 steps — so it invalidates no
banked comparison. V2/V3 differ at one ulp, which across a 1934-step Jacobian action inside a
Newton–GMRES solve is **not** bitwise reproducible and **needs its own unit with its own
equivalence check over every banked orbit**. That is exactly the `R4` problem and I did not
walk into it.

### §2.5 Link to the record's cost model, and an INDEPENDENT corroboration

- T_orbit = 19.334, dt = 0.01 → **1934 steps per Jacobian action**.
- 1072.5 µs x 1934 = **2.074 s per Jacobian action** (derived here).
- `experiments/programme_r4/u3_cost_probe.json`, banked by a **different unit for a different
  purpose**, measured **2.367 s per Jacobian action**. **Agreement to 12.4%.** Neither number
  was derived from the other. The profile is measuring the same object the record's cost
  figures were built from.
- Bonus coherence check on the record: that probe's own seconds-per-epoch (54.700) divided by
  its own seconds-per-action (2.367) gives **23.107**, against its own median Krylov
  dimension **22 + 1 = 23** — which is what an epoch of Newton–GMRES *is* (one action per
  Krylov vector plus the residual evaluation). **The cost model in the record is internally
  coherent.** The gate's `NO` is about the *constant*, not about the model's structure.

## §3 WHAT I COULD **NOT** ESTABLISH

Stated plainly, because a profile that hides its limits is a marketing document.

1. **The absolute per-step cost on a quiet machine.** Never measured. The 1072.5 µs is an
   upper bound taken at loadavg ~4–6 with three sibling units running; the true quiet-machine
   figure is smaller by an unknown factor plausibly in [1.5, 3]. **No absolute in this leg
   should be quoted as a machine constant.** Only the ratios and fractions are claims.
2. **Any N other than 24.** The gate named N=24 and I answered at N=24. The sweep establishes
   the *regime* out to N=512, but the gate ratio was not re-run at other N. The `NO` is a
   statement about N=24; at N≥128 the overhead share collapses and the answer could easily
   flip to `YES`.
3. **Whether landing V2 or V3 preserves campaign results.** Not established, not attempted.
   The 1-ulp difference is fine for one step and says nothing about a 1934-step integration
   inside a Newton solve where the Krylov subspace is built from finite differences. **That
   requires its own unit.** V1's bitwise identity at 200 steps is established; its identity
   across a *full campaign* is not.
4. **The Newton/GMRES layer above `integrate`.** Not profiled. The gate named `_rk4_step`,
   and I profiled `_rk4_step` and `integrate`. Everything above — orthogonalisation, phase
   conditions, the outer Newton — is un-measured. It is plausibly small (§2.5's coherence
   check is consistent with the epoch being ~23 actions and nothing else), but **plausible is
   not measured**, and I am not licensing a claim that the Newton layer is free.
5. **That the reference is an optimum.** It is not. JAX-CFD is itself ~4.0x above the FFTW3
   20-transform floor. The gate asked "within 3x of the reference", and that is what was
   answered. "Within 3x of the achievable floor" is a different and much harsher question,
   against which we are 16.9x.
6. **Anything about the reference's accuracy or physics.** I checked only that it constructs,
   steps, advances, and stays finite. I did **not** verify it solves the same equations to the
   same accuracy. A cost comparison is not a correctness comparison, and I do not claim one.
7. **Memory, allocation and GC.** Not instrumented. Part of the 7.6% Python-frame residual and
   part of the per-call floor is certainly allocation; the split was not resolved.

## §4 THE CEILING ON MY OWN RESULT

- **One machine.** One CPU, one memory hierarchy, loaded by three sibling units. Nothing here
  transfers to other hardware without re-measurement; the whole finding is that we are
  paying *dispatch*, and dispatch is a property of this software stack on this box.
- **One input state.** A single seeded `w_hat`. The step cost of a pseudospectral RK4 is
  state-independent to first order, so I do not expect this to matter — but it was not varied
  and so it is not shown.
- **One numpy, one JAX, one FFTW.** The `numpy.fft`/pocketfft per-call overhead is a fact
  about this numpy build. A different numpy could move the 8.85x.
- **N = 24 only** for the gate ratio (see §3.2).
- **The `NO` is a statement about an implementation, not about a bound.** It says our stepper
  is ~4.3x off a published implementation *on this box today*. It is not a theorem, it does
  not compose with anything, and it does not survive being restated as a general claim about
  pseudospectral solvers.
- **The instrument's own failure mode is documented (§1.4) and was hit once.** I trust the
  paired/duel numbers because the ratio is formed within a round and confirmed on two clocks;
  I do not trust the sequential `--part ref` numbers and I did not use them.

## §5 WHAT THIS RESULT DOES NOT LICENSE

- **It moves no link.** A speedup is not a step toward Clay. Clay remains ~0.05%. Nothing in
  this leg touched the blow-up question, the theorem chain, or any banked orbit.
- **Scale is not evidence.** A 3.4x cheaper solver buys 3.4x more of the same search. It does
  not make the search more likely to find anything, and it must not be reported as though it
  did.
- **It does not authorise a solver rewrite.** Explicitly forbidden in this unit, and correctly
  so: a stepper change invalidates every banked comparison in the programme. The change is
  **priced, not landed.** `solver/kolmogorov2d_nkbasin.py` is byte-identical.
- **It does not invalidate the record's cost figures as a model.** §2.5 shows the epoch model
  is internally coherent and that the profiled object is the one those figures were built
  from. What it says is that the *constant* is ~4x above a published implementation and ~17x
  above the algorithmic floor — i.e. the compute wall is roughly where the record says it is
  **for the code as written**, and could be moved by a bounded, well-understood, single change
  that someone else must do properly.
- **It does not make `rfft2` a discovery.** See §2.3. Published practice, already in the
  reference, and worth 0.94x here, not 2x.
