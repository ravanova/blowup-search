## §4b. TWO EDITS MADE WHILE THE RUN WAS IN FLIGHT — DISCLOSED, NOT SILENT

The driver was committed **before** the first attempt (`fbada1b`) as this unit's
pre-registration. Two things happened afterwards and both are recorded here rather than
smoothed into the diff:

1. **`e_fe_report.py` is a NEW file, not an edit to the driver.** The brief requires
   narrative blocks (`readings`, `ceilings`, `defects_found`, `clay_movement`,
   `sources_3k`, `cost_and_shortfall`) that the driver left as empty placeholders. Putting
   that prose into the pre-registered driver after results existed would have destroyed the
   audit trail even though it touches no number. So the assembler is a separate file: it
   `import`s `gate()` from the driver and uses the returned dict **as returned** — it does
   not re-compute, re-weight or re-threshold any result.
2. **One one-line guard was patched INTO the driver mid-run.**
   `clopper_pearson(k, n)` divided by `n` in its `closed_form_check_upper_at_k0` field, so
   it raised `ZeroDivisionError` for an **empty** group (`n = 0`). That can only happen if
   the run is interrupted with some (row, arm) cell unfilled — i.e. exactly the §3d
   under-resourced case the artefact is supposed to be able to *report*. Carrying a gate
   that crashes in the one situation it must describe is worse than the edit. The change is
   `None if k else ...` → `None if (k or not n) else ...`; it **cannot** alter any value at
   `n = 10` or `n = 160`, and the interval endpoints themselves are untouched. The running
   processes had already loaded their code, so the patch could not affect the run either.

Cross-checked after patching: `clopper_pearson(0, 10)` returns
`two_sided_95_upper = 0.3084971078187607` and `one_sided_95_upper = 0.2588655508930522`,
agreeing with the closed forms `1 − 0.025^(1/10) = 0.30849710781876083` and
`1 − 0.05^(1/10) = 0.2588655508930523` to float64.

### §5.5 The `26%` figure, computed here rather than quoted

`k = 0`, `n = 10`. The Clopper–Pearson upper limit is `1 − α^(1/n)`.

- one-sided (all 5% in the upper tail): `1 − 0.05^(1/10)` = **0.2588655508930523** ≈ **26%**
- equal-tailed two-sided: `1 − 0.025^(1/10)` = **0.30849710781876083** ≈ **31%**

`scipy.stats.beta.ppf` agrees with both closed forms to float64. **My arithmetic AGREES with
§B's 26% under the one-sided convention** and disagrees under the two-sided one. §B asks for
"its exact binomial confidence interval" — an interval is conventionally two-sided — and then
states a one-sided bound. **The defect is the unstated convention, not an arithmetic error**;
both are computed, both are named, and no verdict here rests on the choice. Recorded as **D1**.
