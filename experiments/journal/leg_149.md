and wrong about the set.

`PROGRESS.md`'s ⚠ NEEDS YOU carries **3** items. `DIRECTION.md`'s "Open direction questions for

same three: `PROGRESS`'s item 1 (stage B exhausted, leg 126) lives only in `DIRECTION`'s

`PROGRESS.md`"* — and it is false in both directions at once.

The sharpest single defect is a **stale entry that survived a ruling**, which is the exact
failure mode the gate's second clause was written for. `PROGRESS` item 2 tells the user that
leg 63's γ=2 dissipative gCLM candidate "stays parked pending your ruling." The ruling
**landed**: `DIRECTION` question 2 is headed **"RESOLVED 2026-08-06 by user directive"** and
quotes the user — *"Leg 63, let's pursue this…"* — after which the DM issued its legality
ruling and drafted leg 125 (ROUTE-M2P) into a slot. The user is being asked, on the one surface
they read first, for a decision they already made.

The second defect runs the other way and is the reason this leg is worth its cost. `DIRECTION`
still lists **two** parked escalation branches binding every leg in the repository —
`leg/m2-v1` (leg 63) and `leg/pq-v1` (leg 60). Checked against git rather than against the
other ledger, **leg 60 landed on `main`**: its escalation #4 was resolved by the user and its
correction applied on 2026-08-06. `PROGRESS` is *correct* to carry no leg-60 entry. So the
gate's first clause fails — an item declared parked in `DIRECTION` has no `PROGRESS` entry —
but the fix is to retire two sentences in `DIRECTION`, not to add anything to `PROGRESS`. A
cross-ledger audit that only compared the two ledgers to each other would have reported this
backwards.

And beneath the escalation ledger, the dashboard around it has drifted hard: of the **8** legs
`PROGRESS` declares live this cycle, **7 have already landed on `main`**.

## TECHNICAL

### Why one side is pinned and the other cannot be

Every prior member of this freshness family (JR/72, IX/68, JR2/102, IX2/108, JR3/137, IX3/138)
audited a git-tracked, append-only ledger and pinned it to a SHA. `PROGRESS.md` is
**git-ignored** and rewritten in place: it exists in no commit, at any SHA, so it cannot be
pinned. It also does not exist in this leg's worktree at all — the runner resolves the main
worktree via `git rev-parse --git-common-dir` and reads
`/home/andy/projects/Unsolved/PROGRESS.md`, recording the path and the file's own
self-declared timestamp (`2026-08-06, mid-cycle …`) in the JSON as the closest thing it has to
a version.

That asymmetry is declared in the novelty log **before** the runner was written, not
discovered afterwards: the `DIRECTION` half of this audit reproduces byte-for-byte forever, the
`PROGRESS` half is a statement about the live file at read time. Sizes at read: `DIRECTION.md`
**3754** lines, `PROGRESS.md` **65** lines.

### The matching predicate, and the control that had to be able to fail (lesson 90)

Nearly every item in both ledgers names leg 63, so "shares a leg number" is weak evidence on
its own. Two predicates were therefore evaluated over all **21** cross-ledger item pairs:

- **STRICT** — the two items' leg-number anchor sets intersect.
- **LOOSE** — their content-token sets (stopworded) reach a Jaccard floor of **0.15**.

`MATCHED` requires both. Where they disagree the pair is reported `BORDERLINE`, never silently
counted. The floor is not trivially satisfiable: the **median** pairwise Jaccard is **0.0578**,
the max **0.3969**, and the loose predicate **did** disagree with the strict one: of the **15**
relations recorded (a pair is recorded if either predicate fires), **12 are strict-only** —
they share a leg number and essentially no content — **3 are MATCHED**, and **0 are
loose-only**. A predicate that had matched everything would have been a tautology of the regex.

**3 MATCHED pairs**, one per `PROGRESS` item:

| pair | Jaccard | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `P-1 ~ S-BX-EXHAUSTED` | **0.3969** | stage B exhausted (leg 126) — matched to *Status*, line 656 |
| `P-2 ~ D-OQ2` | **0.2244** | leg 63/125 M2 — matched to open question 2 |
| `P-3 ~ D-OQ3` | **0.3261** | the exit criterion / publication scoping |

`D-OQ1` — `NG` entering the committed sequence, escalation #1 — has **0** matched
counterparts. Its only relations are three strict-only borderlines at Jaccard 0.096, 0.040 and
0.000.

### Finding 1 — one MATCHED pair carries contradicting status markers (a STALE entry)

Presence is not accuracy (lesson 75: two defects in the same problem are not the same defect),
so each matched pair's status markers were extracted and compared:

- `P-2` markers: `PENDING_RULING`, `PARKED`
- `D-OQ2` markers: `RESOLVED`, `PARKED`

**1 of 3 matched pairs is in direct conflict** — `P-2~D-OQ2`, on 5 agreeing numeric literals
including `1908.09385` (Chen's blow-up proof) and `63`, so there is no doubt the two items are
about the same thing. `DIRECTION` records the user's directive and the DM's legality ruling
(stage V's ban stands unmodified; a from-scratch certification of the γ=2 profile is outside
its scope) and reports leg 125 drafted and assigned. `PROGRESS` still says "pending your
ruling."

What *is* still open is narrower and different, and the entry conflates the two: the branch
`leg/m2-v1` is genuinely still parked and unmerged (leg 63 has **not** landed on `main` — the
runner confirms this from git, and it is the control that proves the staleness test can come
out the other way), and promoting the route into the committed sequence remains escalation #1.
But the *ruling* the entry asks for has already been given.

**Blast radius, counted separately rather than summed:** **1** further item (`P-1`) propagates
the same stale wording, describing leg 125 as "still itself parked pending your ruling". It is
recorded as `PROPAGATED`, not as a second independent defect.

### Finding 2 — 2 stale parked-branch assertions, and the remedy is on the DIRECTION side

The branch → owning-leg mapping is **computed from `DIRECTION.md`'s own authoritative
sentence**, not hardcoded — the repository's standing lesson that a quantity you cite must be

> leg 60/PQ (`leg/pq-v1`)."

Checked against the landed set at the pin (**77** distinct legs with `N != 0` have landed;
max **142**):

| branch | owning leg | landed on `main`? | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `leg/m2-v1` | 63 | **no** | **CURRENT** |
| `leg/pq-v1` | 60 | **yes** | **STALE** |

`leg/pq-v1` is asserted parked in **2** places — `DIRECTION.md:93` and `:868` — and leg 60's
own commit on `main` reads *"Leg 60: LEG (landed by orchestrator, user-approved correction) —
Route-PORT v1/v2 reproduction ledger lands; 3 non-ban-bearing discrepancies corrected …

### Finding 3 — the file's own bidirectional invariant is false in both directions

`DIRECTION.md:3679`, immediately under the "Open direction questions for the user" heading:

> "These also appear under `⚠ NEEDS YOU` in `PROGRESS.md`."

- **Forward:** `D-OQ1` (`NG` entering the committed sequence is escalation #1) has **0** matched
  `PROGRESS` counterparts. It carries only the marker `OVERTAKEN` — it was never marked
  `RESOLVED`, although `NG`'s gate answered **YES** at leg 58, the DM ruled it mergeable, and
  leg 58 has landed, which makes its stated reversal option (swap slots LEG-A/LEG-C and mark

- **Backward:** `P-1` (stage B exhausted) matched to *Status*, not to any numbered open

So both lists hold 3 items and neither is a subset of the other. A reviewer checking "3 = 3, no
drift" would pass this. That the counts agree while the sets do not is the shape of the finding
and was pre-registered in §3 of the novelty log as the reason the window was worth auditing.

**On accuracy where the ledgers do agree:** `P-1 ~ S-BX-EXHAUSTED` agrees on **8** numeric
literals — `1686`, `144`, `1032`, `510`, `6.0424`, `126`, `125`, `63`. Leg 126's headline

## Escalate, or not?

The no-branch says: *"escalate only if the gap concerns a parked escalation, not general
hygiene."* Applying it literally, finding by finding:

- **Finding 1** concerns a parked escalation (leg 63/M2, branch `leg/m2-v1`) — but the defect
  is that `PROGRESS` under-reports a ruling that has **already** been given, and the remedy is a
  wording correction the orchestrator can apply. Nothing is being decided, reversed, or asked of
  the user, so this is **not** one of `ORCHESTRATION.md` §8's four escalations. It is flagged as
  the highest-priority correction because it is the one defect that could cause the user to
  re-answer a settled question.
- **Finding 2** concerns a parked escalation (leg 60/PQ) and the entry is stale in the ledger's
  *forbidding* direction. It is likewise a retirement, not a decision: leg 60's escalation was
  resolved by the user and landed. **Not escalated**; reported for the DM, who owns
  `DIRECTION.md`.
- **Findings 3 and 4** are general hygiene by construction. Not escalated.

**Net: 0 escalations raised.** Nothing here moves a link of the L1→L4 chain, lifts a ban,
touches `plan_of_record.py`, or bears on any claim about Walls 1 and 2. Clay stays ~0.05%.

## Defects found in this leg's own instrument, and fixed before it landed

Kept in the record per the standing rule that the negative construction stays in the artifact:

1. The route-tag regex `(\d{2,3})\s*\([A-Z0-9]{2,5}\)` silently dropped every entry whose tag
   is followed by prose — `130 (HPR, YES)` — which had made legs 130 and 132 look absent from
   `PROGRESS` entirely. Fixed to accept a comma; the true absence count is 1 (leg 142), not 3.
2. A fixed 400-character window for the "Live this cycle" bullet bled into the next sentence and
   miscounted reserve leg 129 as live. Bounded to the list.
3. The "nine other exploration legs" claim was searched in un-normalised text and missed,
   because Markdown hard-wraps between "exploration" and "legs". Fixed by flattening whitespace.
4. The first parked-branch check used an ad-hoc `"pq" in branch` test — a predicate that
   encoded the answer it was looking for. Replaced by a branch→leg mapping parsed from
   `DIRECTION.md`'s own sentence and checked against the landed set, which is what lets it clear
   `leg/m2-v1` and flag `leg/pq-v1` by the same rule.
