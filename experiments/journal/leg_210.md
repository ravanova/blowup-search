**The one-sentence reason the magnitudes differ.** Leg 185 refined `a = 0.30` on four grids and

away from as the grid refines. So the two schemes are not disagreeing about a converged
quantity; there is no converged quantity there to disagree about.

## What was built, and why it is independent (lesson 90)

The standing instruction was that the re-derivation be genuinely independent, *not* a re-run of
leg 185's script. Four tiers were pre-committed in the novelty pass **before any number was
computed**, ranked by evidential weight with bare replay **last**:

* **V0 — operators validated against closed forms first.** A reproduction is worthless if it is
  two copies of one bug. Every operator was checked against analysis before it verified
  anything: Hilbert transform vs Chen eq (2.2)'s exact pair `HΩ = U_x` (**1.283e-08** relative),
  cumulative integral vs exact `U = X/(b²+X²)` (**2.236e-09**), first derivative vs closed-form
  `Ω_X` (**5.319e-10**), second derivative vs closed-form `Ω_XX` (**3.468e-10**).
* **V2 — an independently discretized re-derivation.** Residual and Jacobian derived here from
  the equation, not transcribed: **6th-order** difference matrices against leg 125's 4th; the
  second derivative built by the **chain rule** `∂ₓₓ = X_ρ⁻²∂_ρρ − X_ρρX_ρ⁻³∂_ρ` against leg
  125's `D∘D`; a **degree-5 Lagrange panel** quadrature against the 4-point Adams–Moulton rule;
  and the whole system restricted to the **odd subspace**, which removes the exact rank
  deficiency leg 185 absorbed with least squares and leaves a nonsingular system solved by
  **LU** — different linear algebra, not just a different matrix.
* **V3 — an algebraically distinct route.** The dilation covariance used as a *derivation*.
* **V1 — replay of leg 185's own path, ranked last** and labelled in the artifact as
  `"WEAKEST -- determinism/transcription only, NOT a verification"`.

**One operator is shared, and that is declared rather than hidden.** `solver/line_hilbert.py`'s
dense whole-line Hilbert matrix is reused; independently re-deriving a singular-integral
quadrature is a leg of its own. It is therefore *validated* in V0 against two exact analytic
pairs rather than trusted, and the weaker of the two checks is reported: on the `a=0` CLM pair
the error is **2.366e-03**, five orders worse than on Chen's pair, because `Ω₀ = −4X/(1+4X²)`
decays like `1/X` and the truncated grid cannot see its tail. Chen's profile decays like `X⁻³`
and is the regime every solve here uses. A validation you only quote when it passes is not a
validation.

## Clause 1 — `a = 0.30`: confirmed, and it is a real confirmation

Independent ladder, leg 185's own gauge, `a` fixed, dilation gauge imposed, `ν` unknown:

| n | this leg (6th-order, chain-rule, odd-subspace, LU) | leg 185 (4th-order, `D∘D`, full system, lstsq) |
|---|---|---|
| 201 | 0.01799743 | 0.01802863 |
| 401 | 0.01799385 | 0.01799583 |
| 801 | 0.01799363 | 0.01799376 |
| 1201 | 0.01799362 | **0.01799364** |

Both ladders are monotone and converging. Richardson through my three finest grids gives
**0.0179936185** at an observed order **4.12**, against leg 185's banked **0.01799364** —
**1.198e-06** relative, ~5.9 significant digits. Truncation spread over my own domain
variations is small, and both starts (`ν₀ = 0.3` and `0.01`) agree to **0 relative spread**.

Two corroborations that cost nothing and were worth having:

* **The gauge question, settled before it could be mistaken for a discrepancy.** Leg 185's gauge
  is *discrete* (`D[i0] @ chen_profile`), sitting **4.317e-07** relative from the closed form
  `−2/b³ = −8.70929686322908`. Since `ν ∝ g⁻²`, that must move `ν` by twice as much. Predicted
  offset **8.634e-07**, observed **8.634e-07**, **ratio 1.0000**. So the covariance the whole
  reparametrization rests on is verified as a *number*, and the comparison against leg 185 was
  run under **leg 185's own gauge**, so it tests its computation and not its normalization.
* **V3, seeded: the covariance as a derivation.** Solving at gauges deliberately wrong by
  `κ ∈ {0.5, 0.8, 1.25, 2.0}` and rescaling by `κ²` recovers the on-gauge `ν` to worst
  **3.184e-05** across a **4× gauge range** — i.e. leg 185's number is reachable from
  normalizations it never used.

**The sign claim is strengthened, not merely repeated.** Because `Ω(X) → Ω(X/μ)` sends
`ν → μ²ν` and `μ² > 0`, **no dilation can change the sign of `ν`**. So "positive below `a*`,
negative at and above" is a *gauge-independent* property of the branch rather than an artifact
of Chen's normalization. Leg 185 did not establish this; it follows from the covariance leg 185
itself wrote down, and it is the most durable thing in this whole family of legs.

## Clause 2 — `a = 1/2`: the discrepancy, measured

Leg 185 banked **−0.00817525** / **−0.00895316** at `n = 401`. My independent scheme at the same
grid gives **−0.00082927** from *both* starts at relative residual **4.4e-15** — a **10.33×**
magnitude gap on a number the gate names explicitly.

**V5 — the study leg 185 did not run.** Leg 185 refined only `a = 0.30`. Running the grid ladder
at `a = 1/2` **in both schemes** is the decisive instrument, and it exonerates neither:

| n, start | leg 185's own scheme | amplitude | this leg's scheme | amplitude |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 201, ν₀=0.3 | −0.0065478 | 1.2464 | −0.0086810 *(not converged, 1.8e-02)* | 0.9264 |
| 201, ν₀=0.01 | −0.0065478 | 1.2464 | −0.0015446 *(not converged, 7.0e-03)* | 0.4860 |
| 401, ν₀=0.3 | **−0.0081752** | 1.2094 | −0.0008293 | 0.2260 |
| 401, ν₀=0.01 | **−0.0089532** | 1.1664 | −0.0008293 | 0.2260 |
| 801, ν₀=0.3 | **−0.0044345** | 1.2766 | −0.0002025 | 0.1092 |
| 801, ν₀=0.01 | −0.0089529 | 1.1664 | −0.0002419 | 0.1180 |

Read honestly, **both** schemes fail here, in different ways, and I report mine against myself
as readily as against leg 185:

* **Leg 185's scheme is not grid-converged.** Its converged values span **62.2%** relative
  across grids — non-monotone, `−0.00655 → −0.00818 → −0.00443` on one start. Its banked
  `−0.00817525` is the `n=401` point of a sequence that does not settle.
* **My scheme is not grid-converged either, and it is drifting toward the trivial null.** Span
  **119.2%**, and — the part that matters — its **amplitude collapses** from 0.9264 to 0.1092
  (min/max **0.483**), whereas leg 185's stays near Chen's scale throughout. Leg 185's own D1
  named exactly this failure mode: Newton converging to `Ω ≡ 0`, where `ν` is unidentifiable. So
  **my** `−0.00082927` is not the better number; it is a differently bad one.

**The `a = 0.30` control is what makes this a measurement rather than an impression.** Run
identically, at the same three grids, in both schemes:

| `a` | this leg, relative span | leg 185, relative span | this leg, min/max amplitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.30 (control)** | **2.11e-04** | **1.94e-03** | **0.999** |
| 0.45 | 3.911 | 0.666 | 0.006 |
| 0.50 (Chen's) | 1.192 | 0.622 | 0.483 |

The positive side refines cleanly in **both** schemes — spans ~1e-4 to ~2e-3, amplitude flat —
while the negative side is 320× to 5600× worse in the very same code. `a = 0.45`, which leg 185
also banked (`−0.00606636` / `−0.00792936`), is the worst point of all: my scheme's amplitude
there collapses by a factor of **160**, i.e. it is essentially finding the trivial null. So the
instrument works where the claim was refined and breaks where it was not, which is exactly the
pattern that distinguishes a real discrepancy from a broken re-implementation.
* **V3 at `a = 1/2` says the same thing structurally.** Even *seeded with its own dilation
  image*, the covariance recovery there is off by **5.977e-01**, against **3.184e-05** at
  `a = 0.30`. A genuine isolated root returns to its own dilation image; this one does not. That
  is a clean, gauge-free diagnostic separating the region that verified from the region that did
  not.

**Conclusion for this clause, stated at the right altitude.** The two schemes are landing on
different roots of a square system that has several on this side of `a*`, and neither has
refined to a limit. Leg 185 was right that **"only the sign is banked"** — its own stated caveat
is the correct reading. What this leg adds is that leg 185's quantification of that caveat, *"the
two starts differ by up to 31% in magnitude"*, **understates the instability by roughly an order
of magnitude**: across grids its own scheme spans 62%, and across schemes the gap is 10.33×.
The two negative numbers in leg 185's report and in `DIRECTION.md` §210's thesis should not be
quoted as measurements of anything.

## Clause 3 — `a* = 0.3865`: the cross-check does not survive

Leg 185's journal states its `ν(a)` zero crossing *"lands on leg 125's own independently measured
`a* = 0.3864963972206034`"* and calls that *"a cross-check, not a restatement"*. Independently
discretized, my sign-change bracket between converged scan points is **[0.36, 0.37]**, and leg
125's `a*` is **outside** it.

**But the more important observation is internal to leg 185.** Its own `a`-sweep row at
`a = 0.3865` reports **+0.00000035** from one start and **−0.00425080** from the other — its two
starts **straddle zero**. Its own data therefore never pinned the crossing to anything like the
8 digits the agreement implies; the reported coincidence is *one of two disagreeing starts*
matching leg 125. And in my scheme, `a = 0.3865` is one of the points that **fails to converge
outright** (relative residual **6.0e-03**), which is why this leg reports a bracket rather than a
number.

**A methodological note I am recording against myself (lesson 67).** My first pass located `a*`
by **bisection** and printed **0.3659213753** — twelve digits — while silently stepping through
`a`-values where the system does not converge. A bisection returns a precise-looking number
whatever the function does. It was replaced by a **scan that reports each point's convergence**
(12 of 16 converged; the 4 failures are listed in the artifact), so the crossing is quoted as a
bracket between *converged* points and cannot launder a non-convergence into a digit string.

## Clause 4 — the replay tier, and what it is worth

Re-executing leg 185's own `_solve_nu_unknown` reproduces its banked ladder **exactly, to all
eight digits, on all four grids** (0.01802863, 0.01799583, 0.01799376, 0.01799364), and its
`a = 1/2` pair exactly. Leg 185's script and JSON are md5-pinned in the artifact.

So leg 185's report is a **faithful transcription of what its code computed**. There is no
bookkeeping error, no nondeterminism, and no slip between run and report. That is worth knowing
and it is *all* this tier can establish — which is precisely why it was ranked last: it cannot
detect an error shared by the script and its output, and the a=1/2 problem is exactly such an
error.

## What this changes downstream

* **Leg 174's (VBS) "missing rung" catalog.** Object B's `a = 0.30` value is now
  independently established at ~6 digits and can be carried as a measured quantity. Its
  `a = 1/2` values **cannot**, and should be recorded as *sign only*.
* **Leg 187's inviscid construction context.** The qualitative statement it relies on — Object B
  is diffusive below `a*` and anti-diffusive at Chen's `a` — survives fully, and is now known to
  be **gauge-independent**. The location of `a*` is not established to better than a bracket.
* **The general lesson for this repository's postconstruction discipline.** Leg 185 refined the
  number it was proud of and banked the numbers it was cautious about without refinement. The
  caution was correctly *stated* but not *measured*, and an unrefined number acquires
  credibility just by being printed with 8 digits next to a refined one. **A grid ladder is owed
  to every banked number in a sweep, not just the headline.**

## Honest ceiling, pre-committed and unchanged

not an existence proof, not movement on `L1→L4`, and not Clay. Clay stays **~0.05%**. Stage P0
