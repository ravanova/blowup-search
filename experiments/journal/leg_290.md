# Leg 290 — ROUTE-D1XN: does D1's N-dependent coefficient have its own closed-form law?

**Gate: NO.** No single named closed form fits `C(N)` (the coefficient in `D1(κ,N) = C(N)·
κ^{-1/2}`) to the precision this leg family calls "exact" (`1e-10`–`1e-16`, matching leg 281's
`fit_max_relative_residual=1.11e-16` and leg 289's own held-out results of `0.0` and `≤1.53e-16`).
The **best** candidate — `p=1` (matching leg 176's own stated Laguerre-`C/n` truncation
mechanism) — has max relative residual `2.24e-06`, roughly **`2.0e10`×** that floor. Bank the
negative: leg 289's `N`-restriction is real, not an artifact of undersampling.

## 1. The dataset is bigger than the brief anticipated

The brief named leg 249's and leg 281's banked sweeps as the source. Leg 249 carries **no**
`D1` data (confirmed, again, directly — its sweep is over `σ_min`/`A1`, the border weight, not
the dual norm). Leg 281 carries `D1` at exactly **one** `N` (`512`). Between them that is **one**
usable `N` value, not enough to constrain any curve.

Widening the read (read-only, zero new compute, same standard leg 289 used reading leg 288's
probe) to already-banked JSON that leg 281's own file cross-references turned up a genuine
**4-point `N`-ladder** already sitting in the repo: leg 176's `C8_border_row_dual_norm.ladder`
and leg 277's `Q4_border_row_dual_norm.ladder` both carry the same `X*`-dual-norm coefficient at
`N ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512}` (`p2_route_h2c_v1_construction.json`, `p2_route_xun_v1_convert.json`).

**Cross-checked before fitting anything:**
- leg 176's `N=512` value (`0.8873620087421942`) is **bit-identical** to leg 281's own `"quoted"`
  fitted `D1` law constant.
- leg 176's `N=256` value (`0.887345112546454`) agrees with leg 288's independently-fit `N=256`
  coefficient (`0.8873451208021278`) to `8.3e-9` relative — three-plus orders of magnitude below
  every effect tested below.
- leg 176's and leg 277's own ladders agree with each other at `N ∈ {64,128,256}` (their `x_dual`
  / `"repo"` columns are the same construction, cross-published by two different legs).

So this is one consistently measured quantity across 4 independent legs (176, 277, 281, 288),
not four coincidentally-close numbers. `C(N)`:

| N | C(N) |
|---|---|
| 64 | 0.8872380425355713 |
| 128 | 0.8873117900938475 |
| 256 | 0.887345112546454 |
| 512 | 0.8873620087421942 |

Monotone increasing, consistent with convergence to a finite `C_∞` as `N → ∞`.

## 2. F1 — free-exponent power law: falsified by internal inconsistency alone

`C(N) = C_∞ − A·N^{−p}` implies the ratio of consecutive differences across doublings of `N`
equals `2^{−p}` exactly, regardless of which overlapping triple of the ladder is used to
estimate it. Estimating `p` from `(64,128,256)` gives `p = 1.146`; from `(128,256,512)` gives
`p = 0.980` — a **15.6%** disagreement between two estimates that a genuine single power law
would force to agree exactly. This alone falsifies F1 as a single closed form, independently of
any precision test. (The 3-point exact fits also fail to predict the held-out 4th point to the
`1e-6` bar: `2.07e-06` from the low triple predicting `N=512`, `9.05e-06` from the high triple
predicting `N=64`.)

## 3. F2/F3 — fixed-exponent global least-squares fits

Global 4-point fits of `C(N) = C_∞ − A/N^p` for `p ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2}` (linear regression in
`N^{-p}`, exact 2×2 normal equations):

| p | max relative residual | worst N |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | `1.18e-05` | 128 |
| **1.0** | **`2.24e-06`** | **128** |
| 1.5 | `6.62e-06` | 128 |
| 2.0 | `1.37e-05` | 128 |

`p=1` — the value matching leg 176's own stated mechanism ("the exact solution's Laguerre
coefficients decay only like `C/n`") — is the best of the four tried, and by a clear margin
(5.3× tighter than `p=0.5`, 3.0× tighter than `p=1.5`, 6.1× tighter than `p=2`). It is still
**`2.24e-06`** at its worst point, `N=128` — roughly **`2.0×10^10`×** leg 281's own
`1.11e-16` precision floor, and about **120×** *larger* than the very `1.90e-05` `N=256`-vs-
`N=512` gap (leg 289's P2b) this leg set out to explain in closed form. Per-`N` residuals for the
winning `p=1` fit (all four, not just the worst — "at full strength" per the task brief):

- N=64: `−9.33e-07`
- **N=128: `+2.237e-06` (breaks worst)**
- N=256: `−1.80e-07`
- N=512: `−1.12e-06`

No sign pattern consistent with a clean single-term power correction (the residual does not
monotonically shrink with `N`; `N=128` is the worst point, sandwiched between two better-fitting
neighbors), which is itself evidence of at least a second term in the true asymptotic expansion,
not resolvable from 4 points.

## 4. Gate

**NO.** Neither the free-exponent family (F1, falsified by the 15.6% disagreement in its own
exponent estimate before any precision test) nor any of the four fixed exponents tried (F2/F3,
best case `p=1` at `2.24e-06` max relative residual) reproduces `C(N)` to the `1e-10`–`1e-16`
precision this leg family requires to call something an "exact" law. **The `N`-restriction leg
289 flagged is real, not an artifact of undersampling** — even with a genuine 4-point ladder
(not the 2 points the brief anticipated), the best single closed form is 4–5 orders of magnitude
short of the bar, and its worst-breaking point is `N=128` specifically, at `2.237e-06` relative.

## 5. What this does — and does not — change

Leg 289's own report and JSON are **not edited**; its finding stays correct as scoped ("the
coefficient is `N`-dependent [...], flag it, do not apply it across `N`"). This leg adds:
`N`-dependence was checked against a genuine 4-point ladder, not assumed untested, and still
does not collapse to a single closed form — so no future leg may cite "D1's coefficient has a
known `N`-law" without re-deriving it from more `N` points than are currently banked (a 4-point
ladder cannot distinguish a genuine second-order correction from noise; a 5th–6th `N` value,
e.g. `N=1024`, would be the natural next brick if this line is pursued further).
