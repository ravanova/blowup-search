## What the novelty pass changed about the leg

It supplied **three** standards, one more than leg 98 had, and it pre-committed the reading of
two of them so they could not be retrofitted after the measurement.

* **(H1) the theorem's.** `Y_0`, `Z_0`, `Z_1`, `Z_2` are upper bounds on norms in the published
  radii-polynomial theorem (van den Berg–Lessard, Notices AMS 62(9); Hungria–Lessard–Mireles
  James), hence finite and nonnegative *by hypothesis*. The sharper reading, new to this leg
  because this module returns a radius where the siblings returned a verdict: the theorem
  concludes a zero exists **inside the ball of radius `r_min`**, so `closes=True` carrying a
  **non-positive** `r_min` is not conservative either — it asserts a zero inside an empty set.
* **(H2) the supremum's.** A sup is not the max over a finite, **truncated** sample grid. This
  is the founding premise of interval branch-and-bound, not an observation of this leg. Two
  consequences were written down *before* measuring: (a) `farfield_modelling_error_bound`
  already returns `argmax_X`, so *"the argmax landed on the window's last sample"* is an
  in-band self-diagnostic it computes and discards; (b) by **lesson 90**, a grid that
  "confirms the supremum sits at `X0`" cannot come out differently while the integrand is
  monotone — so inside the documented scope that confirmation is a tautology of the code, and
  its only informative regime is exactly the one the module does not guard. Both predictions
  were then borne out.
* **(H3) the norm's.** A weight vector is strictly positive and finite; the seminorm kernel's
  zeros belong on the diagonal only.

It also removed every methodological novelty claim in advance: fault injection and mutation
testing are textbook, and the `1/q if q>0 else 0` substitution is a named anti-pattern.

## Why this is not a repeat of legs 79 and 98

Both siblings audit a module that is **handed** its constants. `nk_bounds.py` also
**manufactures** them, in plain float64, by numerically evaluating a supremum and an improper
integral. So this leg audits two links rather than one — the verdict (`budget`) and the bound
producers — and the second family is outside either sibling's reach entirely.

## The instrument, and the mistake it caught in its own first draft

Family B needs a ground truth for the improper integral `_I_out` estimates by trapezoid on a
log grid. **The first draft used a finer version of the module's own quadrature and was fooled
by it**: it reported a 22.5× "under-report" at `alpha = 1.99` that was entirely its *reference*
over-shooting, because a finer copy inherits the same grid floor and the same truncation.
That is lesson 85's "re-measure your own headline" arriving one step earlier than usual, and it
is recorded here because the wrong number was nearly the leg's headline.

The replacement, `_I_out_reference`, shares no mechanism with the module: composite Simpson on
a **linear** grid over `[0, 50]` where the mass of `(1+y²)^{-α/2}` actually sits, a
log-substituted Simpson beyond it, and an **analytic** tail past the cut so nothing is
discarded. It is validated two ways, both banked in the JSON: it reproduces the sharp asymptote
the module's own docstring states (`_I_out → M_α/X`, i.e. `ref·X/M_α → 1`), and it is
self-converged to **4.90e-15** relative between `n = 8000` and `n = 128000` — nine decades
tighter than the smallest discrepancy it is used to call (`8.00e-06`, at `alpha = 1.9,
X = 1e11`).

## Findings, as magnitudes

**105 cases, 52 hypothesis-violating. 21 false accepts (19 load-bearing, 14 of them a
degenerate or empty "ball"), 12 false bounds, 4 raised.**

### 1. THE HEADLINE — the gate's question, answered YES and decided rather than argued

Substrate: `F(x) = x² − 2` on `ℝ`, zeros exactly `±√2`, `A = 1/F'(1.4)` fixed. The planted
point `x̃ = 1.0` is not a zero: `|F(1.0)| = 1`, nearest zero `0.414214` away. Honest constants
there are `Y₀ = 0.357143`, `Z₀ = 0.285714`, `Z₁ = 0`, `Z₂ = 0.714286`, against a budget
`Y₀_max = 0.178571` — and `budget` correctly **refuses** (`closes=False`). That refusal is the
control, and it is asserted before any poison is read.

One H1 violation — `Z₀ = −1`, which the theorem forbids because `Z₀` is a norm — flips it:

| | |
|---|---|
| reported verdict | `closes = True` |
| reported radius | `r_min = 0.19169540264054277` |
| reported certified ball | `[0.8083045973594573, 1.1916954026405429]` |
| zeros of `F` inside it | **none** |
| distance from the ball to `√2` | `0.2225181597325523` |
| **the same, in ball radii** | **1.1607902780527646** |

So the module reports a certified ball centred on a planted non-solution, containing no zero
of `F` at all, and missing the nearest one by **1.161 ball radii**. Five more poisons do the
same (`P02`, `P03`, `P05`, `P08`, `P09`); `P02` (`Y₀` fabricated to exactly `0`) and `P09`
(`Y₀ = 1e-300`) certify a **zero-radius** ball that is precisely the planted point, and `P03`
(sign-flipped residual) and `P08` return `closes=True` with `r_min < 0`.

`budget` performs exactly **one** domain check, `Z₂ > 0`. It performs none on `Y₀`, `Z₀`, `Z₁`.
NaN and `+inf` are rejected in all four slots, but by the accident that every comparison
against NaN is False rather than by a guard — pinned as a HOLDS gate for that reason.

### 2. A SUPREMUM REPLACED BY A MAX OVER A TRUNCATED WINDOW (latent)

`farfield_modelling_error_bound` returns `max` over 40 samples of `[X0, 10⁴·X0]` as a supremum
over `{X ≥ X0}`. Its docstring states the hypothesis that legitimises this — *"For alpha < 2
both terms decay, so the supremum sits at X0"* — and nothing enforces `alpha < 2`:

| `alpha` | module "bound" | reference (a **lower** bound on the true sup) | under-report | its own `argmax_X` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 2.7372 | 2.7372 | — (sound) | interior |
| 2.25 | 8.5956 | 859.41 | **99.98×** | pinned at window end |
| 2.5 | 76.283 | 762759.8 | **9999.02×** | pinned at window end |
| 3.0 | 6366.7 | 6.36620e11 | **9.99916e7×** | pinned at window end |

The reference is only a *lower* bound on the true supremum, which is enough: a lower bound that
exceeds a claimed upper bound already proves the claim false. In every failing case the
function's own `argmax_X` sits on the window's last sample — the self-diagnostic predicted in
the novelty pass, computed and discarded.

### 3. THE `_I_out` LOG-GRID FLOOR (latent, and the mechanism is measured)

`_I_out` grades its bulk panel `[0, X/2]` logarithmically from `eps = 1e-12·max(X,1)`, joined to
`y = 0` by a single trapezoid. The integrand's mass sits at `y = O(1)`, so once `eps` reaches
that scale the one panel `[0, eps]` replaces the resolved mass region. Predicted crossover
`X = 1e12`; measured onset **`X = 1e11`** (at `eps = 0.1` the shoulder of the mass is already
truncated), worst under-report **2.1241%** at exactly the predicted `X = 1e12`. Past `X ≈ 1e13`
the same panel *over*-shoots instead — at `X = 1e14` the module returns **29.58×** the truth at
`alpha = 1.9` (`19.16×` at `1.5`, `9.23×` at `1.2`), the conservative direction, and the reason
this cannot be found by inspecting large `X` alone.

### 4. `gamma` OUTSIDE THE HÖLDER RANGE (latent)

`gamma ∈ (0,1]` is a Hölder exponent; the near-field half carries a `1/gamma`. At
`gamma = −0.5` the near part goes **negative** (`−2.2479e-02`) and the claimed bound falls to
`9.6455e-02` against `2.7968e-01` at `gamma = 0.5`. `gamma = 0` raises (HOLDS).

### 5. `two_point_dual`'s `1/q if q > 0 else 0` (crafted-only)

Correct on the diagonal, where the suppressed increment is genuinely zero. Off it, an entry
that is zero or negative has an honest contribution of `+∞`, and substituting `0` makes that
reference index artificially cheap so the `min` over `m0` selects it: **2.1901×** below the
honest bound. A negative codomain sup weight does the same through the other term: **3.5675×**.
**Not reachable from `HolderNorm`**, the module's own weight source — minimum off-diagonal
kernel entry `3.5756e-01` and minimum weight `1.0000` across `J ∈ {125, 250, 500}` and
`(alpha, gamma)` spanning the live range. Reported latent, not as an active fabrication.

## THE HONEST CEILING: everything in families 2–5 is OUTSIDE the live operating range

Read off the in-repo callers rather than assumed. `experiments/p2_route_d_v6_bounds.py`,
`p2_route_d_v8_quadratic.py` and `p2_route_d_v10_lower.py` use `alpha ∈ [1.1, 1.8]`,
`gamma = 0.5`, `X0 ≤ 3200` (so the largest `X` any of them evaluates is **3.2e7**), and every
one of them passes `Y₀ = 0.0` as a **literal**. Inside that range the module's numerically
evaluated bounds **dominate** the reference in all 18 sampled `(alpha, gamma, X0)` configurations
(worst reference/module `0.99997`) and `_I_out` keeps a relative slack of `+7.5e-5` to
`+1.15e-4` — 3.49 decades clear of the `X = 1e11` onset. **No banked Route-D number is
affected**, and that is asserted as its own HOLDS gate so a future repair cannot break it
unnoticed.

So the finding splits cleanly:

* **family 1 (`budget`) is a real soundness gap, latent only because every in-repo caller
  happens to hard-code `Y₀ = 0.0`** — the same "latent" verdict leg 79 returned, for the same
  reason, in the sibling module. Any future caller that computes `Y₀` reaches it immediately.
* **families 2–5 are unguarded domains** — documented hypotheses the code does not check —
  each with a measured magnitude and a named distance from the live range.

## Controls (lesson 90: a control that cannot come out differently is not a control)

Every judgement instrument in this battery is shown reporting **both** answers:

* the substrate closes honestly at `x = 1.4` (`r_min = 1.4435e-02`, ball `[1.3856, 1.4144]`
  genuinely containing `√2`) and refuses honestly at `x = 1.0`, before any poison;
* the reference integrator reports `sound` on all 18 in-scope configurations and `false_bound`
  on `alpha = 2.25/2.5/3.0` — the **same** instrument, both verdicts;
* `budget` is shown rejecting NaN, `+inf`, `Z₀+Z₁ ≥ 1` (including at one ulp) and `Z₂ ≤ 0`, so
  "nothing closes any more" cannot masquerade as robustness.

## What was NOT patched, and what a repair would have to do

`solver/nk_bounds.py` is untouched. `test_nk_bounds_adversarial.py` carries **17 gates** in
three kinds — **GAP-PIN** (assert the defect as it stands, so it cannot drift while awaiting
repair; each names the inversion it expects, per leg 84's convention), **HOLDS** (the checks
that are in place and must not regress), **CONTROL**. A repair would need to: validate
`Y₀, Z₀, Z₁ ≥ 0` and finite in `budget`; refuse or flag `closes=True` with `r_min ≤ 0`; enforce
`alpha < 2` in `farfield_modelling_error_bound` or flag an `argmax_X` on the window boundary;
lower `_I_out`'s bulk floor below the mass scale, or bound the `[0, eps]` panel instead of
trapezoiding it; enforce `gamma ∈ (0,1]`; and raise on non-positive `q_cod`/`v_cod` entries off
the diagonal.
