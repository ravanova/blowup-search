## 2. The obstruction, located in the paper's own text

Four independent statements, all verbatim in `writeup/novelty/leg_175.md` §2 with page and
section. The two that decide the gate:

**(a) The certification threshold is stated by the authors to be problem-dependent, and met for
CCF only** (p.8, *Implications for Future Studies*): *"This level of accuracy proves to be
sufficient for utilizing the numerical solution for computer-assisted proofs for this specific
problem², although the required precision for such proofs is generally problem-dependent."*
Footnote 2 reads, in full: *"Manuscript in preparation."*

**(b) The paper attributes its own precision floor to the training method, not to any model**
(p.17, Methods/Multi-stage Training): *"If we only employ methods described up to this point,
we are unable to reach maximum equation residuals much below 10⁻⁸."* Multi-stage training buys
five decades and was applied to **4 of 12** solutions — CCF and IPM, stable and 1st unstable
only. **Every solution left at 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ is one the precision technique was never applied to.**
Boussinesq received none of it, and its whole column sits within a factor ~15 of the stated
single-stage floor. The paper exhibits an *unapplied technique*, not a *model wall*.

Two further obstructions that precision does not touch, recorded for completeness:
**the unstable-spectrum condition** (p.19 — *"For a computer-assisted proof to be feasible, it
is desirable that the spectrum in the right-half µ-plane consists of a finite number of
eigenvalues"* — never established; the mode count comes from a PINN eigensolve *"under the
assumption that there exist eigenvalues with non-negative real part that lie on the real axis"*
and *"restricted to Ψ that lie within the same symmetry class"*), and **the missing SI**
(footnote 1, p.4 — promised in a forthcoming version; arXiv still lists only `[v1] 17 Sep 2025`,
~11 months on, and the SI is where every residual definition, validation grid and linearized
operator a CAP would enclose actually lives).

## 3. The decisive evidence, and it is not inference

The same core author group **named the obstruction and removed it, on unchanged models, 72
days later**: `arXiv:2511.22819` (Wang, Léger, Lai, Buckmaster, 28 Nov 2025, 27 pp;
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22819>), which cites `2509.14185` as its `[1]`.

Its abstract: *"…the accuracy remained insufficient for validation. **The primary obstacle is
the presence of sharp solution gradients.**"* Its fix is a **gradient-normalized loss
function** — a re-weighting, nothing else — and its result (p.2): *"We achieve O(10⁻¹³)
residuals for the full suite of previously discovered CCF solutions and achieve accuracy
between O(10⁻¹¹) and O(10⁻¹³) for the 2-D IPM solutions."*

**An obstruction that a loss re-weighting dissolves on an unchanged model is technique-specific
by construction.** CCF's 2nd unstable went from `10^-6.664` to `O(10^-13)`; IPM's whole column
moved into `10^-11`–`10^-13` and its 4th unstable was newly confirmed.

**Boussinesq did not get the fix.** "Boussinesq" occurs exactly once in the follow-up — in the
abstract's recap of `[1]` — and never in its body.

## 4. The measured numbers

Precision stratification, `2509.14185` Fig. 3b p.6, `log10` max residual (full 12-row table in
the JSON); threshold is the paper's own `O(10⁻¹³)`:

| model | best | worst | multi-stage applied to | distance to CAP at best |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CCF | **−13.714** | −6.664 | stable, 1st unstable | **meets it** |
| IPM with boundary | −11.183 | −7.526 | stable, 1st unstable | 1.82 decades |
| Boussinesq | −8.178 | −7.020 | **none** | **4.82 decades** |

Gate discriminators, computed by the runner:
* **D1** — any solution the technique *was* applied to that still resisted? **0 of 4. No.**
* **D2** — models stranded in `2509.14185` that a later model-preserving technique change
  rescued? **CCF and IPM with boundary. Yes.** Never retried: **Boussinesq**.

→ `TECHNIQUE_SPECIFIC`. The classifier's self-tests show it returns `MODEL_SPECIFIC` if the
technique had been applied and failed, and `NOT_LOCATABLE` if no follow-up existed — so this is
a control that can come out differently (lesson 90).

**The gap has a ladder shape, not an endpoint (lesson 72).** `2511.22819` §2.3.3 p.11 measures
two compounding effects per additional unstable mode, at fixed compute budget: the λ-identifying
signal `Rmax` *decays* ~1 decade per mode, and the background residual you must detect it
against *rises* ~1 decade per mode. **Net margin closure ≈ 2 decades per instability order.**
Cross-checked: the paper's stated 1 decade/mode and its worked IPM example (stable `O(10⁻¹⁴)`
→ 4th unstable `O(10⁻¹⁰)`, 4 modes) agree at **1.00 vs 1.00 decades/mode** — two independent
sentences, so this is a real check, not a tautology. This ladder is *not* removed by gradient
normalization, only pushed back; it is why IPM's 4th unstable stagnates at `10⁻¹⁰`.

## 5. Gap in leg 173's terms (precision / scope / infrastructure), for comparability

* **Precision, CCF — CLOSED.** `O(10⁻¹³)`, full suite, round-off limited.
* **Precision, IPM — CLOSED.** `O(10⁻¹¹)`–`O(10⁻¹³)`, all modes.
* **Precision, Boussinesq / 3D Euler with boundary — OPEN by ~4.8 decades.** Status
  **untested, not measured-dead**: the fix exists and has not been published on this model.
  Headwind is §4's 2 decade/mode ladder.
* **Scope — OPEN, and not a precision question.** The finite-unstable-spectrum condition is
  called "desirable" and assumed. **No amount of residual reduction discharges it.** For an
  *unstable* singularity a CAP must enclose the unstable subspace exactly — its dimension, and
  the absence of further unstable eigenvalues off the real axis and outside the symmetry class.
  Both are assumptions in the paper.
* **Infrastructure — OPEN.** No CAP exists for any of these solutions. Searched
  `export.arxiv.org` this pass: `all:"unstable singularities" AND all:"self-similar"` returns
  exactly two records (`2511.22819`, `2509.14185`); the CAP announced as "manuscript in
  preparation" in Sep 2025 has not appeared ~11 months later; and `2511.22819` still calls its
  own contribution *"an important ingredient for bridging the gap between numerical discovery
  and computer-assisted proofs"* — an ingredient, not the proof.

## 6. The escalation branch, and the near-miss recorded honestly

The gate answered TECHNIQUE-SPECIFIC, so the model-specific escalation does **not** fire. The
tempting near-miss is written down so it is not quietly reinvented later:

**CCF is *not* in this repository's gCLM family, and this leg does not claim it is.** Checked
against `solver/gclm_family.py` lines 11–13, whose model is
`omega_t + a·u·omega_x = omega·H(omega)`, `u_x = H(omega)` (`a=0` CLM, `a=1` De Gregorio). CCF
is `theta_t + (H theta)·theta_x = 0` — **no stretching term at all**, and the velocity is
`H theta` itself rather than its antiderivative `U`. A relative, not a member.
`grep -rn "CCF\|Córdoba\|Cordoba" solver/` returns **nothing**. What is shared is
model-agnostic 1D tooling (`line_hilbert.py`, `interval.py`, `profile_newton.py`) — a statement
about plumbing, not a lane. **No construction is proposed under this leg's authority.**

## 7. What this corrects in the framing under review

*"Still one model class away"* is not supported by the paper or its follow-up. It was **one
loss-reweighting scheme away**, and that scheme landed **72 days later**, on the same models,
closing CCF and IPM to round-off.

What is genuinely still open is narrower than a model class, and it is four separate things:
(a) the fix is unpublished for Boussinesq/3D-Euler-with-boundary, (b) the ~2 decade/mode margin
ladder, (c) the unstable-spectrum enclosure — **a proof obligation, not a numerics one**, and
(d) a CAP announced in September 2025 that has still not appeared.

**No link of the L1→L4 chain moved.** This is a literature-scoping leg on someone else's
