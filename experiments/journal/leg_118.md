# Leg 118 — Route-TPA: adversarial audit of `solver/turning_point.py`'s CLASSIFICATION

**Branch** `leg/tpa-v1`. **Date** 2026-08-06. **Exploration leg, standard, CLAIM-BEARING**
(adversarial audit).

**Gate (verbatim).** *Under an adversarial battery (degenerate branches, poisoned
derivatives), does `solver/turning_point.py` ever silently return a wrong classification
instead of flagging the input?*

## GATE ANSWER: **YES.** Escalated, not patched.

`solver/turning_point.py` and `solver/collocation_newton.py` (which supplies
`critical_radius`/`effective_speed`, the detection primitive every public function in
`turning_point.py` relies on to locate `X_c`) are both untouched by this leg, under the
gate's own instruction. A degenerate `E(X)` — one that touches zero at exactly one grid
node and never actually changes sign — is classified by `critical_radius` as containing a
genuine turning point, and every downstream function accepts that classification with
**zero warnings and zero exceptions**.

## The mechanism, in the module's own construction

`critical_radius(X, E)` (`solver/collocation_newton.py:335`) implements "the smallest
`X > 0` where `E` changes sign" as

```python
s = np.where(np.diff(np.sign(E)) != 0)[0]
if s.size == 0: return inf
i = s[0]; t = -E[i] / (E[i + 1] - E[i]); return X[i] + t * (X[i + 1] - X[i])
```

The classical bracketing condition for "does `[X_i, X_{i+1}]` contain a genuine crossing" is
the **strict** product inequality `E[i]*E[i+1] < 0` (Bolzano's theorem — the condition
`scipy.optimize.brentq` enforces, raising `ValueError: f(a) and f(b) must have different
signs` otherwise). `np.sign(E[i+1]) != np.sign(E[i])` is a different, weaker test: it fires
identically whenever **either** endpoint is exactly `0.0`, i.e. at a **tangency** — a local
extremum of `E` that grazes zero without `E` ever actually going negative. A tangency is not
a turning point under the module's own physical picture (the docstring's own derivation
assumes `E ~ -a*h_c*(X-X_c)`, a genuinely transversal zero). Nothing downstream re-validates
what `critical_radius` returns: `homogeneous_far_field` checks only `np.isfinite(Xc)`, which
a tangency-produced `X_c` passes trivially, and `inner_mode_exponent`, `log_growth_exponent`
and `source_of_the_row` accept whatever `X_c` they are handed with no check at all.

This is the same bug family SciPy itself shipped and later patched
(https://github.com/scipy/scipy/pull/14007, *"Fix root bracketing logic in Brent's
method"*), and it is exactly the edge case general root-bracketing references name for
tangent/double roots — full citations in `writeup/novelty/leg_118.md`.

## What was measured

Eight batteries in `experiments/p2_route_tpa_v1_adversarial.py`, all numbers in
`writeup/data/p2_route_tpa_v1_adversarial.json`. Two complementary adversary constructions,
stated in full in the runner's own docstring: (A) a monkeypatch of
`solver.turning_point.effective_speed` — the one imported name under adversarial control,
restored in a `finally` block immediately after each case, with every other function in the
call graph real and unmodified — used wherever the ground truth (turning point or not, and
where) needs to be known analytically and in advance; (B) a genuinely poisoned `om`
(Fourier coefficient vector) run through every function with **zero** monkeypatching, to
confirm the same failure class is reachable through the ordinary calling convention.

### G1/G2/G3 — the module is not universally broken (controls)

- **G1 (positive control).** A real, J=100, a=0.3, Newton-converged profile's one genuine
  crossing is found correctly: `Xc = 6.133491`, matching between the direct
  `critical_radius` call and `homogeneous_far_field`'s own computation; the fitted inner
  exponent (3.548) and outer exponent (3.330) both land near the predicted `1/a = 3.333`,
  as `test_turning_point.py` already established and this leg does not re-litigate.
- **G2.** Five constructions of an `E` that truly never crosses zero and does **not** land
  on a grid node (offsets 0.38–50.5 from the first grid point): **5/5** correctly report
  `Xc = inf`, and `homogeneous_far_field` correctly raises
  `ValueError: no turning point: E does not change sign`.
- **G3.** A NaN placed at or adjacent to the real crossing produces a `nan` `X_c` — flagged,
  not silently absorbed; `homogeneous_far_field` on a NaN-poisoned `om` raises the same
  `ValueError`; a distant `+inf` well away from the real crossing leaves `Xc` **bit-for-bit
  unchanged** (`6.133491174450277` both ways) — poisoning that never reaches the crossing's
  neighbourhood does not disturb it.

### G4 — THE DEFECT: on-grid-node tangency, 10/10 silent

Five touch locations (10%, 25%, 50%, 65%, 85% of the grid) × two signs
(touch-from-above/below) = 10 constructions where `E` is exactly `(X - X0)^2` (or its
negation), verified `E[touch] == 0.0` exactly and `E` single-signed everywhere else —
**provably no turning point**. **10/10** report a finite `Xc` (equal to the touch location)
from `critical_radius`, and every one of `homogeneous_far_field`, `inner_mode_exponent`,
`log_growth_exponent` and `source_of_the_row` returns fully finite output with **0
warnings** captured (`warnings.catch_warnings(record=True)`, `simplefilter("always")`) and
**0 exceptions**, across all 10 cases. One representative case (touch at 10% of the grid,
`X0 = 0.166446`): `Xc = 0.166446` (silent, finite, wrong), `homogeneous_far_field` returns
`h` fully finite, `inner_mode_exponent` returns `p = 15.638` (a number with no physical
referent, since there is no turning point), `source_of_the_row` returns
`near_Xc_fraction = 1.72e-05` — a small, entirely unremarkable-looking number.

### G5 — the sharpest instance: a spurious touch masks the real crossing, up to 64.5×

A spurious tangency (a perfect square, provably `E >= 0` up to the transition) is placed
*before* a real, well-separated transversal crossing further out, at three touch fractions
(5%, 10%, 20% of the grid):

| touch at (frac of grid) | reported `Xc` | true turning point `X_true` | true/reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 0.086609 | 5.586609 | **64.50×** |
| 10% | 0.166446 | 5.666446 | **34.04×** |
| 20% | 0.333625 | 5.833625 | **17.49×** |

In all three, `critical_radius` reports **exactly** the spurious touch point
(`reported_equals_spurious_touch = True`) and the real turning point is never reached at
all — `homogeneous_far_field` returns a fully finite `h` starting from `1.5×` the *wrong*
`X_c`, with no warning that a second, real crossing exists further out. The magnitude is a
property of where the adversary places the two features (moving the spurious touch closer
to the origin increases the ratio without bound), not a ceiling.

### G6 — severity: latent under every configuration this repository runs today

Across 8 `(J, a)` combinations (`J ∈ {60, 100}`, `a ∈ {0.15, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}`), the real
velocity field `U = col.V @ om` of a Newton-converged profile has **0** interior local
extrema in every single case (`n_interior_extrema_of_U = 0`, `U_monotonic = True`, 8/8). A
monotone `U` can only cross zero *transversally* — the tangency this leg exercises requires
either an adversarially-constructed `E` (as in G4/G5) or an adversarially-poisoned `om` (G7);
no ordinary Newton solve this repository can currently produce reaches it on its own. Both
downstream call sites (`experiments/p2_route_d_v13_turning.py`,
`experiments/p2_route_d_v14_first_integral.py`) always pass a fresh `solved_profile` output,
never a partial or hand-built one, so **exposure today is zero** — the same severity shape
as legs 106 and 120 (LATENT, not ACTIVE).

### G7 — reachable with zero monkeypatching

A single Fourier coefficient of a real, converged profile perturbed by `+1e4`, `+1e8`
(index 3) or `+1e6` (the middle index) moves `Xc` from the clean `6.133491` to `0.0820`,
`0.0825` and `0.0718` respectively — a **>70× silent change**, through the ordinary,
completely unmodified calling convention (no monkeypatch anywhere in this gate). This does
not claim a "true" `X_c` for the corrupted profile (there isn't one — the corrupted `E`
legitimately crosses where it crosses); it demonstrates only that the same finite,
unflagged propagation is reachable from an ordinary poisoned-coefficient input, not solely
from a synthetic `E`.

### G8 — the matrix-inversion side crashes loudly, not silently

`graded_norm_by_radius`, `bordered_norm` and `square_bordered_smin` under NaN/Inf-poisoned
`om` or `c` all raise `LinAlgError: SVD did not converge` — an ugly failure mode (bare
LAPACK diagnostics to stderr), but a **flagged** one, not a silent finite wrong answer.
`source_of_the_row` returns a `nan`-valued dict (also flagged, via the `nan`) rather than a
plausible finite one. This is reported as a **control**, not a second instance of the
defect: the classification primitive (`critical_radius`) is the one place a genuinely
finite, plausible, wrong answer was found.

## Disposition

**ESCALATE. Do not patch under this leg's authority.** The repair, when authorised, is a
guard on `critical_radius` itself — require the strict product `E[i]*E[i+1] < 0`
(equivalently, treat an exact `0.0` endpoint as its own case rather than folding it into the
sign-difference test) — not a change to any exponent or constant the module computes on a
genuine, transversal crossing, none of which this leg contests. `test_turning_point.py`'s
6 gates are untouched and continue to own the module's physics on non-degenerate input.

`test_turning_point_adversarial.py`'s gates 4, 5 and 6 are **GAP-PINS** in leg 84's sense:
they assert the CURRENT unsound classification behaviour precisely so it cannot decay at the
rate of memory, and **they must be INVERTED, not weakened, the day a strict-sign-change
guard lands.**
