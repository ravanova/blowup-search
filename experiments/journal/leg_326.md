## 2026-08-11 — entry 3: the full-text read, and the gate

**The document, re-pinned by independent download this session.**
`https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.7414v1` → `sha256 113e1e17dff47f722643e548286bc5f60ba6a35b17f60c61a7f83b0a7ea07338`,
172471 bytes, 10 pages — **byte-identical to the hash the novelty pass banked at `806d4bd`**,
fetched again on a separate connection. Text extracted with `pdftotext -layout`. Read in full,
all four theorems and both proofs of the vorticity section.

**Leg 313's screened object, quoted from `leg/313-sdss-v1` @ `434b49a`,
`writeup/4_p2_lottery/TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTE_SDSS_V1.md`:**

> *"The object under seeding is leg 251's `NS3D-DSS-NONAXI-LAMBDA-LARGE`: 3D incompressible
> **NS**, **non-axisymmetric** backward DSS, `λ` significantly larger than 1, profile outside
> `L^∞_t L³` — as a search target, a periodic orbit of the dynamically rescaled flow with
> period `T = 2 log λ`."*

Four clauses to check: **(α) the equation is Navier–Stokes**; (β) non-axisymmetric; (γ) `λ`
significantly larger than 1; (δ) profile outside `L^∞_t L³`.

### The theorem, quoted in full

> **Theorem 2.1.** *Let `V(y,s) ∈ C²_x C¹_t (R^{3+1})` be a time periodic solution of **(1.6)**
> with period `S₀ > 0` that has bounded first derivatives and satisfies*
> *(2.1) `lim_{|y|→∞} V(y,s) = 0`, `∀s ∈ [0, S₀)`,*
> *and for some `r > 0`,*
> *(2.2) `Ω := curl V ∈ ⋂_{0<q<r} L^q(R³ × [0, S₀])`.*
> *Then `V = 0` on `R^{3+1}`.*

and its companion,

> **Theorem 2.2.** *… a time periodic solution of **(1.6)** with period `S₀ > 0` that has
> bounded first derivatives satisfying (2.10) `lim_{|y|→∞} sup_{0<s<S₀} |V| + |∇V| = 0`, and
> there exists `q ∈ (0, 3/(1+α))` such that (2.11) `Ω ∈ L^q(R³ × [0, S₀])`. Then `V = 0`.*

Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 are also *"a time periodic solution of **(1.6)**"*, with velocity-integrability
hypotheses instead of vorticity ones. **All four theorems in the paper are statements about
(1.6) and nothing else.**

### What (1.6) is — this is the whole gate

(1.6) is the paper's self-similar transform of **(E)**, and (E) is defined on its first page as

> *"the Euler equations for the homogeneous incompressible fluid flows in `R³ × I`,
> `(E) ∂v/∂t + (v·∇)v = −∇p`, `div v = 0`"*

giving

> *(1.6) `∂_s V + (α/(α+1))V + (1/(α+1))(y·∇)V + (V·∇)V = −∇P`, `div V = 0`.*

**There is no `∆V` in (1.6). The paper writes the Navier–Stokes rescaled equation down
separately, as a different display, in a subsection explicitly headed "Related results for
Navier-Stokes equations":**

> *"For comparison, we review related results for Navier-Stokes equations (NS) **for which we
> add `∆v` to the right side of (E)**. … The corresponding time-dependent Leray's equations for
> `(V, P)` read (1.10) `∂V/∂s − ∆V − aV − a y·∇V + (V·∇)V = −∇P`, `div V = 0`."*

`(1.10) ≠ (1.6)`, by the one term that is the entire difference between Euler and
Navier–Stokes, and **no theorem in the paper is stated for (1.10)**.

### The one generalisation the paper offers, and it stays inviscid

The paper anticipates the reach question and answers it itself, immediately after the proof of
Theorem 2.1:

> *"**Remark after the proof:** The above proof works for more general system
> (2.9) `V_s + aV + b(y·∇)V + (V·∇)V = −∇P`, `div V = 0`, where `a, b` are arbitrary real
> constants with `b ≠ 0`."*

The generalisation is over the two **constants** `a` and `b`. **`−∆V` is not a member of that
family for any choice of `a, b`.** This is the load-bearing clause of the whole read: the
authors state exactly how far the proof carries, and it carries to a two-parameter family of
**inviscid** rescaled equations, not to (1.10).

### The paper's own status report on the screened object

§1.1, verbatim, on precisely the backward-DSS-NS question:

> *"For the backward case `a < 0` … **The existence of backward DSS solutions has not been
> addressed in literature**, except that if `V ∈ L^∞(R, L³(R³))`, which is equivalent to
> `v ∈ L^∞((−∞,0), L³(R³))`, it must be zero by the result of [12]. Thus one is concerned,
> e.g., if `V` only has the bound (1.11) `|V(y,s)| ≤ C_*/(1+|y|)`, `∀(y,s) ∈ R^{3+1}`, for some
> large constant `C_*`."*

Two facts fall out at once. First, the paper declares the screened object's question **open**,
in 2013, in its own words. Second, the **only** exclusion it credits for backward DSS NS is
`V ∈ L^∞(R, L³)` (Šverák [12]) — and clause (δ) of the screened object specifies the profile
**outside `L^∞_t L³`**, i.e. leg 251 specified the object precisely outside the one NS result
this paper knows about. The two definitions do not overlap; they were built not to.

And on clause (γ), the paper's closing sentence of §1.1:

> *"In view of the forward case, one may hope that the case with the **DSS-factor `λ`
> sufficiently close to 1** might be easier to exclude."*

The screened object has **`λ` significantly larger than 1** — the side of that remark the
authors flag as the *harder* one, and this is within Euler, before viscosity is even reached.

### GATE — answered in its pre-committed wording

> **Gate.** Does the full text yield a definite answer — (i) the hypothesis covers the screened
> object (the rigidity theorem BITES), or (ii) it does not reach it (the theorem is silent;
> the packet's balance is unchanged)?

## **YES — and the definite answer is (ii). CHAE–TSAI DOES NOT BITE.**

The theorem is **silent** on `NS3D-DSS-NONAXI-LAMBDA-LARGE`. Three independent grounds, in
order of strength; **the first alone is decisive and the other two are recorded so the finding
does not rest on a single reading**:

1. **Wrong equation (decisive, and it is in the title).** Every theorem hypothesises *"a time
   periodic solution of (1.6)"*, and (1.6) is the rescaled **Euler** system. The screened object
   is a solution of **Navier–Stokes**. The authors' own reach remark (2.9) extends the proof to
   arbitrary real constants `a, b` with `b ≠ 0` and **not** to the addition of `−∆V`; the paper
   displays the NS rescaled equation (1.10) separately and proves nothing about it. Clause (α)
   of the screened object fails the hypothesis outright.
2. **The decay hypothesis is far stronger than the screened object's, even on the Euler side.**
   (2.2) requires `Ω ∈ L^q(R³ × [0,S₀])` **for every `q ∈ (0, r)`**. Small `q` makes `L^q(R³)`
   integrability at infinity a *severe* decay demand — `|Ω|` must decay faster than `|y|^{-3/q}`,
   which blows up as `q ↓ 0`. The screened object carries only a Type-I-scale bound of the
   shape (1.11), `|V| ≤ C_*/(1+|y|)`, which the paper itself introduces as the case **not**
   covered. So even the counterfactual "if it were Euler" reading does not reach it: this is
   the *"merely a decaying subclass"* horn that leg 313 named and refused to guess, and the
   full text confirms that horn — Theorem 2.1 governs a strongly-decaying subclass, and the
   screened object is not in it.
3. **The `λ` regime is on the side the authors call harder.** Screened object: `λ` significantly
   larger than 1. Paper: `λ` *sufficiently close to 1* "might be easier to exclude."

Clause (β), non-axisymmetry, never becomes relevant — the hypothesis fails before symmetry is
reached. It is recorded here only to close the ledger of four clauses.

**Neither horn of the "ambiguous" branch applies.** The paper is not ambiguous: it names its
equation in its title, its abstract (*"discretely self-similar solutions of the three
dimensional incompressible **Euler** equations"*), its every theorem statement, and its own
remark on how far the proof generalises. The unknown leg 313 recorded honestly is now **known**,
and a single full-text read resolved it, exactly as the dispatch predicted it might.
