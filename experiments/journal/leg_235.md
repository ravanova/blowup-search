## Why this is a second instance and not leg 202 restated

Different verdict, different diagnostic, different mechanism — and, decisively, **leg
202's prescribed fix does not repair it.** Leg 202 named the `converged` flag from
`profile_newton.py`'s `continuation`, trusted by v11's `a_max_machine` / `GA_boundary`
claims. This is a **third** claim in the same artifact, which leg 202 did not name, and
its mechanism is not a bad convergence flag but a **min/max selection**: both ends are
computed, and the headline is built from the favourable one. Every row in the `good` set
passes the artifact's own on-branch tests, so repairing `continuation` leaves this exactly
where it is.

**Stated against my own case:** the worst row, `a = 0.45`, sits at `1.5196e-02` between
neighbours at `2.3745e-08` (`a = 0.40`) and `7.4528e-07` (`a = 0.50`) — a ~6-decade spike
across one step of a smooth continuation, which is leg 202's off-branch signature. That row
may *also* be an instance of leg 202's mechanism. This does not merge the findings; it
sharpens the point: taking the **max** is exactly the operation that would have surfaced
leg 202's defect from inside v11's own budget block, without opening `profile_newton.py`
at all. The rejecting signal was one `min` away from being used.

**Scope caveat, stated plainly.** It is in the **same runner** leg 202 already escalated,
and **zero** instances were found in any of the other 195 banked JSONs. A reviewer who
scopes the gate's *"every other banked headline verdict"* by **artifact** rather than by
**verdict** should read this as *NO-plus-a-sharpening of 202*. This leg reports YES because
the gate's unit is the verdict, and because the dispatched 202 repair does not touch it.

## Method and its named limits (lesson 91)

Five rules over all 196 banked JSONs — `R-SPREAD` (verdict fixed while a diagnostic moves
≥6 decades), `R-JOIN` (verdict list and diagnostic list as siblings joined on a key),
`R-THRESH` (name-matched threshold violated), `R-MAGN` (error-named quantity > 1),
`R-SELECT` (headline built from the favourable end of a range whose other end violates the
block's budget) — then a **wiring check** against the producing runner *and* `solver/`, then
adjudication with every dismissal reasoned in the JSON.

- **25,514 dicts / 15,133 lists walked; 4,320 positive verdicts in 131 of 196 files.**
- **190 screened hits, all adjudicated, 0 left unresolved**: 93 `DISMISS-WIRED`, 68
  `DISMISS-PROBE-RECORD` (15 adversarial/repair/verification/calibration logs, where a bad
  number beside a positive flag is the finding the audit leg deliberately banked), 19
  `DISMISS-INTENDED`, 6 `DISMISS-INPUT-VARIABLE`, 2 `DISMISS-SCALE`, 1 `DISMISS-REPORTED`,
  1 `DISMISS-UNRELATED`. The 24 that the automatic rules could not settle were opened at
  source and adjudicated by hand, each with its reason and line reference banked in
  `HAND_ADJUDICATED`.
- **The realization this NO-for-the-other-195 holds in:** banked `writeup/data/*.json`
  paired with their producing runners, via the five rules above. **162 of 4,320 positive
  verdicts (3.75%) are structurally out of reach** — no numeric sibling in their own record
  and no joinable sibling list. The census is **silent** about those, not clearing them.
- **Leg 237 (Route-SIRC) landed mid-flight** and was folded in: its `p2_route_sirc_v1_census.json`
  is a calibration log whose `converged: true` rows carry `escaped: true` in the same
  record, so its 6 hits are `DISMISS-PROBE-RECORD`, adjudicated by name.

Two rules exist only because the control forced them. `R-JOIN` was added when the control
proved leg 202's strongest pairing is cross-block (`v4_grids/rows` vs `v4_grids/verdict`,
joined on `a`); `R-SELECT` was added when the control's `v5_budget` reading appeared. A
census blind to the instance it is calibrated on is not a census.
