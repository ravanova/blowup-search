## 2. What leg 348 and leg 350 actually require, read rather than assumed

Leg 348 (`experiments/journal/leg_348.md` §3–4) named the obstruction precisely: every located
periodic-orbit/branch CAP closure (Zgliczyński's KS fixed points, van den Berg–Breden–Sheombarsing's
periodic-parabolic integration, van den Berg–Breden–Lessard–van Veen's 3-torus NS periodic orbits,
the Taylor-Couette UPO paper, both Arioli-Koch-line papers) closes its tail-domination (neglected-
mode) estimate against a **compact** domain using a **discrete, geometrically/exponentially
decaying** spectral basis (Fourier or Chebyshev). Route 4's object (leg 260, re-read this leg) needs
the opposite: an unbounded `R³` domain, `L^p(R^3)` `p>3` / weighted `L^2(ρ)` `ρ=(1+|y|)^{-s}` `s>1`
— thresholds leg 260 measured sharp by shell-ratio quadrature, both crossing at the stated exponent
to 5 significant figures — a space that explicitly EXCLUDES the one weight family (Gaussian) that
would supply an analogous discrete, rapidly-decaying basis "for free."

Leg 350 (`experiments/journal/leg_350.md`) is a genuinely different thing from what the dispatch's
one-line gloss ("350's own log-Boyd composition") might suggest at a distance: it is a **1D, single
scalar-target, internal instrument** — a log map removing an endpoint branch point, composed with
one fixed Boyd algebraic map `u/(u+L)`, `L=16` frozen — measured against leg 313's own
`(1-X)^{1-iκ}` boundary-block function, with a 25.7×–28.5× mode-count win over plain Chebyshev and
two falsification controls, explicitly NOT a certificate by its own leg's account ("CEILING: TIER 2
unchanged"). Reading it in full before citing it (rather than trusting the dispatch's one-line
gloss) mattered: it is not itself a candidate basis with a published tail/completeness theorem, and
this leg's table treats it accordingly — as the fifth, uninventoriable row, not folded into the
Boyd/rational-Chebyshev row as if it were the same thing.

Five rows: plain Hermite (Gaussian weight, excluded baseline), generalized/algebraic-weight Hermite
(Xiang–Wang/Zhang–Guo/Guo–Zhang), rational Chebyshev (Boyd/Christov), mapped Laguerre
(Coulaud–Funaro–Kavian, the one paper located), and leg 350's own log-Boyd composition (internal,
uninventoriable against external literature). Full citations, quotes/paraphrases at the read depth

## 4. The headline finding

**No row publishes a result meeting all four of the bridge's asks simultaneously.** Every genuinely
spectral candidate located is 1D/scalar in its published form — no R³ or vector/Leray-projected
extension was located for any of them. The closest weight-match (generalized Hermite with algebraic
weight `(1+x²)^{-γ}`) carries an unresolved, structurally suggestive concern: Boyd's own 1980 result
that Hermite-series convergence depends on the target's real-axis decay exponent `a`, read alongside
leg 334 §3.3's internal (different-operator) finding that the algebraic weight yields a continuous
rather than discrete spectrum, together SUGGEST — not prove — that the coefficient-decay rate
achievable under this basis family may itself be algebraic rather than geometric, which would be a
structural mismatch with the exponential-tail-bound method every located compact-domain CAP closure
actually uses. This is recorded as a suggestive concern requiring a full-text read to confirm or
refute, not as a proven obstruction — the two citations behind it (Boyd 1980, Zhang-Guo 2014) were
both read at snippet/abstract level only this leg, and `sciencedirect.com`'s Zhang-Guo page itself
returned HTTP 403, so the paraphrase is carried from search-engine snippets, not the primary text.

The least-sourced row (mapped Laguerre — one paper, one uncited contrastive sentence, no theorem
statement extracted) is banked as itself decision-relevant per the gate's own instruction: the
literature genuinely underdetermines this row at the search depth this leg could reach.
