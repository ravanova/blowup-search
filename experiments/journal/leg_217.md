### M1 is not a one-line sign flip, and the reason is structural

The angular direction *can* be chosen pointwise because it lives inside a tridiagonal block
Thomas solves either way. The radial direction fixes **the order of the sweep itself**:

* uniformly positive `s_rho` → block **lower** bidiagonal → outward sweep, `prev = f_{i-1}`;
* uniformly negative `s_rho` → block **upper** bidiagonal → **inward** sweep, `prev = f_{i+1}`,
  equally exact;
* **mixed** signs → neither, so **no single sweep is its exact inverse** and the routine now
  raises rather than return the exact inverse of an operator it was not asked for.

`s_rho ≡ 0` carries no radial coupling, is admissible in either direction, and comes back
**bit-identical** — which matters because that is the configuration the module's own ADI
negative control runs at (`p2_route_l_v1_precond.py:172` passes `0 * one`).

### The control leg 200's battery could not have contained — lesson 90

**Every one of leg 200's nine `s_rho` cases is MIXED-sign** (one entry of an otherwise positive
profile driven negative). So a "repair" that simply *refused every negative entry* would pass
leg 200's battery **identically**, and nothing in it could tell the difference. That is a
control that cannot come out differently.

So this leg added the case: a **uniformly negative** `s_rho`. The repaired sweep must *return*
the exact inverse there, not refuse — and it does, at **2.11e−16** against the densely
assembled advertised operator. **Pre-repair the identical input came back `SILENT` at 1186.6
relative error** — finite, correctly shaped, no exception. This is the only evidence in the leg
that distinguishes *upwinding by sign* from *refusing when nervous*, and it is the one case
leg 200's battery structurally could not contain.
