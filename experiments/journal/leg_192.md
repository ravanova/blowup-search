## Conjunct 2 — Xu's closed form: YES, and leg 176 UNDERSTATES itself by 7.1× (V2, V3)

`f` is a finite Laguerre combination, so `G = b²f` is a **rational** function with denominator
`b⁴`; Xu's `G₂ = G − G₀ − G₁t` then has a double root at `0` by construction, and
`∫₀^y G₂/t² dt` is the integral of a proper rational function. Expanding the resulting cubic
about `t = −i/2` gives an **exact antiderivative**: three algebraic terms plus one principal
complex log. The path `t: 0 → y` keeps `b = t + i/2` on the line `Im = ½`, strictly inside the
upper half-plane, so no branch is crossed for either sign of `y`. The algebraic part is
evaluated in exact rational arithmetic, so it suffers **no cancellation** at small `|y|` — the
exact defect leg 176 had to engineer around with Taylor coefficients.

Against that reference, over leg 176's own six data and twelve `y`-nodes:

* **leg 176's `u(y)` is correct to `6.738e-16` relative** — about 2 ulp. Its quadrature
  machinery does what it says.
* Its banked headline is `4.767e-15`, **7.08× larger**. That number is a *residual*, not an
  error, and it is the arithmetic floor of its own metric: `−u − yu′ + iu/b` cancels three
  terms each `8.0–17.4×` larger than `f` and then divides by `|f|`, and
  `observed / (amplification × ε)` is **0.85–2.88 across all six seeds**. This is lesson 86
  exactly — *a bound dominated by its own evaluation error is a statement about the code* —
  and here it works in leg 176's favour.

**The ODE identity is proved, not sampled.** With `u = −Φ/b²`, `Φ = I + c₀ + c₁y`, `I = yA`:
`−u − yu′ + iu/b = (yΦ′ − Φ)/b²` (using `−2y − i = −2b`), and `yΦ′ − Φ = y²A′ + G₀ = G − G₁y`.
So the pointwise residual is **exactly `−ℓ(f)·y/b(y)²`**. Two consequences, both checked:
Xu's kernel solves the equation identically *on* `{ℓ(f) = 0}` and only there, and
**`G′(0) = ℓ(f)` is an exact rational identity** (6/6 trials on data with `ℓ(f)` deliberately
non-zero — a control that would have failed by an `O(1)` amount had the identity been wrong).
Xu himself reports a float substitution residual of `3 × 10⁻¹⁶` under his eq. (4.23); leg
176's `u` sits in the same class.

### The correction that matters, and it strengthens leg 176

Leg 176 reads its `N = 1024` row as *"the float floor of an `X` Gram whose entries reach
~1e12"* and stops its reliable window at 512. **That diagnosis attributes to the Gram what
belongs to the whitening.** The rise is a property of `_sym_sqrt`'s eigendecomposition: `G ⪰ I`
while `‖G‖ ~ 1e12`, so `eigh`'s backward-stable absolute error `ε‖G‖ ~ 1e-4` lands as a
*relative* error on the eigenvalues near 1 — precisely the ones the whitening inverts. A
Cholesky whitening of the same Gram **stays monotone at `N = 1024`**, continuing to
`0.0907911`. The reliable window is wider than leg 176 claimed, and the evidence for a
positive limit is therefore *stronger*, not weaker.

It remains **float64 evidence of a positive limit, not a proof of one**, and this leg does not
upgrade that — leg 176's own wording on this point was already correct and is left standing.

## A suspected defect, raised and REFUTED (V6) — the negative control

Reading `_cum_branch`, its Taylor branch integrates Xu's `G₂ = G − G₀ − G₁t` while its Gauss
branch integrates `(G − G₀)/t²`. Side by side those are different integrals off the
solvability subspace, and `A` would carry a spurious `G₁ log(|y|/0.25)`.

**Tested by driving `ℓ(f)` over six decades** (`1e-9` to `1e-1`) against the exact residual of
V3. A real defect scales *linearly* with `ℓ(f)`; the discrepancy instead stays **flat at
~1.3e-14**, i.e. the residual-assembly rounding of V2 and nothing else. This control could
have come out the other way by a factor of `1e8` and did not (lesson 90).

**The mechanism, which is worth banking:** `B = ∫G′/t dt` picks up the *same*
`G₁ log(|y|/0.25)` as `A`, because `G₁` is the constant term of `G′`; and the residual sees
`A` and `B` only through `y(B − A)`, in which the two logs cancel identically. Leg 176's
quadrature is correct here. **Nothing repaired, because nothing needed repairing.**
