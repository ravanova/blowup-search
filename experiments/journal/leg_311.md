## The gate, and the answer

> Does (iv_a), measured directly on the three non-incompressible models, exclude candidates the
> way it excluded the 18 fluid rows?

## **GATE: YES** — **3 of 3 non-incompressible models killed**, same as leg 261's 18 of 18.

`verdict_code = SCREEN_IV_A_BITES_OFF_AXIS_MECHANISM_NONLOCALITY_NOT_INCOMPRESSIBILITY`.

**The pool is NOT materially larger than leg 261 implies — but the reason generalises past what
leg 261 could see from the fluid cell alone.** Leg 261 measured the Leray-projection instance of
Remark 40's silence (18/18 fluid rows, screen (iv_a) "constant on the fluid cell"). This leg
measures the same clause's *other* named exclusions — Hilbert transform, Biot–Savart-type
inversion, fractional dissipation `Λ^γ`/`(-Δ)^α` — directly on three models built with none of
NS's incompressibility structure, and finds them equally excluded. **Mechanism, named per lesson
91: the operative reach failure is NONLOCALITY, not incompressibility specifically.**
Incompressibility (via the Leray projector) is one instance of a nonlocal operator Remark 40
doesn't name; the Hilbert transform and fractional dissipation dial are two more, and this leg's
thesis — that non-incompressible models carry no such constraint, so the screen might not bite —
is answered NO because the thesis conflated "incompressible" with "the only reason (iv_a) bites."

## Liveness / self-test

`self_test()` runs three checks, all pass (`liveness.all_passed = true`):

1. **Baseline**: all three killed — the headline result.
2. **Dissipation-stripped counterfactual** (pretend every model's dissipation term is the
   ordinary Laplacian, `s=1`, which Remark 40 *does* name): all three **still killed**, because
   the nonlinearity operator (Hilbert transform / Biot–Savart) is independently unnamed. This
   rules out the table being vacuously "always killed" regardless of what is actually measured —
   the dissipation term is not carrying the whole verdict.
3. **Positive control** (generalised viscous Burgers, leg 255's own worked-example row, local
   polynomial `v²v_x` + ordinary Laplacian): **passes** (iv_a), confirming the classifier can
   report the other answer.

## BLOG

Leg 261 found that Breden–Chu's certification machinery can't reach *any* incompressible fluid
model — 18 out of 18 candidates, killed by one line of Remark 40 that never mentions the Leray
projection. The natural next question: is that a fact about *fluids*, or a fact about
*nonlocality in general* — and if it's the latter, does the same trap catch models that were
never incompressible to begin with?

We took three models this project already built for a different reason (measuring where
fractional dissipation kills a self-similar blow-up) and ran the exact same reach clause against
their equations. All three use a Hilbert transform or a Biot–Savart-type velocity recovery, and
two of the three also dial in fractional dissipation. Every one of those is on Remark 40's list
of things it doesn't cover — separately from the pressure term that killed the fluid rows.

Result: 3 for 3, same as 18 for 18. The pool of models this particular certification method could
reach isn't secretly bigger once you leave the fluid axis. But the *reason* generalizes in a way
worth having on record: it was never really about incompressibility. It's about the method's
reach being local-polynomial, full stop — anything genuinely nonlocal, whatever physical reason
it's nonlocal for, is outside it.

## TECHNICAL

**Object.** Screen (iv_a), operationalized in leg 261/255 as Breden–Chu Remark 40
(`arXiv:2404.04054`, line 1687): a candidate's governing PDE passes only if every nonlinear/
principal-part operator is local-polynomial in `u` and its derivatives, over Gaussian-weighted
`H²(μ)` with a Laplacian/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck principal part, `d ≤ 3`. Leg 255's own reading lists
the excluded operator classes explicitly, including two this leg targets that are independent of
incompressibility: nonlocal nonlinearity operators (Hilbert transform, Biot–Savart) and nonlocal
dissipation (`(-Δ)^α`, `Λ^γ` non-even `γ`).

**Method.** No solver constructed or run. The three target models' governing equations are read
verbatim from their own module docstrings (`solver/fractional_gclm.py`,
`solver/critical_dissipation.py`, `solver/fractional_boussinesq.py`, all pre-existing, committed
by Route-F/G/H) and each operator classified against Remark 40's reach as a boolean field
(`named_by_remark_40`), never asserted at the row level. Verdict is computed:
`killed_by_iv_a = (# unnamed operators > 0)`.

**Numbers (full values in `writeup/data/p2_route_ivax_v1.json`).**

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| models measured | 3 |
| models killed by (iv_a) | **3** |
| fluid-adjacent models (of 3) | 1 (`FRAC-BOUSSINESQ`) |
| non-fluid-adjacent models killed | 2 of 2 |
| fluid-adjacent models killed | 1 of 1 |
| unnamed operator classes found | 2 (nonlocal nonlinearity; nonlocal dissipation) |
| leg 261 reference (fluid rows killed) | 18 of 18 |
| liveness checks | 3, all passed |

**Reading.** The gCLM/critical-dissipation pair fails on the Hilbert transform alone (their
dissipation, `Λ^{2s}`, is itself built from `H`, so it is doubly unnamed rather than
independently so — recorded, not treated as double-counting evidence). `FRAC-BOUSSINESQ` fails on
its Biot–Savart-type stream-function inversion and, independently, on `(-Δ)^s` whenever `s≠1`.
The self-test's dissipation-stripped counterfactual confirms the nonlinearity operator alone is
sufficient to kill every row — the result is not an artifact of double-counting the dissipation
term.

**Scope, stated precisely.** This measures ONE certification method's stated reach (Breden–Chu's
`H²(μ)` / Remark 40) against THREE specific models' governing equations as documented in this
repository's own solver modules. It does not survey other certification frameworks, does not
claim these models cannot be certified by any method, and does not move any link of L1→L4. No
stage claimed; `plan_of_record.py` untouched.
