## §2. THE THREE COMPONENTS, IN THIS REPOSITORY'S NOTATION

### 2.1 The self-similar concentrating vortex

`ℓ_r = q^(1/2)` (parabolic, from `X = r²/2q` bounded), `ℓ_z = q^D = q^(1/2-h)`, core volume
`q^(3/2-h)`, tangential speed `q^(-1/2-h)`, radial speed `O(q^(-1/2))`.

**THE IDENTITY THAT MAKES IT WORK — re-derived, not transcribed:** the three leading rates
**coincide at exactly `q^-1`** — radial transport `|u_r|/ℓ_r`, axial transport `q^-A/ℓ_z`, and
radial diffusion `1/ℓ_r²`. That coincidence is what turns the leading balance into a profile
problem at all.

**THE ONLY SMALL PARAMETER:** axial/radial diffusion `= ℓ_r²/ℓ_z² = q^(2h)`. It exists **only
because the core is anisotropic**. Planted control **C2** replaces `ℓ_z` by `ℓ_r` and the
parameter collapses to `q^0` *and* the three-rate coincidence breaks — one change, two failures.

### 2.2 The oscillatory pulses

`A_wave = q^(-1/2-h/2)`, `ℓ_wave = q^(1/2+h/2)`. The pulses are **quieter than the background by
`q^(h/2)` and shorter than the core by the same `q^(h/2)`** — one parameter controls the whole
separation, and it is the same `h` the energy condition constrains.

**The cancellation, exactly:** stress divergence `A_wave²/ℓ_r = q^(-3/2-h)` **==** leading
tangential residual `u_θ × q^-1 = q^(-3/2-h)`. Planted control **C1** sets `A_wave = q^(-1/2-h)`
— the amplitude a reader would guess, *"as strong as the background"* — and the ledger returns
`q^(-3/2-2h)` against `q^(-3/2-h)`: **no cancellation.** The instrument says no.

### 2.3 The correction cycle

`σ₀ = 1/5`, `σ_{j+1} = σ_j + 1/10`, so `σ_j → ∞`; 8 stages to reach `σ = 1`.

**What `σ_j → ∞` MEANS is flatness** — every Cartesian space-time derivative `O(q^N)` for
**every** `N`, not a smallness estimate but an infinite-order vanishing (Theorem 3.1(iii)).
Control **C5**: a cycle with step `0` does not reach it.

**Energy:** `E_core = q^(1/2-3h)`, `D_core = q^(-1/2-3h)`. Two *different* equations —
`E_core = 0` and `D_core = −1` — with the **same threshold `h = 1/6`**, which the test **checks**
rather than the docstring asserting. Controls: `h = 1/6` **fails**, `h = 1/5` fails, `h = 1/7`
passes.

## §4. WHAT PAYS FOR COMPACT SUPPORT OF `f`

**The cutoff is free.** `c = χ_x χ_t` on `(A, B, p_loc)`, **then** curl (so incompressibility
survives), then zero-extension. Spatial support in `K` and vanishing near `t = 0`: immediate.

**What is not free is that the resulting `f` is SMOOTH THROUGH `t = 1`.** Cut off an object whose
residual blows up at the singular time and you get a force that blows up at the singular time.
Such an `f` is not in `C∞_c(ℝ³ × (0,∞))` and does not satisfy Fefferman **(5)**.

Three things buy it:

1. **the flatness estimate (3.4)** — `|∂ₓ^α ∂ₜ^b R(u,p)| ≤ C q^N` for every `N`. Bought by
   **Sections 4–9 and Appendices A–C**.
2. **Theorem 3.1(ii)** — every derivative of `A`, `Be_θ`, `p` uniformly bounded up to the
   one-sided boundary `τ = 0` away from the origin, with compatible limits. Without it the
   **cutoff-transition** terms have no limit at `t = 1` even if the residual near the origin does.
3. **the exact heat exterior (3.5)** — `A = 0`, `B = K(r,τ) = r^{-1-2h}H_ext(τ/r²)` solving the
   radial swirl heat equation. Bought by **Appendix A**.

Then, and only then, Lemma 10.2 gives the derivative limits at `t = 1` and **Lemma 10.3** realises
them from `t > 1` by a Borel-type sum `Σ_j χ₀(b_jσ) σ^j F_j / j!`, support in `K × [0,2]`.
