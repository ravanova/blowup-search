### The headline: the obstruction is in print, peer-reviewed

Chen–Hou–Huang, **CPAM 74: 1282–1350 (2021)**, doi `10.1002/cpa.21991`, **§2.3**:

> *"the linearized operator `L` is not a compact operator due to the Hilbert transform
> `u_x = Hω` and the non-compact part of `L` cannot be treated as a small perturbation. Thus we
> cannot approximate the linearized operator by a finite rank operator which can be estimated
> using numerical computation."*

The finite-block-plus-tail decomposition, declared unavailable by the authors of a
peer-reviewed certified inviscid blow-up. **Precision recorded and not elided:** their stated
reason is the Hilbert transform's non-compactness, this repository's measured obstruction is the
far-field block's **shift** structure. Related, not identical; the identification is **not**
asserted.

## Corrections to the prediction as it was written

`TECHNICAL_P2_ROUTEL1_V2.md` phrases the law **dissipative vs inviscid**. Two rows show that is
a proxy: **DS-NLS** (`2406.16597`) is certified, self-similar, blow-up, and has *no dissipative
term* — its unbounded part `−iΔ` **is** a multiplier, so a diagonal tail was available and was
**not used** (1 `Fourier` in 83 pp; the profile is an ODE). The defensible sharpening is
**whether an infinite-dimensional linearization must be inverted at all**, not viscosity. This is
a narrowing, not an upgrade, and §3.5 of the novelty file states what it does not license.
