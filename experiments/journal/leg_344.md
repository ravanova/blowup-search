## 1. The label under test: "Hou's own PINN/KAN machinery"

The first finding is a correction to leg 342's own phrasing, made by reading `2506.19243` to
full text rather than trusting the abstract snippet leg 342 read. `2506.19243` mentions
"Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs)" exactly once, as a citation in its Related Works section
to *other authors'* papers on KAN — it is never used in this paper's own experiments. The
architecture actually demonstrated is a plain MLP (tanh, 4×20 for 1D Burgers; SiLU, 7×30 for
2D Boussinesq), trained with Adam then self-scaled BFGS.

The KAN half of leg 342's label traces instead to a *different* paper: `arXiv:2604.16842`
(Y. Wang's own PhD thesis, 18 Apr 2026), which does introduce a KAN architecture — but applies
it to the Nonlinear Heat equation, Complex Ginzburg-Landau equation, and 3D Keller-Segel with
logistic damping. Navier-Stokes appears in the thesis abstract only as motivating context
("one of the seven Clay Prize problems"); Euler is not mentioned at all.

**So: no single paper combines PINN and KAN on a fluid-singularity problem.** "Hou's PINN/KAN
machinery" is a label for two distinct tools from two distinct papers by overlapping authors,
neither of which has been jointly applied to the target problem class. This matters for
costing: a retarget that wants the KAN half is not retargeting demonstrated machinery — it is
building KAN-for-fluid-singularity from scratch, because that combination doesn't exist yet
anywhere in the literature found this leg (both search-instrument passes, see novelty file
§1 for the WEAK-flagged zeros that were checked and NOT banked as confirming this by absence
— the claim rests on positive enumeration of what does exist, not on a search returning zero).

## 2. The successor/competitor line 342 didn't have: arXiv:2509.14185

Leg 342's net (dispatched under a different search session) did not surface
`arXiv:2509.14185`, "Discovery of Unstable Singularities" (Wang, Bennani, Martens, Racanière,
Blackwell, Matthews, Nikolov, Cao-Labora, Park, Arjovsky, Worrall, Qin, Alet, Kozlovskii,
Tomašev, Davies, Kohli, Buckmaster, Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Jiang, Lai — Google DeepMind
paired with Tristan Buckmaster and Javier Gómez-Serrano, two of the field's leading rigorous
fluid-singularity authors, plus Gonzalo Cao-Labora and Ching-Yao Lai), submitted 17 Sep 2025,
v1 only (`v2` checked directly, returns HTTP 404 — confirmed no update exists).

This is a materially different and more capable machine than `2506.19243`'s. Read to full
HTML text (not abstract-only, since the abstract truncates hard under the tool's quoting
limit):

- **Systems solved**: CCF model (1D, whole-space, explicitly a proxy for 3D Euler/NS), 2D IPM
  with boundary, 2D Boussinesq with boundary (stated by the authors as "mathematically
  analogous to the incompressible 3D Euler equations under the assumption of axial symmetry").
  All stationary self-similar. None non-axisymmetric, none 3D, none boundary-free, none
  time-periodic.
- **What's new relative to Hou's line**: this is the first systematic discovery of *unstable*
  self-similar singularities (not just stable ones), with an empirical asymptotic formula
  relating blow-up rate to instability order for IPM/Boussinesq.
- **Architecture**: small MLPs with compactified (q,β) coordinates and hand-structured
  "solution envelopes" enforcing symmetry/decay/origin behaviour, trained with a full-matrix
  Gauss-Newton optimizer (kfac-jax) plus a second-stage Fourier-feature network for residual
  correction. No KAN component.
- **Precision**: stable/first-unstable CCF solutions reach residuals O(1e-13) — near
  double-float machine precision, "constrained only by the round-off errors of the GPU
  hardware" in the authors' own words. IPM/Boussinesq range O(1e-11) to O(1e-7). A 4th
  unstable Boussinesq candidate is explicitly flagged by the authors as unresolved.
- **Failure modes, in the authors' own words** (the strongest, most explicit failure-mode
  record found in this literature): standard optimizers (Adam, L-BFGS) are "insufficient for
  producing high quality solutions to the self-similar equations"; gradient-based training of
  the eigenvalue λ is "unreliable for discovering unstable, high-order solutions"; CCF
  higher-order profiles have "extremely sharp gradients," suspected to trap optimization in
  local minima; the authors explicitly warn against "overoptimism in using PINNs as
  general-purpose PDE solvers"; artificial-viscosity regularization (a technique from Hou's
  prior work) "raises unresolved questions about whether such regularized solutions still
  relate to the original equations."
- **Explicitly stated open problem, in the authors' own words**: "a key challenge is to
  discover self-similar solutions to the incompressible 3D Euler equations (or similar
  equations such as IPM) in the absence of boundary." This is named as unattempted future
  work requiring better qualitative understanding to guide future network design — not solved,
  not claimed solved, anywhere in this paper.

## 3. Inherit vs rebuild, mapped onto leg 342's five-module floor

Leg 342's costed floor: 5 modules total (3D non-axisymmetric NS solver in similarity
variables; 3D Leray/Biot-Savart formulation; unbounded-domain discretisation; time-periodic
PINN/KAN technique — four gap modules against a baseline of one already-demonstrated PINN
capability), at 342/49 ≈ 6.98 legs/module ≈ 35 legs.

This leg's inventory does not remove any module from that gap — nothing found is 3D,
non-axisymmetric, boundary-free, or time-periodic anywhere in the literature searched. What it
does is reclassify *which existing partial art* each remaining module would draw on:

- **Unbounded-domain discretisation module**: PARTIAL INHERIT confirmed twice, from two
  independent lines — `2506.19243`'s compactified-coordinate + hard-parity-constraint
  technique, and (better-validated) `2509.14185`'s compactified (q,β) coordinates +
  solution-envelope structuring.
- **Optimizer/training-regime module** (not separately named in 342's floor but implicit in
  "PINN/KAN technique"): the retarget's better base is `2509.14185`'s Gauss-Newton +
  multi-stage Fourier-correction discipline, reported by its own authors as superior to the
  Adam/SSBroyden discipline in `2506.19243`. This is new information this leg adds: 342 had
  only the weaker base to point at.
- **KAN component specifically**: NO EXISTING ART on the target problem class. `2604.16842`
  is the only fluid-singularity-thesis-adjacent paper using KAN at all, and it never touches
  NS/Euler. If the retarget wants KAN rather than plain PINN, that piece is a from-scratch
  build with zero literature discount — this is new, sharper information than 342 had (342's
  label implied KAN was already part of a working NS/Euler-adjacent tool; it is not).
- **3D non-axisymmetric NS solver + Leray/Biot-Savart modules**: REBUILD, unchanged from 342 —
  now reinforced by a second, more recent, more authoritative source (`2509.14185`'s own
  authors) explicitly naming boundary-free 3D Euler as their own next open problem, not
  something either line has approached.
- **Time-periodic (DSS) technique module**: REBUILD, unchanged from 342 — now confirmed against
  a second, independently-evolved literature line. Zero papers found by either search pass, in
  either the Hou line or the DeepMind/Buckmaster/Gómez-Serrano line, address time-periodic
  self-similar profiles. Every hit is stationary self-similar.

## 4. Net effect on the 35-leg number

**The floor arithmetic is not moved: still ≈35 legs.** No module is discounted further and no
new module is added. What changes is the quality and specificity of the estimate's inputs:

1. A labelling error in 342's costing text is corrected: "PINN/KAN machinery" was never one
   demonstrated tool; it is two separate tools from two separate papers, and neither has been
   applied to the target problem, so the KAN half of any inherit claim was never available to
   discount against in the first place — dropping KAN from the retarget plan (using plain
   PINN/Gauss-Newton machinery instead) changes nothing in the cost, since KAN's contribution
   to the 35-leg number was already effectively zero.
2. A stronger, more recent, better-documented base for the PINN half is now named:
   `arXiv:2509.14185`'s Gauss-Newton/envelope architecture over `2506.19243`'s Adam/SSBroyden
   architecture — same module-floor cost, better starting technique, and a much more explicit
   failure-mode record to plan risk against.
3. The DSS/time-periodic gap, which is the single largest structural risk in the 35-leg
   number, is now confirmed against two independent literature lines rather than one — this
   raises confidence in the "REBUILD, no discount" read for that module, it does not lower the
   estimate.
