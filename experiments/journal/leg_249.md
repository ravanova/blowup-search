### A quadrature defect that was real, was caught, and is kept (lessons 76, 86)

The first rule was a single `y = sinh(u)` trapezoid, `|u| ≤ 7`. It gave **5.75e−4** on the
`L²` identity and **4e−15** on the second-derivative identity — *eleven orders apart on the
same nodes*, which is the tell. The cause is not the trapezoid: `|φ|²` decays only like

while `|φ″|² ~ y^{−6}` has none. Replaced by the double exponential `y = sinh(sinh u)`, cut at
`y ~ 1.7e19`; both integrals then reach `1.3e−15`. The accuracy is a property of the integrand
again, not of the cut.

## 10. W5/W6/W7

**W5 — `σ_min` is convention-relative, and nothing had said so.** The `X ⊕ ℂ` Gram puts weight
`1` on the border amplitude while the `X` block carries the bare Laguerre normalization (no
`2π`, no half-line `½`). That is a *choice*. Sweeping the weight over `10⁻²…10²` moves `σ_min`
across `0.01086 → 0.13580`, a factor of **12.5**; adopting Xu's own `y`-space normalization for
the `X` block (i.e. carrying the `2π`) gives **0.0420** instead of 0.0908. Reported, not called
a defect: the property leg 176's gate turns on — bounded away from zero uniformly in the
truncation — **is invariant**, since a positive weight cannot send a positive limit to zero.
The *digits* are not invariant, and PUB2 quotes the digits without the convention.

**W6 — Xu (4.23) by exact partial fractions; the residual identity proved, not sampled.**
`G = b²f` is rational for finite Laguerre data, so `G₂/t² = Q₃/b⁴` with `Q₃` cubic and
`∫₀^y G₂/t²dt` has an exact antiderivative — one principal log on a path that never leaves the
upper half-plane, plus an algebraic part in exact rationals so nothing cancels at small `|y|`.
None of leg 176's Taylor/Gauss-panel/grading machinery enters. With `u = −Φ/b²`,
`Φ = yA + c₀ + c₁y`, `c₀ = −G₀`, `A′ = G₂/y²`:

```
    −u − y u′ + i u/b = (y Φ′ − Φ)/b²        (using −2y − i = −2b)
    y Φ′ − Φ = G − G₁ y
    so   L₀⁺u − f  =  −G₁ y / b(y)²  =  −ℓ(f) y / b(y)²     IDENTICALLY
```

`G′(0) = ℓ(f)` confirmed as an exact rational identity on six random data. Consequences: on the
solvability subspace Xu's closed form solves the equation **exactly**, so leg 176's banked
`4.767e−15` measures its own floating-point residual assembly (lesson 86), not an approximation
error — against the exact evaluation its `u(y)` is correct to **6.74e−16**, i.e. leg 176 is
**conservative about itself by 7.1×**. **Control that could have gone either way:** driving
`ℓ(f)` over six decades *off* the solvability subspace leaves the module's discrepancy against
the exact residual flat at **1.33×** spread over a `1e8` change in `ℓ(f)`. A real defect would
have grown by `1e8`. It did not.

One difference from the 192 salvage, at a place where being right off the subspace matters: it
uses `Φ′ = A + (G − G₀)/y + c₁`, which differs from the correct `Φ′ = A + yQ₃/b⁴ + c₁` by
