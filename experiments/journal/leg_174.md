## The distinction the whole question turns on, and nobody had made it

"A viscous certified blow-up" silently names two different things. Once they are separated the
literature answers immediately, and the answer is not the one either branch of the gate
expected.

> **GRADE A** — the certificate is *on the dissipative object*. The interval arithmetic encloses
> a solution of an equation that **itself carries the dissipative term**.
>
> **GRADE B** — the *theorem* is about the dissipative PDE, and a computer-assisted step is an
> essential, non-removable ingredient of its proof — but the **enclosed object may be an
> inviscid auxiliary**, with an analytic argument transferring it to the viscous equation.

Grade A implies Grade B. Cross that with fluid-adjacency and the literature fills in like this
(`writeup/data/p2_route_vbs_v1_scoping.json`, `occupancy_matrix`):

| | **Grade A** (certificate on the viscous object) | **Grade B** (theorem viscous, certificate inviscid) |
|---|---|---|
| **fluid-adjacent** | **— EMPTY —** | `arXiv:2208.09445` (compressible Navier–Stokes) |
| **not fluid-adjacent** | `arXiv:2410.05480` (CGL) | — |

**The rung is occupied twice over, and never in the cell the question meant.**

## Gate (a): YES — and it is a completed argument, not a branch short of one

`arXiv:2410.05480`'s CGL theorems are stated as existence results **about the PDE**, not about
the ODE. Theorem 4.1, verbatim:

> *"In Case I (d = 1, σ = 2.3) there exists (at least) 8 continuous curves,
> {(ϵ_j(s), µ_j(s), κ_j(s))}, such that for each s ∈ [0,1] and each j, **the CGL equation with
> ϵ = ϵ_j(s) has a self-similar singular solution** of the form u(x,t) = … Q(…)"*

and Theorem 1.2 at the ϵ = 0 end of the same branches:

> *"There exists 8 nontrivial radial self-similar singular solutions **u** of the form (2) with
> Q ∈ C^∞([0,∞)) ∩ L³([0,∞)) **satisfying Equation (1)**"*

**Nothing is missing between the certified profile and the singular solution.** The step is
exact substitution of Ansatz (2) into Equation (1) — algebra, not analysis. There is no
"blow-up argument" left to complete, which is precisely what the gate's no-branch would have
required. And the dissipation is **inside** the certified object: the enclosed ODE (3)/(4)/(5)
carries the factor `(1 − iϵ)` throughout, and Theorem 4.1's branches satisfy `ϵ′_j(0) > 0`, so
ϵ is strictly positive along them. **Grade A.**

**A methodological note that belongs in the record.** Asked this question against the
*abstract*, an automated summariser answered: *"The abstract claims existence of self-similar
profiles solving an ODE, not blow-up solutions of the PDE itself."* That is **wrong**, and only
the full text shows it. This repository has been caught by abstract-depth reading three times
(legs 53, 64, and the ledger row leg 63 wrote); this is a fourth instance, caught **before**
anything was banked on it.

### The three qualifications, stated so they cannot be over-read

None of these makes the argument incomplete. All three are why it does not answer the user's
question.

1. **Infinite mass.** `Q(ξ) ∼ ξ^{−1/σ − iω/κ}`, and DF themselves record `Q ∈ L³` and **not**
   `L²` in Case I. It is an exact singular solution, not a blow-up from localised finite-mass
   Cauchy data.
2. **Stability unproved.** Remark 1.6: *"Proving these stability results would be of great
   interest."*
3. **Not a fluid.** CGL is a semilinear scalar equation for a complex field: no transport
   nonlinearity, no incompressibility, no Biot–Savart law.

**What makes it a strong occupant anyway, and this is the part worth the user's attention:**
the dissipation is *not* weak. The `ϵΔu` term enters at **exactly the same scaling order** as
the dispersive term, so it is scale-invariant under the blow-up scaling — structurally the same
position viscosity occupies for backward-self-similar Navier–Stokes. The blow-up is not
surviving a dissipation that scaling already defeated. Leg 48 measured the same thing from the
other side: switching dissipation on *improves* the conditioning 26×, until the branch folds.

## The row the gate did not anticipate, and it is the headline

`arXiv:2208.09445` — **Buckmaster, Cao-Labora, Gómez-Serrano, *Smooth imploding solutions for
3D compressible fluids*** (Forum of Mathematics Pi 13 (2025) e6). Theorem 1.3, verbatim:

> *"The initial data (u₀, ρ₀) is smooth and has finite energy … At time T, the solution (u, ρ)
> becomes singular at the origin: for any ε > 0, limsup_{t→T} |u(R,t)| = ∞ and
> lim_{t→T} ρ(0,t) = ∞."*

for equation (1.2) — the **3D isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations**, with Lamé
viscosities `µ₁ > 0`, `2µ₁ + µ₂ > 0`, and initial density **constant at infinity** (their own
stated reason: *"in order to rule out the possibility that the singularities are an artifact of
vacuum"*). Smooth data, finite energy, a genuine viscous fluid equation, a finite-time
singularity, stable modulo finite codimension.

And the computer is essential to it (§1.3):

> *"Furthermore, we employ a computer-assisted proof to compute the first 10000 coefficient
> pairs (W_j, Z_j) at r = r\*, with rigorous error bounds. … In order to perform rigorous,
> error-free calculations, interval arithmetic will be used as part of the proof whenever
> needed. … This part of the calculation takes about 14 hours on a single CPU."*

γ = 7/5 — the diatomic case, the one Theorem 1.3 is stated for — is **precisely** the case that
needs it: §5 is the γ = 7/5 analysis Theorem 1.2's profile rests on, its inequalities are proved
by the computer-assisted Lemmas A.27/A.28, and Appendix B is *"Implementation details of the
computer-assisted part"*. Theorem 1.3 then consumes Theorem 1.2's profile. **Remove the computer
and the theorem does not stand as published.**

### Why it is Grade B and not Grade A, which is the honest limit of the claim

The object the interval arithmetic encloses is the self-similar profile solving system (1.5) —
the ODE reduction of the **inviscid** compressible Euler system (1.3). Navier–Stokes is reached
from it by the analytic stability argument of §§7–8. The authors are explicit about the price
(§7, opening):

> *"The stability for the Euler equation will follow in general, while in the Navier-Stokes case
> we need to restrict the parameter r to a regime where **the self-similar profile dominates the
> dissipation**."*

So the viscous term is **dominated by choosing the self-similar exponent**, not overcome at its
own scaling — it becomes an exponentially decaying forcing in self-similar time. That is the
sharp structural contrast with DF-CGL, where ϵ sits at the same scaling order as the leading
term and cannot be scaled away. **Two occupants, two different senses, neither one the cell the
question meant.**

## Gate (b): the fallback catalog, seven entries

Reported regardless of (a), as the gate requires. Full rows with locators and quotes in the
JSON; **a catalog is not a claim** and nothing here is escalated on its own.

| key | model | dissipation | analytic proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MRRS-NS` | 3D compressible Navier–Stokes | full viscosity | Merle–Raphaël–Rodnianski–Szeftel, Ann. of Math. 196 (2022), Bôcher Prize 2023 |
| `KVW-PRANDTL` | Prandtl boundary layer | viscous, NS-derived | Kukavica–Vicol–Wang, Adv. Math. 307 (2017) 288–311 |
| `KNS-FRACBURGERS` | fractal Burgers | `−(−Δ)^α`, blow-up for α < 1/2 | Kiselev–Nazarov–Shterenberg, Dyn. PDE 5 (2008) 211–240 |
| `TAO-AVGNS` | averaged 3D Navier–Stokes | **full 3D viscosity + exact energy identity** | Tao, JAMS 29 (2016) 601–674 |
| `CHEN-GCLM-DISS` | dissipative gCLM | `ν Λ^γ` | J. Chen, Nonlinearity 33 (2020) 2502 — **leg 125's retired candidate** |
| `MILLER-HYPO` | Fourier-restricted hypodissipative NS | hypodissipative | `arXiv:2307.03434` |
| `PALASEK-SHELL` | shell model of 3D NS | full viscosity | `arXiv:2605.13827` |

Three readings worth having:

* **`MRRS-NS` is the template of what a certificate buys.** Its hypothesis is, in BCG's own
  words (§1.1.2), *"described in terms of the non-vanishing of an analytic function. This
  condition is not proven for any specific γ; however it may be checked numerically."* An
  analytic theorem, true for *almost every* γ, constructive at *none* — and BCG then discharged
  it at γ = 7/5 with interval arithmetic. That is the cleanest available demonstration that
  certification is doing real mathematical work in this area, not decoration.
* **`TAO-AVGNS` is the only entry that does not weaken the dissipation.** It keeps full 3D
  viscosity and the exact energy identity and weakens the *nonlinearity* instead. Its caveat is
  correspondingly sharp: the averaged bilinear operator is engineered for the blow-up, so
  certifying it would certify a constructed counterexample.
* **`PALASEK-SHELL` is the most tractable target on the list**, and this is the only
  forward-looking remark in the catalog: a shell model *is* a countable ODE system, which is the
  setting rigorous numerics is strongest in. Recorded as an observation, not a proposal.

## Why the rung stayed empty after leg 125, stated precisely

Leg 125 was aiming at **exactly the empty cell** — fluid-adjacent **and** Grade A, a certificate
on a dissipative fluid equation's own self-similar object — and its candidate dissolved on
literature grounds before any numerics could decide anything. Chen `arXiv:1908.09385` contains
no γ = 2 dissipative self-similar profile at all; §2.6's last line is *"Since ν(t) converges to
0, such profile is the same as the inviscid profile associated with a."*

**So the cell is empty for want of a TARGET, not for want of a method.** The method exists and
is already Grade A on a non-fluid parabolic equation: `arXiv:2404.04054` runs
Newton–Kantorovich in a weighted Sobolev space on self-similar profiles of parabolic PDEs
**including viscous Burgers** — a Grade-A certificate on a dissipative object, already in this
repository's own ledger as `ADJACENT`. What has never been found is a *dissipative fluid
self-similar profile worth enclosing*: every fluid blow-up profile the literature certifies is
inviscid, and every viscous fluid blow-up the literature proves either has no self-similar
profile (Prandtl, Tao) or reaches viscosity from an inviscid profile (BCG, MRRS).

That is a sharper statement of the user's point than the user's own framing, and it survives
this leg intact — with its scope corrected.
