## 2. The enriched basis

**Construction.** `u = -log(1-X)` sends the boundary branch point at `X=1` to infinity and
turns `(1-X)^{1-iκ} = exp(-(1-iκ)u)` into an ENTIRE function of `u` — pure exponential decay
times oscillation, no branch anywhere finite. `u` is then compactified by a single fixed Boyd
algebraic map `v = u/(u+L)`, so `X -> v` is one composite map, Chebyshev-in-`v` is again a
basis on `[0,1]`, and the essential singularity that remains sits only at `v=1` (where the
function is `C^∞`-flat, not branched).

**L is fixed, not per-κ tuned.** A 10-point sweep (`L ∈ {0.1,...,64}`, `N=2^15`) was run for
every κ row and is banked in the JSON (`step2_enriched_basis.L_sweep_rows`); `L=16.0`, frozen
and reused unchanged for every κ, sits within 5% of the sweep's own best mode count at every
row (in fact it IS the sweep optimum at every row tested) — so the comparison below is one
instrument across five κ values, not five separately tuned ones.

**Measured mode counts, `N=2^17`, `1e-6` relative truncation, `L=16` fixed:**

| `κ` | baseline (plain-X) | enriched (v-map) | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 823 | **32** | 25.72× |
| 2 | 1482 | **54** | 27.44× |
| 5 | 3564 | **126** | 28.29× |
| 10 | 7086 | **249** | 28.46× |
| 20 | 14149 | **496** | 28.53× |

**Resolution-stability check** (leg 313's own S3.7 lesson, carried to the new instrument): the
enriched-basis mode count is IDENTICAL — not just close — across `N ∈ {2^14, 2^15, 2^16,
2^17}` for every κ row. This is not an array artefact; it is the object's own cost under the
new basis.

## 3. Two controls, each able to come out differently (lesson 90)

**`κ=0` control.** On plain-X, `(1-X)^1` is a linear polynomial and terminates at 2 modes
(leg 313's own control, reproduced). Under the v-map, the SAME object costs **15** modes —
worse, not better. This is expected and reported, not hidden: the map exists to buy the `κ≠0`
boundary defect, not to compress an already-polynomial function, and a control that always
"won" under the transform would not be a real control.

**Falsification control: the smooth function, mapped.** Leg 313's own asymmetric-entire smooth
control (no boundary singularity at all) carried through the identical v-map costs **31**
modes — *more* than the 10 it needs directly on plain-X. If the enriched basis had made this
control CHEAPER too, the 25–28× factor above would be a transform artefact (compression always
wins) rather than a genuine property of matching the basis to the boundary-singularity type.
It did not: the map is a net loss on a function it was not built for, which is the falsifiable
shape this leg needed and got.

## 4. Verdict

basis, at the identical `1e-6` truncation, on a single fixed map scale. **Enrichment factor
25.7×–28.5×, mildly increasing and saturating with `κ`** (rather than flat), reported as a
range because that is what was measured, not compressed into one number.
