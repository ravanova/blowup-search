## The two near-misses, at full strength — because "NO" alone would misrepresent the record

**`arXiv:1902.00384` (2019-02-01) — van den Berg, Breden, Lessard, van Veen, *Spontaneous
periodic orbits in the Navier–Stokes flow*.** Forced autonomous Navier–Stokes **on the
three-torus**, Taylor–Green forcing, Newton–Kantorovich, computer-assisted, geometrically
decaying Fourier coefficients. **Maxime Breden has already done Grade-A CAP on genuine 3D
Navier–Stokes with the viscous term inside the certified equation.** It fails this gate strictly
and on two counts: **five years BEFORE** the parent, so not "subsequent"; and it certifies a
**periodic orbit of a smooth flow** — a regular solution — not a self-similar blow-up profile.
Wrong direction in time, wrong object in kind. **But the group's fluid capability is
demonstrated, not hypothetical**, and a follow-up leg should start here rather than at the parent.

**`arXiv:2607.13567` (2026-07-15) — Cadiot & Haziot, overhanging periodic gravity water waves
with constant vorticity.** Highest fluid grade anywhere in the closed set: genuinely fluid,
**explicitly vorticity-bearing**, computer-assisted Newton–Kantorovich, three weeks old. Fails
the conjunct the gate names, **structurally rather than marginally**: the model is **inviscid**
(Euler free-boundary), so there is **no viscous term to enclose** — the property that made `[BC]`
Grade A is *absent*, not weaker. Also: steady wave on a bifurcation branch, not a blow-up
profile; conformal mapping + global bifurcation, not the parent's H²(e^{|x|²/4}) eigenbasis;
neither parent author on it; and its "constant vorticity" is a **prescribed background shear**,
not evolving vortex dynamics.

## The finding, which is not the absence

Two verbatim locators from the parent's own md5-pinned extraction
(`ff7a34b776bfe5edf97397e5eabdbb7a`, 2060 lines):

**Line 1669 confirms this repository's own ledger is right.** The paper's framework is stated on
ℝ^d, which could have made "1D viscous Burgers" look like a transcription error. It is not:
*"We focus on a generalised viscous Burgers equation on ℝ₊ with Neumann boundary condition"* —
`∂_t v + v²∂_x v = ∂_xx v`, similarity `t^{−1/4}u(x/√t)`, profile `Lu − u/4 + u²∂_x u = 0`.
**The framework is d-general; the viscous example is not.**

**Line 1687, Remark 40, is the leg:**

> We deal with a one-dimensional example here for simplicity, but terms like **(u·∇)u** could in
> principle also be handled **in dimension d ∈ {2,3}**, as u ∈ H²(μ) is then still enough to
> guarantee that (u·∇)u ∈ L²(μ) since u ∈ L^∞(ℝ^d).

`(u·∇)u` **is** the Navier–Stokes/Euler nonlinearity. The precedent's own authors state their
Grade-A machinery reaches it in d = 2 or 3 in principle, **and give the reason**.

And **28 months later**, the same group's most recent paper (`2603.27198`, 2026-03-28, line 273)
says extending merely from an interval to a **higher-dimensional rectangle** — far short of a
fluid domain — *"is non-trivial but will be studied in a future work."*

**So leg 174's empty Grade-A/fluid cell is not unoccupied for want of anyone noticing it.** It is
flagged tractable-in-principle by the group best placed to fill it, at one end, and bounded by
that same group's own statement of difficulty at the other. **The gap between what is reachable
in principle and what has been done is the finding, and the authors documented it at both ends
themselves.**
