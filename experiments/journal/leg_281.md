**The mechanism, stated once.** Every **CF** row is convention-free for the *same* reason
leg 277 gave for `B1`: the quantity carries **no border coordinate**, so `κ` multiplies domain
and range Grams alike and cancels in the Rayleigh quotient. Every **CR** row mixes an `X`-norm
with a border amplitude carrying a *different* weight, so `κ` rescales only one of two summands
and cannot cancel. **`D1` is the single row where it reaches exactly one factor**, giving the
only closed-form conversion law in the certificate.

## 3. Q4, the finding this leg exists for

**PUB2's §3.5 argument does not rest on `0.0908`** — §0 already caveats that digit. It rests on
the ladder *flattening*, and L437 states that in the very sentence that says what survives the
convention freedom: *"a monotone-decreasing ladder that **flattens** (0.0908878 → 0.090804 over
`N = 64…512`, **0.0920 %**; 0.139 % over the full 16-fold range)"*.

**Measured: that digit is the most convention-sensitive number in the entire enumeration.**

* over the sweep it runs **`1.76e−07 … 3.05e−03`** — a factor **`17321`**, i.e. **`139×` more
  sensitive than `σ_min` itself**, the number PUB2 does caveat;
* across just the **three named readings** it is `0.140 % / 0.052 % / 0.027 %` — a factor
  **`5.16`**, so even restricted to defensible conventions the quoted `0.139 %` moves five-fold;
* the quoted value sits at the **repo** convention, whose spread (`0.140 %`) is the
  **fourth-largest of the eleven** — the flattening looks **better**, by `2.7×` and `5.2×`, in
  both of Xu's readings than in the one PUB2 prints, and worst of all at `κ = 0.1` (`0.305 %`).

**But the claim the digit supports survives.** The ladder is **monotone-decreasing in all
eleven conventions**. So §3.5 contains two sentences printed as one: *"the ladder is
monotone-decreasing and flattens"* — **CF, and now measured to be so, across eight decades** —
and *"it flattens to `0.139 %`"* — **CR, at `17321×`, and nowhere disclosed**. Leg 277 §7's
"the gate answer stands unchanged" now has a measured basis rather than an argued one, and it
is the *qualitative* half that carries it.

## 4. The comparison that mixes classes — and survives

PUB2 §4.5 reconciles legs 163 and 176 by **`0.0908 ≤ 0.71465`**. The left side is **CR**; the
right side is **CF** (`1.87e−16`). A comparison between a convention-relative number and a
convention-free one is exactly the defect family legs 270/276/277 each found one instance of,
so this leg stress-tested it on a **61-point fine sweep over twelve decades**, `κ ∈ [10⁻⁶,10⁶]`:

* `sup_κ σ_min = ` **`0.13580`**, attained at `κ = 0.1`;
* the inequality **holds at every one of the 61 points**, with headroom **`5.26×`**;
* and `sup_κ σ_min` equals **leg 249's own four-decade sweep maximum to `3e−14`** — leg 249's
  window already contained the global maximum, which nobody had checked.

**So PUB2's reconciliation is correct in every convention, not just in the one it is written
in.** That is a positive result and it is banked as one.

**Two corrections to PUB2's own caveat, in opposite directions.** L442–446 says *"any claim
that the digits `0.0908` and `0.71465` are convention-independent"* does not survive — and
**for `0.71465` that is wrong: it is convention-free at `1.87e−16`**, being `1/max(‖u‖_X/‖f‖_X)`
over a finite family, a ratio of `X`-norms with no border coordinate. PUB2 **overstates** the
defect there. Meanwhile **`E6`, the "optimistic by `7.87×`" factor** printed at L420 and L249,
is a CF quantity divided by a CR one and therefore fully CR: it ranges **`5.265 … 656.95`**, a
**`125×`** swing. PUB2 **understates** the defect there, and no leg has flagged it.
