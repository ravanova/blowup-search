## 3. What the literature actually says — the three levels of resolution

### A. There is a dedicated survey of exactly this hierarchy

**Cheskidov, Dai, Friedlander, "Dyadic models for fluid equations: a survey",
`arXiv:2209.10203`** (submitted 21 Sep 2022, v2 12 Apr 2023). Abstract, verbatim in part:

> "insights into the full PDEs have been sought via the study of simpler approximating systems
> that retain some of the original nonlinear features. One such simpler system is an infinite
> dimensional coupled set of nonlinear ordinary differential equations referred to a dyadic
> model. In this survey we provide a brief overview of dyadic models and describe recent
> results. In particular, we discuss results for certain dyadic models in the context of
> existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions."

Two of the three authors (Friedlander, Cheskidov) are the exact author line the leg spec named.
**A survey of the hierarchy, by the hierarchy's own authors, exists and postdates most of it.**
This alone does not settle the gate — a survey can exist without answering the placement
question — so it is recorded as level one and the sharper levels are below.

### B. The placement of 3D NS is a *published rational number*, and it is α = 2/5

This is the decisive finding, and it comes from the paper's **own source text**, not its
abstract. **Cheskidov, "Blow-up in finite time for the dyadic model of the Navier-Stokes
equations", `arXiv:math/0601074`** (v1 4 Jan 2006, v2 8 Jun 2006; Trans. AMS). The e-print
source was fetched and read directly (`dyadic1.tex`, dated 8 Jun 2006).

The model (Introduction, eq. `e:dintro`, verbatim from source) is

```
d/dt u_n + ν λ^{2αn} u_n − λ^n u_{n−1}²  + λ^{n+1} u_n u_{n+1} = g_n ,   n ∈ ℕ,  u_0 = 0
```

with `λ > 1`, viscosity `ν ≥ 0`, and **`α > 0` the dissipation degree** — the single scalar
that indexes the hierarchy. Cheskidov states that **both** Katz–Pavlović's dyadic model and
Friedlander–Pavlović's three-dimensional vector model reduce to this one system, so `α` is the
hierarchy's coordinate, not one paper's parameter.

**The calibration, verbatim from the source (Introduction, immediately after the model):**

> "Note that for $\alpha=2/5$ the following are sharp estimates on the inertial term:
> $|(B(u,u),Au)| \lesssim |Au|^{3/2}|A^{1/2}u|^{3/2}$, where $(\cdot,\cdot)$ and $|\cdot|$ are
> the $l^2$-inner product and norm respectively. **The best known estimates on the inertial term
> of the 3D NSE are the same**, with $(\cdot,\cdot)$ and $|\cdot|$ being the $L^2$-inner product
> and norm."

**That sentence is the answer to this leg's gate.** 3D Navier–Stokes sits at **α = 2/5** in the
dyadic hierarchy, and the calibration is not an analogy — it is the statement that the sharp
estimate on the model's nonlinear term at α = 2/5 is *the same estimate* as the best known one
for the 3D NSE's inertial term.

The same paragraph places 4D NS too, by the same method:

> "It is remarkable that in the critical case $\alpha =1/3$ the following are sharp estimates on
> the nonlinear term: $|(B(u,u),Au)| \lesssim |Au|^{2}|A^{1/2}u|$. Note that the best known
> estimates on the nonlinear term of the 4D NSE are exactly the same."

So the hierarchy carries a **calibrated dimension axis**: α = 1/3 ↔ 4D NS, α = 2/5 ↔ 3D NS.

### C. Where the flip is — and the punchline

The blow-up/regularity structure along that same α axis, all from the same source:

| α range | status | who |
|---|---|---|
| α < 1/4 | finite-time blow-up | **Katz–Pavlović** (the original) |
| α < 1/3 | finite-time blow-up (sharpening of the above) | **Cheskidov `math/0601074`**, main theorem |
| α = 1/3 | critical; sharp nonlinear estimate = **4D NS**'s | Cheskidov, Introduction |
| **α = 2/5** | **←— 3D NS sits here** | Cheskidov, Introduction |
| α > 1/3 | local regularity | Cheskidov |
| α ≥ 1/2 | **global regularity**; weak global attractor becomes a strong one | Cheskidov |

**`1/3 < 2/5 < 1/2`.** Numerically: `1/3 = 0.3333…`, `2/5 = 0.4`, `1/2 = 0.5`. The distance
from the blow-up threshold is `2/5 − 1/3 = 1/15 ≈ 0.0667`; the distance to the global-regularity
threshold is `1/2 − 2/5 = 1/10 = 0.1`; the open gap is `1/2 − 1/3 = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667` wide, and 3D
NS's calibration point sits `(1/15)/(1/6) = 2/5 = 40%` of the way across it. **3D NS's placement
lands strictly inside the one interval of the hierarchy that the hierarchy has not decided.**

Cheskidov says so himself, in the source, in the paper's own words:

> "Note that there is still a gap between the regions of global regularity and blow-up in finite
> time, which means that the developed technique is not sharp enough to separate these two
> behaviors. Since most of the proofs in the theory of the Navier-Stokes equations go through for
> the dyadic model, a better understanding of the dyadic or similar shell models might provide
> insight into the regularity problem for the Navier-Stokes equations."

And the Introduction's opening states the placement conclusion outright:

> "In this paper we study a dyadic model, which has similar properties to the 3D NSE, **the same
> estimates on the inertial term, and the same open question concerning the regularity of the
> solutions.**"

**The literature's answer to "where does 3D NS sit in the dyadic hierarchy" is therefore: at
α = 2/5, inside the model's own undecided window — the dyadic model reproduces 3D NS's open
problem, at NS's own calibration point, as an open problem.** That is a complete answer to the
gate's question and it has been in print since 2006.

## 4. Which structural feature flips blow-up on — also answered, in four places

The gate's second clause. The literature identifies not one but **four** distinct structural
knobs, each with a named paper and, in three of four cases, a number:

**(i) The dissipation degree α** — §3.C above. Flip somewhere in the closed-open window
`[1/3, 1/2)`. Blow-up strictly below 1/3, global regularity at and above 1/2.
`arXiv:math/0601074`.

**(ii) Sign structure / whether backward energy transfer exists.** This is the sharpest
structural flip in the whole hierarchy, and it is a *clean* one:
- The **dyadic/Katz–Pavlović** models, whose nonlinearity is forward-cascade-only with positive
  coefficients, **blow up**.
- The **sabra shell model**, which retains an NS-like sign structure and a second quadratic
  invariant, is **globally regular** — *not* conditionally, and at any positive viscosity:
  **Constantin, Levant, Titi, "Analytic Study of Shell Models of Turbulence",
  `arXiv:physics/0511075`** (9 Nov 2005, v2 4 Jul 2006): *"We prove the global regularity of
  solutions and show that the shell model has finitely many asymptotic degrees of freedom,
  specifically: a finite dimensional global attractor and globally invariant inertial
  manifolds."* Their companion **`arXiv:physics/0607060`** (7 Jul 2006) does the inviscid case:
  weak solutions exist globally, a BKM-type blow-up criterion holds, an Onsager analogue holds
  under `|u_n| ≤ C k_n^{-1/3}(√n log(n+1))^{-1}`, and there is **global regularity in the
  "two-dimensional" parameter regime**.
  Note the direction of that last clause: the shell hierarchy has its *own* 2D/3D axis and
  behaves on it the way the real equations do.
- Cheskidov–Zaya, **`arXiv:1310.7612`**, *"Regularizing Effect of the Forward Energy Cascade in
  the Inviscid Dyadic Model"* (28 Oct 2013), makes the mechanism explicit and quantitative:
  forward cascade **regularizes**, forcing every solution to `3/5` `L²`-based (or `1/10`
  `L³`-based) regularity for all positive time, conjecturally up to Onsager's `5/6` and `1/3`.
- Cheskidov–Friedlander–Pavlović, **`arXiv:math/0610814`** and **`arXiv:math/0610815`**
  (both 26 Oct 2006): the inviscid dyadic model with forcing has a unique equilibrium, **every**
  solution blows up in finite time in `H^{5/6}`, Onsager's conjecture is confirmed for the model,
  and — the striking part — after the blow-up time solutions re-enter `H^s` for `s < 5/6` for
  almost all time and the energy dissipates, with the unique equilibrium an exponential global
  attractor. Cheskidov calls this "a result of a **self-dissipation mechanism** due to the loss
  of regularity of solutions."

**(iii) Whether the model retains the enstrophy identity.** **Evan Miller,
`arXiv:2307.03434`**, *"Finite-time blowup for the Fourier-restricted Euler and hypodissipative
Navier–Stokes model equations"* (v1 7 Jul 2023, v5 28 Aug 2025). This is the *most*
structure-retaining model in the hierarchy — abstract verbatim: the models *"respect both the
energy equality and the identity for enstrophy growth from the full Euler and hypodissipative
Navier–Stokes equations respectively, which is a substantial advance over the previous
literature on Euler and Navier–Stokes model equations"*, and the `(u·∇)u` nonlinearity is
**unchanged**, only the Helmholtz projection is replaced. And the price of retaining that much
structure is that blow-up **retreats**: it is proved only for hypodissipation
`α < log(3)/(6 log 2) ≈ 0.264`, against 3D NS's `α = 1`. **The gap is `1 − 0.264 = 0.736`, and
it opens in the direction that hurts:** the more NS structure a model keeps, the further its
blow-up threshold sits from NS's own dissipation.

**(iv) The trade-off itself, stated in one sentence by the most recent entry.**
**Stan Palasek, `arXiv:2605.13827`**, *"Finite-time blow-up in an elementary model of the 3D
Navier-Stokes equations"* (13 May 2026, 20 pp) — already on this repo's books via leg 172 and
leg 174, but never before read for its *placement* content. Abstract, verbatim:

> "Previously studied models either exhibit a turbulent cascade that regularizes the
> three-dimensional viscous dynamics, or rely on highly artificial interactions not
> transparently realized in the true Euler nonlinearity."

That is the hierarchy's structural dichotomy, in the literature's own words, and it is exactly
the "which feature flips blow-up on" question the gate asks — answered as a *dichotomy between
two failure modes* rather than a single knob. Palasek then claims a model escaping both horns
(blow-up with full, "smooth" i.e. rapidly-decaying-in-frequency viscosity, with forcing; plus an
inviscid unforced case with singularity formation "just above the energy level"), and closes
with *"a discussion of the prospects for embedding the behavior of the dyadic model into the
full Euler and Navier-Stokes equations"* — i.e. the placement question is not only answered, it
is the **explicitly stated forward agenda of the newest paper in the field**.

## 5. The direct quantitative comparison papers — the gate's question as a paper title

Two papers exist whose entire purpose is this leg's gate, by the same pair of authors:

- **Vincenzi & Gibbon, "How close are shell models to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations?",
  `arXiv:2009.01583`** (3 Sep 2020, 22 pp). The title is this leg's question verbatim. Abstract,
  verbatim: *"The goal of this study is to make a rigorous comparison between shell models and
  the NSEs. It turns out that **only the estimate of the mean energy dissipation rate is the
  same in both systems.** The estimates of the velocity and its higher-order derivatives display
  a **weaker Reynolds number dependence for shell models than for the 3D NSEs.** Indeed, the
  velocity-derivative estimates for shell models are found to be equivalent to those
  corresponding to a **velocity gradient averaged version of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations
  (VGA-NSEs)**, while the velocity estimates are even milder."*

  This is a *second, independent* placement result, obtained by a completely different method
  from Cheskidov's (Reynolds-number scaling of derivative estimates rather than sharp nonlinear
  estimates), and it agrees in direction: shell models are **strictly weaker** than 3D NS, and
  their exact equivalent is named — a velocity-gradient-averaged NS.

- **Gibbon & Vincenzi, `arXiv:2412.07064`**, *"Dynamics of vorticity moments in shell models of
  turbulence: A comparison with the Navier-Stokes equations"* (10 Dec 2024, 17 pp). Abstract,
  verbatim: *"**The contribution of the nonlinear term turns out to be much weaker than its
  equivalent for the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations.** Consequently,
  pointwise-in-time estimates are shown to exist for the vorticity moments for shell models of
  any order. … it is shown that the estimates for shell models formally correspond to those for
  the Navier-Stokes equations **'on a point'**."*

  A third independent placement, in a third currency (vorticity moments / `L^{2m}` norms),
  agreeing again: the shell nonlinearity is *weaker*, and the correspondence to NS is "on a
  point" — i.e. the shell models drop precisely the spatial structure.

**This is the load-bearing redundancy for the gate.** Three independent methods —
sharp nonlinear estimates (Cheskidov 2006), Reynolds scaling of derivative estimates
(Vincenzi–Gibbon 2020), vorticity moments (Gibbon–Vincenzi 2024) — each place 3D NS relative to
the shell hierarchy, and they do not contradict each other. The placement question is not merely
answered; it is **answered three times over, and cross-checked**.

transferred to the real equations, and why Palasek's newest paper has to spend its conclusion on
